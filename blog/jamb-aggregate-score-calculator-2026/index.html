How to Calculate a JAMB Aggregate Score in 2026

AfroTools TeamAug 15, 202615 min read

A JAMB aggregate score is not one national number produced by a universal formula. It is usually an institution's screening result, calculated under that institution's rules for a specific admission cycle. The formula may use a UTME score, a post-UTME score, O-level results or another published screening component. The institution decides the method. JAMB controls the national admission framework and the Central Admissions Processing System, known as CAPS.

Open the AfroTools JAMB Screening Calculation Worksheet after you have the current formula from the institution. Enter the institution and programme, the source URL and date, the UTME and post-UTME weights, and the scores on the scales shown. The calculation runs locally in your browser and is a planning result, not an admission decision.

The primary sources in this guide were checked on August 15, 2026. JAMB's May 18, 2026 bulletin records the 2026/2027 national minimum tolerable UTME scores and the CAPS rule. The University of Lagos notice published June 9, 2026 shows why a national minimum and an institution's screening threshold are different.

Quick answer: First verify eligibility and the institution's current formula. If the published method gives the UTME and post-UTME percentage weights, normalize the UTME score from 400 to 100, multiply each score by its weight, then add the contributions. Do not assume a 50/50 split or treat the result as an admission guarantee.

Separate the five numbers candidates often call a cutoff

Many calculation errors begin before any arithmetic. A candidate hears the word "cutoff" and treats several different controls as if they were one number. Keep the labels used by the official notice, because each number answers a different question.

Number or ruleWhat it meansWhat it does not mean
UTME scoreThe candidate's JAMB examination score on a scale from 0 to 400It is not automatically an institution's final screening aggregate
National minimum tolerable scoreThe lowest national benchmark agreed for an institution type in the admission cycleIt is not a promise that every institution will screen or admit at that score
Institution screening thresholdThe minimum UTME score or other condition an institution sets for access to its screening exerciseIt is not necessarily the final programme admission score
Screening aggregateThe result of the institution's published method for combining the required componentsIt is not valid if it was calculated with guessed weights or an old-cycle formula
Programme admission cutoffA programme-level decision applied during the institution's admission processIt is not established by a third-party calculator or an unsupported social post

JAMB's bulletin states that the 2026/2027 national minimum tolerable score is 150 for universities, 150 for colleges of nursing and 100 for polytechnics. It also says institutions that fixed higher minimum scores for their programmes may retain them. No institution may admit below the national benchmark or below the higher minimum it set for itself.

That distinction is visible in the University of Lagos notice. UNILAG required 2026/2027 candidates to have chosen the university as first choice and scored 200 or above before applying for its post-UTME online screening. It also required five relevant O-level credit passes at one sitting, including English Language and Mathematics. A university candidate with 150 meets the national floor, but does not thereby meet UNILAG's published 200 screening gate.

Check programme eligibility before calculating

An aggregate cannot repair the wrong subject combination or a missing required credit. Start with JAMB's IBASS Eligibility Check. The live checker asks for entry mode, institution type, institution, programme and O-level details. It also checks requirements such as credit and pass grades, A-level subjects where applicable, and the UTME subject combination.

Use the exact programme name and entry mode. A requirement for Direct Entry should not be copied into a UTME calculation, and the requirements for one programme should not be carried into another because both sit in the same faculty. If the result flags a subject or grade mismatch, reopen the institution's current admission notice or official brochure and resolve that issue before relying on a score worksheet.

Then review the institution's own notice for conditions that IBASS or arithmetic alone cannot settle. These may include first-choice status, an age rule, the number of O-level sittings, a registration window, a post-UTME test, an upload deadline or an institution portal step. Only include a condition when the current official notice actually states it.

For example, UNILAG's June 9 notice required five relevant credits at one sitting, participation in its online aptitude test and an O-level upload to JAMB CAPS and the UNILAG portal by the stated deadline. Those are eligibility and process controls. A calculated aggregate does not replace them.

Collect the current formula as evidence

Before opening a calculator, save enough evidence for another person to reproduce the method. The strongest source is the institution's official admissions page, registrar notice, screening portal instruction or signed publication for the current cycle. A coaching blog can help you discover a question, but it should not be the authority for weights, score scales or deadlines.

Record all of the following:

  • The institution and exact programme.
  • The admission cycle, such as 2026/2027.
  • The official page title, URL and publication or access date.
  • Every component included in the screening calculation.
  • The original scale for each component, such as UTME out of 400 and post-UTME out of 100.
  • The percentage weight assigned to each component.
  • Any stated screening benchmark, rounding rule or tie-breaking rule.
  • Eligibility conditions that sit outside the formula.

Check that the weights add to 100 percent if the institution expresses them as percentage shares. If they do not, stop. You may have mixed two methods, copied a raw-score allocation as a percentage, missed a component or found an incomplete explanation. Do not adjust the weights yourself to force the total to 100.

Also confirm whether the post-UTME result is already on a 100-point scale. If an institution reports that test on another maximum, use only its published conversion. Dividing by a convenient number without authority changes the result and makes the worksheet unauditable.

Use the normalized weighted formula

The AfroTools worksheet supports a method in which UTME is entered on its 0 to 400 scale, post-UTME is entered on a 0 to 100 scale, and the two percentage weights total 100. Under that method, the calculation has four steps:

  1. Normalize UTME: UTME score divided by 4. This converts the 400-point score to a 100-point scale.
  2. Calculate the UTME contribution: normalized UTME multiplied by the UTME weight, then divided by 100.
  3. Calculate the post-UTME contribution: post-UTME score multiplied by the post-UTME weight, then divided by 100.
  4. Add the contributions: UTME contribution plus post-UTME contribution equals the screening aggregate on a 100-point scale.

This formula is valid only when it matches the institution's published method and score scales. If the institution assigns raw points, includes O-level grades, uses a different post-UTME maximum or publishes a direct conversion table, follow that method instead. Do not translate it into this two-component formula merely because the worksheet is available.

Keep full precision while checking the contributions and round only as the institution instructs. If no rounding rule is published, retain the precise worksheet result and clearly label any displayed rounding as your presentation choice, not an official rule.

Do not mix a JAMB aggregate with a WAEC aggregate

UTME, post-UTME and O-level results can all appear in an admissions conversation, but they are not interchangeable. A WAEC result reports subject grades such as A1 through F9. An O-level aggregate is a separate summary produced under a stated selection or institutional method. A JAMB screening aggregate combines the components named by the institution.

If the institution includes O-level grades in its screening method, copy its exact grade-to-point table and weighting. Do not use an unrelated school's WAEC aggregate method or assume that an A1 is worth a particular number of screening points everywhere. Read the WAEC Result Guide to interpret subject grades, and use the WAEC Result Calculator only under the method required for the specific task.

Keep separate records for eligibility and arithmetic. The eligibility record shows that the required subjects, grades, sittings and programme combination were checked. The calculation record shows which published formula and scores produced the aggregate. Combining them into one unexplained number makes an error harder to find.

Complete the admission process after the calculation

A screening result is one checkpoint, not the end of the process. JAMB's May 18 bulletin says institutions must not conduct admissions outside CAPS. A candidate should therefore continue checking the official institution channel and the appropriate JAMB services rather than treating a calculator result or an unofficial list as an offer.

Use this post-calculation checklist:

  1. Confirm the O-level results and required subject combination in IBASS.
  2. Confirm that the institution and programme choices are correct for the current cycle.
  3. Complete the institution's stated screening registration and test steps within its official window.
  4. Upload O-level results through the required official channels by the published deadline.
  5. Keep receipts, application references and the exact email or phone number used for official communication private and accessible.
  6. Monitor the institution's official notices for corrections or later admission instructions.
  7. Check the admission status through the official CAPS process and follow its instructions.

Do not pay an intermediary to alter a score, upload fabricated credentials or promise admission outside the stated process. A number that looks competitive cannot make false information valid, and admission reported outside the official channels requires verification.

Common JAMB aggregate mistakes in 2026

Treating 150 as every university's screening cutoff: JAMB's 150 is the 2026/2027 national minimum tolerable university score. UNILAG's official screening gate was 200. Always check the institution.

Assuming a 50/50 formula: A balanced split is not a default rule. Use it only if the current institution notice publishes it.

Multiplying a UTME score out of 400 directly by a percentage: In a normalized two-component method, convert UTME to the 100-point scale first. If the institution publishes a different conversion, use its method.

Using an old formula with a new score: A prior-cycle page may still rank in search. Record the 2026/2027 cycle and source date beside every input.

Calling a WAEC aggregate a JAMB aggregate: The results and scoring methods answer different questions. Only combine O-level points when the institution explicitly includes them.

Ignoring non-score requirements: First-choice status, subject combinations, number of sittings, age, uploads and test participation can determine eligibility even when the arithmetic is correct.

Publishing personal records: A result slip, application number, email, phone number and portal screenshot may expose personal information. Share only what a trusted reviewer needs and use official support channels for account problems.

Build a calculation another person can verify

A useful screening worksheet is an evidence record, not only a total. Label it with the candidate's chosen institution and programme without exposing unnecessary personal identifiers. Record the admission cycle, official formula link, access date, original score scales, weights, calculations and any rounding. Keep a separate note of outstanding eligibility or portal actions.

If the institution changes its notice, do not overwrite the earlier calculation silently. Save a new version with the new source date, identify what changed and recalculate from the same verified scores. This lets a candidate or adviser explain why two totals differ and prevents an old screenshot from circulating as current guidance.

Finally, use the number for the narrow purpose it supports: checking the arithmetic under a published method. It cannot predict how many places will be available, how an institution will resolve ties or what final programme decision will appear in CAPS unless the responsible authority has published those facts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there one official JAMB aggregate formula for every Nigerian institution?

No. JAMB publishes national admission policy and minimum tolerable UTME scores, while each institution may publish its own screening method, weights, eligibility threshold and programme requirements. Use the current notice for the institution and admission cycle.

Does a UTME score of 150 guarantee university admission in 2026?

No. JAMB's 2026/2027 national minimum tolerable score for universities is 150, but an institution can set a higher minimum and apply additional programme, O-level and screening requirements. Admission must also be processed through CAPS.

Is a WAEC aggregate the same as a JAMB screening aggregate?

No. A WAEC or O-level aggregate summarizes examination grades under a stated method. A JAMB screening aggregate may combine a UTME score with a post-UTME or other institution-defined component. Do not substitute one for the other.

Can the AfroTools worksheet confirm admission?

No. It calculates a planning result from the scores and current institution-published weights that the user enters. It cannot verify eligibility, predict a programme cutoff or replace the institution and JAMB CAPS.
