Mobile Money Fees in Africa: How to Compare Current Tariffs

By AfroTools Team Published Mar 10, 2026 11 min read
AfroTools Team Mar 10, 2026 Last reviewed: September 13, 2026 11 min read

Mobile-money charges are not one continent-wide price list. M-Pesa, MTN MoMo, Airtel Money and other wallets publish different fees by country, amount band and transaction type. A send fee can differ from an agent-withdrawal fee, and a domestic transfer can have a completely different cost structure from a cross-border transfer.

This guide was materially reviewed on September 13, 2026. The previous version included unsupported cross-country fee ranges and incorrectly said Ghana still charged a 1% Electronic Transfer Levy. The Ghana Revenue Authority records the E-Levy as repealed in April 2025. The old comparison tables have therefore been replaced with a bounded method and amounts tied to current official provider pages.

There is no reliable cheapest mobile-money network for all of Africa. Compare the exact country, provider, action, amount band, recipient network, tax treatment and cash-out requirement. Why a mobile-money fee comparison goes stale

Mobile-money pricing has several moving parts. Operators can change bands, run promotions or price the same action differently across markets. Governments can add, change or repeal levies. Interoperability can alter the route used for an off-network transfer. A wallet-to-bank transfer can also use a different tariff from a wallet-to-wallet transfer.

The old version of this guide flattened those differences into broad labels such as "M-Pesa fee" or "Airtel fee". That is not reliable enough for a financial decision. A useful comparison needs to answer all of these questions:

  • Which country is the wallet registered in? Provider brands do not have one Africa-wide schedule.
  • What action is being priced? Sending, depositing, withdrawing, paying a bill, paying a merchant and transferring to a bank are separate products.
  • Is the recipient on the same network? On-network and off-network transfers can follow different schedules.
  • What exact amount band applies? A one-unit difference can move the transaction into another fixed-fee band.
  • Is tax included? Some schedules show a provider fee and a government levy as separate lines.
  • Will the recipient cash out? The sender fee is not the complete household cost when the recipient must pay an agent-withdrawal fee.
  • Is foreign exchange involved? A cross-border route can include an exchange-rate margin even when the visible transfer fee looks small.

Ghana's E-Levy was repealed

The previous article said Ghana charged a 1% E-Levy on electronic transfers above GHS 100. That statement was already stale when the page was published. The Ghana Revenue Authority says the Electronic Transfer Levy was repealed in April 2025, and Ghana's Ministry of Finance also records the repeal as part of the 2025 tax changes.

Repeal of the government levy does not mean every Ghana mobile-money action is free. An operator's own send, withdrawal, merchant or bank-transfer charges can still apply. Check the current Ghana provider schedule for the exact action instead of subtracting the old levy from an old total.

How to compare M-Pesa, MTN MoMo and Airtel Money

Start with the exact route, not the brand. A useful comparison row should identify the sending country, receiving country, sending network, receiving network, amount, currency, payout type and time checked. If any of those fields differ, the two prices may not be comparable.

  1. Open the official tariff or in-app confirmation. Treat search snippets and old screenshots as discovery, not price proof.
  2. Choose the exact action. Compare send with send, agent withdrawal with agent withdrawal, and bank transfer with bank transfer.
  3. Find the correct amount band. Read both the minimum and maximum. Check whether the endpoints are inclusive.
  4. Add separate taxes and recipient charges. The operator fee alone may understate the total cost.
  5. For cross-border transfers, record the recipient amount. Compare the exchange rate and all fees against the amount that actually reaches the recipient.
  6. Confirm on the final screen. Operator schedules can change after an article's review date.

M-Pesa, MTN MoMo and Airtel Money can all support different domestic and cross-network routes. For example, Safaricom's official M-PESA Tuma Popote guidance explains that a registered M-PESA user can send to registered Airtel Money or T-Kash users in Kenya. That confirms the route exists, but it does not justify copying a historical tariff into every M-Pesa comparison. The current charge still needs to be checked in the provider's confirmation flow.

Domestic and cross-border comparisons are different

A domestic wallet transfer can usually be compared in one currency. A cross-border transfer requires a fuller record. The sender may pay a visible transaction fee, the provider may apply an exchange rate, the recipient may pay a cash-out fee, and the route may use a partner that sets separate limits or charges.

Do not describe a cross-border fee only as a percentage unless the provider publishes that percentage for the exact corridor and payout method. Instead, compare:

  • the amount debited from the sender;
  • the sending fee and any tax;
  • the exchange rate used;
  • the amount credited to the recipient wallet;
  • the recipient's cash-out fee, if cash is required;
  • the transfer limit and estimated delivery time.

Use the Remittance Compare tool to structure a corridor comparison, and use the Currency Converter as a reference-rate check. A reference exchange rate is not an executable provider quote, so the provider's final recipient amount remains the deciding figure.

Practical ways to reduce mobile-money costs

  • Price the full journey. Include send, tax, recipient and cash-out costs rather than looking only at the first fee.
  • Avoid assumptions about on-network pricing. Some networks charge for low-value sends, while others use promotions or free bands.
  • Keep money digital when it genuinely helps. A merchant payment can avoid a cash-out step, but only if the merchant route and consumer charge are confirmed.
  • Check band boundaries before splitting a transaction. Two smaller sends can cost more than one larger send, or the reverse, depending on the published schedule.
  • Save source details for business reconciliation. Record the provider, action, amount, fee, tax, date checked and confirmation reference.
  • Never pay an agent an unpublished extra charge. MTN Uganda's tariff page states that fees are deducted automatically and warns customers not to pay agents for transactions.
