Tractor Hire Quotation Checklist for African Farms

Idris DialloOctober 5, 202611 min read

A tractor-hire quotation needs to describe the work that will be delivered, the basis of the charge and the conditions that can change the bill. A price per hectare is difficult to compare when one provider includes fuel and transport while another charges those separately. Before paying a deposit, turn each offer into a common job sheet and ask the provider to confirm the remaining gaps.

Use the Tractor and Equipment Lease vs Buy Calculator to explore the longer-term ownership decision. Its country and equipment assumptions are planning inputs, not a supplier quotation. Keep the actual hire offers in a separate comparison sheet. This guide focuses on preparing that sheet, checking the booking and reconciling the completed work.

Sources reviewed: October 5, 2026. The FAO/CIMMYT manual was published in 2018 and the ILO agriculture code in 2011. Their current publisher pages and the African Conservation Tillage Network training manual were checked for this guide. They provide technical and business context, not today's local hire prices or country-specific contract law. Obtain current written terms and local agronomic advice for your field.

Write the field job before asking for a price

Describe the required operation in terms the farmer and provider can both verify. Land preparation, ripping, harrowing, ridging, planting and transport are different jobs. Name the intended operation and the implement, specify which fields are included, and identify the person who can approve a change. Ask a qualified local adviser to confirm the operation is appropriate for the crop, soil and production system.

Record the area, its measurement method and the unit. Distinguish the total holding from the area actually scheduled for work. Exclude buildings, access tracks and other unworked portions where appropriate. If the provider will measure the area, agree when that happens and how a disagreement will be resolved. Keep the field reference on both the request and the quotation.

Identify the desired work window rather than writing only a month. Record access restrictions, the location of the entrance and any known obstacles that require inspection. Share enough information for the provider to assess the job, while keeping personal and land documents out of public posts. A booking request should not expose an identity document or an unrelated ownership record.

Confirm the tractor and implement combination

The FAO/CIMMYT mechanization hire-service manual treats machine selection as a technical and financial decision. Engine power alone does not establish what a particular implement will accomplish in a field. Ask the provider to identify the tractor, implement and proposed operation together, with a site assessment where conditions are uncertain.

Record the implement's working width or configuration if it matters to the job. For planting, ask who supplies and checks the crop-specific setup and who approves it before work begins. For tillage, agree how the requested work will be assessed. Do not write a universal depth, speed or number of passes into a quotation simply because another farm used it.

Ask how substitutions will be handled. If the named equipment is unavailable, the provider should explain the replacement and obtain approval before changing the agreed scope. Record whether the replacement changes the charge, timing or acceptance checks. A photograph can help identify an implement, but it does not establish its condition, compatibility or suitability.

Put each charge on an explicit unit

Keep the original quote and create a comparison column beside it. Identify whether the provider charges per hectare, per acre, per hour, per day, per trip or for the whole job. Ask what counts as a billable unit. A daily charge needs a definition of the included work period; an hourly charge needs a definition of when the clock starts and stops.

For an area-based offer, clarify whether the price covers one operation, one pass or a package. Repeated work on the same area can create additional charges. Record each operation separately before comparing a combined offer. For an hourly offer, distinguish productive field time from transport, waiting, refuelling and repair time under the actual agreement.

Use the Unit Converter for consistent quantities and keep the conversion basis beside the source value. Never compare an acre price directly with a hectare price or treat a machine hour as an area measurement. Keep the currency and quotation date visible. A conversion makes two units comparable; it does not make two different service scopes equivalent.

Build the complete quoted cost

The African Conservation Tillage Network's hire-services training manual includes costing and business records. For the customer's comparison, separate the field-service charge from the other amounts payable. Mark every line as included, separately charged, supplied by the customer or still awaiting confirmation.

Calculate the known total from the quoted quantities and rates, then add separately confirmed fixed charges. Show unresolved items below that total. A blank fuel line should remain an open question, rather than becoming zero. Do not add a guessed local fuel price to make the comparison appear complete.

If fuel is customer-supplied, agree the receiving and use records in advance. Retain purchase evidence and the agreed method for reconciling remaining fuel. If the provider supplies it, ask whether the quotation includes all fuel for the agreed work or permits a surcharge. Record the actual clause, its calculation basis and any approval requirement.

Check capacity and the booking window

Ask the provider how the proposed equipment and work schedule fit the field. The FAO manual distinguishes potential field capacity from actual performance, which is affected by turns, travel, field conditions and downtime. Treat a claimed daily area as something to discuss and verify, rather than as a guaranteed completion date.

Record the planned arrival, the expected sequence of fields and the contact responsible for confirming readiness. Ask what happens if earlier jobs delay arrival. A provider's general availability is different from an accepted slot for your job. Keep the confirmation attached to the same quotation version as the deposit request.

Agree the weather and field-readiness decision process with the responsible operator and adviser. Record who can postpone work, when the customer will be informed and whether transport or waiting charges apply. Do not instruct an operator to work unsuitable ground to preserve a date. A revised booking should show the new scope and cost implications.

If farmers coordinate nearby jobs, identify each customer's area, approvals and payment responsibility. Ask the provider whether grouping affects the transport charge or order of work. Keep any saving as a confirmed quotation term. Avoid promising neighbours a discount or a start time that the provider has not accepted.

Make safety responsibilities part of the booking

The ILO's 2011 agriculture code of practice addresses trained operators, machinery hazards and protection of people around the work. Ask who supplies the competent operator, who checks the equipment and who controls access to the working area. Follow the actual machine manual and applicable local requirements.

Before arrival, identify the person who will brief the operator about access, obstacles and people nearby. Keep children and bystanders outside the work area under the agreed safety arrangements. Questions about guards, rollover protection, safe routes or a damaged machine require competent assessment. A low price does not resolve an unsafe condition.

Ask the provider to explain the incident and breakdown reporting route. Record whom the customer should contact and who can authorise resumption after a problem. Do not attempt a repair or prescribe recovery steps from a quotation checklist. Refer technical work to qualified personnel using the correct equipment documentation.

For model-specific information, the manufacturer's John Deere publication search illustrates why model, serial range and market edition matter. Other makes need their own documentation. The presence of a manual or a brand name on an offer is not independent proof that the hired machine has been inspected.

Resolve deposits, cancellation and additional work

Ask for the quotation's validity date, deposit amount, balance trigger and accepted payment route. Verify the provider and payment details through a known channel before sending money. Retain the quotation, acceptance and payment reference together. This checklist supplies no universal deposit percentage or cancellation rule across African countries.

Read what happens if the customer cancels, the provider cancels, weather intervenes or the equipment breaks down. Ask whether the deposit is refundable, transferable to another slot or partly retained for stated costs. If the written offer is silent, request clarification before paying. Have a qualified local adviser review disputed or high-value contract terms.

Make extra work an approval step. Record the requested change, reason, quantity, unit, price and person authorising it. Ask the operator to refer commercial changes to the agreed contact. A verbal request for another pass can otherwise become an invoice disagreement. Keep the initial scope visible when a revised quotation is issued.

Separate a price adjustment from a service complaint. Record each issue with its supporting evidence and the requested resolution. Do not erase an earlier offer or backdate acceptance to close a dispute. The comparison sheet should show what was agreed and what remains unresolved, with dates that reflect the actual exchange.

Measure and reconcile the completed job

At completion, compare the work record with the agreed field list and operations. Record the area completed using the agreed measurement basis, the work dates and any approved extras. For an hourly booking, reconcile the time record with the agreed chargeable categories. Preserve pauses and disputed time as separate entries.

Review the outcome against the acceptance checks established before work. Note incomplete areas and any issue that needs the provider or adviser to inspect. A signed attendance sheet proves a visit, not automatically satisfactory completion. Keep photographs narrowly focused on the work and avoid capturing unrelated personal documents or bystanders.

Match the invoice to the accepted quotation and approved changes. Explain quantity differences, fixed transport charges, fuel treatment and payments already made. If an amount cannot be reconciled, ask for the missing basis before marking the job closed. Retain the final invoice and receipt alongside the completion record.

Machinery timing should fit the production plan reviewed with local advisers.

Keep a reusable machinery-hire file

Keep one folder containing the field request, provider identity, dated offers, comparison sheet, accepted terms, payment evidence, approved changes and completion records. Use job references rather than personal identifiers in filenames. Share only the material necessary for the provider or adviser to resolve a specific question.

After the season, compare quoted and actual quantities, timing and charges. Record the reason for each difference so the next request can improve. Preserve measured experience without turning one completed job into a universal rate or productivity promise. The next booking still needs a current quote, an appropriate machine and an accepted work window.

Frequently asked questions

Can a price per hectare be compared directly with a price per hour?

No. Confirm the actual scope and billing basis first, then compare the complete quoted job cost. Keep area, time and transport charges distinct.

Does the calculator use my supplier quote?

Its current hire comparison uses built-in country assumptions. Keep actual supplier offers and additional charges in a separate comparison sheet.

Are the training-manual prices current local hire rates?

No. The manuals provide technical and business context. Obtain current dated quotations for your field and requested operation.

What evidence should be kept when the job finishes?

Retain completed area or time records, agreed acceptance checks, approved changes, the reconciled invoice and payment evidence.
