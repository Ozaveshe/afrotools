South Africa's UIF calculator needs two different questions: how much is deducted from pay, and what benefit might be paid after a qualifying loss of income. The payroll contribution is a capped percentage. The unemployment benefit depends on average remuneration, a sliding replacement rate, verified credit days and the stage of the claim. A payslip deduction alone cannot tell you the approved benefit.

Official sources checked: October 4, 2026. This guide uses SARS contribution guidance, the Department of Employment and Labour's benefit publications, and the government's May 25, 2026 portal announcement. It is a planning guide, not a claim decision or legal advice. The older benefit publications remain useful for the formulas; their employee portal instructions have been superseded by UIF Online.

Use the South Africa UIF Calculator for contribution and benefit planning, then submit or follow up through the official UIF channel. Keep personal identity documents, bank details and employment records out of calculator notes or public sharing links.

What Is UIF and Who Qualifies

UIF provides temporary relief for qualifying unemployment and other covered losses of income. For ordinary unemployment, the Department's Know Your UIF Rights and Obligations booklet identifies employer termination or expiry of the employment contract, registration as a work seeker, and available credits. Voluntary resignation and absconding are excluded unless constructive dismissal is proven.

The ordinary unemployment application window is 12 months from termination of service. The six-month deadline in the booklet belongs to illness claims. Check the benefit type before applying; maternity, illness and other benefits have their own conditions and paperwork. Do not treat a deadline as a reason to postpone gathering the documents.

SARS contribution guidance excludes employment of less than 24 hours a month for an employer, national or provincial government officers and employees, and specified public office holders. Domestic workers are covered subject to the applicable conditions. If you have several employers or uncertain employment status, ask UIF to check your record rather than assuming one payslip settles eligibility.

Contribution rates and ceiling

SARS specifies an employee contribution of 1% and an employer contribution of 1%. The monthly remuneration ceiling is R17,712, effective from June 1, 2021 and still stated on the SARS page checked for this review. The maximum employee deduction is R177.12 monthly; the employer matches it, giving a combined R354.24.

These are contribution amounts, not a savings balance that you withdraw after leaving work. Separate the employee deduction, employer payment and benefit estimate when checking a payroll breakdown. A salary above the ceiling does not produce a larger contribution on the excess remuneration.

How Ordinary UIF Benefits Are Calculated

The Department's normal UIF benefit calculation fact sheet uses average salary over the final six months before termination, capped at R17,712 monthly. It converts monthly remuneration into daily income using calendar days:

Daily income (Y1) = capped average monthly remuneration × 12 ÷ 365.

The income replacement rate is 29.2 + 7,173.92 ÷ (232.92 + Y1), expressed as a percentage. Apply the applicable rate to daily income to obtain the daily ordinary benefit. Dividing monthly pay by 21.67 working days is not the daily-income method in this publication.

Before entering a figure, distinguish gross remuneration from take-home pay and obtain the salary schedule used for the claim. If pay varied, do not substitute your latest payslip for the required average. Keep the underlying records so a difference between a planning estimate and the assessed amount can be investigated.

Credit Days and the Lower Later Tier

The Easy Aid Guide sets ordinary benefits at the sliding 38% to 60% rate for the first 238 credit days. Days 239 to 365 use a flat 20% rate. A projection covering the full period must account for both tiers, rather than applying the initial rate to every day.

Credits accrue at one day for every four days of contributory employment, subject to a maximum of 365 days in a four-year cycle. Previous claims and periods without contributions can affect what remains available. Four years on a CV is not sufficient evidence that the full credit balance is available now.

Use verified available credits in the calculator. If you do not know that balance, ask UIF to confirm it and treat any interim projection as incomplete. A calendar duration, working-day count and credit-day balance describe different things; avoid converting months worked into credits with an unsupported working-days table.

For planning, record the salary basis, requested period and credit balance alongside the result. Recalculate when any of these changes. Keep the estimated amount separate from a household budget's confirmed income until UIF approves the claim and payment period.

Maternity, Illness and Other Benefit Types

The Department's 2022 rights booklet describes maternity benefits at 66%, for up to 121 days, with qualifying employment of at least 13 weeks before application. Employer leave pay and the applicable ceiling affect the benefit calculation. These conditions should not be transferred to an ordinary unemployment claim.

For illness, the booklet specifies an application within six months of ceasing work because of illness, medical evidence for illness lasting more than seven days, and disclosure of employer remuneration during leave. Confirm the claim-specific requirements with UIF before submitting.

Adoption, parental, dependant and reduced-work-time claims have separate conditions. The AfroTools calculator keeps ordinary unemployment and maternity planning distinct; it does not decide the other claims. Where circumstances do not match a supported calculation, use the official guidance and a Labour Centre instead of forcing the case into the nearest calculator mode.

Apply Through UIF Online

The government statement of May 25, 2026 confirms that the legacy uFiling employee claims portal closed on May 20, 2026. UIF Online launched on April 1, 2025 as its replacement. Start new employee claims at UIF Online.

The same statement distinguished the employer portal and announced a planned migration of employer functions. That announcement does not by itself prove that every employer service migrated on schedule. This guide directs employee claimants to UIF Online without making an unsupported claim about the current employer migration status.

Prepare a private claim folder

The rights booklet lists identity evidence, UI-19, a salary schedule and work-seeker registration for ordinary claims, with monthly continuation paperwork. Use the current portal or Labour Centre to confirm what it requires for your application. Banking information belongs in the official claim process, not in a public calculator result.

  1. Check that the employer's recorded termination date and reason match your records. Ask for correction if they do not.
  2. Gather readable documents and keep the originals. Use a private folder with clear filenames so you can find the requested record without uploading unrelated information.
  3. Open the official UIF Online address, follow its registration or sign-in process, and choose the relevant benefit type.
  4. Review the application before submission. Save its reference and any official requests for missing information.
  5. Follow the claim's continuation and status instructions. Ask a Labour Centre for help if the online process cannot be completed.

Old portal screenshots and menu instructions can mislead after a system change. Follow the current official interface rather than expecting a historic menu label to appear. The public landing page was accessible when checked; this review did not sign in, submit a claim or verify a claimant's payment.

Resolve Missing Records Without Assuming Rejection

A delayed application, missing declaration and formal rejection need different follow-up. Keep a short record of the date, reference and exact status shown by UIF. When contacting the Fund, ask which record or action is outstanding instead of assuming that the calculator's estimate should already have been paid.

If employment or contribution records appear incomplete, retain payslips and correspondence and request an official review. Do not conclude that an employer's missing declaration automatically removes every possible entitlement. For a formal refusal, ask for the decision and applicable review process; a blog cannot overturn it.

There is no guaranteed payment date in this guide. Avoid spending against a promised processing time or paying an intermediary simply because the claim is taking longer than expected. Verify contact details from the Department or UIF Online before sharing records.

Frequently Asked Questions

The Department publishes a ceiling calculation using R17,712 monthly remuneration: R582.31 daily income and about R221.28 daily ordinary benefit at 38%. That is a planning reference, not your approved payment. UIF must verify average remuneration, available credits and the payable period; later benefit days use the lower 20% tier.

Voluntary resignation and absconding do not qualify for ordinary unemployment benefits. The Department recognises an exception where constructive dismissal is proven. Ask UIF or a Labour Centre to assess the recorded reason for termination and supporting evidence.

Do not budget against a guaranteed payment date. Track the application through UIF Online and follow up with UIF using the claim reference if documents, employment declarations or payment details need attention. An estimate does not confirm approval or a bank payment.

Domestic workers are covered subject to the applicable conditions. SARS excludes employment of less than 24 hours a month for an employer. Confirm the relevant employment declarations and contribution record with UIF, especially when work is split across households.
