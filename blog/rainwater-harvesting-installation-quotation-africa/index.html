Rainwater Harvesting Installation Quotation Checklist for Africa

Idris DialloOctober 11, 20269 min read

A rainwater harvesting quote should describe a working route from the roof to the intended point of use. Buying a tank is only one part of that route. Gutters, diversion, storage, delivery and maintenance all need a defined scope before the household can compare offers or approve spending.

This guide is for requesting and reviewing a new installation quotation across African household settings. It complements the existing sizing and maintenance guides. It does not provide a structural design, plumbing approval, drinking-water treatment specification or current price list. The installer must confirm the actual site, product instructions and applicable local requirements.

Source review: October 11, 2026. The primary references below include FAO roof-water guidance, JoJo's installation guide and RotoTank's 2025 installation guide. Manufacturer examples support specific purchasing questions, not a universal African installation standard. The procurement checklist is editorial guidance; no installation, water sample or supplier quotation was inspected for this article.

Specify the water's intended use first

Write down the uses the installation is expected to serve: garden watering, an outdoor cleaning point or a separately designed internal supply. State whether the request is rainwater-only, municipal backup or a combined system. Those descriptions should remain visible on the quotation rather than disappearing behind a bundle name such as complete home water solution.

Ask the supplier to identify any proposed connection to the building's existing plumbing and who will confirm its acceptability. Keep the intended use distinct from the quality of water arriving from the roof. A tank labelled for water storage does not establish that its contents are suitable for every household activity. Drinking-water use needs an appropriate assessment and treatment plan from the responsible qualified provider.

Record the owner or landlord's permission and access constraints in your private project notes. In a shared compound, identify who controls the collecting roof, tank location and outlets. Resolve ownership of the installation and maintenance responsibility before requesting competing offers. Otherwise two suppliers may quote different systems for the same informal request.

Give suppliers a measured collecting area

Prepare a simple roof sketch showing the sections intended to drain into the tank, their approximate horizontal areas and the current gutter outlets. Label measurements as measured or estimated. Do not treat the entire building footprint as connected catchment when some roof sections drain elsewhere. Ask the installer to confirm the area that can actually be collected.

Include roof material, visible condition, nearby trees and access limitations in the request. Keep inspection and work at height with appropriately equipped professionals. A household quote review does not require climbing onto the roof. Where the surface or its history is uncertain, request an assessment rather than guessing whether runoff is suitable for the intended use.

FAO's roof-water practice describes collection as a system of roof, gutters, downpipe, base and storage. Its published example uses a 7 m by 7 m roof, annual rainfall of 750 mm and 75 percent efficiency to give about 28,000 litres annually. That is a source example, not a forecast for your house or a promise of uninterrupted supply.

Separate annual potential from tank capacity

Use the Water Tank Sizing Calculator to organise a first planning view, then document which assumptions you used. Its household-demand values depend on the selected property type. Its rainwater estimate uses roof area, approximately 1,000 mm annual rainfall and 80 percent efficiency. Selecting a country does not turn that rainfall assumption into a verified local weather record.

The tool's monthly figure divides the annual result by twelve. It is not a month-by-month rainfall simulation, a drought forecast or a measurement of usable stored water. Keep that distinction beside any screenshot used in a purchasing request. The annual litres displayed cannot be stored all at once in a smaller tank, and the annual average does not describe the timing of individual storms.

RotoTank's installation guide illustrates the volume relationship with 100 square metres of roof and a 25 mm rainfall event producing 2,500 litres before site-specific losses. Use that published illustration to understand the units. Ask the installer to state the rainfall evidence, connected roof area, diversion and losses behind their own estimate.

For household reserve planning, read Water Tank Sizing for an African Home. Decide which demand will actually draw from the rainwater tank and which supply remains separate. Keep the reserve-duration question alongside the catchment question; neither can answer the other on its own.

Require a component schedule with exact products

Ask for a list identifying the tank model, nominal capacity, intended installation type and dimensions. Add gutters or repairs, downpipes, inlet fittings, debris screens, first-flush equipment, overflow, outlets and isolation arrangements where included. The quotation should say whether each item is supplied, installed, reused or excluded. A photograph of a bundle does not substitute for this schedule.

JoJo's installation guide distinguishes the tank and foundation from pre-filtration, first-flush diversion and insect screening. Use those distinctions to question omissions in a proposed package. Keep the selected manufacturer's instructions with the actual model; fittings and installation details need to match the product being supplied.

Make the system boundary explicit. If existing gutters are assumed usable, ask who checks them and what happens if repairs are needed. If a quoted filter is only a debris screen, record that description. If treatment is proposed, ask the provider to specify the intended water quality, equipment and verification separately. Avoid accepting a vague clean-water claim as a technical scope.

Compare the base, access and drainage work

Ask suppliers to inspect the proposed tank location before finalising civil-work quantities. The quotation should identify the foundation work, ground preparation, access for delivery and any reinstatement. Record whether excavation, removal of unsuitable material, finishes and waste removal are included. Do not prescribe a slab thickness from a different installation or use the calculator as a foundation design.

RotoTank's guide calls for a solid, level base capable of supporting the filled tank. It also treats available space as a product-selection input. Request the relevant dimensions and support requirements for the chosen model, including service access. Leave structural suitability and any raised installation to the responsible qualified professional.

Ask where overflow and diverted first-flush water will go and who is responsible for the drainage arrangement. Mark the proposed discharge route on the site sketch. The quote should resolve the destination rather than leaving a loose pipe as an unnamed future task. Confirm any local requirements with the installer or relevant authority before approving the connection.

Define delivery to the actual point of use

State whether water will be drawn from a tank tap, gravity arrangement or pumped supply. If a pump is included, require its exact model, proposed operating duty, controller, protection and electrical scope. Ask how the supplier selected it for the actual pipe route and outlets. Motor power alone does not describe the complete service the system will provide.

The calculator's pump and pipe suggestions are broad planning guidance. They are not a site-specific selection based on measured pressure losses or simultaneous demand. Keep those outputs separate from the supplier's design. Ask for the assumptions used for any promised outlet performance and identify who will test that performance at handover.

Separate electrical connection work from plumbing work on the quotation. Record who supplies the power point, weather protection, controls and any required electrical certification. For a household with backup power, ask the relevant professional to confirm compatibility with the actual equipment. Do not infer it from a marketing phrase or an unrelated solar calculation.

Put competing offers on the same cost basis

Compare the installed system total in one currency, with the same intended uses and scope. Separate tank supply, collection fittings, civil work, delivery, plumbing, pumping, electrical work and commissioning. Record tax treatment, quote validity, payment stages and exclusions. Ask suppliers to mark uncertain quantities explicitly so they can be resolved before work begins.

The tool's tank-price output comes from fixed approximate values in its implementation. It is not a live supplier feed, an installed-system quote or a guarantee of availability. Do not use that figure to reject a supplier's quotation without reconciling the actual model and included work. Obtain dated quotations for the chosen site and retain their original scope descriptions.

List recurring requirements separately: replacement components, filter servicing, pump energy and any testing or maintenance agreed for the system. Ask who supplies these services and on what terms. Use documented quotations or product instructions for the figures. This guide makes no annual savings, payback or service-life claim because those depend on the installation and local circumstances.

Agree the handover evidence before ordering

Write the acceptance checklist into the order. It should identify the supplied products, completed works, intended outlets, inspection records and operating instructions. Ask the installer to demonstrate the collection and delivery functions included in the contract, explain any remaining limitations and record unresolved items with an owner and agreed next action.

Keep the as-installed diagram, product documents, invoice and warranty terms together. Record any substituted component and its approval. Ask which checks can be completed immediately and which need a later rainfall event. Do not label an unobserved storm response as tested merely because a dry-weather demonstration passed.

Use the household tank maintenance checklist to assign the future inspection and service record. Keep access, cleaning arrangements and replacement-part information usable after the installer leaves. Record only the necessary technical details in shared documents; addresses, payment details and household identities belong in your private project files.

Close the quotation with unresolved fields visible

Approve only after the intended use, connected catchment, storage model, installed scope and responsibility for technical assessment are clear. Keep a question list attached to the quotation until each answer is written down. Where the supplier revises the offer, compare the revision with the original and record what changed rather than replacing the old file without explanation.

A smaller, clearly scoped installation may be easier to evaluate than a package with unexplained extras. The decision should follow your actual use and site constraints. Retain the calculator assumptions as planning evidence, the quotation as commercial evidence and the handover record as execution evidence. Each answers a different question about the project.

Frequently asked questions

Does the annual rainwater estimate choose my tank size?

No. Collection timing, intended demand, usable storage and site constraints need separate review. Annual potential is not continuously available water.

Does choosing a country load verified local rainfall?

No. The current tool's roof-water estimate uses a fixed approximate annual rainfall assumption. Ask for dated local rainfall evidence when designing the installation.

Does a first-flush device make water safe to drink?

No. It is part of managing incoming runoff. Drinking-water suitability requires an appropriate assessment, treatment and verification for the actual supply.

Can I use the displayed tank cost as an installed quotation?

No. The fixed approximate tank figure excludes site-specific scope. Obtain a dated offer identifying the complete supplied and installed system.
