Ethiopia VAT Guide 2026: 15% Rate, Registration and Returns

A practical guide to Ethiopia's current VAT threshold, 15% rate, registration checks, invoices, input credits and monthly return controls.

Ethiopia VAT compliance starts before the calculation. A business has to determine whether it must register, identify which supplies are taxable, zero-rated, exempt or outside the system, issue the right invoice, retain credit evidence, reconcile its records and file by the statutory date. Multiplying by 15% is only one step in that chain.

This guide was verified on September 9, 2026 against Ethiopia's Value Added Tax Proclamation No. 1341/2024, the Council of Ministers VAT Regulation No. 570/2025, the current VAT registration directive listed by the Ministry of Justice, and the Ministry of Revenue's law and taxpayer-verification pages. The 2024 proclamation replaced the older framework and is the main source for the rate, ETB 2 million threshold, invoice rules, input credits and return deadline used here.

The tool does not register a business, determine whether a supply is exempt, send an invoice to the Ministry of Revenue, file a return or turn a document into tax evidence.

The ETB 2 million figure is a legal registration threshold, not a general tax-free allowance. A registered person charges and accounts for VAT according to the treatment of each supply. A person below that figure can still face voluntary registration or a direction created by the current registration rules. The Ministry of Justice lists a separate VAT registration directive, so businesses should test turnover, legal form, taxpayer category and bookkeeping status rather than relying on turnover alone.

The proclamation also looks forward and backward. A person can become liable at the start of a 12-month period when there are reasonable grounds to expect taxable supplies above the threshold, or after a 12-month or shorter period when supplies have already exceeded it. A signed contract or expanding sales pipeline can therefore matter before year-end accounts are ready.

Build a registration decision file

Start with identity and activity. Confirm the legal name, TIN, business licence, premises, branches, online channels, responsible tax office and activities that produce taxable supplies. The Ministry of Revenue provides an official TIN verification search, which is useful for checking the identity connected to a tax record. It does not replace the VAT registration certificate or current written status.

Next, prepare a turnover schedule. Include actual taxable supplies for completed periods, forecast taxable supplies for the coming 12 months, large contracts, related activity that may be relevant, and items excluded under the proclamation. Article 12 says capital-goods sales used in the taxable activity are ignored for the threshold test, and it gives specific treatment to a sale caused solely by disposing of or permanently ending an activity. Those exclusions should be documented, not assumed from a ledger label.

Check the current registration directive separately. The Ministry of Justice records the directive as an active Ministry of Finance instrument covering VAT registration. Because classification and bookkeeping obligations can affect the registration analysis, a business near the boundary should obtain the current form or written Tax Authority guidance before concluding that it can remain outside VAT.

When liability is clear, Article 12 requires the application within seven days. Keep a copy of the application, supporting documents, submission reference, effective date and certificate. Display and notification duties continue after registration. The proclamation requires a registered person to notify the Tax Authority in writing within 30 days of specified changes, including contact details, business locations, internet address, social media account used for business, and the nature of the business.

Keep standard, zero-rated and exempt supplies separate

Article 8 sets two VAT rates: 0% for a qualifying zero-rated supply and 15% in any other taxable case. Exempt supplies are dealt with separately. Zero-rated and exempt do not mean the same thing, and the distinction can affect invoicing and input-tax recovery.

Use separate tax codes for standard-rated, zero-rated, exempt and outside-scope items. Every non-standard code should point to a current provision and evidence. A customer location, foreign currency, industry label or invoice description does not by itself prove zero-rating or exemption. Classify the actual supply and retain the documents that support the treatment.

Mixed invoices need line-level control. If a document contains a 15% taxable item and an item treated differently, calculate each line using its own tax code and supporting reason. Do not apply one average rate to the total. The invoice record should preserve net amount, tax treatment, VAT amount and gross amount for each line.

Remote and reverse-charged supplies also have specific rules in the proclamation. A registered person, government entity or large unregistered person receiving a qualifying reverse-charged supply may have to account for VAT. A business buying cross-border services should therefore record supplier location, recipient, use, payment, invoice and the reason for its reverse-charge conclusion before the return is prepared.

How to add and extract Ethiopia VAT

For a VAT-exclusive amount at 15%, multiply the net amount by 0.15 and add the result to the net amount. For a VAT-inclusive amount, divide the gross total by 1.15 to find the net amount, or multiply the gross total by 15 and divide by 115 to isolate VAT. Keep unrounded values in the calculation record and apply one documented rounding policy.

A common error is multiplying a VAT-inclusive total by 15%. That calculates 15% of the gross amount instead of extracting the tax already inside it. Use 15 divided by 115 for extraction. Another common error is subtracting all purchase VAT from sales VAT without checking whether the purchase is creditable and supported.

Invoice and input-credit evidence

The proclamation requires registered suppliers to issue tax invoices in the prescribed circumstances, and the 2025 regulation supplies detailed implementation rules. A controlled invoice should identify the supplier and customer where required, VAT registration details, invoice number and date, description, quantity, net amount, rate, VAT amount and gross total. Use the current regulation and approved invoicing system for the exact particulars.

Preserve one original invoice trail. The proclamation permits clearly marked copies in specified circumstances and prohibits duplicate originals. If a registered customer has not received the required original invoice, the customer may request it within 60 days after the supply. The supplier must comply within 14 days after receiving that request. Keep the request and response with the transaction record.

Input VAT becomes creditable only when the legal conditions and documents are satisfied. Article 29 says that if the required evidence is not held when a return is filed, the credit is deferred until the first accounting period in which the evidence is held. A missed credit may be claimed in a later period only within 12 calendar months after the period in which it arose.

Do not treat every purchase as creditable. Article 30 restricts input credits for passenger vehicles and related costs unless the taxable activity meets the stated dealing or hiring condition. It also restricts entertainment and social or recreational membership costs, subject to the law's exact exceptions. Separate blocked, mixed-use and fully creditable purchases during the month instead of correcting the whole register at filing time.

Newly registered persons have a specific opening-credit rule. Article 31 can allow input credit for qualifying trading stock acquired within six months before registration and qualifying capital goods acquired within three years before registration, provided the goods are held at registration, used for taxable supplies and supported by satisfactory evidence. Prepare the opening inventory and document pack before the first return.

Monthly return and payment workflow

Article 58 requires a registered person to file for each accounting period by the last day of the next calendar month. A return is required whether or not net VAT is payable. Article 59 makes the net amount due on that same filing date. Confirm the assigned accounting period and current electronic process with the responsible Tax Authority before relying on an internal calendar.

  1. Confirm status and period. Check the VAT certificate, TIN, assigned accounting period, filing channel, return deadline and payment owner.
  2. Freeze the sales register. Record every invoice, credit note, debit note, tax code, net amount, VAT and gross amount. Investigate missing or duplicate numbers.
  3. Freeze the purchase register. Link each proposed input credit to the supplier, original invoice, business purpose, receipt or service evidence and payment record.
  4. Reconcile output VAT. Compare invoice totals with the general ledger, cash and bank receipts, contract records and any electronic sales system.
  5. Review input credits. Remove unsupported, late, duplicate, private, restricted or exempt-activity costs. Record deferred credits separately.
  6. Calculate net VAT. Apply the proclamation's output less allowed-input structure, including relevant adjustments and reverse-charge amounts.
  7. Review the return. A second person should check identity, period, rates, non-standard treatments, adjustments, carried credits and the ledger-to-return bridge.
  8. Submit and pay. Preserve the filed return, acknowledgement, payment instruction, receipt, bank evidence and any portal error or correspondence.

Excess input tax generally carries forward under Article 48. If an amount remains after six accounting periods, the registered person may apply for a refund within the statutory conditions and time limit. Export-heavy taxpayers and qualifying large capital-goods cases have separate refund provisions. A credit on a spreadsheet is not proof that cash will be refunded.

Controls that prevent VAT errors

Use one effective-dated rate table. Lock the current 15% and 0% paths and keep the legal source beside them. Historical invoices should retain their original framework without changing current calculations.

Monitor the threshold continuously. Review actual and forecast supplies every month. Keep exclusions and any aggregation decision documented so the seven-day application window is not discovered late.

Verify counterparties. Use the Ministry of Revenue TIN search as an identity check, then retain the supplier's VAT evidence required for the credit. A search result alone does not prove that every invoice is valid.

Protect invoice sequencing. Investigate gaps, duplicates, cancelled documents and manual overrides. Corrections should remain traceable through credit or debit notes rather than deleting history.

Separate deferred credits. An invoice that arrives late should not be forced into the wrong period. Keep a controlled deferred-credit schedule and apply the 12-month claim limit.

Reconcile three ways. Match transaction registers, VAT control accounts and the submitted return. A return that agrees with only one total can still contain a wrong code or duplicate invoice.

Record change notifications. Changes to business locations, contact details, online channels or activity can trigger the 30-day written notification rule. Put tax registration review into the same process that updates licences and company records.
