A Namibia VAT return starts with the right tax period and supporting invoices, not just a percentage applied to sales. You need to know whether the business is registered, which supplies belong in the return, what input tax is deductible and whether the final submission and payment reached the correct account.

Sources reviewed on August 31, 2026. This guide uses the consolidated Value-Added Tax Act published by the Namibia Revenue Agency, including Act 5 of 2024, together with NamRA's return and ITAS guidance. It is a preparation guide, not tax advice, a classification ruling or an official filing service. Special sectors and disputed transactions need individual review.

Use the Namibia VAT Calculator & Invoice to check standard-rate arithmetic and invoice totals against confirmed inputs. It does not access ITAS, register a business, submit a return or establish entitlement to a deduction. Keep account passwords, tax identifiers and customer records out of general calculator notes.

1. Resolve registration before charging VAT

The general compulsory registration threshold is taxable supplies exceeding N$1 million in a 12-month period. Section 15 of the consolidated VAT Act includes both a backward-looking test and a forward-looking test where there are reasonable grounds to expect that amount to be exceeded. It is not simply a test of the last financial year's profit.

The 2024 amendment took effect on 1 October 2024. At this review, the ITAS tax-summary page still displayed N$500,000. That older summary should not override the amended Act. If your accountant, software settings and an old registration checklist use different amounts, resolve the difference before deciding that the business is outside VAT.

Section 15 requires an application within 21 days of becoming liable. Voluntary registration is a separate route with its own conditions, including the N$200,000 expected taxable-supplies test in section 15(4). It is not automatic approval for every small business. Existing registration also does not disappear simply because turnover falls below the compulsory threshold: cancellation is a separate process under section 17.

For your working file, retain the turnover analysis, the period covered, any forward contracts used in the assessment and the actual registration decision. Record the effective date shown by NamRA. Those records explain why you started accounting for VAT when you did; an undated spreadsheet total cannot do that.

2. Check the assigned tax period, then the due date

Under section 24, a registered person must file by the 25th day of the month following the end of the tax period, including when no tax is payable. Section 30 links ordinary return tax payment to that deadline. A return-filing extension does not itself extend the payment deadline, as section 24(5) expressly states.

Do not read a calendar entry marked VAT on the 25th as proof that every business files monthly. Section 23 provides two-month periods for persons registered under section 15(1), divided into categories assigned by the Commissioner. Category A ends in January, March, May, July, September and November; Category B ends in February, April, June, August, October and December.

The Act separately provides elected periods for farming-only taxable activities and six-month periods for section 15(4) registrations, subject to its conditions and approved changes. Check the registration record and the period presented in ITAS. A spreadsheet organised by calendar month can still support a two-month return, but both months must be included exactly once.

Our recommended control is a short filing register with the tax type, period start, period end, due date, preparer and reviewer. Before opening a new return, compare its period with the previous submission. Escalate an unexpected period or missing return to NamRA rather than forcing figures into the nearest available form.

3. Build the return from transaction records

Separate sales from purchases, and separate the value before VAT from VAT itself. Keep standard-rated, zero-rated and exempt transactions distinguishable. Zero-rating and exemption are different legal treatments, not interchangeable labels for a transaction where the customer paid no VAT. Sections 9 and 10 and Schedules III and IV set the relevant boundaries.

NamRA's VAT 2 return shows the relationship between sales, capital goods sold, output adjustments, local purchases, imported purchases, capital goods and input adjustments. Use it as a reference for how the totals connect, not as authority to ignore the current ITAS form or to reuse old banking details printed on a downloaded form.

Prepare separate schedules for sales invoices, purchase invoices, credit notes, debit notes and imports. Each entry should identify the document, transaction date, relevant return period, value, tax treatment and VAT amount. For a disputed treatment, add the precise statutory provision or written advice relied on. A description such as business expense is not enough to establish deductibility.

Reconcile the schedules to the accounting ledger and investigate differences before submitting. Look for duplicate invoice numbers, missing sequences, documents posted after the cut-off and credit notes disconnected from their original invoices. Keep an explanation for each adjustment so a reviewer can follow the movement from transactions to return totals.

4. Check the invoice before claiming input VAT

Schedule VI of the Act specifies tax-invoice particulars. The standard requirements include a prominent tax-invoice label; the supplier's name, address and VAT registration number; the recipient's name and address; an individual serial number and issue date; a description and quantity or volume; and the tax, consideration before tax and consideration including tax.

Use that list to review the actual document, while recognising that the Act contains specific exceptions and permissions. A quotation, payment request or bank confirmation does not automatically become a tax invoice because its total includes an amount labelled VAT. Ask the supplier to correct deficient documentation through its own records rather than editing the supplier's invoice yourself.

Section 18(2) generally requires the relevant tax invoice, tax note or permitted documentation to be held when the return claiming the deduction is furnished. For imports, the statutory evidence includes the bill of entry. Section 19 contains further deduction rules, including restrictions and mixed taxable-and-exempt activity treatment. Paying for an item through a business account is not, on its own, proof that all its VAT is deductible.

Maintain an unresolved-document list separate from approved input tax. Record what is missing, who requested it and the next action. This makes the claimed total reviewable without implying that a pending supplier correction is already complete. Get advice on the appropriate claiming period and statutory time limits instead of moving old invoices between periods without an explanation.

5. Reconcile the VAT calculation without inventing a case

Section 6 sets the standard rate at 15%. Once the transaction is confirmed as standard-rated, the arithmetic below follows directly from that rate. These are formula checks, not fictional taxpayer examples or proof of a transaction's legal treatment.

  • VAT on a confirmed VAT-exclusive amount: multiply that amount by 15%.
  • VAT-inclusive total: multiply the VAT-exclusive amount by 1.15.
  • VAT-exclusive value from an inclusive total: divide the total by 1.15.
  • VAT contained in an inclusive total: multiply the total by 15/115.

The last distinction matters: multiplying an already VAT-inclusive total by 15% overstates the VAT component. Compare the calculator's inclusive and exclusive modes with the wording on the invoice before copying a result into your working schedule. Keep rounding consistent with the actual invoice and the filing requirements.

The VAT 2 form provides a useful factual cross-check: total output tax flows from block 12 to block 5, total input tax from block 19 to block 6, and the difference goes to block 7. A return is therefore not simply 15% of bank deposits less 15% of payments. Eligibility, timing and documented adjustments come first.

Where a sale is cancelled, goods are returned or agreed consideration changes, sections 20 and 22 govern adjustments and tax notes. Preserve the original invoice and the linked correction. Do not silently rewrite the original transaction to make the current ledger match. A negative result also needs review; it is not evidence that a refund has been approved.

6. Keep import obligations distinct from the domestic return

Import VAT has its own declaration and payment rules. Section 14 distinguishes ordinary imports of goods, goods handled through an approved VAT import account and taxable imports of services. A domestic VAT return should not be used as a reason to overlook a separate import deadline.

For an approved VAT import account, section 14(1)(c) generally requires payment by the 20th day of the following month and provides for a no-import declaration where applicable. Section 14(2), as amended in 2024, gives the declaration and payment timing for the taxable service imports it covers as within 20 days following the month of import.

Not every payment to an overseas supplier is automatically a taxable import of services. The NamRA summary distinguishes services imported by a non-registered resident or for exempt supplies from services imported by a registered person to make taxable supplies. Confirm the statutory treatment of the actual arrangement, including any exemption, before calculating a liability.

Keep the overseas invoice, contract, customs or import declaration and payment evidence together. Reconcile any eligible input claim to the import documentation so the same tax is not claimed twice. For regional purchases, do not assume that membership of a customs arrangement removes Namibia VAT obligations.

7. Submit through ITAS and retain the actual outcome

The official ITAS guidance page links registration instructions, a user manual covering VAT returns and a guide explaining return statuses. Start from that page or the official ITAS portal. Confirm that the correct taxpayer account is linked and that the authorised preparer can access the relevant VAT period.

Before submission, compare the on-screen totals with the reviewed schedules. Check the tax period, output VAT, deductible input VAT and adjustments individually. Resolve validation messages rather than repeatedly resubmitting unchanged figures. Save the submitted return and the acknowledgement or reference provided by the service.

Maintain separate internal states for prepared, reviewed, submitted and paid. Saving a draft is not submission; a submission reference is not payment allocation. Use the official status guide to interpret the result actually displayed, and keep any follow-up request with the return's record.

If access fails near the deadline, record the time, error and support contact privately and ask NamRA for the appropriate next step. Do not assume an outage grants an extension. Avoid sharing passwords or full account screenshots in ordinary group chats. Send only the material needed to the authorised adviser or official support channel.

8. Close the period with payment and record checks

Obtain current payment instructions from NamRA or ITAS and use the required taxpayer, tax-type and period reference. After paying, compare the banking evidence with the account record. Investigate a payment that remains unmatched rather than assuming the bank debit alone has cleared the liability. Keep filing and payment evidence side by side.

If the return shows excess input tax, section 38 governs refunds and related handling. A calculated credit is not a guaranteed cash receipt on a chosen date. Follow any verification request and track the official outcome. Do not finance payroll or supplier commitments on the assumption that an unapproved refund is already available.

Section 48 requires tax invoices, tax notes, customs documentation and accounting records, with English-language and location requirements and specific provisions for computerised records. Keep a readable archive and a backup that an authorised reviewer can retrieve. Ask for advice before deleting older records or changing storage arrangements; a successful current return does not erase earlier obligations.

For nearby but different tasks, use the Namibia PAYE guide for employee-tax preparation and the African VAT rates guide for country comparisons. Neither replaces the evidence for this Namibia return. Assign the next review date and leave a clear list of any unresolved invoices, classifications or account allocations.

Frequently asked questions

Is Namibia's compulsory VAT threshold still N$500,000?

No. Act 5 of 2024 raised the general compulsory threshold to taxable supplies exceeding N$1 million in a 12-month period from 1 October 2024. Older ITAS summary text still shows the former amount.

Must I file when no VAT is payable?

Yes. Section 24 requires a registered person to submit a return for each tax period whether or not tax is payable.

Does every business file Namibia VAT monthly?

No. Section 23 provides different tax periods. Check the period assigned to your registration instead of assuming monthly filing.

Can AfroTools submit my return or approve a refund?

No. The Namibia VAT calculator supports arithmetic and invoice preparation only. Use NamRA and ITAS for filing, account records and refund decisions.
