Replacement Tyre Quotation Checklist for Africa

Amara MoyoOctober 10, 202611 min read

A replacement-tyre quotation needs to identify what will be fitted, what the workshop will do and what the vehicle owner will pay. A price for an unspecified tyre cannot answer those questions. For households, delivery operators and small fleets across Africa, the useful comparison starts with an approved specification and ends with a documented installation.

Use the AfroTools Vehicle Operating Cost Calculator for the broader annual ownership picture. Keep the actual tyre quotation beside that estimate: the calculator uses fixed maintenance assumptions and does not accept a separate tyre invoice or decide which tyre fits your vehicle. This guide supplies the purchasing checklist that the calculator cannot provide.

Define the vehicle and replacement job

Start the request with the vehicle make, model, year, variant and normal use. Record whether the job concerns a private car, van, pickup or another vehicle class. This checklist concentrates on cars and light vehicles; a commercial truck, agricultural machine or motorcycle needs its own technical fitment guidance. The same shop may sell all of these without the same specification applying to each.

Locate the approved tyre information in the owner manual or vehicle placard. Photograph or transcribe it for your private purchasing record. Also record the existing tyre markings, but do not assume they are correct simply because the vehicle arrived with them. A previous owner may have changed wheels or fitted a substitute. Ask the professional to resolve any disagreement before comparing prices.

Describe the actual operating conditions without using them to invent a new specification. Regular loads, unpaved access roads, long highway trips and delivery stops help explain the job. They do not authorise a higher payload, a different wheel diameter or an improvised inflation pressure. Keep vehicle suitability and purchasing convenience as separate questions.

Make every offer identify the exact tyre

Require the brand, product line, full size designation, load index, speed rating and relevant construction or original-equipment markings in writing. A quotation saying only the rim diameter leaves too much unresolved. Record the number of tyres, intended wheel positions and whether the spare is part of the job. Ask the supplier to identify any proposed substitution explicitly.

Michelin Africa's rating guide explains that the load index refers to the load capacity of an individual tyre and the speed rating is a separate specification. Neither marking increases the vehicle's permitted carrying capacity. Use the vehicle's approved requirements and ask a specialist to confirm compatibility, including any special fitment conditions.

Continental's purchasing guide directs buyers to the vehicle documentation and explains the importance of size, load and speed specifications. Treat those as acceptance fields on the quotation. A cheaper product that fails the required specification is not a comparable offer; request a corrected quote rather than assigning a discount to the mismatch.

Separate the inspection decision from the purchase

Ask the workshop to document why replacement is recommended. Useful observations include the wheel position, measured tread condition, visible damage, recurring pressure loss and relevant date markings. Keep the inspection result with the quote. A recommendation based on damage needs different evidence from a routine budget allowance or a seller's promotional upgrade.

Michelin Africa's tyre-life guidance, updated March 25, 2026, explains the four-digit manufacturing-date code and recommends annual professional checks after five years of use. Its precautionary ten-year replacement recommendation also includes spares. These are attributed manufacturer recommendations, not proof that every younger tyre is usable or that every country applies that age rule.

Do not reduce the decision to date or tread alone. Keep any report of damage or abnormal wear visible to the person approving the purchase. A budgeting article cannot clear a tyre for continued driving, diagnose a sidewall or assess an internal injury. If the workshop identifies a safety concern, obtain its specific handling instructions rather than delaying the matter until the next budget review.

Resolve pairs, mixed fitment and special systems

When replacing fewer than all tyres, write down which tyres remain on each axle. Ask the specialist to confirm the proposed combination against the vehicle instructions. This is especially important when front and rear specifications differ, or the vehicle has an all-wheel-drive system. A quote for two tyres should include the installation positions and any reason the remaining set is acceptable.

Continental's mixed-tyre guidance recommends consistent tyres and highlights vehicle-approved exceptions. Do not convert that into a universal permission to mix whenever stock is scarce. Obtain the fitment decision for the particular vehicle, and retain it with the approved quote. Availability alone does not establish compatibility.

Record any run-flat requirement, tyre-pressure monitoring system or special original-equipment marking. Ask whether the proposed product changes an existing system and what the vehicle manufacturer permits. Include the spare arrangement where relevant. Do not approve a conversion merely because the initial purchase is cheaper: the technical decision and any additional equipment need their own written explanation.

Compare the complete installed price

Request a dated, itemised quotation in one stated currency. Keep the per-tyre price and quantity separate from removal, fitting, balancing, valve work, monitoring-system service, wheel checks, alignment and disposal. Not every service is required in every job. The point is to identify what is included and what still requires inspection or separate approval.

Ask how taxes are displayed and when payment is due. Record the quote's validity, stock status, branch and installation appointment. If a deposit is requested, obtain the cancellation, refund and substitution terms before paying. A stock promise on a dated quote is different from an undated catalogue listing, and neither confirms that the installed product will match unless you check it.

Compare offers only after matching their scope. A supply-only price and a fitted price answer different questions. If alignment is quoted conditionally, retain that condition rather than treating it as a settled expense. Ask the workshop to contact the authorised approver before extra work. A clear approval limit prevents the collection invoice from becoming the first record of additional services.

The installed total is the sum of the approved tyres and services, plus applicable taxes and fees, less documented discounts. Build that total from the actual quote. This guide supplies no tyre prices or invented fleet example. Differences between suppliers should be traceable to a product, service, term or charge that you can inspect.

Keep cash spending separate from the annual model

The vehicle calculator currently models maintenance as a percentage of vehicle value: six percent for its listed car categories and four percent for motorcycles. Its registration and depreciation values are also planning assumptions. Those figures are implemented model defaults, not local authority charges, workshop quotes or a tyre-replacement schedule. Read the tool's assumption note before interpreting its total.

Keep the full tyre invoice in the cash budget for the period when payment occurs. Separately, record how your operating-cost estimate treats maintenance. If that estimate already contains a maintenance allowance, simply adding the whole tyre bill to the same total may count part of the maintenance twice. Label the model total and actual expense ledger separately until you reconcile them.

The vehicle running-cost guide explains the broader cost-per-distance workflow, while the fleet fuel-variance checklist covers fuel records. Use measured odometer and expense records for later review. Do not promise a replacement interval or expected mileage from a catalogue claim, and do not divide a tyre price by an invented service life.

Check the installation before closing the job

At handover, compare the invoice and fitted product with the approved quotation. Record the brand, line, size, load and speed markings, date codes and wheel positions. Resolve a substitution before accepting the job as complete. Retain the installation date and odometer reading so that later wear observations have a starting record.

Ask which pressure setting was applied and where its recommendation comes from. Michelin's pressure guidance explains checking pressure when tyres are cold and using the recommended values. Do not replace the vehicle's specified setting with a generic number copied from another car or the sidewall's maximum marking.

Obtain instructions for follow-up checks and any monitoring-system work performed. If the quote included alignment or another measured service, request the corresponding report. Record unresolved faults, warnings or vibration for the workshop to address. These records support accountability; they do not turn the owner's visual check into a professional safety inspection.

Keep warranty and fleet records usable

Ask the seller for the current warranty document, claim contact, exclusions and any registration requirements. Distinguish manufacturing-defect cover from separately offered road-hazard protection. Keep the invoice and product identifiers. Do not assume that a manufacturer's programme in one country or sales channel applies to another supplier, a different product line or commercial use.

For a small fleet, assign the expense to the vehicle and wheel position rather than filing every tyre invoice in one undifferentiated folder. Record transfers between vehicles, rotation work and removals with dates and odometer readings. This makes subsequent cost review possible without pretending that every removal represents wear-out or that every tyre has the same operating history.

Keep registration numbers, driver details and supplier payment information in your private records. The public guide and calculator do not need those identifiers to explain the budget. Share only the technical and commercial fields necessary for a quote or claim, through a channel you choose. Keep a portable copy of your invoices and approvals independently of any account service.

Close with a clear approval record

Before approval, confirm five things: the specification is supported, the replacement scope is explained, the installed price is complete, the supplier's terms are understood and the technical questions have an accountable answer. Mark unresolved fields rather than filling them with assumptions. The lowest visible unit price is only one input to this decision.

Use the roadworthiness preparation guide when the vehicle also needs an official inspection. Tyre procurement, routine maintenance and statutory certification remain separate workflows. Preserve the quote, approval, invoice and technical reports together, then review the actual expense against your annual budget using the same currency and accounting period.

Frequently asked questions

Can the calculator identify the right replacement tyre?

No. It estimates annual operating costs with fixed assumptions. Use the vehicle documentation and a qualified tyre professional for fitment.

Is the tyre's manufacturing date enough to decide replacement?

No. Date, condition, use and manufacturer guidance all matter. A qualified professional must assess damage and suitability.

Should I add the invoice to the calculator's total?

Keep actual cash expenses separately and reconcile them with the maintenance allowance before combining totals, to avoid double counting.

Does a new tyre invoice prove roadworthiness?

No. An invoice documents a purchase and installation. Official inspection and certification follow the applicable local process.
