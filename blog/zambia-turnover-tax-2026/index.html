Zambia Turnover Tax 2026: 5% Rate, Filing and VAT

AfroTools Editorial TeamSeptember 1, 202612 min read

Zambia's turnover tax rules changed for the 2026 charge year. The enacted schedule now puts annual turnover of K30,000 or less at 0% and annual turnover above K30,000 up to K5 million at 5%. It also reduces the monthly charge on an overdue turnover tax payment from 5% to 0.5% of the unpaid amount. These rules took effect on January 1, 2026.

This is a gross-turnover tax, not a profit tax. A business must classify the income correctly, record all sales, file the monthly return and keep payment evidence even when expenses are high. Use the Africa Business Tax Calendar to organise the filing reminder. If VAT also applies, use the Zambia VAT Calculator for invoice arithmetic, then keep the VAT and turnover tax records separate.

Sources checked: September 1, 2026. The controlling rate and threshold are taken from Zambia's Income Tax (Amendment) (No. 2) Act, 2025, which came into operation on January 1, 2026. ZRA's live tax-information summary still displays the older K800,000 threshold and 4% rate, so this guide does not use that older summary for the 2026 bands.

The annual table is simple, but the operating boundary is not. Consultancy income is excluded. Mining has a separate rule, including a 2026 threshold change for artisanal and small-scale mining. Public service vehicles use their own presumptive-tax schedule. Rental income also has its own rate structure. Do not label every small-business receipt as turnover tax merely because the amount is below K5 million.

2. Why the enacted 2026 text matters more than the old web summary

The Income Tax (Amendment) (No. 2) Act, 2025 received assent on December 23, 2025 and states that it came into operation on January 1, 2026. Section 16 replaces Part II of the Ninth Schedule with the K30,000 exempt band and the 5% band up to K5 million. Section 7 writes the K5 million ordinary-business limit into section 64A.

The National Assembly's Act page provides the official publication trail. The 2026 Budget Speech gives the policy explanation: raise the exempt amount, align the artisanal and small-scale mining threshold, reduce the overdue-payment charge and allow businesses that qualify for voluntary VAT registration to remain eligible for turnover tax.

The current ZRA tax-information page is still useful for procedure, registration, record retention and changeover guidance. Its rate section, however, shows the superseded K800,000 limit and 4% rate. A current compliance file should record that conflict and use the newer enacted schedule. Do not average the old and new numbers or choose the lower rate without a legal basis.

3. Who can fall within ordinary turnover tax

The enacted rule allows the Commissioner-General to make a standard assessment for a person carrying on a business with annual turnover of K5 million or less. The rule excludes income from consultancy services and mining operations. The same amendment creates a separate K5 million threshold for holders of artisanal or small-scale mining licences, but it points that activity to a different part of the Ninth Schedule.

Start by identifying the activity that produced each income stream. A shop's sales, a consultant's professional fee, rent from property and a passenger-transport receipt can follow different tax treatments even when they enter the same bank or mobile-money account. Separate them before calculating the monthly return.

ZRA's published guidance also lists income that is already subject to final withholding tax as outside ordinary turnover tax. This includes specified categories such as dividends and some non-resident payments. The classification depends on the payer, recipient, source and whether the withholding is final. Keep the certificate or other source record instead of treating the bank deposit alone as proof of the tax result.

A business also needs a TPIN and the correct tax-type registration. ZRA guidance says a new taxpayer should notify the Commissioner-General within 30 days of starting business and register the eligible tax types. The registration choice should match the actual business activity. It is not enough to have a TPIN if the turnover tax account is missing or the business is registered under the wrong activity.

4. How the 5% calculation works

The legal table has two bands:

Annual turnover2026 rateCalculation boundary
K30,000 or less0%No turnover tax within the exempt band.
Above K30,000 up to K5,000,0005%Apply 5% to turnover above the exempt band.

A formula walkthrough can be made directly from the enacted table without inventing a business story. If annual turnover is K130,000, the first K30,000 is within the 0% band. The remaining K100,000 is multiplied by 5%, giving K5,000 for the year before considering monthly filings and any ZRA account adjustments. If annual turnover is K30,000, the table puts it in the 0% band.

The Budget Speech described the new exempt amount as K2,500 per month, while the enacted Ninth Schedule states K30,000 per annum. When preparing a monthly return, follow the current ZRA return fields and portal calculation. Keep the annual reconciliation so cumulative turnover and the annual band can be checked. Do not deduct purchases, wages, rent or fuel from turnover before applying the rate. Those costs matter to business profit, but turnover tax is charged on gross turnover.

Credit sales still form part of sales records. Cash, bank, card and mobile-money collections should reconcile to invoices and the sales ledger, but payment-channel totals are not automatically the same as taxable turnover. Owner capital, loans, refunds, transfers between accounts and VAT collected may require separate treatment. Preserve a reconciliation that explains every difference.

5. Monthly return and payment workflow

The ZRA payment due-date page says turnover tax payment is due by the 14th day of the month after the month of turnover. ZRA's tax-information guidance says an electronic return is due by the 14th, while a manual return is due earlier, by the 5th. Confirm the current channel shown in the taxpayer account before relying on a paper process.

  1. Close the sales period. Lock the invoice, receipt, cashbook, bank, card and mobile-money records for the month.
  2. Classify receipts. Separate business turnover from loans, capital, transfers, refunds, final-withholding income and excluded activities.
  3. Reconcile gross turnover. Explain missing invoice numbers, cancelled sales, credit notes and timing differences.
  4. Prepare the return. Use the current TaxOnline return fields and retain a calculation note tied to the ledger.
  5. Submit before the applicable deadline. Do not leave a zero-liability period unfiled merely because no payment is expected.
  6. Create and settle the payment reference. Match the payment registration number or other official reference to the correct tax period.
  7. Save proof. Keep the submitted return, acknowledgement, payment record and reconciliation together.

The ZRA filing FAQ describes a return as a legally binding declaration and recommends electronic filing through TaxOnline. It also explains that a payment registration number is a time-limited reference for the intended tax payment. Generate the reference when payment is ready and confirm that the ZRA account shows the correct period after settlement.

6. What the 0.5% overdue-payment rule does and does not cover

Section 9 of the 2025 amendment inserts a 0.5% charge for each month or part of a month in which turnover tax remains unpaid. The charge applies to the unpaid tax or unpaid part. It replaced the much higher 5% monthly overdue-payment rate from January 1, 2026.

That change does not turn the 14th into an optional date. It also does not prove that every filing penalty, interest amount or other compliance charge is 0.5%. A late return and a late payment are different failures. If a ZRA statement contains an assessment, interest or filing penalty, read the line description and period before calculating what should be paid or disputed.

Do not delay payment merely because the percentage is smaller. A partial payment leaves the unpaid balance exposed, and a part of a month still counts under the enacted wording. If the account is wrong, use the official objection, amendment or support process rather than changing the ledger to match an unexplained ZRA balance.

7. Turnover tax and VAT can coexist

The 2026 amendment removed the older wording that excluded a business qualifying for voluntary VAT registration from turnover tax. The budget material explains that the change allows an eligible business to use turnover tax while voluntarily registered for VAT. A business above the compulsory VAT threshold may also have VAT obligations while remaining below the K5 million turnover tax ceiling.

VAT and turnover tax should never be collapsed into one calculation:

8. Monitor the K5 million boundary throughout the year

Use a cumulative turnover column in the monthly ledger. Include the current month, the year-to-date total and a supported forecast for signed work or recurring sales. The purpose is to identify a likely threshold crossing early enough to obtain advice and prepare the next tax-year records.

ZRA's published changeover guidance says a taxpayer who discovers that annual turnover will exceed the threshold should notify the Commissioner-General immediately. The taxpayer continues paying turnover tax through that charge year and moves to the income-tax system from the beginning of the following charge year. The guidance also says the migrated taxpayer files an annual income-tax return for the preceding year and receives credit for turnover tax already paid, subject to the assessment.

Do not split one business into informal ledgers or related accounts to conceal the threshold. Keep branches, mobile-money tills, card processors and online sales within the same controlled reconciliation where they belong to the same taxpayer. If the ownership or entity boundary is genuinely different, retain the legal and operational documents that support it.

9. Records to keep for a ZRA-ready file

ZRA's tax-information guidance says turnover-tax business records should be retained for up to six years. A useful monthly file includes:

  • numbered invoices, receipts and credit notes;
  • daily cash and sales summaries;
  • bank, card and mobile-money settlement records;
  • a reconciliation from channel receipts to gross turnover;
  • notes for excluded income, final-withholding income, owner funds and transfers;
  • the submitted return and electronic acknowledgement;
  • the payment reference, bank or wallet proof and ZRA account confirmation;
  • VAT schedules and invoices in a separate section when VAT applies;
  • the cumulative annual threshold monitor;
  • registration, tax-type and change notifications.

Keep records in a format that another reviewer can trace. A screenshot of a total is not enough if the underlying sales cannot be identified. Preserve exported files with the tax period in the filename, restrict access to customer and payment data, and keep a backup outside the device used for daily sales.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Zambia's turnover tax rate in 2026?

The Income Tax (Amendment) (No. 2) Act, 2025 sets a 0% rate on annual turnover of K30,000 or less and 5% on annual turnover above K30,000 up to K5 million, effective January 1, 2026.

When is Zambia turnover tax due?

ZRA's payment due-date page says payment is due by the 14th day of the month after the month of turnover. ZRA guidance also distinguishes earlier manual filing from electronic filing by the 14th.

Can a turnover tax business also register for VAT?

Yes, where VAT registration applies or the business qualifies for voluntary VAT registration. VAT remains a separate tax with its own invoices, returns and payments.

Is consultancy income covered by Zambia turnover tax?

No. The enacted 2026 wording excludes income earned from the provision of consultancy services from the ordinary turnover tax rule.

What should happen if annual turnover will exceed K5 million?

ZRA guidance says the taxpayer should notify the Commissioner-General immediately, continue turnover tax through that charge year, and move to the applicable income-tax system from the next charge year.
