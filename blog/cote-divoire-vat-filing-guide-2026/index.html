Côte d'Ivoire VAT Filing Guide 2026: Rates, Returns and Refunds

A practical DGI workflow for the 18% common rate, evidence-gated 9% treatment, monthly filing, FNE invoice evidence, input deductions and VAT credits.

An Ivorian VAT return is a reconciliation exercise, not a percentage exercise. Before filing, the business must confirm that it is entitled to charge VAT, classify each sale, calculate output VAT, test purchase deductions, reconcile certified invoice records to the ledger and determine whether the period ends in tax payable or a credit. A calculator can check the arithmetic, but it cannot perform those legal and evidence checks.

This guide was verified on September 25, 2026 against the Direction Générale des Impôts, or DGI, 2026 Finance Annex material, the current calendar of tax obligations, the official notice on the 9% reduced rate, the DGI VAT guide, the normalized electronic invoice portal and the current VAT-credit refund procedure. The sources do not remove the need to check the taxpayer's assigned regime, DGI unit and current e-Impôts account before submission.

The 18% rate is the safe starting point only after the supply has been confirmed as taxable. It should not be used for an exempt or outside-scope transaction, and a 9% rate should not be selected merely because a product seems socially important. The January 2026 ordinance followed the Finance Annex and placed specified products and operations at 9% instead of the common 18% rate. The exact ordinance item and transaction facts are the evidence gate.

The 2026 Finance Annex also changed specific VAT treatments. The DGI analysis says factoring remuneration is now expressly subject to VAT, and the tax must be shown separately if the customer is to support a deduction. It also says credits generated by operations where VAT was borne through special Treasury cheques became eligible for the refund procedure. These are targeted changes, not permission to reclassify unrelated supplies.

Confirm who may charge VAT and which rate applies

Start with the taxpayer's regime. The DGI's guide to the Ivorian tax system distinguishes the entrepreneur, microenterprise, simplified real and normal real regimes. It states that taxpayers under the microenterprise tax regime are not allowed to invoice VAT or transfer a deduction right. A qualifying microenterprise may have an option into the simplified real regime, but the option must exist in the official taxpayer record before the business acts as a VAT collector.

For a business under a real regime, build a tax-treatment map for each product and service. The map should record the description, customer type, place of supply, common, reduced, exempt or other treatment, the legal source, evidence required and the date last reviewed. Review new products, bundled supplies, credit notes, imports and non-resident transactions separately.

The common rate is 18%. The current 9% treatment is restricted to the goods and operations named in the 2026 ordinance. A supplier catalogue, competitor invoice or old accounting code does not prove eligibility. If the source wording and actual transaction do not match, hold the invoice for review or use the confirmed common treatment. Do not silently copy a former exemption into the 9% band.

Exports and other special treatments also need specific evidence. Keep customs, transport, customer and payment records with the invoice and ledger entry. The fact that the customer is outside Côte d'Ivoire is not enough on its own to determine the VAT result for every service or digitally delivered supply.

Use the deadline for the taxpayer's DGI unit and activity

The current DGI calendar lists VAT declaration and payment on the 15th for taxpayers under a real tax regime. It then gives separate dates for taxpayers managed by the Direction des grandes Entreprises, or DGE, and Direction des moyennes Entreprises, or DME: the 10th of the following month for industrial, petroleum and mining businesses, the 15th for commercial businesses, and the 20th for service providers.

Those dates make the taxpayer's assignment and activity classification operational controls. Save the DGI registration details, assigned service, regime, principal activity and the due date shown in e-Impôts. A group with different entities should not assume that every company shares the same deadline. A business with mixed activities should obtain a confirmed filing date rather than choosing the latest date in the calendar.

The return and payment should be planned together. Set an internal close before the legal deadline for invoice cut-off, sales and purchase reconciliation, review and payment approval. Retain the submitted declaration, electronic acknowledgement, payment reference, bank evidence and any DGI message. A bank debit without a matching declaration and period reference is not a complete filing record.

How to calculate 18% VAT

For a VAT-exclusive amount at the common rate, multiply the net amount by 0.18, then add the VAT to the net amount. For a VAT-inclusive amount, divide the gross total by 1.18 to find the net amount, or multiply the gross amount by 18 and divide by 118 to isolate the VAT. Apply one documented rounding method across the invoice, sales register, ledger and return.

These are arithmetic examples using the published common rate, not fictional taxpayers. The XOF 100,000 base exists only to make the formula transparent. A 9% calculation belongs in the return only after the supply has passed the exact current-law test.

Do not multiply a VAT-inclusive amount by 18%. That calculates 18% of the gross amount rather than extracting the VAT already included. Also do not treat the result of one invoice calculation as the return liability. The period position depends on all classified sales, allowed deductions, credit notes, prior credits and corrections.

Connect FNE records to the VAT return

Côte d'Ivoire's normalized electronic invoicing system is now part of the VAT evidence chain. The DGI says the FNE process generates or certifies invoices with an official QR code, FNE visual and annual serial. A professionally formatted PDF is not equivalent to that certification. Read the separate Côte d'Ivoire FNE setup guide for registration, platform, API and TERNE workflows.

For output VAT, reconcile every certified sales invoice and credit note to the sales register, revenue, receivables, cash or bank receipt and applicable rate. Investigate sequence gaps, duplicate serials, cancelled drafts, credit notes without an original reference and accounting entries without a certified document.

For input VAT, match the supplier's certified invoice references, supplier identity, date, description, net amount, rate, VAT and gross total to the purchase register and ledger. Confirm the business purpose and receipt of the goods or services. Separate fully deductible amounts from blocked, private, mixed-use or pending-evidence amounts. An invoice showing VAT does not by itself prove that the buyer has a deduction right.

The DGI's normalized-invoice note says businesses must attach a deduction statement to VAT declarations showing invoice references and purchase prices for goods and services that open a deduction right. Build that statement from controlled source records. Do not type a fresh summary that cannot be traced back to the certified invoices and ledger.

The DGI VAT guide also says a deduction missed within the normal period may be used within 12 months from the invoice date. Before relying on that rule, confirm it has not been changed for the period and document why the invoice was omitted, when it was identified and how the correction is reflected. Do not overwrite a closed-period workpaper.

Close the VAT return in eight controlled steps

  1. Confirm the filer. Check the legal entity, NCC, tax regime, DGI unit, period, activity and deadline shown in e-Impôts.
  2. Freeze sales. Record all certified invoices and credit notes with customer, date, classification, net amount, rate, output VAT and gross amount.
  3. Freeze purchases. Link each proposed deduction to the certified supplier invoice, receipt, business purpose, payment and ledger entry.
  4. Reconcile revenue. Compare invoice totals with revenue, receivables, stock movement, cash, bank and any operational sales system.
  5. Review rates and exceptions. Retain the exact legal item for 9%, exemption or other special treatment and remove unsupported codes.
  6. Build the deduction statement. Populate invoice references and purchase amounts from controlled records, then reconcile the statement to the return and ledger.
  7. Calculate and approve. Reconcile output VAT, allowed input VAT, prior credit, adjustments and the resulting payable or credit. Use a second-person review where practical.
  8. Submit, pay and archive. Retain the declaration, acknowledgement, payment proof, workpapers, FNE exports, deduction statement and reviewer sign-off.

Keep the return file reproducible. A reviewer should be able to start from the submitted total, trace it to the sales and purchase registers, and then reach the certified invoices and ledger entries. Unsupported spreadsheet overrides should be listed and resolved rather than hidden in formula changes.

Carry forward and refund VAT credits carefully

A VAT credit can arise when allowed input VAT and valid adjustments exceed output VAT for the period. First confirm the credit through invoice-level reconciliation. Check duplicate purchases, credit notes, imports, timing, blocked items and prior-period carry-forwards before deciding that the balance is real.

The DGI publishes normal and accelerated refund procedures. The normal route applies to eligible refund applicants generally. The accelerated route is restricted to businesses holding the Director General's approval and to banks and financial institutions within the stated leasing operations. The published conditions for accelerated approval include applying before January 31, meeting the stated export-share test, filing VAT returns regularly, avoiding a fraud finding and producing the required documents.

A refund file should identify the taxpayer, period, amount, bank account and the source of the credit. It should include the VAT declarations and deduction statements, invoice and customs evidence where relevant, the required attestations and any security or approval required by the applicable route. Use the current DGI document list. Do not rely on an old file merely because an earlier claim was accepted.

The 2026 DGI analysis says credits from operations financed with special Treasury cheques are now eligible for the refund procedure. That rule concerns the specified public projects and payment mechanism. It should not be treated as a general acceleration for ordinary commercial credits.

If a refund is not being claimed, reconcile the carried-forward balance every month. Keep a schedule by origin period, confirm how much has been used and investigate old amounts that cannot be traced to certified evidence. A credit in the ledger should never remain indefinitely without an owner and review date.

Month-end control checklist

  • Confirm the taxpayer's regime, DGI unit, activity and current e-Impôts deadline.
  • Lock the invoice and credit-note sequence after all approved documents are recorded.
  • Reconcile certified FNE sales to revenue, receivables, cash, bank and stock movement.
  • Match every input deduction to the supplier, certified invoice, purchase and business purpose.
  • Retain the exact legal basis for 9%, exempt and other special treatments.
  • Reconcile the deduction statement to the purchase register, ledger and return.
  • Separate current input VAT, prior credit, adjustments, refund claims and carry-forward balances.
  • Archive the return, acknowledgement, payment proof and reviewer sign-off together.

Treat this guide as a planning and control aid, not an official filing decision or tax opinion. Current DGI instructions, the taxpayer account and transaction-specific law control the actual return.

Côte d'Ivoire VAT questions

What is the standard VAT rate in Côte d'Ivoire in 2026?

The DGI's 2026 material states a common rate of 18%. A 9% reduced rate applies only to products and operations covered by the January 2026 ordinance.

When is the VAT return due?

The DGI calendar places declaration and payment on the 15th for real-regime taxpayers. DGE and DME taxpayers follow sector dates of the 10th, 15th or 20th of the following month.

Can every business charge VAT?

No. The DGI says taxpayers under the microenterprise tax regime cannot invoice VAT or transfer a deduction right unless they validly enter an eligible real regime.

Does a normal PDF invoice support input VAT?

Not by itself. Businesses in scope must use the normalized electronic process, and the buyer must match the certified invoice to the purchase, business purpose, ledger and return.

Does the AfroTools calculator file through e-Impôts?

No. It performs local planning arithmetic. It does not classify a supply, certify an FNE, validate a deduction, submit a return or approve a refund.
