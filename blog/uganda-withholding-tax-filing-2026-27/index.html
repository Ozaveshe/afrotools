Uganda withholding tax is not one flat deduction applied to every supplier invoice. The payer first has to identify the payment, decide whether the duty arises because the payer is a designated withholding agent or because the transaction itself is covered, check the recipient's status, apply the supported rate, file the return, remit the amount and issue evidence to the payee. A clean invoice alone does not complete that chain.

This guide focuses on the practical 2026/27 workflow for Ugandan businesses, finance teams, event operators, platforms and suppliers. It separates the current changes in the Uganda Revenue Authority's FY 2026/27 amendments booklet from the earlier rate categories in URA's 2025/26 handbook. That distinction matters because a recycled rate table can miss a new payment category or the final-tax status of a commission.

The strongest operating rule is to decide the tax treatment before payment approval. If withholding is considered only after the supplier has received the full amount, the payer may have to fund the tax, recover it later or explain why the filed return does not match the payment record.

Classify The Payment Before Applying A Rate

URA defines withholding tax as income tax deducted at source by a withholding agent when paying a payee. The definition is simple, but the classification is not. The same business can make professional-fee payments, buy goods, import stock, pay interest, distribute a commission, book an entertainer and contract with a non-resident service provider. Those payments do not automatically share one rate or one final-tax result.

Start with the underlying agreement and the actual service. Record the payer, payee, residency, gross amount, payment date, currency, invoice description, contract scope and the reason the payer believes withholding applies. If the payment combines goods and services, or includes reimbursable costs, do not split or combine lines merely to reach a preferred tax result. Use the contract, invoice and applicable URA rule.

Next, decide whether the tax is final or creditable. URA explains that final withholding tax settles the income-tax liability for that income. The payee does not aggregate that income, claim related deductions or seek a refund for the final tax. Creditable withholding is an advance payment. The payee may have to report the income and use the certificate as a credit against the assessed liability. A finance team should therefore store the final-or-creditable decision with the rate decision.

Do not deduct from the net amount. URA rate descriptions commonly refer to the gross payment. Establish the supported gross base first, then calculate the deduction. If VAT, expenses or mixed supplies affect the base, confirm the treatment before releasing payment.

What The FY 2026/27 Amendments Add Or Change

URA's official Tax Amendments FY 2026-2027 booklet gives the current change set. The points below are taken from that booklet and should be read with the enacted law and any later URA guidance.

  • Betting or gaming winnings: a payer must withhold 15% on winnings. The booklet defines winnings as the difference between the payout and the amount staked, and lists exclusions for winnings from a licensed national lottery and licensed land-based casinos.
  • Telecommunications commissions: a telecommunications service provider paying commission for telecommunications retail services, mobile network services or mobile money services must withhold 10% of the gross payment.
  • Public entertainers: a person paying a public entertainer must withhold 6% of the gross payment. The booklet's definition covers public, camera and microphone performances for entertainment or similar purposes, including stage, radio, television and digital performers.
  • Insurance-agent commissions: the booklet states that the 10% withholding on commission paid to an insurance agent is final tax.

These changes show why supplier labels are not enough. A creator appearing at an event, a digital performer producing commissioned content and a company supplying an unrelated professional service may all issue invoices, but the payment classification can differ. Event organisers, agencies and platforms should update their vendor forms and approval checklists so the nature of the performance or commission is captured before payment.

The changes also alter the payee's reconciliation. Final-tax treatment for an insurance-agent commission is not the same as a creditable deduction. The certificate and payment record remain important, but the annual income-tax treatment differs. Payroll, accounts payable and the external tax preparer should use the same classification rather than keeping separate undocumented interpretations.

Common Baseline Categories In URA Guidance

URA's 2025/26 Taxation Handbook remains useful background for categories not replaced by a later change. It describes 6% withholding on gross management or professional fees paid by a resident person to a resident professional, subject to the stated exclusions. It also describes 15% on specified interest payments, 15% on many international payments and 15% on gross payments under a Ugandan-source service contract with a non-resident.

The same handbook discusses 6% withholding on imports based on customs value and 6% on covered goods-and-services payments by government bodies, local authorities, government-controlled companies and designated agents where the aggregate contract payment exceeds UGX 1,000,000. The exact payer, payment, exemption and current-law position still need confirmation. Do not use this paragraph as a complete rate table.

For the arithmetic, preserve the formula in the approval record: supported gross base multiplied by the supported withholding rate equals tax withheld. The amount released to the payee is the gross base less the withholding only when the contract and tax treatment support that presentation. Keep VAT and PAYE separate. File, Pay And Issue The Certificate

URA's Domestic Taxes FAQs list the WHT due date as 15 days after the start of the following month. The handbook states the obligation as payment within 15 days after the end of the month in which the payment was made. In practical terms, close the schedule and file by the 15th of the following month, subject to the exact date displayed by URA for the period.

  1. Close the payment register. Reconcile every payment marked as potentially subject to withholding to the contract, invoice, payee identifier and payment date.
  2. Confirm the category and rate. Record the official source, section or current URA guidance used for each category.
  3. Prepare the URA return. URA's return-forms archive lists DT-2006 as the Withholding Tax Return Form. Follow the current portal workflow and current template rather than a renamed or archived copy.
  4. Validate and upload. URA's FAQ instructs taxpayers to use the downloaded template, validate it and upload the generated file through e-Returns for the selected period.
  5. Register and complete payment. Keep the payment registration number and confirmation with the filed return.
  6. Issue the certificate. URA says the withholding agent must issue a withholding tax credit certificate to the payee after withholding. Match the certificate to the same payment and tax amount.

A withholding return should reconcile to the ledger. The gross payments in the schedule, the deductions posted, the amount remitted and the certificates issued should tie together. If one number differs, resolve it before the annual income-tax return turns the difference into a disputed credit.

Verify Current Exemption Status, Scope And Dates

URA opened the application window for the July to December 2026 exemption period on April 7, 2026 and closed it on June 7, 2026. The official exemption notice says approved status is valid from July 1 to December 31, 2026 and can be revoked when the taxpayer or associates are noncompliant.

Before treating a supplier as exempt, use the current URA exemption search. Search the identifier, check the exact taxpayer name and verify whether the list marks income-tax WHT, VAT WHT or both. Confirm the start date and end date. A result for VAT withholding does not automatically prove an income-tax withholding exemption, and a certificate from an earlier half-year does not prove current status.

Save a dated result or reference in the payment file without exposing it publicly. URA's May 2026 registration-details notice also warns that taxpayers with outdated NIN, BRN, telephone, address or business information can be prompted to update their profile before using portal transactions. That makes identity matching a real month-end control, not administrative decoration.

Build A Withholding Tax Month-End Control File

A reliable file should let another reviewer reproduce the treatment without relying on memory. For each payment, retain the contract or purchase order, invoice, supplier identity, residency evidence where relevant, payment date, gross base, classification, rate source, exemption result, calculation, ledger entry, return line, payment confirmation and certificate.

Use the AfroTools Invoice Generator to prepare a clear invoice record, but do not assume it determines the tax category or files with URA. The tax decision belongs in the supporting schedule. Where the payee is an employee, use the Uganda PAYE workflow instead of forcing employment income into an ordinary supplier row.

URA guidance says records of payments and corresponding withholding should be kept for five years after the end of the relevant year of income. Keep them longer when a proceeding still needs the evidence. Store the certificate with the same reference used on the ledger so the payee's credit can be traced back to the return and remittance.

Before signing off the month, ask five questions: Did every covered payment enter the register? Does each rate have a current official source? Was any exemption checked for the exact period and tax type? Do the return, payment and certificates reconcile? Can the evidence be retrieved without exposing supplier data outside the finance team?
