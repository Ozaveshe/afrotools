Cameroon VAT Filing Guide 2026

Prepare the monthly return from a controlled sales and purchase record, apply the 19.25% general rate only where it belongs, preserve DGI-compliant evidence and separate ordinary VAT from authorized withholding.

Cameroon's DGI states that the effective general VAT rate is 19.25%, qualifying businesses file monthly within the first 15 days of the next month, and input VAT needs compliant evidence. The 2026 withholding order applies only to authorized entities and requires a system-generated certificate.

This guide uses the Cameroon DGI VAT fact sheet, the MINFI 2026 VAT withholding order, the DGI's 2026 Finance Law circular page and its current e-services links. The official sources were checked on August 20, 2026.

The article is a preparation guide, not a filing service or tax opinion. A rate calculation does not prove that a sale is taxable, that an expense is deductible or that a customer is authorized to withhold VAT. Confirm unusual transactions, exemptions, zero-rating, place of supply and refund eligibility with DGI Cameroon or a qualified adviser.

The XAF 50 million figure is a review boundary, not permission to self-classify from one sales total. DGI also lists activities that are attached to the real regime, including upstream petroleum, gas, mining other than artisanal mining, first-tier banking, telecommunications, credit, microfinance, insurance, notarial work and public procurement. A business near the boundary should confirm its status before changing invoice or filing treatment.

Who enters the VAT workflow

DGI describes VAT as a consumption tax collected by businesses for the state. Its fact sheet covers individuals and legal entities, public-law bodies and decentralized local authorities that independently carry out taxable operations on a habitual or occasional basis. It also identifies the turnover and activity routes into the real regime.

Taxable operations can include supplies of goods, services, imports, professional real-estate operations, leasing, games, travel-agency commissions, e-commerce platforms and certain commercial subsidies or debt releases. This list does not make every receipt taxable. The place of supply, exemption rules and the exact legal character of the transaction still matter.

Do not use the calculator or this article to convert a registration question into a yes-or-no result. Keep a registration decision file that shows the turnover period used, which revenue was included, the activity category, any DGI correspondence and the effective date of the confirmed status.

Tax point and return period

The DGI fact sheet says VAT generally becomes due when goods are delivered, when payment is collected for services, and when imported goods are entered for consumption. These timing rules affect which month receives the output tax. A sales invoice date alone may not answer every timing question.

Build the monthly return from a cut-off schedule. For each sale, record the supply type, customer, invoice number, net amount, tax treatment, VAT amount, delivery or collection date and the return period selected. For imports, link the customs entry and payment evidence. For services, preserve the collection record that supports the tax point used.

At month end, compare the cut-off schedule with bank receipts, cash records, invoice sequences, delivery records and the general ledger. Investigate late-posted invoices, deposits, credit notes and cancelled documents before the return is submitted. That control is more reliable than trying to rebuild the period from the bank statement on filing day.

Reconcile output and input VAT

For an ordinary transaction confirmed at the effective general rate, the arithmetic is:

  • Output VAT: tax-exclusive value multiplied by 19.25%.
  • VAT-inclusive total: tax-exclusive value plus output VAT.
  • VAT extracted from a tax-inclusive amount: inclusive amount multiplied by 19.25 divided by 119.25.

A tax-exclusive amount of XAF 10,000 at the confirmed general rate produces XAF 1,925 of VAT and an XAF 11,925 inclusive total. This is a mathematical walkthrough of DGI's published rate, not a fictional transaction and not proof that a specific product qualifies for the general rate.

Reconcile the return in layers. First total taxable sales by treatment. Then compare output VAT to the sales ledger. Next total potentially deductible input VAT from the purchase and import ledgers. Remove amounts without valid evidence, amounts outside the business activity and other blocked items. Finally reconcile VAT withheld, prior credits, adjustments and the amount payable to their supporting documents.

Do not net sales and purchases before the audit trail exists. The return may show a net amount, but the business should retain separate gross schedules for output tax, input tax, credits and withholding certificates.

Invoice and deduction evidence

DGI says VAT-registered businesses must issue compliant invoices that identify the parties and show the tax-exclusive price, VAT rate and VAT amount. Its fact sheet also says input VAT deduction requires compliant invoices generated from the tax administration's invoicing system, or another specified document such as a customs declaration or eligible withholding certificate.

The same source says a cash payment above XAF 100,000 does not support deduction. Treat that as a payment control as well as a bookkeeping rule. Staff should know the limit before paying a supplier, not after the return is prepared.

A purchase ledger should not mark VAT as deductible merely because an invoice displays a VAT number. Check the supplier and buyer identities, invoice sequence, transaction date, description, tax-exclusive amount, rate, VAT, total, payment evidence and business purpose. Link the document to the accounting entry and keep exceptions in a review queue.

The AfroTools Invoice Generator can organize a draft commercial invoice, but it does not connect to DGI, generate an administration-system fiscal invoice or establish deductibility. Use it only as a local drafting aid where that boundary is understood.

VAT withholding in 2026

Cameroon VAT withholding is not a buyer preference. MINFI's order dated January 5, 2026 identifies public enterprises, private companies and nonprofit organizations authorized to withhold VAT and income-tax advances. A supplier should check the current list and the customer's authority rather than assume that every large organization can withhold.

The order confirms 19.25% for VAT withholding and requires authorized organizations to pay withheld taxes to the Treasury no later than the 15th of the following month. It prohibits offsetting taxes withheld against the collector's own taxes.

The authorized customer must issue an Attestation de Retenue à la Source generated by the tax administration's system. The order states that a VAT withholding certificate issued outside that system does not support a deduction. Keep the certificate reference, customer, invoice, gross VAT, amount withheld and return-period allocation together.

If an expected certificate is missing, do not silently create a credit in the return schedule. Put the item in an exception log, follow up with the customer and confirm the treatment before filing. The neutral withholding scenario in the Pan-African VAT Calculator can test arithmetic, but it cannot determine authorization or create the certificate.

A controlled monthly filing workflow

  1. Confirm the period. Lock the sales, purchase, import and withholding cut-off for the month being filed.
  2. Validate sales treatment. Separate general-rate, zero-rated, exempt and review-needed items. Do not force uncertain items into the 19.25% column.
  3. Reconcile output VAT. Match the sales tax schedule to invoices, collections where relevant and the general ledger.
  4. Test input evidence. Check each deduction against a compliant fiscal invoice, customs declaration or eligible system-generated withholding certificate and the business-purpose rule.
  5. Review payment methods. Remove or escalate input VAT attached to cash payments above the DGI limit and other unsupported records.
  6. Allocate withholding. Match every amount to the authorized customer and the administration-system certificate.
  7. Calculate the position. Show output VAT, allowable input VAT, withholding, valid prior credit and the resulting payable or credit separately.
  8. Submit through the correct DGI service. DGI publishes separate e-service links for taxpayer registration, teleservices and different taxpayer segments. Use the account and route assigned to the business.
  9. Pay and preserve proof. Keep the submitted return, acknowledgment, payment reference, schedules and source documents in one period file.

DGI states that the monthly declaration is due within the first 15 days of the following month. Complete the preparer and approver checks before that window closes. A portal outage, unresolved certificate or late ledger close should be documented and escalated promptly rather than hidden through an unsupported number.

Credits, corrections and records

Where deductible VAT exceeds output VAT, DGI describes a VAT credit. Its fact sheet says the credit can be carried forward without a time limit and may be refundable under specified conditions for categories including exporters and certain businesses with structural credits. Refund timing and eligibility depend on the category and evidence, so a credit balance is not automatically cash available to the business.

Maintain a credit roll-forward that starts with the prior confirmed balance, adds the current period position, identifies amounts used or adjusted and ends at the return balance. Reconcile it every month. Keep refund applications, DGI responses and supporting invoices separate from ordinary carry-forward records.

If a filed return needs correction, preserve the original return, the discovered error, the corrected schedule, approval and DGI submission evidence. Never overwrite the period workbook in a way that removes the original trail. DGI's January 2026 taxpayer charter explains that declarations are presumed accurate and sincere but remain subject to tax control, which makes traceable records essential.

A complete VAT period file normally contains the sales tax schedule, purchase deduction schedule, import declarations, fiscal invoices, credit notes, withholding certificates, bank and payment evidence, ledger reconciliation, return acknowledgment, payment receipt, exception log and any DGI correspondence. Retention periods and special industry documents should be confirmed against the current tax code and advice for the business.

Frequently asked questions

What is Cameroon's standard VAT rate in 2026?

The DGI VAT fact sheet states an effective general rate of 19.25%, consisting of 17.5% VAT plus communal additional tax equal to 10% of the base VAT.

When is a Cameroon VAT return due?

DGI says qualifying businesses file monthly within the first 15 days of the following month and pay the VAT due. Confirm the exact electronic cutoff and service assigned to the taxpayer.

Can every customer withhold Cameroon VAT?

No. The 2026 order applies to listed authorized entities. The customer must issue a withholding certificate generated by the tax administration's system.

What evidence supports input VAT?

DGI points to compliant administration-system invoices, customs declarations and eligible withholding certificates, subject to the other legal conditions. An invoice amount alone does not establish deductibility.

Does the Cameroon VAT Calculator file the return?

No. It is a local planning calculator. It does not connect to DGI, decide tax treatment, issue a fiscal invoice, file a return, make a payment or create a withholding certificate.
