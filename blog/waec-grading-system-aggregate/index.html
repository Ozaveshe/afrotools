The WAEC Grading Scale

WAEC uses a numerical grading scale from A1 (the highest) to F9 (a fail). Each grade corresponds to a specific score range and carries a grade point value used for aggregate calculations. Understanding this scale is the foundation for everything else.

Read grades and credits separately

  • A1–C6: Credit-level results. Count them, then check whether they include the subjects your intended programme requires.
  • D7–E8: Passes below credit level. Keep these visible when checking a specific programme’s requirements.
  • F9: A failed subject. The planner highlights it so you can see what needs attention.
  • Grade points: A1 maps to 1 and F9 to 9. A lower sum means stronger grades among the subjects counted; it does not establish eligibility.

Use the country-specific result planner

Nigeria: count credits and inspect the best-five planning index

Enter your actual subject names and grades in the WAEC/NECO result planner. Review the credit count and the five subjects counted in its planning index. This index is an AfroTools comparison aid, not a nationwide post-UTME formula or an admission score. A good total cannot replace a missing required subject.

Ghana: select the programme core and electives

Choose Ghana in the same planner. Its supported model counts English, Core Mathematics, your selected programme core and three electives. Review the counted subjects and any missing-subject messages. This explains the tool’s model; use your intended institution’s current programme requirements to decide which subjects and grades are acceptable.

Take the next official requirement check

For Nigeria, open JAMB’s official IBASS Eligibility Checker. Select your entry mode, institution and programme, then supply the subjects and UTME combination it requests. JAMB’s official FAQ directs applicants to this checker before applying.

  1. Keep your planner’s subject-and-grade list beside your result slip.
  2. Compare it with the requirements for the exact programme and institution.
  3. Check the institution’s own admissions portal for the current cycle, accepted exams, sittings and screening process.
  4. For Ghana, use your intended institution’s official admissions requirements; the Nigeria IBASS link is not a Ghana eligibility service.

There is no course cut-off table here because a generic best-five total cannot establish admission to Medicine, Law or any other programme. Your useful next step is an exact requirement check, not an inferred acceptance chance.

WAEC, NECO and combining sittings

The Nigeria planner accepts WAEC or NECO grade entry. This is a tool feature, not a guarantee that every institution accepts every examination combination or number of sittings. Confirm the exact programme’s current policy before combining results.

Make a useful study or application plan

  • Missing a required credit? Identify the particular subject to improve instead of judging your whole result by a total.
  • Considering another programme? Check its official subject requirements first; do not assume a lower planner index guarantees entry.
  • Preparing for a resit? Use your result slip to choose subjects for focused revision and practise their past questions.
  • Need to compare options? Keep a separate subject checklist for each institution and programme. Do not assume a later transfer or Direct Entry route is guaranteed.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is WAEC aggregate calculated?

Add the grade points of the subjects required by the calculation you are using. AfroTools shows its counted subjects: a best-five planning index in Nigeria mode, or the supported Ghana core-and-elective model. Check the target institution’s own method before using a total for an application.

What is the best aggregate score in WAEC?

Five A1 grades add to 5; six A1 grades add to 6. These are arithmetic totals, not universal admission cut-offs. Required subjects and the institution’s published admission method still matter.

What WAEC grade do I need for university admission in Nigeria?

JAMB’s eligibility checker separates credits (A1–C6) from passes (D7–E8) and checks subjects against the selected institution, programme and entry mode. Use it with the institution’s current admissions requirements; this planner does not issue an admission decision.

Is C6 a credit in WAEC?

Yes. A1 through C6 are credit-level grades; D7 and E8 are passes, and F9 is a fail. A credit count is useful, but it does not establish that you have the particular subjects a programme requires.

What is the difference between best of 5 and best of 6?

Best of five adds five grade points; best of six adds six. A Ghana core-and-elective calculation is not simply the six lowest grades. Use the country-specific planner and check the required subject selection rather than switching totals to get a lower number.
