Battery backup hours in Ghana depend on usable stored energy and the real load over time. Multiplying battery voltage by amp-hours gives a nominal energy figure, but depth-of-discharge limits, inverter losses, battery age, temperature and surge reduce what can be delivered safely.

A material or power estimate is only as reliable as its measurements, product specification and safety assumptions. Area alone is rarely enough: thickness, overlap, openings, wastage, starting surge and usable capacity can change the purchase.

Quick answer

List appliance watts and hours, calculate total watt-hours, and distinguish continuous load from startup surge. Estimate nominal battery energy from the manufacturer's specification, apply an appropriate usable-depth limit and system efficiency, then divide usable watt-hours by the expected average load. Have a qualified installer verify battery chemistry, inverter rating, protection, ventilation and cable design.

Why this calculation or checklist matters

Keep a measurement sheet beside the estimate. Label every dimension and unit, identify the chosen product, record the manufacturer coverage or effective width, and separate measured quantity from contingency. Structural and electrical choices still require a competent professional.

A large amp-hour label alone does not state backup time. Voltage matters, and batteries with the same nominal energy may have different usable limits, cycle life, discharge-rate behaviour and safety requirements. Appliance efficiency also changes the result.

A good working file also makes disagreement cheaper. Instead of arguing about a total, the people involved can inspect the quantity, unit, date, source and assumption that produced it. That is the difference between a reusable estimate and a number copied into a message.

Step-by-step workflow

  1. Step 1. Separate essential from optional loads and measure or read each appliance's power.
  2. Step 2. Multiply watts by expected hours to calculate daily or outage watt-hours.
  3. Step 3. Identify the maximum simultaneous watts and relevant starting surge for inverter sizing.
  4. Step 4. Calculate nominal battery energy and apply the manufacturer's usable-depth and discharge limits.
  5. Step 5. Allow for inverter and wiring losses, then estimate runtime under base and pressure loads.
  6. Step 6. Verify the design, product approvals and installation safety with a qualified Ghana installer.

Convert the appliance plan to watt-hours

Watts describe power at a moment; watt-hours describe energy over time. A 60 watt appliance used for five hours requires 300 watt-hours before losses.

Create separate day and night profiles if usage changes. Refrigerators cycle, and pumps may run briefly at high power, so measured logs are better than assuming everything operates continuously.

  • Appliance watts
  • Quantity
  • Hours or duty cycle
  • Total watt-hours

Nominal versus usable battery energy

Voltage multiplied by amp-hours gives nominal watt-hours for a battery rated on that basis. Usable energy is lower after applying the product's allowed depth of discharge and system losses.

Do not invent a universal usable percentage. Battery chemistry, warranty, discharge rate, temperature and management system requirements differ.

  • Nominal voltage and capacity
  • Manufacturer usable limit
  • System efficiency
  • Age and operating condition

Inverter and installation checks

Runtime capacity and inverter power are separate. The battery may store enough energy while the inverter is unable to start a motor or support the simultaneous load.

Ghana regulates inverters and renewable-energy batteries within its product and efficiency framework. Check approved product information and use competent installation with correct protection and ventilation.

  • Continuous inverter watts
  • Short-duration surge
  • DC cable and protection
  • Earthing, enclosure and ventilation

Build an evidence pack another person can audit

Walk the site with the drawing, product data and estimate. Check each dimension at the point it is used and photograph labels or conditions that affect coverage, thickness, overlap, rating or safety. Record who measured, the instrument, date and unit. If the drawing and site disagree, stop the quantity decision and obtain a resolved instruction instead of averaging the two numbers.

Use short filenames that begin with the date and describe the record. Keep the original source separate from calculations and annotations. Where a file contains identity, financial, health or commercial data, share the minimum necessary information and use the official or trusted channel. A checklist should reduce exposure, not create another uncontrolled copy of sensitive material.

Set the decision gate

Ask the responsible engineer, electrician, quantity surveyor, installer or manufacturer to confirm the safety-critical specification and installation assumptions. The commercial order should name the exact product, size, rating, finish and accessory scope. Keep substitutions pending until their dimensions and performance have been recalculated and approved.

Write a clear stop condition before commitment. A stop condition might be a missing official search, an unverified payment account, an unresolved name mismatch, an unaffordable pressure case, an unapproved safety specification or a supplier quote that excludes essential scope. When it appears, pause and resolve the evidence rather than pushing the same uncertain assumption into the final output. Record who made the final decision, the date and the evidence they reviewed so a later update has an honest starting point.

Stress-test the result

Calculate a measured case, a procurement case and a contingency case. The measured case shows the theoretical quantity. The procurement case rounds to the packs, sheets or equipment sizes actually sold. The contingency case adds only an explained allowance for cutting, breakage, rework, surge or future load.

Add one high-use evening appliance, reduce usable capacity and inverter efficiency, and test the refrigerator or pump startup. If runtime is too short, first reduce optional loads before automatically increasing battery capacity.

Write the decision beside the scenario. Examples include delaying a purchase, collecting a larger deposit, reducing scope, changing packaging, adding route density, choosing a different loan, or asking a qualified adviser to verify an exception. A scenario without a decision is only another spreadsheet column.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using amp-hours without battery voltage.
  • Treating nominal energy as fully usable energy.
  • Ignoring inverter losses and motor surge.
  • Assuming every appliance runs at its nameplate power for the same hours.
  • Selecting battery chemistry or wiring from calculator cost alone.

One final check catches many errors: ask whether a different person could reproduce the answer from the saved inputs. If not, label the missing assumption before using the result in a quote, purchase, application or public promise.
