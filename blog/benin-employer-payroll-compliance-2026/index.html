Benin Employer Payroll Compliance 2026: ITS, VPS and CNSS Controls

A practical employer guide to taxable remuneration, monthly salary tax, payroll levies, social contributions, filing dates and evidence that survives review.

Benin payroll compliance requires more than subtracting one tax percentage from basic salary. An employer must identify every taxable payment and benefit, withhold Impot sur les Traitements et Salaires, account for the employer's Versement Patronal sur Salaires, separate employee and employer CNSS contributions, file through the applicable channel, pay by the deadline and retain records that reproduce the calculation.

This guide was verified on September 13, 2026 against the Direction Generale des Impots 2026 General Tax Code, DGI salary-tax guidance, the official 2026 Finance Law page, the DGI e-services portal and current Caisse Nationale de Securite Sociale contribution guidance. The 2026 code remains the controlling source for the tax rules described here. The live portal determines the form and workflow available to a registered employer.

These percentages do not all use one automatic base. The Code says the VPS base is the same as the ITS base, while CNSS states that its contributions are calculated on all remuneration received by the worker, including premiums, allowances and benefits in cash or in kind. Payroll should therefore maintain an explicit bridge from gross remuneration to each liability rather than copying one figure across all columns.

The English payroll guide has a different search job from the existing Benin VAT guide. VAT concerns taxable supplies and invoicing, while this article concerns employee remuneration, withholding and employer payroll charges. Businesses that have both obligations should reconcile them separately.

Build taxable remuneration from the full pay record

Article 119 of the 2026 General Tax Code brings public and private salaries, emoluments, wages and accessory remuneration into ITS. It also covers specified end-of-career payments and remuneration paid to company directors within the stated scope. The territorial rules can reach remuneration where the beneficiary is domiciled in Benin, and certain remuneration for work performed in Benin or paid by a Benin employer where the beneficiary is domiciled elsewhere.

Do not start the calculation from net bank pay. Build a remuneration register with separate fields for basic salary, overtime, bonuses, commissions, cash allowances, benefits in kind, exceptional remuneration, reimbursements, approved exemptions and employee deductions. Record the payment period and the date on which the amount became available to the worker.

The DGI salary-tax guidance says taxable monthly salary includes gross salaries, bonuses, overtime, benefits in money or kind and allowances of every type, including transport and sickness allowances, subject to the Code's specific exclusions. Benefits in kind are generally measured at real value, although the Code gives fixed or percentage methods for listed items. A label used in an employment contract does not by itself make an amount non-taxable.

Article 120 lists exemptions that need evidence and careful scope review. They include specified statutory dismissal compensation, qualifying social-security benefits, permitted employer contributions to a collective supplementary pension arrangement and narrowly defined mandatory internships for students or apprentices under age 30. A payroll team should link every exemption to the current Code paragraph and the supporting contract, invoice, benefit statement or institutional document.

Reimbursements require the same discipline. A DGI tax ruling on mission expenses distinguishes an employment relationship from an independent service arrangement and says the treatment depends on the facts and documents. Preserve mission orders, travel evidence, expense purpose and approvals. Do not convert a recurring cash allowance into a reimbursement merely by changing its payroll label.

Calculate monthly ITS progressively

Article 125 of the current code applies ITS directly to monthly taxable remuneration through five marginal bands. The first XOF 60,000 is taxed at 0%. The next XOF 90,000 is taxed at 10%. The next XOF 100,000 is taxed at 15%. The next XOF 250,000 is taxed at 19%. Only the amount above XOF 500,000 is taxed at 30%.

A factual band calculation at exactly XOF 500,000 of monthly taxable remuneration produces XOF 71,500 of base ITS. The 10% band contributes XOF 9,000, the 15% band contributes XOF 15,000 and the 19% band contributes XOF 47,500. The first band contributes zero. Reaching the XOF 500,000 boundary does not cause the whole amount to be taxed at 19%.

The Code adds a levy for the Office de Radiodiffusion et Television du Benin to the calculated ITS. The published amount is XOF 1,000 on March salary and XOF 3,000 on June salary. The June amount does not apply where taxable income does not exceed the first ITS band. Keep March and June visible in the payroll calendar so the difference from an ordinary month is explainable.

Article 126 gives exceptional remuneration a separate method. It covers listed payments such as end-of-career compensation, voluntary-departure premiums, notice and paid-leave compensation on termination, thirteenth-month pay and similar remuneration. The official method uses the average salary for the prior twelve months, a ratio for the payment month and a 25% abatement for exceptional remuneration. A simple addition to normal monthly pay can therefore be wrong.

At XOF 500,000 in an ordinary month, employee CNSS at the published 3.6% rate is XOF 18,000. Subtracting that and the XOF 71,500 base ITS gives a planning take-home of XOF 410,500 before any other lawful deduction. This is the same standard case protected by the calculator's verification test. It does not classify benefits, exceptional pay, contract deductions or a real employee's status.

Keep employer VPS outside employee take-home

Articles 191 to 195 govern the Versement Patronal sur Salaires. The charge applies to persons that pay salaries and accessory remuneration within its scope. Article 193 says the VPS base is identical to the ITS base. Article 194 sets the general rate at 4% and reduces it to 2% for private educational establishments.

VPS is an employer liability. It should not reduce the employee's take-home pay. In a simplified calculation using a XOF 500,000 VPS base, the general 4% rate produces XOF 20,000. A private educational establishment using the 2% rate would produce XOF 10,000 on the same assumed base. Confirm that the employer and remuneration are in scope before using either figure.

Article 192 lists exemptions, including rules for certain new businesses, first employment of a Beninese national, qualifying internships and specified domestic employment. Each exemption has conditions and some depend on CNSS declaration and payment. Do not switch off VPS because a payroll label resembles an exemption. Retain the company-formation date, employment evidence, nationality or internship documents, CNSS proof and the current legal paragraph supporting the decision.

Article 195 places VPS on the same declaration and payment timetable as ITS. That shared filing surface does not make VPS part of the worker's tax. Post the employee withholding and employer charge to separate liability accounts, and make sure the declaration total can be traced back to both.

Reconcile employee and employer CNSS

CNSS Benin's current contribution page says contributions are calculated on all remuneration received by workers, including premiums, allowances and other benefits in kind or cash. It publishes a 3.6% employee pension share. The employer bears 9% for family benefits, 6.4% for pension and an occupational-risk rate from 1% to 4% based on the enterprise's activity.

The published employer CNSS total is therefore 16.4% at a 1% occupational-risk rate and 19.4% at a 4% rate. On XOF 500,000 of contribution base, those endpoints are XOF 82,000 and XOF 97,000. Use the rate assigned to the employer. Do not choose the lower endpoint because it gives a preferred cost.

CNSS also states that the employer is debtor for the total contribution, including the employee share withheld at each payroll. This means the employer cannot treat the 3.6% deduction as settled merely because it appeared on a payslip. The amount must enter the declaration, payment and reconciliation process with the employer portions.

The official page asks for a salary-and-contribution declaration and a nominal staff list on the stated cycle. The CNSS employer-control page explains that control compares remuneration in the employer's salary register, financial statements and accounting or administrative records with remuneration declared to the Fund. It also checks employer registration, worker affiliation and payment on time.

For each worker, reconcile the employment start or end date, CNSS identifier, remuneration base, employee pension share, employer family-benefit share, employer pension share and occupational-risk share. Investigate differences between the tax base and contribution base rather than forcing them to agree. A benefit can require different treatment under the two systems.

Declare and pay through the applicable channel

Article 127 requires a person paying taxable remuneration to withhold ITS for the Treasury. The payer must record the date, nature and amount of the withholding and the reference of the tax receipt for each beneficiary. Where labour law requires a pay document, the ITS withheld must appear on that document.

The DGI guidance states that the monthly declaration must reach the competent tax office by the 10th of the following month. Article 129 applies the same following-month deadline to payment. A weekend, portal outage, approval delay or unresolved payroll exception should not be left until the statutory date. Confirm the live due date shown for the return period and arrange payment early enough to retain acknowledgement.

Official DGI doctrine describes a unified monthly salary declaration covering permanent, seasonal, temporary and occasional workers. It says the declaration should show employment periods and salaries, include required schedules, and cover all branches and sites in one employer return. It directs employers inside the relevant system perimeter to declare and pay online through DGI e-services, while the published guidance describes separate CNSS handling for others until integration applies.

Portal screens and administrative coverage can change. Use the employer's current DGI and CNSS account instructions rather than assuming a historic form remains available. Save submission acknowledgements, payment references and any accepted correction. A successful bank debit without an accepted declaration, or an accepted declaration without a matching payment, leaves the month incomplete.

Benin monthly payroll close checklist

  1. Freeze authorised inputs. Confirm joiners, leavers, attendance, salary changes, overtime, bonuses, benefits, exceptional pay and approved reimbursements.
  2. Build the remuneration register. Record every cash and in-kind component once and attach the evidence for exclusions or exemptions.
  3. Calculate standard ITS. Apply the five monthly bands progressively and keep the calculation by band.
  4. Handle exceptional remuneration separately. Use Article 126 rather than adding a thirteenth month or termination payment to ordinary pay without adjustment.
  5. Add the correct ORTB amount. Apply the March or June levy only when the published rule requires it.
  6. Calculate VPS as employer cost. Confirm the 4% or 2% rate and document any claimed exemption.
  7. Calculate CNSS by branch. Show employee pension, employer pension, family benefits and occupational risk separately.
  8. Reconcile outputs. Gross cash pay minus employee deductions must equal net pay, and the net-pay total must agree to the approved payment file.
  9. Prepare the declarations. Match worker identifiers, employment dates, remuneration and each liability to the required schedules.
  10. Approve before the deadline. Assign preparer, reviewer and payer, then retain accepted returns and payment evidence.

After filing, compare the DGI liability accounts for ITS and VPS with the return and receipt. Compare the CNSS liability account with employee and employer contribution schedules and the CNSS receipt. Clear rounding differences through an approved rule, not through unexplained journal entries.

Restrict access to payroll records because they contain salary, identity and sometimes banking data. Exports and workpapers should remain in the employer's controlled storage with access and retention rules.

Frequently asked questions

What are the Benin ITS bands in 2026?

The official monthly schedule applies 0% up to XOF 60,000, 10% from XOF 60,001 to 150,000, 15% from XOF 150,001 to 250,000, 19% from XOF 250,001 to 500,000, and 30% above XOF 500,000 of taxable remuneration.

When is Benin ITS due from an employer?

The DGI guidance and Code require the monthly declaration and payment by the 10th of the month following the withholding period.

What are the published CNSS rates?

CNSS publishes a 3.6% employee pension share. Employer rates are 9% for family benefits, 6.4% for pension, and 1% to 4% for occupational risk according to activity.

Is VPS deducted from the employee?

No. VPS is an employer charge. The general rate is 4%, with 2% for private educational establishments, subject to the Code's scope and exemptions.

Does the AfroTools calculator file payroll returns?

No. It provides a private local planning estimate. It does not register a worker, classify remuneration, submit a return or pay DGI or CNSS.
