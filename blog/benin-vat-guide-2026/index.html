Benin VAT Guide 2026: 18% Rate, Filing and e-MECeF

Benin's standard VAT rate is only the starting point. A compliant process also needs the right registration evidence, monthly return, input VAT documents and normalized electronic invoices.

This guide is based on the Direction Generale des Impots documentation page, the official 2026 General Tax Code, and DGI's live filing and invoicing portals. The sources were reviewed on August 15, 2026. Article numbers below refer to that consolidated 2026 code.

The guide deliberately does not publish a numeric VAT registration threshold. Article 228 says the amount is fixed by a ministerial order, but the code itself does not state the figure. An older threshold copied from another year is not reliable evidence for a 2026 registration decision. Confirm the current order with DGI or a qualified Benin tax adviser.

This snapshot is a control list, not a classification of a particular transaction. Benin's code contains exemptions, zero-rated treatment and special rules. Before charging VAT, establish what is being supplied, where it is supplied, whether the seller is liable, and whether a specific exemption applies.

Who enters the VAT system?

Article 226 covers natural and legal persons carrying out taxable operations habitually or occasionally, independently, for consideration as part of an economic activity. Employees acting within an employment relationship are not treated as independent for this rule. Legal form and the person's position under other taxes do not by themselves remove an otherwise taxable operator from scope.

Compulsory liability is then connected to turnover. Article 226 says operators carrying out non-exempt transactions become liable when turnover exceeds the threshold referenced in Article 228. If that threshold is reached during the year, liability starts on the first day of the following month. A business whose turnover later falls below the limit continues its declaration and payment obligations for three consecutive financial years under Article 226.

Article 227 adds two practical conditions for a person relying on taxable-person status: registration with the tax administration to obtain an Identifiant Fiscal Unique, or IFU, and accounting under the normal OHADA accounting system. DGI provides a live IFU service and a tax registration workflow.

Voluntary VAT registration

Article 225 allows certain below-threshold sales and services to enter VAT by express option. The request is made in writing to the tax office for the principal establishment, and the article gives the administration eight days to respond. It also lists conditions that include up-to-date tax obligations, regular accounting with specified professional oversight, a business bank account and an effective business address.

Voluntary registration should not be treated as a shortcut to one input VAT claim. It brings continuing tax, accounting and invoice duties. Confirm eligibility, effective date and operational readiness before applying.

How to calculate Benin VAT at 18%

Article 241 fixes the VAT rate at 18%. For a standard-rated amount stated before VAT, multiply the taxable base by 18%. Add the result to the base to reach the VAT-inclusive total.

Tax-exclusive example: XOF 1,000,000 multiplied by 18% gives XOF 180,000 VAT. The VAT-inclusive total is XOF 1,180,000.

When a price already includes 18% VAT, multiplying the gross amount by 18% overstates the tax. Divide the gross total by 1.18 to recover the tax-exclusive amount, or multiply the gross total by 18/118 to isolate VAT.

Tax-inclusive example: XOF 1,180,000 multiplied by 18/118 gives XOF 180,000 VAT. The remaining XOF 1,000,000 is the tax-exclusive base.

These are arithmetic examples derived from the Article 241 rate. They do not decide whether the supply is standard-rated, zero-rated or exempt. They also do not determine the time when VAT becomes chargeable. Use the code and transaction documents for those decisions.

Input VAT deductions depend on evidence

Article 243 establishes the basic mechanism: VAT borne upstream on the elements of the price of a taxable operation is deductible from VAT applying to that operation. That principle does not make every amount marked "VAT" automatically recoverable.

Article 244 specifies the evidence. Import VAT must appear on the consumption declaration with proof of payment from the competent administration. A self-supply uses the taxpayer's declaration. VAT withheld at source uses the receipt issued by the customer. In other domestic cases, the VAT must appear on a normalized invoice.

The same article requires the goods or services to be necessary for and used exclusively for the business, recorded in the accounts, and not expressly excluded by law. It also requires the VAT to have been invoiced by a taxable person liable for VAT. This makes supplier status, invoice validation and business purpose part of the deduction file.

Article 245 places the deduction in the month when the deductible tax becomes chargeable at the supplier, with a separate import rule. A deduction omitted from that period may be included in returns filed no later than April 30 of the year following the omission. The article also requires purchases of goods or services of at least XOF 100,000 excluding VAT to be paid by cheque, bank card or bank transfer. That payment rule is an important control for the purchase ledger.

Article 247 lists exclusions from deduction and was modified by the 2026 Finance Law. The list includes passenger or mixed-use vehicles and related costs, subject to stated exceptions, as well as other restricted expenditure. Do not infer deductibility only from a business label in the accounts. Review the specific exclusion and any exception that applies.

Monthly VAT reconciliation

A working reconciliation starts with output VAT from taxable sales, then subtracts only eligible, documented input VAT. For example, output VAT of XOF 1,800,000 less eligible input VAT of XOF 1,080,000 produces an arithmetic balance of XOF 720,000. The amount belongs in a return only after invoice status, timing, business use, exclusions and payment evidence have been checked.

The monthly return is due by the 10th

Article 259 requires every VAT taxable person to file a prescribed return by the 10th of each month for the previous month. The return identifies taxable and non-taxable operations, gross VAT charged, deductions, and the resulting VAT payable or credit. VAT due is paid directly with the return.

DGI's live e-Services portal provides online tax registration, filing, payment, receipts and taxpayer document management. Portal availability does not change the statutory deadline. Close the ledger early enough to investigate rejected invoices, missing IFUs and differences between billing data and the return.

Article 259 points late filing and late payment to penalties elsewhere in the code. This guide does not quote a single penalty rate because the outcome depends on the breach and applicable provisions. The safer control is a monthly timetable that finishes validation before the 10th.

Normalized invoices and e-MECeF

Article 481 generally requires a normalized invoice, or an equivalent document such as a works statement or fee note, for transactions by persons subject to corporate income tax, business profits tax, the synthetic professional tax or VAT, unless DGI expressly grants a derogation.

The normalized invoice must include its sequence number and date; supplier name, address, IFU and relevant registration; customer name, address and IFU; the nature of the transaction; quantity and precise description; unit and total prices; the amount before VAT; the VAT rate and amount or an exemption statement; the total due; other applicable tax items; the certified machine identifier; and the signature and electronic code. Buyers covered by the article are required to demand and receive the normalized invoice.

Article 482 says the invoice is issued and transmitted through a production-mode electronic invoicing machine certified by DGI. That can be a billing unit or a DGI-approved electronic invoicing system connected to a control module, including a dematerialized version. A commercial or internally developed business billing system must meet DGI specifications and be approved before use in Benin. It must also protect data integrity, security, retention and archiving.

DGI's live e-MECeF portal says a taxpayer registers with an IFU and can create PDF invoices and periodic reports. A business that connects its own invoicing software still needs DGI approval. The AfroTools Invoice Generator can help organize a draft invoice, but it does not replace the certified DGI process or turn a draft into a normalized fiscal invoice.

Article 260 adds a serious invoice control: a person who states VAT on an invoice or equivalent document owes that VAT by the fact of invoicing it. Staff should therefore avoid putting a VAT amount on a quotation, receipt or invoice without confirming the tax treatment and approved process.

Electronic platforms and nonresident suppliers

Article 258 assigns platform operators responsibility for calculating, declaring and remitting VAT on sales of goods and services made through electronic-commerce platforms on behalf of suppliers. The platform also declares and remits VAT on commissions from sales carried out in Benin. Operators must apply for tax registration, and DGI provides a specific foreign e-commerce registration form.

Article 262 says a taxable person not domiciled in Benin must appoint a representative domiciled in Benin to perform the required formalities and payments. Without that representative, VAT and penalties can become due from the recipient, beneficiary or a person positioned to invoice or collect amounts for the foreign provider.

These rules mean a platform transaction needs more than a seller invoice. The operator, seller, customer and payment flow can each affect who registers, reports and remits. Cross-border suppliers should document the supply chain and obtain advice before launch, rather than applying the domestic calculation alone.

A practical month-end VAT checklist

  1. Export the month's sales from the DGI-certified invoicing system and check invoice sequence continuity.
  2. Separate standard-rated, zero-rated, exempt and out-of-scope items using the current code and transaction evidence.
  3. Reconcile taxable sales and output VAT to the general ledger and e-MECeF reports.
  4. Validate supplier VAT status, IFU and normalized invoices before including domestic input VAT.
  5. Match import VAT, self-supply and withholding claims to the distinct evidence listed in Article 244.
  6. Check business purpose, accounting entry, Article 247 exclusions and the cashless payment rule for purchases of at least XOF 100,000 excluding VAT.
  7. Review credit notes, cancelled invoices and prior deductions without breaking invoice sequence or audit history.
  8. Reconcile output VAT, eligible input VAT, the payable amount or credit, and the cash payment approval.
  9. Prepare the Article 259 return, complete review and submit with any tax due by the 10th.
  10. Archive the return, payment receipt, invoice reports and supporting evidence together.

Assign a named owner to each step and preserve the evidence behind adjustments. A clean total without a traceable normalized invoice or payment record may still fail the legal conditions.

Frequently asked questions

What is the standard VAT rate in Benin in 2026?

Article 241 of Benin's consolidated 2026 General Tax Code sets the VAT rate at 18%.

When is Benin's monthly VAT return due?

Article 259 requires the return for the previous month by the 10th of each month. VAT due is paid with the return.

What is Benin's VAT registration threshold in 2026?

Article 228 says the threshold is fixed by an order of the finance minister. The consolidated code does not print the amount, so confirm the current order with DGI rather than relying on an older online figure.

Does an ordinary invoice support input VAT deduction?

For ordinary domestic purchases, Article 244 generally requires a normalized invoice and satisfaction of the other deduction conditions. Imports, self-supplies and VAT withheld at source use different specified evidence.

What is e-MECeF?

e-MECeF is DGI Benin's electronic invoicing service. DGI says taxpayers register with their IFU and can issue compliant PDF invoices and periodic reports through the service.
