Zimbabwe employer payroll compliance is a sequence, not a single tax calculation. The employer must register, identify every form of remuneration, apply the correct PAYE table for the payroll currency, submit the long P2 return, make the remittance, confirm that the return and payment reached the right TaRMS ledger, account for social security separately and preserve evidence for the annual record.

This guide was verified on August 18, 2026 against current Zimbabwe Revenue Authority pages and Public Notice 44 of 2026, which ZIMRA published on July 24, 2026 for returns and payments due in August. The ZIMRA pages, public-notice PDF, TaRMS portal and NSSA employer portal were reachable on the verification date. This is an operational checklist, not tax advice, payroll processing, a filing service or a substitute for current legislation and professional advice.

The August notice separates the return from the remittance. Paying by August 10 without filing by August 5 does not complete both obligations. Filing and paying with the wrong taxpayer identity, or leaving the payment unposted, also leaves an unresolved control.

1. Register Before The First Payroll Deadline

ZIMRA's employer-obligation page defines an employer broadly as a person who pays or is liable to pay employee remuneration, including salaries, wages, fees and other compensation. It says resident employers and non-resident employers paying employees in Zimbabwe are within scope, with a resident representative required for the non-resident case described by ZIMRA.

The registration deadline is 14 days after becoming an employer. ZIMRA also says address changes and cessation as an employer should be reported within 14 days. Payroll setup should not wait until the fifth or tenth of the next month. Confirm the taxpayer identity number, PAYE obligation, legal employer name, payroll contact and TaRMS access before the first period closes.

Register with NSSA through its employer process as a separate workstream. The live new-company form asks for legal and trade names, industry code, business activity, first employee start date, employee count, currency and wage bill. Those details should agree with incorporation and payroll records. A ZIMRA account does not prove NSSA registration, and an NSSA number does not replace the ZIMRA identity used for PAYE.

2. Close Remuneration And Currency Inputs

Start with a controlled employee master. Confirm joiners, leavers, identity details, pay frequency, salary currency, bank instructions and approved changes. Then gather basic salary, overtime, commissions, bonuses, allowances, taxable benefits, pension deductions, approved credits and corrections. ZIMRA places responsibility on the employer to calculate PAYE from all remuneration and asks employees to provide information supporting deductions and credits.

Currency is not a cosmetic setting. ZIMRA states that payments should be made in the currency in which income was earned. Its PAYE explanation gives a specific instruction for mixed local and USD remuneration: use the USD tax tables and apportion the tax due. Do not convert the whole payroll using an informal rate because the accounting ledger has one reporting currency.

Create a payroll-input sign-off before calculation. Show the period, employee count, gross-pay total by currency, variable-pay files, benefit changes, pension inputs, exceptions and reviewer. Save evidence supporting each material adjustment. This makes the P2 return a summary of a controlled payroll rather than the first point at which missing facts are discovered.

3. Calculate PAYE Without Reusing A Stale Table

ZIMRA describes PAYE as income tax collected from remuneration by the employer. Its sequence starts with gross income, removes exempt income and allowable deductions, applies the relevant tax table, deducts qualifying credits and then applies the 3% AIDS levy to the income tax chargeable. The treatment of an allowance, benefit, deduction or credit depends on the law and facts, so a label in payroll software is not enough evidence.

A critical freshness boundary applies in August 2026. The public ZIMRA tax-table index visible during this review still listed the January to December 2025 USD and ZWG tables as its newest downloads. AfroTools therefore does not reproduce a numeric 2026 band table here. Before finalising 2026 payroll, confirm the legally applicable table with ZIMRA, current Finance Act material or a qualified Zimbabwe tax professional. Do not assume an old PDF or payroll template was updated.

The AfroTools calculator is useful for a planning cross-check, but preserve the official table or advice used for the actual run. Record its version, currency, payroll period and special treatment. Compare PAYE and AIDS levy totals with employee-level calculations. Document rounding differences. Correct benefit, currency or table mismatches before filing.

4. Submit The Long P2 Return Through TaRMS

Public Notice 44 states that returns for the month ended July 31, 2026 were due by August 5 and remittances by August 10. It directs online returns to the Self-Service Portal and specifically requires employers to submit the long PAYE return on TaRMS under Public Notice 05 of 2025. It also links to ZIMRA's employee-management menu path.

Build the return from final payroll, not the amount the bank is expected to pay. Reconcile employee count, remuneration, deductions, PAYE and AIDS levy by currency and total. Confirm that the period is July 2026 in this factual notice example and that the employer identity matches the ledger. Submit early enough to resolve file-format, employee-master or portal errors before the fifth.

Keep the uploaded file, submission confirmation, portal status and final return. If the system rejects employee records, correct the underlying identity, format or payroll data and preserve the error trail. If a period-specific ZIMRA notice changes a normal timetable, the specific current notice should control the internal calendar.

5. Prove Payment And TaRMS Posting

Public Notice 44 explains how the Single Account System connects payments to TaRMS. Banks validate the taxpayer identity number and name. A taxpayer does not need to identify the tax obligation in bank payment fields, but the related return is required for money to be recognised and posted to the taxpayer ledger.

This creates three proof points: the bank accepted payment, ZIMRA received it under the correct identity, and TaRMS posted it against the return. A debit on a bank statement proves only the first step. After filing and payment, review the ledger and retain the bank confirmation, return acknowledgement and posting evidence together.

The notice warns that funds without a return will not be correctly accounted for and can affect automatic tax-clearance issuance. File the return, pay with validated identifiers, then confirm the ledger. Escalate unmatched funds rather than carrying them as unexplained items into the next month.

6. Keep NSSA And Workers Compensation Separate

NSSA obligations are related to payroll but are not part of the ZIMRA P2 return. Maintain the social security registration number, enrolled employee population, insurable earnings, employee and employer contributions, payment evidence and portal statements in a separate reconciliation that agrees to payroll.

NSSA's accessible employer guide contains a 4.5% employee and 4.5% employer Pension and Other Benefits Scheme example, but it is marked 2021 and says maximum pensionable earnings can change monthly. It also says Accident Prevention and Workers Compensation Scheme rates depend on industry classification and are reviewed. This guide therefore does not claim a current 2026 ceiling or industry rate. Confirm values directly with NSSA or the applicable gazetted instrument.

Use NSSA self-service to manage the account, check statements and preserve compliance evidence. Reconcile joiners, leavers, split-currency earnings and any ceiling application. PAYE can be correct while NSSA is wrong, so neither reconciliation substitutes for the other.

7. Reconcile Monthly Payroll To The Annual Record

ZIMRA requires records of remuneration paid or payable, allowable deductions and PAYE withheld for at least six years. Its new-business guidance says the ITF16 contains annual earnings, deductions, credits and PAYE for each employee and should be submitted within 30 days after year-end.

Do not wait until January to find that monthly identifiers, currencies or totals do not reconcile. Maintain a year-to-date schedule after every close. For each employee, roll forward gross remuneration, taxable benefits, allowed deductions, credits, PAYE and AIDS levy. Tie it to each P2 return and the general ledger.

For leavers, preserve the final payroll and year-to-date totals. For corrections, record the original entry, reason, approval, revised amount and affected return. The annual file should be reproducible from twelve controlled monthly packs without rebuilding payroll from bank statements.

8. Zimbabwe Monthly Payroll Close Checklist

  1. Confirm ZIMRA and NSSA registrations, identifiers and portal access remain active.
  2. Lock joiners, leavers, salary changes, variable pay, benefits, deductions and currencies.
  3. Verify current official PAYE tables and NSSA values for the period. Record each source version.
  4. Calculate employee PAYE, AIDS levy and separate NSSA amounts from approved inputs.
  5. Review exceptions and reconcile gross pay, deductions and net pay by currency.
  6. Prepare the long P2 return and tie employee and tax totals to final payroll.
  7. Check the current ZIMRA notice for exact return and payment dates.
  8. Submit through TaRMS, pay through the approved route and retain both confirmations.
  9. Confirm payment posted to the correct ledger and clear unmatched funds.
  10. Reconcile NSSA separately and update the year-to-date ITF16 schedule.

A manager should be able to review the period, sources, employee population, currency treatment, calculations, return, payment and ledger posting without asking the preparer to recreate the logic. That is the difference between payroll that produced net pay and a close that can support an employer compliance review.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is a Zimbabwe PAYE return due in 2026?

Public Notice 44 set the July 2026 PAYE return deadline at August 5 and remittance at August 10. Check the notice for the relevant period.

Where does an employer submit the P2 return?

ZIMRA directs employers to submit the long PAYE return through TaRMS. Retain the upload file, confirmation and status.

How long should PAYE records be retained?

ZIMRA says records of remuneration, deductions and PAYE withheld must be retained for at least six years.

Does AfroTools file PAYE or NSSA returns?

No. AfroTools provides a planning calculator and checklist. Verify and submit through official systems.
