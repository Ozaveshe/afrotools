Mauritius individual income-tax filing is not the same task as checking a monthly payslip. The annual return brings income, deductions and tax already withheld into one filing record. The useful starting point is a complete set of documents and the correct assessment year, not a salary figure entered into a calculator.

Primary sources reviewed on August 30, 2026. This guide covers preparation for the Mauritius Revenue Authority's 2026/27 individual return. It is not personalised tax advice, a determination of residence or an official filing service. Cross-border income, disputed deductions and unusual business arrangements need specific MRA or professional guidance.

Use the AfroTools salary and tax tools to identify a relevant calculation aid. The Mauritius PAYE Calculator is a payroll sense-check, not an annual-return engine: compare its displayed period and assumptions before using its result. Do not enter a TAN, password, bank account or dependent's identity into a calculator. Complete the actual filing through MRA.

1. Label the income period before collecting figures

The MRA standard-return notes for assessment year 2026/27 cover income from 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026. Note 24 gives Thursday 15 October 2026 as the electronic filing and payment deadline. These are different dates with different jobs: one identifies the income being reported; the other tells you when the filing and any payment are due.

Put both dates at the top of your working checklist. Label each annual statement with its covered period before extracting amounts. A bank statement downloaded during August is not automatically an August-income record, and a document labelled 2026 may cover a calendar year rather than this income year. Resolve the period from the document itself.

Keep current payroll declarations separate. The MRA EDF notes for the year ending 30 June 2027 concern a later income period. Do not transfer figures or eligibility assumptions from that declaration into the return for the year already ended. Use a separate folder and checklist for each period so a new employer request does not overwrite annual-return evidence.

2. Choose a return by eligibility, not by its name

The current MRA filing page provides Standard, Presumptive and Simplified routes. It says all self-employed individuals must file. Standard-return triggers include net income above Rs 500,000, business gross income above Rs 2 million, PAYE-withheld emoluments, TDS-subjected income or chargeable income. The simplified route has conditions including gross income below Rs 2 million and total net income below Rs 500,000.

The separate 2026/27 presumptive-return notes restrict that option to specified activities: agriculture, forestry, fishing, manufacturing excluding restaurants, retail goods including off-premises food, and wholesale goods. They set a Rs 10 million gross-income ceiling and a Rs 400,000 limit involving other income and emoluments or directors' fees. The presumptive calculation is 1% of gross income. It is not a general tax rate for every freelancer or small business.

Write down the activity you actually conduct and every other income source before comparing these routes. A business-registration label alone does not settle the question. If income is exactly on a boundary, activities are mixed, or a condition is unclear, obtain clarification rather than rounding the figure to fit a preferred form. Keep the answer with the working file.

This article does not replace the eligibility tests or enumerate every special case. Its purpose is to help you gather the facts needed to use the correct official route. The existing Mauritius employer payroll guide covers the employer's monthly and annual responsibilities; your individual filing decision is a separate review.

3. Build a document index before opening the form

MRA's required-information checklist includes identification and account details, employment and TDS statements, business profit-and-loss information, partnership or succession income certificates, investment information, deduction evidence and relevant CPS receipts. The checklist identifies CPS quarters ending September 2025, December 2025 and March 2026. Gather only the items that apply, but do not silently mark an unavailable item as zero.

Request missing employment statements early enough to reconcile them. If you changed jobs, list each employer separately and check that all employment periods are covered. Do not combine two statements merely because the payer names look similar. First establish whether they are separate employers, a replacement statement or duplicate copies.

4. Keep income, tax withheld and cash received separate

Notes 9 and 23 of the standard-return guidance distinguish employment income, PAYE, TDS and CPS. Match employment entries to the employer statement and tax credits to the relevant supporting records. Net salary received in a bank account is not the same field as taxable emoluments. The payslip explanation guide helps distinguish those labels before you compare totals.

Prepare one reconciliation row per income source. Record the amount reported by the issuer, the corresponding amount in your own records, and the explanation for any difference. Investigate disagreements before changing a number. A correction should have a reason, a document reference and a date, rather than simply making two totals look equal.

For self-employment, keep the sales record, expense record and profit-and-loss account distinct. Cash left in an account is not a substitute for a prepared income statement. Transfers between your own accounts, customer receipts, drawings and borrowing should be identifiable in the working records so they can be reviewed appropriately. Ask a qualified adviser about tax treatment instead of deciding it from the bank description alone.

Use the same discipline for credits. Check the period and issuer before taking a figure from a withholding statement. Keep a query list for missing statements or apparent duplications; do not create a replacement certificate yourself. Where a corrected document arrives, retain both versions and label which one supports the final return.

5. Review deductions with evidence, not assumptions

MRA's common-errors list warns about omitted income, duplicate dependent claims, unsuitable education claims and medical premiums paid by an employer. These are factual review prompts, not reasons to assume every household expense qualifies. The standard-return notes also contain a housing-interest paragraph referring to June 2025 inside the 2026/27 document. Seek MRA clarification before relying on that inconsistent period; this guide does not resolve it by guesswork.

Create a short record for each proposed deduction: what was paid, by whom, for whose benefit, during which period, and which current rule you believe applies. Attach the supporting evidence privately. If the person preparing the return cannot explain why a claim belongs there, move it to the query list rather than treating a receipt as sufficient approval.

Coordinate household information before submission. Compare proposed claims with the other person's intended return where relevant, using only the information needed to resolve overlap. A joint discussion is more useful than two independently completed checklists that never meet. Record the agreed treatment and retain any official clarification.

Do not change an amount just to reproduce last year's refund. Income, withholding, household circumstances and applicable rules may differ. Compare the underlying documents, not only the final balance. A prior return can help identify records to request, but it is not current evidence for a new claim.

6. Review the pre-filled form and capture acknowledgement

MRA says its pre-filled return can be edited, and the taxpayer remains responsible for completeness. A draft can be saved. Electronic amendment is available for returns other than Simplified and Presumptive returns. Check the applicable official process before attempting a correction.

Work through the form against your document index. Mark each reviewed section, not just each populated field. Pre-filled information should prompt a comparison with your records; a blank field should prompt a relevance check. Keep unresolved questions visible until they are answered.

Before submission, check the taxpayer identity, assessment year, return type and final totals together. Download or retain the permitted review copy in your private folder. If someone assists, distinguish their preparation work from your review of the completed return. Do not send a password or authentication code through an informal message thread.

MRA specifically warns that saving or printing is not submission. Look for the acknowledgement page with its ID after the submission action. Retain it alongside the filed version. If the browser closes without that evidence, verify the account's filing state before sending another return. This avoids treating uncertainty as either success or a reason for repeated submissions.

7. Track payment or refund separately from filing

The official MRA electronic-payment facility explains one-off direct debit using the individual's personal bank account and payment date. It distinguishes that process from a recurring direct-debit arrangement. Before authorising payment, compare the tax type, period and amount on your payment instruction with the filed return.

Keep three separate status entries: return submitted, payment instructed, and payment confirmed. A calculation showing tax payable is not a transaction receipt. Equally, a bank debit without a matching tax period needs investigation. Save the relevant reference and compare it with the account evidence available through MRA.

If the return shows excess tax paid, review the refund account details through the official service. Do not promise yourself a payment date based on a calculator result or a previous year's experience. Keep the submitted result and follow the official enquiry process if further information is requested.

For a failed or uncertain payment, record the time, method and non-sensitive error description. Check the bank and official tax record before retrying, particularly if the first attempt may have succeeded. Share transaction evidence only through the appropriate support channel, with unnecessary personal details removed.

8. Close the file with a short evidence checklist

A useful final review asks four questions: can you identify the filed version, trace its major figures, prove the submission action, and explain the payment or refund status? Keep the document index, supporting records, acknowledgement, payment evidence and unresolved correspondence together without conflating them.

Use clear version names such as preparation, filed and amended, with dates but no sensitive identifiers in the filename. Do not delete an earlier filing record just because a later version exists. Retain documents according to the applicable requirements and any ongoing enquiry; this article does not set a universal retention period.

For help, start from the contact details on the current MRA filing page. Describe the exact question and period rather than sending an entire archive unsolicited. After the answer arrives, update the relevant checklist item and record what remains outstanding. Filing is easier to follow when every open issue has a specific next action.

Frequently asked questions

Which income period does this return cover?

The 2026/27 assessment covers income from 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026. MRA's return notes set 15 October 2026 as the filing and payment deadline.

Does a saved draft prove submission?

No. MRA says to check for the acknowledgement page and its ID after submitting. A saved draft or printed preparation copy is not that evidence.

Can AfroTools file the return or confirm a refund?

No. AfroTools supports preparation and arithmetic checks. Only the official MRA process can receive the return, record payment or determine refund processing.
