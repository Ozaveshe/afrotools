Sierra Leone GST Filing Guide 2026: Registration, Returns and Payment

A practical NRA source guide to the 15% rate, NLe 500,000 registration threshold, invoices, ITAS returns, input tax and month-end controls.

Sierra Leone calls its broad consumption tax Goods and Services Tax, or GST. People may search for a Sierra Leone VAT return, but the statutory and National Revenue Authority name is GST. A sound filing process begins before anyone opens the return. The business must confirm registration status, classify each supply, control invoice numbering, test input tax evidence, reconcile its ledgers and preserve submission and payment proof.

Official sources were reviewed on September 10, 2026 against the NRA's current GST and tax-return pages, Finance Act 2024, Finance Act 2026, the NRA tax-law collection, current notices and the ITAS portal. Finance Act 2024 supplies the current NLe 500,000 registration threshold. Finance Act 2026 confirms the payment timing and makes current changes affecting digital services and GST Schedules. The NRA pages provide the 15% standard rate and the operational return route.

Use the Sierra Leone GST Calculator and Invoice tool to add or extract standard GST in SLE and screen the compulsory threshold. It runs locally in the browser and rejects the obsolete SLL currency code. It does not decide whether a product is zero-rated, approve voluntary registration, validate an invoice, claim input tax, submit through ITAS or issue an NRA receipt.

The threshold is a registration trigger, not a general tax-free allowance for a registered business. Once registered, the business accounts for GST according to the treatment of each supply. A result below NLe 500,000 also does not prove that the business can ignore GST. Voluntary registration remains subject to the Commissioner-General's decision, while Finance Act 2026 creates a specific registration reach for a person supplying digital services to consumers in Sierra Leone whether or not that person has a physical presence in the country.

Finance Act 2026 also amended parts of the Second and Third Schedules. That is why a generic label such as water, food, education, health, machinery or digital service is not enough to select a zero or exempt rate. The exact item, use, customer, certificate and current wording can change the result. Keep the law or NRA evidence used for each non-standard code.

Build a registration decision file

Start with the taxpayer identity. Record the legal name, trading name, TIN, registration address, branches, business activities and responsible NRA office. Then map the supplies that count toward the section 15 test. Separate taxable trading supplies from capital disposals, exempt activity and amounts that are not business consideration. If the classification is uncertain, do not settle it with a spreadsheet label.

The current AfroTools screen tests three figures used by the legal source contract: taxable supplies in the past 12 months, taxable supplies expected in the next 12 months, and taxable supplies in the past four months. The main threshold is NLe 500,000. The four-month boundary is one third of that amount, which is NLe 166,666.666 recurring. The tool compares without rounding that boundary down, so NLe 166,666.66 remains below it and NLe 166,666.67 meets it.

Forecasts need evidence. Signed contracts, accepted orders, recurring service agreements, project schedules and a new online channel can support a reasonable next-12-month expectation. Keep those records beside the threshold schedule. Review the schedule monthly, because a business may cross the boundary before its financial year ends.

For digital activity, identify the platform, seller, buyer, fee, commission, location of the consumer, physical-presence position and local representative. Finance Act 2026 expanded the definition of a digital marketplace and added a registration rule for taxable digital services supplied to consumers in Sierra Leone without requiring physical presence. It also requires a supplier of digital services without physical presence or a registered office in Sierra Leone to appoint a representative by written notice. A cross-border platform should obtain current NRA advice rather than applying a domestic retail checklist unchanged.

When registration is required, preserve the application, supporting records, effective date, certificate and NRA correspondence. If the business is below the compulsory threshold and wants voluntary registration, record the commercial reason and the Commissioner-General's decision. Do not charge GST merely because an application was submitted.

Keep standard, zero-rated, exempt and relieved supplies separate

The NRA states a single standard rate of 15%. The GST framework then distinguishes standard-rated supplies from zero-rated, exempt, relieved and outside-scope amounts. These categories affect output tax, invoice wording and whether purchase tax can support a credit. They should have separate codes in the sales and purchase registers.

A zero-rated supply is still within the GST system at a 0% rate when the current First Schedule applies. An exempt supply follows the current Second Schedule and is not the same as zero-rating. Relief under the Third Schedule has its own certificate path. Finance Act 2026 says persons and organisations in the Third Schedule are issued a GST Relief Certificate for purchases unless the Commissioner-General has cause to withhold it because of possible abuse.

Use exact evidence for every special treatment. Record the provision, effective date, description of the supply, quantity, customer status, destination, certificate where relevant and the documents that prove the conditions. If an invoice combines a standard item with a non-standard item, calculate and record each line separately. Do not average the rate across the gross invoice.

The 2026 amendments illustrate the freshness risk. They changed Second Schedule wording for water and other listed supplies. An old checklist can therefore produce a wrong code even when the 15% standard rate has not changed. Recheck the current Schedule whenever the product, service, customer type or supporting certificate differs from the normal pattern.

How to add and extract Sierra Leone GST

Use SLE, also written as the new leone or NLe, for current records. Do not mix current SLE amounts with historical SLL amounts. The AfroTools calculator rejects SLL so a redenomination mismatch cannot silently produce a thousand-fold error.

For a GST-exclusive amount at 15%, multiply the net amount by 0.15, then add that GST to the net amount. For a GST-inclusive total, divide by 1.15 to find the net amount, or multiply the gross amount by 15 and divide by 115 to extract GST. Keep a consistent rounding policy and ensure the invoice, sales register and return use the same rounded figures.

Control invoices and input-tax evidence

The NRA GST guide says a registered supplier must issue a GST invoice for taxable supplies and lists core particulars such as supplier name, address and TIN, time of supply, a number from a consecutive series, customer details where the customer is taxable, and a description and quantity or extent of the supply. Use the current legal and NRA form requirements for the complete invoice, especially where electronic cash registers or special treatments apply.

Protect the invoice sequence. Investigate gaps, duplicates, cancellations and manual documents. A correction should remain visible through the authorised correction method rather than deleting the original trail. Finance Act 2026 also addresses replacement of damaged, faulty or defective electronic cash register equipment, so an equipment failure should have a logged incident, replacement evidence and a controlled bridge for transactions during the interruption.

Build the purchase register separately from the sales register. For each proposed input amount, capture supplier identity, invoice number and date, description, business purpose, net amount, GST, gross amount, payment evidence and the tax period. Then test whether the cost supports taxable activity and whether the required invoice or import evidence is held. Do not deduct all GST that appears on an expense report automatically.

The published GST return form separates standard-rated sales, zero-rated supplies, exempt supplies, relieved supplies, locally purchased inputs, imported inputs, total input GST, allowable input tax and the net amount payable or creditable. That structure is a useful reconciliation map even when the current return is completed online. A value in the total-input box is not yet the allowable-credit figure.

Keep rejected and deferred input amounts in a controlled schedule. State why each item was excluded or postponed, who reviewed it and what evidence would be needed. This prevents the same unsupported invoice from being added again in a later month.

Prepare and submit the GST return through ITAS

The NRA's current return instructions direct a taxpayer to sign in to ITAS, open the return area, choose the outstanding tax type and period, download or upload a template where required, complete mandatory attachments, review the summary, certify that the information is true and submit. The taxpayer should then retain the confirmation and refresh the account to check that the return status and balance reflect the submission.

  1. Confirm the filing identity. Check legal name, TIN, GST status, tax period, due date, currency and authorised submitter before working on figures.
  2. Close the sales register. Account for every invoice, credit note, debit note and receipt. Separate standard, zero-rated, exempt and relieved amounts.
  3. Close the purchase register. Match each proposed credit to the supplier record, document, business purpose and payment or import evidence.
  4. Reconcile output GST. Compare invoice totals with the general ledger, cash records, bank receipts, contracts and electronic cash register output.
  5. Review input GST. Remove duplicates, unsupported invoices, private costs, non-creditable items and amounts assigned to the wrong period.
  6. Bridge to the return. Map each ledger bucket to the corresponding return field and explain every manual adjustment.
  7. Complete the ITAS workflow. Select the correct return and period, use the required template or attachment, review the summary and certify only after approval.
  8. Submit and pay. Finance Act 2026 places payment no later than the end of the month following the tax period. Preserve acknowledgement, payment instruction, bank evidence and NRA receipt.
  9. Verify the account. Refresh ITAS, confirm the return status and balance, and log any portal error or unresolved amount for follow-up.

The NRA's general return page says GST returns are submitted at the end of the month following the transactions. Finance Act 2026 uses the end of the month following the tax period for payment. A business still needs to confirm its assigned tax period and the exact date shown in ITAS. Do not assume every taxpayer has the same period simply because the operational deadline rule is expressed monthly.

A nil amount does not automatically remove the filing duty. The NRA's published GST return includes a specific nil-return indicator, which shows that a no-tax period still belongs in the return process. Check the taxpayer's account and current NRA requirements before skipping a period.

Month-end controls that prevent GST errors

Maintain one source register. Record the GST Act, later Finance Acts, current NRA guidance, review date and owner. Finance Act changes should trigger a review of rate codes, thresholds, Schedules, portal steps and templates.

Monitor registration monthly. Update actual 12-month, expected 12-month and past-four-month taxable supplies. Attach contracts or forecasts supporting the expected figure.

Lock special codes. Zero-rated, exempt and relief codes should require exact current evidence and reviewer approval. Do not infer treatment from a broad industry label.

Reconcile three ways. Compare transaction registers, GST control accounts and the return. Agreement with the bank balance alone does not prove that invoice classification is correct.

Separate preparation and approval. One person can prepare the registers and return bridge; another should review taxpayer identity, period, rates, special treatments, adjustments, credits and payment.

Retain portal evidence. Save the filed return, confirmation, payment receipt and any correspondence. A screenshot of a completed form before submission is not proof that the NRA accepted it.

Keep live failures visible. If ITAS is unavailable, log the time, period, error and actions taken, retain the ready return pack and follow current NRA instructions. Do not backdate an internal status to make the close appear complete.
