A SARS payment arrangement addresses an outstanding tax balance that cannot be settled immediately. For a South African employer, business owner or individual, the practical job is to establish the debt, prepare a credible repayment proposal, obtain the actual decision and carry out the agreed payments. Those are separate evidence steps. A saved plan or a payment instruction does not complete all of them.

It is preparation guidance, not tax or legal advice, approval, filing or a debt-negotiation service. The working papers below are AfroTools editorial recommendations. Use the South Africa PAYE calculator to estimate payroll deductions from confirmed inputs when preparing future cash requirements. It cannot identify an assessed debt, calculate a SARS repayment agreement or verify remittance. The small-business tax calendar helps organise recurring obligations. This article focuses on the separate task of resolving existing debt.

1. Establish the balance and the account behind it

Start with the official record, not an amount copied from a message. SARS's outstanding-debt guidance describes requesting a statement of account through eFiling or the MobiApp and obtaining assistance through its authenticated contact channels. Keep the statement, relevant assessments, submitted declarations and payment evidence together.

Prepare a debt inventory with one line for each tax account and period. Record the statement date, tax component, penalties, interest and balance where shown. Identify which entries are understood and which require explanation. Keep a reference to the original document beside each number rather than transcribing a total without its context.

Compare the inventory with your own records. A payment made from the bank, an employer declaration and a SARS allocation can have different dates and references. Mark differences for investigation instead of silently changing the official amount to match the bookkeeping total. Ask the responsible reviewer to resolve missing documents before approving the proposal.

2. Choose the right resolution path

SARS distinguishes payment by instalments, compromise of debt and suspension where debt is disputed. Its debt page says an arrangement may be declined, interest continues on unpaid debt and failure to comply can terminate the agreement and restore normal collection. A repayment proposal therefore needs both affordability evidence and a plan for meeting its conditions.

If the amount itself is disputed, review the SARS dispute guide. It describes separate remission, objection, appeal, reasons and suspension functions. The relevant route depends on the decision and tax type. Check the actual notice and current rules promptly; do not assume that an arrangement application replaces an objection or supplies a suspension decision.

3. Resolve filing and account obstacles first

The SARS compliance-functionality guide explains that outstanding returns can prevent an arrangement request through My Compliance Profile. Review the actual registration, return and debt indicators rather than assuming that one overdue balance is the only issue. The guide provides account-level options including payment, an arrangement request and account correction.

Create a short obstacle log before starting the application. Record the displayed issue, the tax and period involved, the person responsible and the supporting document needed. Close an item only when its evidence exists. A promise to submit a missing return later is different from a submission acknowledgement.

For employers, distinguish historic debt from the next payroll declaration and payment. A repayment proposal based on future payroll cash should preserve a line for new obligations as well as old arrears. Reconcile the payroll working paper with the official declaration separately; the PAYE calculator is a planning aid and cannot certify either figure.

4. Prepare an affordable proposal with a clear evidence index

SARS's payment-arrangement FAQ lists qualifying circumstances, including temporary liquidity constraints, anticipated receipts and security where required. It also says the arrangement must cover the entire debt and outstanding returns or reconciliations must be submitted. Previous defaults require reasonable, valid explanations. Meeting a planning checklist does not guarantee acceptance.

Build the cash review from documented receipts and commitments. Label expected receipts separately from money already available. Give each material assumption a source, date and reviewer. Explain why the proposed instalment can be paid on its intended date, rather than presenting an annual surplus that ignores monthly timing.

The working paper should connect opening cash, expected receipts, essential commitments, current taxes and the proposed debt payment. Identify the remaining headroom and the assumptions that could consume it. Avoid invented sales forecasts or unexplained balancing figures. Where an amount is uncertain, disclose the uncertainty and ask the reviewer what evidence is needed.

Use a document index that describes each attachment and the question it answers. Keep the current version separate from superseded drafts. The official application determines the documents required for the actual request; your index helps check completeness without claiming that one fixed packet works for every taxpayer.

5. Review the actual eFiling request

The SARS deferral guide describes requests for one tax type at a time. Its simulation starts at six months, with an option to propose different terms. Review the displayed balance, dates and instalments. A simulation is a proposal, not an approval. The guide covers assessed income tax, PAYE, UIF, SDL and VAT among its supported obligations.

Before submitting, compare the on-screen details with the approved working paper. Check the taxpayer, tax type and contact information. Read the agreement terms rather than treating the declaration checkbox as an administrative formality. If the screen differs from the reviewed proposal, stop and establish what changed.

The guide lists business supporting material including bank statements, a forward cash-flow statement, financial statements where applicable, management accounts, an asset register and debtor/creditor analysis. For salaried individuals it lists bank statements, the latest payslip and outstanding-account evidence. Required documents must be supplied to finalise the request. Saved simulations can expire at midnight; retain your own preparation records.

Have the authorised user inspect each attachment before submission. A readable filename is not proof that the right period or taxpayer appears inside. Record the submission result and reference. Keep access credentials within the authorised account process; an editorial checklist never needs an eFiling password.

6. Separate submission, approval and compliance status

Keep three distinct records: the proposal reviewed internally, the request submitted to SARS and the official outcome. When a decision arrives, compare its conditions with the proposed terms. Record who will carry out each action and where its evidence will be stored. Escalate an unexplained difference before the first payment is due.

The SARS TCS overview says compliance includes required registrations, current particulars, submitted returns and no outstanding debt unless an arrangement or suspension has been agreed. Compliance is broader than a repayment application. Its PIN enables an authorised third party to view status at the time of checking; it does not expose the full taxpayer record.

If a supplier or employer also needs compliance evidence, use the South Africa TCS PIN guide for that separate workflow. Do not mark procurement or onboarding complete simply because an arrangement request has been sent. Preserve the actual status result and date for the purpose it serves.

7. Make the payment and verify its allocation

The SARS payment rules say payment is recognised when received into its bank account. For eFiling credit-push payments, the payer must authorise the request through the banking product. Use the correct payment reference from the relevant SARS form and allow for bank cut-off times. An instruction awaiting bank authorisation is not a completed transfer.

Put the operational handover in writing. Name the person who prepares the payment, the person who authorises it and the person who checks the resulting account entry. Where one person performs several steps, keep the evidence for each step distinguishable. Store the official reference privately instead of copying full identifiers into shared reminders.

After payment, compare the bank evidence with the SARS statement and intended tax period. Record any discrepancy, the query reference and the next check date. Do not close the instalment line merely because the bank balance fell. Allocation matters when several tax accounts or periods are being reviewed.

8. Handle collection contact and financial documents carefully

The SARS debt-outsourcing FAQ explains formal handover notices and verification of appointed collectors. It tells taxpayers to use approved SARS payment channels and avoid third-party bank accounts. Check a collector against the official list and your account evidence before responding to a payment demand.

Keep the financial packet in a controlled folder. Share only the documents required by the authorised review or official channel. Use a redacted task summary when colleagues need to know the next action but do not need full statements, account numbers or identity documents. Avoid placing these records in public share links or support screenshots.

SARS's scam guidance warns about messages seeking banking and login information. Reach the official site independently when a message creates doubt. An urgent tone, familiar logo or accurate-looking amount does not establish the sender's authority.

9. Maintain a monthly evidence trail

Use a private tracker with due date, agreed amount, payment reference, bank confirmation, account allocation and unresolved query fields. Link each entry to its underlying evidence. Add an owner and next action to open items so another authorised reviewer can continue without reconstructing the entire history.

Review the tracker alongside current filing obligations. Keep the original decision and later correspondence together, with dates and versions. If cash circumstances deteriorate, use the official contact route promptly and preserve the response. Do not unilaterally replace the agreed schedule with a smaller payment and label it an amended arrangement.

The final review asks whether the current account evidence matches the intended outcome. A tidy spreadsheet is useful preparation, but the official decision and account records establish what happened. Retain the records needed to answer a later query without relying on memory or an isolated screenshot.

Frequently asked questions

Does a saved payment plan mean SARS approved it?

No. Keep the saved proposal, submission acknowledgement and official decision separate. Check the outcome and conditions before treating an arrangement as agreed.

Does the arrangement remove interest?

SARS says interest accrues on unpaid debt. Review the actual agreement and account statement rather than assuming that instalments erase interest.

Can one request combine every tax type?

The current SARS deferral guide describes one tax type per request. Review each relevant account separately and follow the official workflow.

Can AfroTools submit or approve the arrangement?

No. AfroTools supports planning calculations and preparation. Approval, account correction, filing and remittance verification belong to the authorised SARS workflow.
