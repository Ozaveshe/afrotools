A Kenyan withholding income tax certificate connects a deduction made by a payer to the recipient's tax records. For a supplier, consultant or business preparing an income tax return, the useful question is whether each certificate matches the income, transaction and year being reported. A certificate file, bank receipt and return credit should be reconciled before the final submission.

Sources reviewed: October 8, 2026. This guide uses public KRA guidance and the December 2025 and January 2026 iTax enhancements note. It is preparation guidance, not a tax opinion, official verification or filing service. The working papers and review controls below are AfroTools editorial recommendations. No private taxpayer account was accessed.

The Kenya withholding tax guide covers the broader rate and remittance questions. This article concentrates on the recipient's certificate file, income reconciliation and credit evidence. Use the Kenya WHT calculator for a planning estimate after confirming the applicable treatment. Its result does not establish what KRA has received or credited.

1. Identify the tax and the recipient before matching amounts

Start with the legal recipient recorded on the contract, invoice and certificate. Check that the records refer to the same person or entity, even when a trading name is used in correspondence. Record the relevant tax obligation and income category. Keep withholding income tax separate from withholding VAT, payroll PAYE and other deductions; a similar document name is not enough to establish identical treatment.

The KRA withholding FAQ describes withholding as a deduction at source, with the balance paid to the recipient and the deduction remitted on that recipient's behalf. It describes the certificate as evidence of deducted and remitted tax. Accordingly, keep three evidence items distinct: the payer's deduction schedule, the remittance record and the certificate issued following remittance.

Build a simple identity sheet for the review. Include the recipient, payer, income category, period and references to the underlying documents. Store full identifiers privately. If the certificate names a different recipient, flag that discrepancy before including the amount in the return working paper. A correct-looking total cannot resolve a wrong identity.

2. Assemble a complete certificate and income inventory

Collect certificates from the available authorised records, then compare them with the receipts and deductions in the bookkeeping file. Work from the entire period rather than the most recent email. Keep the original electronic documents intact and create a separate index. Mark whether each row is confirmed, awaiting a certificate or awaiting clarification from the payer.

For each transaction, record the contract or invoice reference, gross amount, deduction, net receipt, transaction date and certificate reference. Add the period shown on the certificate and the observed ledger period. These are recommended working-paper fields, not a prescribed KRA form. Retain the supporting document for every amount instead of relying on a manually typed summary.

When several receipts relate to one invoice, preserve that relationship explicitly. When a receipt covers several invoices, document the allocation rather than duplicating the full receipt against each row. Reconcile the inventory's total with the books and keep unresolved differences visible. This makes the review reproducible without inventing a balancing certificate.

3. Verify through KRA's own service

The KRA online-services directory lists a Withholding Tax Certificate Checker separately from its PIN and Tax Compliance Certificate services. Open the checker through that official directory and use the information requested by the current service. This guide does not certify its authenticated availability or promise a particular response for a taxpayer.

Record when the check was performed, which certificate reference was used and what result was observed. Compare the returned identity and certificate information with the source file where the service supplies it. Keep a private copy of the result needed for the review. A failed connection should be recorded as verification unavailable, rather than certificate invalid.

Do not post a certificate, taxpayer PIN or account screenshot in a public support channel. Use the authorised KRA route for account-specific questions. If a document arrived through an unfamiliar link, reach the official service independently before entering identifiers. The document inventory should point to the controlled original and the observed verification result.

4. Establish whether withholding is final or creditable

The certificate alone does not answer the final-tax question. KRA's annual return guidance says that where withholding is not final, the related income must be declared and the withholding applied as a credit against tax payable. Have the preparer determine the treatment for the actual recipient and income category before building the credit total.

Give the working paper separate columns for recorded withholding and reviewed return treatment. Attach the source or advice supporting the classification. Leave a disputed item open rather than classifying every certificate as creditable. Residence, income category and case-specific rules require their own review; the presence of a resident address does not settle every question.

Keep gross income and the deduction separate in the accounts-to-return bridge. A net bank receipt is an amount received after a deduction, not a substitute for the income record. The preparer needs to explain both the income reported and the credit claimed. Do not remove income from that bridge merely because the payer withheld tax.

5. Reconcile the credit to the correct year

The KRA iTax enhancements note for December 2025 and January 2026 explains that its described WHT credit posting uses the captured payment date, which it calls the tax point, rather than the PRN generation date. It gives an official example: a PRN generated on January 31, 2025 with January 1, 2023 captured as the payment date posts the WHT income tax credit to the 2023 ledger.

That published example is a reason to inspect the dates, not a direction to backdate a transaction. Put invoice date, captured payment date, PRN generation date, remittance date and ledger year in separate fields. If they appear inconsistent, compare the original records and ask the payer or KRA to resolve the specific entry through an authorised process.

Take a dated snapshot of the relevant account view for the private review file. Record which year was inspected and which certificate was matched. Keep an earlier snapshot if a later correction changes the position. Do not assume the calendar year of an email attachment or downloaded filename controls where the credit belongs.

6. Understand the 2026 income-validation boundary

KRA's November 10, 2025 public notice says validation begins on January 1, 2026 for submission of 2025 year-of-income or accounting-period returns. Its named data sources are TIMS/eTIMS, withholding income tax gross amounts and customs import records. It also identifies electronic-invoice support where applicable, subject to the stated exceptions. Record that scope rather than labelling every historical return with one new rule.

The enhancements note says the described gross-turnover validation uses the higher of TIMS/eTIMS sales and gross amounts associated with WHT credits for the period. It describes net electronic-invoice sales after credit-note adjustments, considers non-final WHT transactions and permits upward adjustment of gross turnover or income where applicable. Read the source with the actual return type and period.

Prepare a bridge between bookkeeping income, electronic-invoice data and the certificate inventory. A validation message is a signal to investigate those records, not permission to change correct accounts to an unexplained portal amount. Check missing invoices, corrections, duplicates, transaction classification and period mapping. Write down the reason for any adjustment and retain the record supporting it.

7. Give missing or mismatched credits an evidence trail

Use a discrepancy list with one issue per certificate or transaction. Describe what was expected from the records, what was actually observed and the question requiring resolution. Add an owner and the next authorised action. Separate a missing document, an unverified certificate, an incorrect identity and a credit appearing in another year; they need different enquiries.

For an enquiry to the payer, prepare a private evidence pack linking the invoice, deduction and relevant receipt to the disputed certificate. For a KRA enquiry, use the current official support or tax-service-office route and provide the information requested for that case. Keep the enquiry reference and response. This guide makes no promise about response time or correction approval.

A local spreadsheet edit changes your working paper, not the official record. Preserve the observed discrepancy until the authorised correction or explanation is available. If the filing deadline creates a decision that cannot wait, escalate it to the responsible tax preparer with the unresolved evidence. Do not guess a credit or use another transaction's certificate to fill the gap.

8. Review the actual return before submission

Choose the return for the taxpayer's actual obligation and period. KRA's filing page currently identifies the 2025 individual year of income as January 1 to December 31, 2025, with its filing window ending June 30, 2026. On this guide's October review date, that window has already ended. This is a reconciliation guide, not an announcement of an extended deadline.

Compare the prepared return with the reviewed income bridge and certificate schedule before submitting. Check that the same transaction was not included twice through an imported schedule and a manual entry. Preserve the file version approved by the reviewer. A final review should identify both resolved differences and decisions about any items still unresolved.

The KRA filing FAQ explains that return-period selection affects the relevant rates and reliefs applied by the return. Some examples on that FAQ refer to earlier years. Use it as background, then confirm the current form and instructions for the actual period. Do not transplant an old example's treatment into a 2026 filing without that check.

9. Preserve submission and follow-up evidence

After the authorised filing, keep the submitted return, acknowledgement and approved reconciliation together. Record the actual submission date and reference. Compare subsequent account information with the expected position and preserve any new query separately. A successfully uploaded file, accepted return and reconciled account are distinct observations.

For handover, create a short index that another authorised reviewer can follow: period, income bridge, certificate schedule, open issues and official references. Use the PDF workspace for document preparation where useful, while keeping sensitive account records under your own access controls. The tool does not send a reconciliation to KRA or fix an account entry.

The Kenya TCC guide addresses the separate compliance-certificate workflow. A verified WHT document is not itself a TCC, and a TCC does not replace transaction-level reconciliation. Keep those checks in separate sections of the file so the next reviewer knows exactly what has been established.

Certificate-to-return review checklist

  • Recipient, payer and tax type match the underlying records.
  • Every deduction links to an invoice or income record and receipt evidence.
  • Certificate verification has an observed result and review date.
  • Final-tax or credit treatment has been reviewed for the actual income.
  • Captured dates and ledger year have been checked separately.
  • Books, electronic-invoice data and WHT gross records have a documented bridge.
  • Missing items and period mismatches have owners and enquiry references.
  • The submitted version, acknowledgement and follow-up account evidence are preserved privately.

Frequently asked questions

Is a payment registration number a withholding certificate?

Keep the generated payment reference, actual remittance evidence and issued withholding certificate separate. KRA describes the certificate as evidence of tax deducted and remitted.

Does every withholding certificate produce a credit in the annual return?

No. Determine whether the tax is final for the particular income. KRA states that non-final withholding belongs with the related income in the annual return and is applied as a credit.

Which year should contain a withholding credit?

Check the transaction and the actual ledger entry. The December 2025 and January 2026 KRA enhancements note explains that the captured payment date, rather than PRN generation date, controls the described WHT credit posting.

Can AfroTools verify a certificate or fix iTax?

No. Use the official KRA checker and authorised account workflow. AfroTools tools support planning calculations and document preparation; they do not certify a tax credit or submit a return.
