Source check: 27 September 2026. Ghana's resident PAYE bands changed on 1 September 2026 under the Income Tax (Amendment) Act, 2026 (Act 1178). This guide uses that schedule, plus SSNIT's 2026 insurable-earnings notice. It explains how a qualifying voluntary pension deduction can affect PAYE. It is a planning example, not a claim that every private pension payment qualifies for tax relief.

The order matters: identify gross and basic salary; subtract the employee's 5.5% SSNIT contribution and any approved deductions; apply the PAYE bands to chargeable income; then subtract cash deductions from gross pay. Try your own figures in the Ghana PAYE calculator.

Ghana resident PAYE bands from September 2026

GRA applies each percentage only to the part of monthly chargeable income in that slice. The first GHS 588 is in the 0% band; GHS 7,056 is the equivalent annual 0% band. These are chargeable-income figures after eligible deductions, not gross-salary thresholds.

How do the three pension tiers work?

Tier I and Tier II: statutory contributions

SSNIT describes a combined statutory contribution of 18.5% of basic salary: 5.5% from the employee and 13% from the employer. Of that combined amount, 13.5% goes to SSNIT and 5% goes to the second-tier occupational trustee. The second-tier remittance is part of the 18.5% total, not another 5% deducted from employee cash pay. Check both your SSNIT record and the trustee statement.

SSNIT's 2026 maximum insurable earning is GHS 69,000 monthly. Its maximum 13.5% contribution of GHS 9,315 confirms the period. At the ceiling, the employee's 5.5% contribution is GHS 3,795 monthly. A GHS 6,000 monthly basic salary is below the ceiling and generates GHS 330 employee SSNIT.

Tier III: voluntary savings

Tier III covers voluntary provident-fund and personal-pension arrangements. GRA's PAYE guidance lists contributions to an approved provident fund of up to 16.5% of basic salary among possible deductions. Approval and payroll treatment are essential. Ask the fund manager and payroll team how the limit applies to your chosen arrangement before assuming tax relief.

Tier III contributions can lower the current PAYE bill but also move cash into a fund with its own fees and withdrawal rules. Compare cash available today, retirement savings and the tax effect separately. Do not treat a projected tax saving as a guaranteed investment return.

How a qualifying contribution changes PAYE

A qualifying deduction lowers chargeable income before the PAYE slices are applied. If the entire deduction falls in a 25% marginal band, GHS 100 of qualifying deduction reduces PAYE by GHS 25. If the deduction crosses a band boundary, part of it saves tax at one rate and part at another, so a single marginal-rate shortcut overstates the saving. Actual cash take-home also falls by the contribution amount, offset partly by lower PAYE.

Before changing payroll, check the scheme's approval status, written deduction instructions, how your employer records the amount on the payslip, any contribution cap, fees, lock-up and withdrawal rules. Keep both payslip and fund statement. The calculator can model a scenario; the provider and GRA remain the authorities for a specific deduction.

GHS 8,000 gross with GHS 6,000 basic: worked example

Assume monthly gross pay of GHS 8,000, made up of GHS 6,000 basic and GHS 2,000 allowances. Employee SSNIT is 5.5% of basic, or GHS 330. Assume resident status and no other reliefs. For the second scenario, assume GHS 660 (11% of basic) is paid through an approved voluntary scheme and qualifies as a PAYE deduction. The example contribution is a choice, not a universal maximum.

Compare cash, savings and tax

The assumed GHS 660 qualifying contribution cuts PAYE by GHS 165 per month because both scenarios' remaining chargeable income stays in the 25% band. Cash take-home falls by GHS 495; GHS 660 moves into retirement savings. Cash plus the new savings contribution totals GHS 6,313 for that month before fund fees or investment change, versus GHS 6,148 without it. These are different uses of the same salary, not an extra GHS 165 paid by an employer or a guaranteed fund gain.

Over twelve identical months, the modeled PAYE difference is GHS 1,980. Real payroll can vary with bonuses, changes to basic pay, approved reliefs and contribution timing. Recalculate against the actual payslip and use the schedule in force for that month.

Check a Tier III arrangement before payroll deducts it

  1. Verify the provider and scheme. Use the National Pensions Regulatory Authority to confirm licensing and ask the provider for the scheme's approval and current terms.
  2. Ask payroll about tax treatment. Confirm whether the contribution will be deducted before PAYE, how the stated limit is applied and what evidence payroll needs.
  3. Compare the full trade-off. Check take-home pay, contribution amount, fees, access restrictions and the fund's statement rather than looking only at PAYE saved.
  4. Reconcile records. After the first deduction, compare payslip, employer remittance and fund statement. Raise a discrepancy with payroll and the provider.
