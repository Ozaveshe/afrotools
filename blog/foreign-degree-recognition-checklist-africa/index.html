Foreign Degree Recognition Checklist for Africans

AfroTools TeamAug 29, 202613 min read

A foreign degree is not recognised by a single global office. The decision owner changes with the destination and the purpose: further study, non-regulated employment, immigration, or entry into a licensed profession. Even when two applications concern the same certificate, they may require different authorities, reports and delivery methods.

Start with the AfroTools Foreign Degree Recognition Route Checker. It asks only for broad route information and the evidence you already have. It does not upload documents, convert grades or declare an equivalency. Source check: August 29, 2026. This guide uses current official guidance from UNESCO, Nigeria's Federal Ministry of Education, Kenya's Commission for University Education, Ghana's Tertiary Education Commission, South African authorities and the United States Department of Education.

The safe order: identify the recipient and purpose, confirm who makes the recognition decision, verify the awarding institution, ask which report and delivery method are accepted, prepare the exact evidence, then pay and submit. Recognition evidence is not an admission, employment, immigration or licensing guarantee.

Start with the decision, not the evaluator

Before searching for a credential service, write down the destination, intended use, recipient, programme or occupation, and deadline. Ask the recipient in writing whether it evaluates qualifications itself or requires a named authority or provider. Also ask whether it needs a basic statement, a course-by-course report, direct transcript delivery, verification from the issuer, certified translations, or a report sent directly to the recipient.

The United States Department of Education illustrates why this step matters. Its official page separates education institutions for study, employers for work, state licensing authorities for professional practice, and federal immigration authorities for visa matters. It says some recipients assess credentials themselves while others request a private evaluation. The Department does not perform evaluations or endorse an individual service.

PurposeFirst decision owner to confirmDo not assume
Admission or further studyThe receiving university, college or admissions systemThat an immigration or employment report will satisfy admission
Non-regulated employmentThe prospective employer or its stated evaluation authorityThat a recognition statement guarantees a role, grade or salary
ImmigrationThe named immigration programme and its designated providersThat a university's academic assessment is an immigration assessment
Licensed professionThe competent professional or statutory regulatorThat general degree recognition grants a licence to practise

Keep the recipient's reply or the current official instruction page with your application record. If the page names a provider, report type or direct-delivery route, use those exact instructions. A cheaper, faster or more familiar report is not useful if the recipient will not accept it.

Verify the awarding institution and programme first

A certificate title alone does not establish the status of the institution or programme when the award was earned. Find the higher-education regulator, qualifications authority or recognised-institution list for the country where the institution operated. Check the institution name, campus or delivery partner, programme, award level and relevant study dates where the source allows it.

Ghana's Tertiary Education Commission tells applicants to check its accredited, unaccredited and unrecognised institution lists or contact GTEC. Nigeria's National Universities Commission publishes separate official lists for federal universities, state universities, private universities and approved affiliations. Use the regulator responsible for the actual institution type. A university list may not cover a polytechnic, professional college or foreign branch campus.

Record the official page URL and the date checked. Save a neutral note rather than copying sensitive student data into a shared tracker. If the institution changed name, merged, closed, or used an affiliated college, gather the official history and ask the receiving authority what proof it accepts. Do not infer recognition from a logo, social-media profile, marketing brochure or the fact that other graduates say they were accepted.

Institution recognition and individual qualification evaluation are related but not identical. Kenya's CUE guidance, for example, can distinguish an institution's recognised status from the equation of a particular qualification. The evaluator may consider the institution, entry requirements, programme level, study duration, credits, transcripts and verification from the issuer.

Use the official route for the destination

National processes are not interchangeable. These examples show how the owner and evidence change across African destinations. They are starting points, not a complete list of every sector or profession.

Nigeria: authentication and evaluation are different services

The Federal Ministry of Education's Evaluation and Accreditation System separates authentication of Nigerian qualifications for use abroad from evaluation of foreign qualifications for use in Nigeria. The portal also covers eligibility or proficiency letters. Its current guidance says that where an application requires an institutional transcript, the awarding institution must send the official transcript directly to the service.

Select the purpose that matches the actual use. Do not submit a foreign award under the Nigerian-certificate authentication route or treat a Ministry result as a substitute for a professional regulator's decision. Confirm any additional NYSC, employer, institution or licensing requirements with that owner.

Kenya: prepare certified evidence and purpose documents

Kenya's CUE Recognition and Equation of Qualifications portal covers qualifications awarded by foreign universities and institutions. Its current pre-application page lists certificates, academic transcripts, identity evidence and a declaration. It distinguishes certification for Kenyan citizens from notarisation for foreign citizens and asks foreign applicants for purpose evidence such as a provisional admission letter or an employment invitation, together with relevant immigration documents.

Read the current portal instructions before preparing copies or making payment. Certification, notarisation, translation and identity rules are formal requirements, not formatting preferences. If names differ across documents, CUE lists a sworn affidavit among the evidence to prepare.

Ghana: use GTEC's credential service and accreditation checks

GTEC's site directs applicants to its Credential Evaluation Management System and says applicants should have copies of the certificate and transcript. The CEMS information page explains that evaluation is case by case, may involve verification, and does not determine every course studied, professional rank or workplace grade. It also says non-English documents must be accompanied by certified English translations and copies of the source documents.

Confirm the current submission and delivery method on GTEC's live pages before acting. Do not rely on an old fee screenshot or a third-party agent's timeline. GTEC notes that verification and unavailable information can delay a case, so build time for issuer responses into the plan.

South Africa: SAQA evaluation may be only one layer

SAQA evaluates foreign qualifications within the South African National Qualifications Framework. Its current page says only qualification holders may apply and that completed evaluations are issued electronically. It also displays revised tariff information effective from April 2026. Check the live SAQA page for the current application, document and payment rules rather than copying a rate into a long-lived checklist.

A SAQA result does not remove profession-specific steps. South Africa's Department of Basic Education shows this clearly for teaching: a person with foreign qualifications may need SAQA evaluation, a separate employment-in-education evaluation, and registration with the South African Council for Educators. Other regulated professions have their own competent authorities and requirements.

Build the evidence packet the owner actually requests

Do not upload every academic document you possess. Build a controlled checklist from the official instructions and mark each item as available, requested from the issuer, awaiting certification, awaiting translation, or delivered. The exact packet varies, but recognition systems commonly investigate the identity of the holder, the final award, the academic record, the institution and the route by which evidence can be verified.

Evidence areaWhat to verify before submission
IdentityName, date of birth where requested, current identity document, and formal evidence for any name difference

Academic recordOfficial transcript, subjects or modules, grades, credits and completion status required for the report type

Ask the registrar or records office how it sends transcripts and verification responses. Some authorities will not accept a transcript forwarded by the applicant, even if the file looks official. Do not open, repackage or redirect a sealed or institution-sent record in a way that breaks the required chain.

If the award is provisional, the programme was not completed, or the institution cannot verify the record, disclose that status and ask the evaluator what alternative evidence is permitted. Do not alter dates, marks, seals or names to make a file match an expected format. A cleaner scan cannot cure missing authority.

Resolve names, translations and document quality early

Compare the name on the certificate, transcript, identity document and application account. Differences caused by marriage, spelling, transliteration or order of names can trigger a request for evidence. Follow the authority's specific route for a name-change certificate, affidavit, statutory declaration or other proof. Keep both the former and current name visible where the official form requests them.

For documents not in the accepted language, confirm who may translate them and whether certification, notarisation or an embassy stamp is required. Do not translate only the convenient parts. Seals, annotations, grading keys and reverse pages may be relevant to the evaluator. Attach the source-language document when required so the translation can be traced.

Check every scan at normal size and high zoom. Confirm complete edges, page order, colour where seals depend on it, and readable signatures, stamps and security features. Keep an untouched original and prepare a submission copy. If the portal sets a file limit, use the application PDF packet guide to organise pages and the local PDF compressor only after confirming that compression preserves legibility.

Keep academic recognition and professional licensing separate

A recognition statement can help describe the level or status of an academic qualification. It does not automatically confirm competence to work as a doctor, nurse, teacher, engineer, lawyer, accountant or another regulated professional. The professional authority may assess curriculum content, supervised practice, examinations, language, good standing, registration history or local law separately.

Write two lines in the plan when the occupation is regulated: one for academic recognition and one for professional registration. Identify the owner, application, evidence, fee, deadline and result for each. Ask the regulator whether it accepts the academic report you are considering before ordering it.

The same separation applies to immigration. An immigration credential assessment may serve a points or eligibility rule without deciding university admission or a licence. Keep each report labelled by purpose so it is not reused beyond the recipient's stated acceptance.

Understand what the Addis Convention does and does not do

UNESCO describes the Addis Convention as a legal framework for fair and transparent recognition of higher-education qualifications in Africa. It was adopted in 2014 and entered into force in 2019. UNESCO's page records 14 states parties as of February 2025 and identifies an African network supporting implementation.

This regional framework supports mobility and cooperation, but it is not a universal instant-conversion table for every African award. National authorities, institutions and professional bodies still apply their mandates and procedures. Check whether the relevant country is a party and which national structure handles the actual request. Do not tell an employer or school that the Convention guarantees acceptance.

Control cost, timing and privacy

Build a dated budget from the official portal after the recipient confirms the route. Separate the evaluation fee, transcript charge, certification or notarisation, translation, courier or digital delivery, and any professional-registration cost. Do not copy a fee from an old blog, agent or screenshot. SAQA, CUE and GTEC all publish live service information that can change.

Work backwards from the recipient's deadline. Allow time for the awarding institution to locate records, send transcripts, answer verification questions and correct an incomplete submission. A paid express option, where offered, cannot guarantee that an outside institution will respond immediately.

Academic records and identity documents are sensitive. Use the official portal named by the authority, inspect the domain carefully, and avoid sending raw documents to an unverified agent, public chat or search form. Keep application references and status notes without duplicating full certificate numbers, identity numbers or transcript contents in shared trackers.

Final recognition checklist

  • Destination, purpose, recipient and deadline are written down.
  • The recipient confirmed the decision owner, accepted provider and report type.
  • The awarding institution and programme status were checked with the proper regulator.
  • Certificate, transcript and issuer-verification route match the current instructions.
  • Name differences and translations have the required official evidence.
  • Academic, immigration and professional decisions are tracked separately.
  • Fees and delivery charges came from current official pages.
  • Documents will travel only through the accepted secure route.
  • The application record contains dates and references, not unnecessary raw personal data.
  • No result is described as an admission, job, visa or licensing guarantee.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a credential evaluation guarantee university admission or a job?

No. An evaluation can provide evidence about a qualification, but the receiving university, employer, immigration programme or professional regulator makes its own decision.

Should I pay an evaluator before asking the recipient?

No. First ask which authority or provider, report type, document-delivery route and validity period the recipient accepts. A report from the wrong provider may not satisfy the application.

Are recognition and professional licensing the same process?

No. A recognition statement may be only one input. A regulated profession can require a separate regulator, examinations, supervised practice, registration or other evidence.

Can AfroTools decide what my degree is equivalent to?

No. The tool identifies likely decision owners and evidence gaps from broad selections. It does not inspect documents, verify institutions, convert grades or issue an equivalency verdict.

What if my certificate and identity document use different names?

Check the receiving authority's current instructions. Prepare the official name-change or sworn evidence it requires, and keep the original document trail intact.
