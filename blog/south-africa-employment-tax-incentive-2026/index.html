South Africa's Employment Tax Incentive, usually shortened to ETI, lets an eligible employer reduce the employees' tax it pays to SARS when the employer has qualifying employees and satisfies the monthly rules. It is not a deduction from the employee's wage, not a reduction of the PAYE shown on the employee's IRP5, and not a reason to relax minimum-wage or payroll controls.

Official sources reviewed: August 19, 2026. The review covered SARS's Guide to the Employment Tax Incentive, Issue 5, dated July 27, 2026, the SARS employer ETI operational guide, the SARS ETI topic page, the 2026 reconciliation validation guide and the official 2026 National Minimum Wage notice. The ETI topic page itself showed an August 19, 2026 update when checked. The guide is planning support for payroll teams and employers. It is not tax or labour-law advice, an ETI calculator, an EMP201 filing service or a binding SARS ruling.

ETI is a monthly test. A person who qualified last month can fail this month because of age, remuneration, hours, wage compliance, studying activity or another condition. An employer who was compliant last month can also be unable to reduce PAYE this month because a required return or tax debt is outstanding. Build the control around each payroll month rather than relying on a permanent employee flag.

1. Confirm That The Employer Is Eligible

SARS's Issue 5 guide starts with PAYE registration. The employer must be registered for purposes of withholding and paying employees' tax. A business cannot treat ETI as a substitute for registering as an employer. The payroll entity making the claim must be the entity that meets the employer rules and accounts for employees' tax.

Registration is not the only employer test. The ETI Act excludes the national, provincial and local spheres of government, listed public entities unless specifically designated, and municipal entities. An employer can also be disqualified because of employee displacement or other prescribed conditions. If the organisation has a public-sector connection or a complicated group structure, do not assume that a PAYE number proves ETI eligibility.

Tax compliance also matters at month-end. SARS Issue 5 says an employer cannot claim ETI for a month when it has an outstanding return or outstanding tax debt, subject to limited exceptions for qualifying payment arrangements, compromises, suspended debt and amounts below R100. The check covers all relevant tax types, not only PAYE. Review the SARS account before finalising the EMP201 rather than discovering a blocked claim during reconciliation.

2. Test Every Employee For The Month

The ordinary age rule requires the employee to be 18 to 29 at the end of the month in which ETI is claimed. SARS explains that a person can qualify in the month they turn 18 and ceases to qualify in the month they turn 30. A qualifying company operating in an approved special economic zone can have an age exception when the employee renders services mainly inside the qualifying SEZ. That exception is fact-specific and should not be applied merely because an employer is near an industrial zone.

The employee must possess an accepted identity document, asylum-seeker permit or refugee identity document. The employee must not be a connected person in relation to the employer and must not be a domestic worker as defined for this purpose. SARS notes that the domestic-worker exclusion does not include a farm worker. The employee must have been employed by the employer or an associated person on or after October 1, 2013.

The employee must also meet the current remuneration and studying conditions. Monthly remuneration must be less than R7,500. An employee who is mainly involved in studying during the month does not qualify unless the employer and employee have entered into a qualifying learning programme. SARS interprets "mainly" as more than 50% and says the comparison is based on actual hours spent studying and employed.

Only qualifying months belong in the employee's 24-month ETI history, but failing to claim an available amount does not preserve that month for later. SARS's example shows that months in which the employer was entitled to claim still count even when the employer omitted the claim. Months with associated employers also count. A group transfer cannot restart the first 12-month rate.

3. Pass The Wage And Hours Tests Before Using The Formula

An otherwise eligible employer cannot claim ETI for an employee paid below the applicable wage floor. Start with any binding wage-regulating measure, bargaining-council agreement or sector rule. Where the National Minimum Wage applies, the official 2026 notice sets the ordinary minimum at R30.23 per hour from March 1, 2026. Expanded Public Works Programme workers and qualifying learnerships have separate published amounts.

Do not mix wage and monthly remuneration. For the wage test, SARS focuses on money paid for ordinary hours. Overtime and many bonuses or allowances are generally not wage for that test. For the ETI calculation, monthly remuneration can include a broader set of cash amounts. One payroll export can therefore require two different classifications before the employee reaches the formula.

SARS Issue 5 analyses the wage rule employee by employee, while the SARS Budget 2026 FAQ warns in broader language that paying any employee below the National Minimum Wage can affect the employer's entire ETI claim for the month. The safe operational response is not to claim ETI for an underpaid employee, correct the wage issue, and obtain SARS or professional guidance before relying on the remainder of that month's claim. Do not resolve a source-sensitive compliance conflict by choosing the more convenient wording.

Hours matter when the employee is employed and paid for fewer than 160 hours. The equivalent monthly remuneration is grossed up to 160 hours to find the formula band, then the calculated ETI is apportioned back to the actual hours. Keep ordinary hours, overtime hours and the remuneration attached to each visible. SARS's Issue 5 worked examples show why counting only calendar days or net pay can produce the wrong result.

4. Calculate The Monthly ETI Amount

For the position on or after April 1, 2025, SARS Issue 5 publishes the following table. The higher amount applies during the first 12 qualifying months and the lower amount during the next 12 qualifying months. The 12-month groups need not be consecutive.

Monthly remunerationFirst 12 qualifying monthsNext 12 qualifying months
R0 to R2,499.9960% of monthly remuneration30% of monthly remuneration
R2,500 to R5,499.99R1,500R750
R5,500 to R7,499.99R1,500 minus 75% of the amount above R5,500R750 minus 37.5% of the amount above R5,500

A formula check using the published table shows the taper. At R6,000 monthly remuneration in the first 12 qualifying months, the amount is R1,500 minus 75% of R500, which equals R1,125. In the next 12 qualifying months, the amount is R750 minus 37.5% of R500, which equals R562.50. This arithmetic does not prove that the employer or employee qualifies. All earlier tests must pass first.

SARS also provides a worked EMP201 example. Its employer had total employees' tax of R32,800 and valid ETI of R3,500 for the month. The amount paid to SARS was R29,300. The employees' remuneration and employees' tax records remained unchanged by ETI. This demonstrates that ETI reduces the employer's total monthly PAYE payment, not the employee's PAYE deduction.

5. Claim ETI Through EMP201 And Reconcile It

Build an employee-level schedule before completing EMP201. For each claimed person, record identity evidence, age, start date, associated-employer history, qualifying-month number, ordinary and overtime hours, wage test, monthly remuneration, formula band, calculated amount and reviewer approval. The sum of valid employee amounts becomes the current-month ETI figure, subject to the PAYE and roll-over rules.

The EMP201 return and payment must reach SARS no later than seven days after month-end. If the seventh is a Saturday, Sunday or public holiday, the deadline moves to the last business day before it. Retain the EMP201 confirmation, payment reference number, payment proof and SARS account view with the employee schedule.

ETI cannot reduce net PAYE below zero. SARS allows qualifying excess amounts to roll forward in specified circumstances, but unclaimed or rolled amounts face reconciliation-period limits. The key checkpoints are the six-month reconciliation periods ending August and February. SARS Issue 5 says unclaimed ETI must be addressed by the last month of the relevant reconciliation period or the amount can be forfeited under the statutory rules.

Reconcile ETI during EMP501 work instead of treating it as a separate spreadsheet. Use the South Africa EMP501 reconciliation guide to connect monthly EMP201 values, payments and employee certificates. SARS's 2026 validation guide warns that overstated ETI can attract an understatement penalty and that misuse can attract an additional penalty.

6. Keep An Audit-Ready ETI Evidence Pack

SARS Issue 5 says records, books of account and documents relating to ETI claims must be retained for five years from the return submission date. A final spreadsheet total is not enough. The file should allow a reviewer to reproduce eligibility and the calculation for every employee and month.

Protect employee records. Identity documents, payroll data and wages are sensitive personal information. Limit access to payroll roles, avoid putting raw employee data in analytics or shared URLs, and use synthetic information for testing.
