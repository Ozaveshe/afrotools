Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Warehouse Receipt System (WRS)?

A warehouse receipt records stored goods and may support financing when a lender accepts it as collateral. Confirm the warehouse, receipt, goods, insurance and lender requirements before committing.

How much can I borrow against my grain?

Use the loan-to-value percentage in your lender’s written offer. The calculator multiplies that percentage by the entered harvest value. Its undated presets are illustrative, not approved loan offers or promised payment times.

Where can I check warehouse receipt arrangements?

Start with the relevant regulator, warehouse operator and lender. Kenya’s Warehouse Receipt System Council describes receipt-based financing; AFEX describes collateral management using electronic warehouse receipts. These references do not verify this calculator’s numeric presets or your eligibility.

What happens if grain prices fall during storage?

Enter a negative price change to explore a fall. The final cash balance deducts costs and loan principal; a negative balance indicates a modeled shortfall. Price gains are uncertain, and unentered losses, taxes and other fees are excluded.

What is the minimum grain quantity I can deposit?

Ask the selected warehouse for its current minimum quantity, quality requirements and aggregation rules. The calculator accepts positive quantities for planning and does not verify warehouse acceptance.

What Is Warehouse Receipt Financing?

Warehouse Receipt Financing (WRF) can help African farmers compare immediate sale against stored-grain financing. The system allows you to deposit grain at harvest and receive an official warehouse receipt. You may take that receipt to a bank as collateral and receive a loan based on a share of the grain's value. Later, you can compare the sale proceeds against loan interest, storage, insurance, quality loss, price risk and market access costs. Results are projections, not guaranteed profit.
