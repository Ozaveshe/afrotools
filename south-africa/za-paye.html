South Africa PAYE Tax Guide 2026/27

    

    

      

        

          
South Africa’s Pay As You Earn (PAYE) system is administered by the South African Revenue Service (SARS) and applies to all individuals earning remuneration in South Africa. Employers are required to register as PAYE agents and deduct income tax monthly using SARS tax tables, remitting the amounts by the 7th of the following month via SARS eFiling or EasyFile. The tax year runs from 1 March to 28/29 February.

        

        

          
The 2026/27 tax year uses seven progressive SARS tax brackets ranging from 18% to 45%. Only the income inside each band is taxed at that band’s rate. Three rebates reduce calculated tax: the primary rebate (R17,820 for all taxpayers), secondary rebate (R9,765 for those 65+), and tertiary rebate (R3,249 for those 75+). These rebates create a tax-free threshold of R99,000 for taxpayers under 65.

        

        

          
Beyond income tax, employees contribute 1% of remuneration to UIF (Unemployment Insurance Fund), capped at a monthly earnings ceiling of R17,712. Employers match this contribution and, if liable, also pay SDL (Skills Development Levy) at 1% of the applicable remuneration balance after allowable deductions. This salary-only estimate subtracts the modelled allowable retirement deduction; it does not determine liability or other remuneration exclusions and donations. Neither SDL nor the employer’s UIF share is deducted from the employee’s salary. Retirement fund contributions to approved pension, provident, or retirement annuity funds are tax-deductible up to 27.5% of the greater of remuneration or taxable income, capped at R430,000 per year.

        

        

          
Medical Scheme Fees Tax Credits provide a direct reduction in tax payable: R376 per month for the main member, R376 for the first dependant, and R254 for each additional dependant. South Africa also offers an Additional Medical Expenses Tax Credit for out-of-pocket medical costs exceeding a threshold. For cross-country payroll checks, compare with the Kenya PAYE calculator, Tanzania TRA PAYE calculator, or Africa PAYE calculator hub.

      South Africa Tax FAQ
      
Common PAYE Questions

    

    

      

        

          What are the SARS income tax brackets for 2026/2027?
          
Seven progressive bands apply: 18% up to R245,100; 26% from R245,101 to R383,100; 31% from R383,101 to R530,200; 36% from R530,201 to R695,800; 39% from R695,801 to R887,000; 41% from R887,001 to R1,878,600; and 45% above R1,878,600. The primary rebate of R17,820 effectively makes income below R99,000 tax-free for taxpayers under 65.

        

        

          How much can I deduct for retirement contributions?
          
Contributions to approved pension funds, provident funds, and retirement annuity (RA) funds are deductible up to 27.5% of the greater of your remuneration or taxable income, with an annual rand cap of R430,000. This directly reduces your taxable income before PAYE is applied. This salary-only model does not cover every income adjustment or section 11F limit.

        

        

          How is UIF calculated in South Africa?
          
Employee UIF is 1% of gross salary, and the employer also contributes 1%. The monthly earnings ceiling for 2026/27 is R17,712. If your gross salary exceeds this, UIF is capped at R177.12 per month as the employee contribution. UIF funds temporary unemployment, maternity, illness, and death benefits.

        

        

          What are medical tax credits and how do they work?
          
Medical Scheme Fees Tax Credits (MTC) are fixed monthly amounts deducted directly from your PAYE tax payable, not from your income. For 2026/27 the credits are R376 per month for the main member, R376 for the first dependant, and R254 for each additional dependant. Your employer applies these automatically if you contribute via payroll.

        

        

          What is the difference between SDL and UIF for employers?
          
Both are employer obligations. UIF is 1% of each employee's remuneration (up to the R17,712 ceiling), paid to the Department of Employment and Labour. SDL (Skills Development Levy) is 1% of the applicable remuneration balance after allowable deductions, paid to SARS for workplace training. The employer UIF share and SDL are employer costs; the separate employee UIF contribution is deducted from salary. This salary-only estimate assumes the employer is liable and subtracts only the modelled allowable retirement deduction. Other remuneration exclusions and donations are not modelled; it is not a complete employer-cost or compliance assessment.

        

      

      

        

          Do I need to file a tax return in South Africa?
          
If your employer deducts PAYE and you have only one income source below R500,000, you may qualify for auto-assessment and not need to file. You must file if you have other income (rental, freelance, investments), want to claim deductions not applied by your employer, or received a tax directive. Check the filing instructions and assessment notices issued by SARS. This calculator does not determine whether you must file.

        

        

          What are the primary, secondary, and tertiary rebates?
          
Tax rebates reduce your calculated PAYE directly. For 2026/27: primary rebate is R17,820 (all taxpayers), secondary rebate is R9,765 (age 65+), and tertiary rebate is R3,249 (age 75+). These mean the tax-free threshold is R99,000 for under-65s, R153,250 for 65–74, and R171,300 for 75+. Rebates are applied automatically by your employer.

        

        

          What is the tax-free threshold in South Africa?
          
Due to the primary rebate of R17,820, individuals under 65 effectively pay no tax on income below R99,000 per year (approximately R8,250/month). For those aged 65–74, the threshold rises to R153,250 thanks to the secondary rebate. For those 75 and older, it increases further to R171,300 with the tertiary rebate.

        

        

          How does the two-pot retirement system affect my tax?
          
From September 2024, retirement fund contributions are split into a savings pot (one-third) and a retirement pot (two-thirds). Withdrawals from the savings pot before retirement are taxed as income at your marginal rate. R2,000 is the minimum withdrawal, not a tax-free allowance. SARS applies the relevant marginal-rate tax directive; the final assessment depends on your circumstances.

        

        

          Can I claim travel allowance deductions?
          
For employees’ tax, 80% of a travel allowance is normally included in remuneration. If the employer is satisfied that at least 80% of vehicle use during the tax year will be for business, only 20% of the allowance is included. A logbook and the applicable conditions matter for the final assessment. The 2027 SARS guide lists a simplified rate of R4.95/km; this calculator does not calculate travel allowances or determine eligibility. Check the current SARS travel-allowance guidance.

      Why is my marginal rate not my real tax burden?
      
Because SARS taxes income progressively and then applies rebates and credits that change the effective rate.

Calculation methodology

          
The calculator annualizes salary where needed, applies modeled employee statutory deductions, runs taxable income through the country PAYE bands, and derives net pay from gross pay minus modeled PAYE and statutory deductions. Employer-cost lines are informational where the page exposes them.

  • Informational estimate only. It is not professional tax, legal, payroll, or filing advice.
