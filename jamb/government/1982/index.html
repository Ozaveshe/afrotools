Question 1

Universal Suffrage means

  1. Adult participation in politics
  2. giving voting right to women above the age of 21
  3. property ownership
  4. citizens who understand why they vote
  5. the right of qualified adults to vote
Answer and explanation

E: the right of qualified adults to vote

Universal suffrage is the right of eligible adults to vote without property, sex or similar exclusions. It concerns voting rights rather than all forms of political participation.

Question 2

In the Parliamentary System of government executive powers are vested in

  1. The President
  2. Parliament
  3. the Party in government
  4. the Cabinet
  5. a few powerful men in the country
Answer and explanation

D: the Cabinet

In the parliamentary model, the cabinet led by the prime minister exercises executive power. Parliament holds the cabinet accountable, but does not itself conduct day-to-day executive administration.

Question 3

In a federal system, powers that are allocated to both Central and State governments are called

  1. Exclusive
  2. Concurrent
  3. Arbitrary
  4. Copyrights
  5. Residual
Answer and explanation

B: Concurrent

Concurrent powers are assigned to both central and state governments within a federation. Exclusive powers belong to one level, while residual powers concern matters not expressly allocated.

Question 4

In a democracy, political power is exercised by

  1. the majority
  2. the literate public
  3. the few wealthy
  4. one person
  5. the monarchy
Answer and explanation

A: the majority

Democratic decisions commonly follow majority choice while preserving political participation and rights. Among these options, majority rule contrasts with control by wealth, literacy, one ruler or a monarchy.

Question 5

The purpose of registration before elections is to

  1. know the total population of a country
  2. know the names of all taxable adults
  3. know the number of people that support each political party
  4. Guard against fraud at elections
  5. identify citizens and non-citizens of a state
Answer and explanation

D: Guard against fraud at elections

Voter registration establishes a list of eligible voters and helps prevent impersonation or multiple voting. It serves electoral integrity rather than counting taxpayers or party supporters.

Question 6

  1. the head of state
  2. selected from the upper chamber
  3. a graduate of a university
  4. the prime minister
  5. elected by the whole country

The prime minister is normally the head of government in a parliamentary system. The office is distinct from the head of state and is tied to legislative confidence.

Question 7

In the presidential system in Nigeria the Chief Executive remains in office for

  1. a period of eight years
  2. a fixed term
  3. as long as he enjoys popular support
  4. two terms
  5. a period of six years
Answer and explanation

B: a fixed term

A presidential term has a constitutionally fixed duration. Popular support alone does not extend the term, and eligibility for another term is distinct from the duration of the current one.

Question 8

The idea of democracy as a system of government was inherited from

  1. the USA
  2. Italy
  3. England
  4. Greece
  5. China
Answer and explanation

D: Greece

The term democracy and an early influential form of democratic government come from ancient Greece, especially Athens. Ancient participation was restricted and differed greatly from modern universal suffrage.

Question 9

We say that a constitution is rigid if

  1. the sources are too many
  2. the constitution is foreign
  3. it has no chapter on human rights
  4. the provisions are too detailed
  5. the process of changing it is too cumbersome
Answer and explanation

E: the process of changing it is too cumbersome

A rigid constitution requires a more demanding amendment process than ordinary legislation. The defining feature is the special process for change, not the number of sources or amount of detail.

Question 11

Anonymity of the civil servant means that civil servants must

  1. serve any government impartially
  2. be politically neutral
  3. have job security
  4. avoid nepotism and favouritism
  5. not receive the credit or blame for any good or bad policy
Answer and explanation

E: not receive the credit or blame for any good or bad policy

Civil-service anonymity means officials normally work behind the public political leadership. Ministers publicly take responsibility for departmental policy, rather than officials seeking personal political credit.

Question 16

In the British parliamentary system, if parliament passes a vote of no confidence on the government, the Prime Minister

  1. must change some ministers
  2. can ask the Queen to resign
  3. can dismiss some top civil servants
  4. and his cabinet may feel obliged to resign
  5. can Dissolve the opposition party
Answer and explanation

D: and his cabinet may feel obliged to resign

A British government depends on parliamentary confidence. Defeat on a confidence vote can require the prime minister and cabinet to resign or seek a constitutional route to a new election.

Question 18

An unwritten constitution is the one where

  1. no part of the constitution is ever written down
  2. the whole constitution is not contained in any one document
  3. the constitution is based on the wishes of the prime minister
  4. the constitution can be amended by a simple majority
Answer and explanation

B: the whole constitution is not contained in any one document

An unwritten or uncodified constitution is not consolidated into one document. Many of its rules may nevertheless be written in statutes and judicial decisions.

Question 21

The idea of putting everyone under two levels of government at the same time is called

  1. dual policy
  2. dual mandate
  3. dual citizenship
  4. federalism
  5. nationalism
Answer and explanation

D: federalism

Federalism divides governmental authority between a central government and constituent governments. Citizens are consequently subject to both levels within their respective constitutional powers.

Question 22

  2. the predominance of the public sector in the economy
  3. the elimination of all forms of competition
  4. the rule of the few
  5. the entrenchment of social rights
Answer and explanation

A: that the major factors of production are privately owned

Private ownership of productive assets is a defining feature of capitalism. State ownership dominating production would instead describe a different economic arrangement.

Question 23

A Federal system of government is the one in which

  1. power is concentrated in the hands of one of the governments
  2. Subordinate units of government exercise sovereign power
  3. the state governors can sue the central government
  4. power is shared between the central government and local governments
  5. power is shared between the central and state governments
Answer and explanation

E: power is shared between the central and state governments

Federalism divides constitutional powers between the central government and constituent states. Neither level simply holds all governmental authority over the other.
