Question 2

The process by which political beliefs and values are transmitted from one generation to another is best known as political

  1. socialization
  2. culture
  3. training
  4. decentralization
Answer and explanation

A: socialization

Political socialization is the process of learning and passing on political beliefs, values and attitudes. Families, schools, peers and public institutions can all contribute to it.

Question 3

The fusion of the executive and legislative organs of government is associated with

  1. monarchy
  2. the presidential system
  3. fascism
  4. the parliamentary system
Answer and explanation

D: the parliamentary system

In a parliamentary system, the executive is drawn from and accountable to the legislature. This institutional connection is described as a fusion of executive and legislative powers.

Question 4

One criticism of delegated legislation is that it

  1. subjects’ citizens to executive decrees
  2. makes laws too flexible
  3. makes laws too technical
  4. is not useful in emergency situation
Answer and explanation

A: subjects’ citizens to executive decrees

Delegated lawmaking gives the executive authority to make detailed rules. A criticism is that citizens can become subject to executive-made rules with less direct legislative debate.

Question 6

Which of the following defines a constitution?

  1. Any written legal document
  2. Basic laws for the conduct of modern government
  3. Rules adopted in legislative proceedings
  4. The doctrine that limits the power of government
Answer and explanation

B: Basic laws for the conduct of modern government

A constitution sets out the basic rules for governing a political community. Ordinary legal documents or rules used only inside a legislature are narrower than a constitution.

Question 7

Which of the following ideologies advocates governmental control over all sources of power?

  1. totalitarianism
  2. feudalism
  3. liberation
  4. democracy
Answer and explanation

A: totalitarianism

Totalitarianism seeks comprehensive state control over political and social life, including competing centres of power. Democracy instead permits opposition and limits governmental authority.

Question 8

Which of the following is NOT an acceptable means of achieving democracy?

  1. referendum
  2. recall
  3. initiative
  4. riots
Answer and explanation

D: riots

Referendum, recall and initiative are formal mechanisms for public participation in political decisions. Riots are violent disorder and are not a constitutional democratic decision-making procedure.

Question 11

Law making under military regimes is done through

  1. delegated legislation
  2. administrative enactments
  3. the promulgation of decrees
  4. the ministry of justice
Answer and explanation

C: the promulgation of decrees

Military governments in the Nigerian historical context enacted laws through decrees at the national level. This bypassed the ordinary elected legislative process.

Question 14

Electoral colleges have been criticized because they

  1. are very expensive
  2. create more seats in the legislature
  3. make the legislature too responsive
  4. sometimes negate the wishes of the electorate
Answer and explanation

D: sometimes negate the wishes of the electorate

An electoral-college outcome can differ from the preference expressed by the largest number of individual voters. That possible divergence is a common criticism of indirect electoral arrangements.

Question 15

Universal adult suffrage is usually restricted in most political systems to

  1. those with landed property
  2. literates
  3. citizens
  4. those not more than 70 years old
Answer and explanation

C: citizens

Universal adult suffrage usually concerns adult citizens subject to lawful eligibility requirements. It does not require property ownership or literacy.

Question 16

An election in which citizens are called upon to vote for or against a specified issue is called a

  1. mandate
  2. referendum
  3. by election
  4. runoff election
Answer and explanation

B: referendum

A referendum asks voters to decide directly on a specified public issue. A by-election fills a vacancy, while a runoff chooses between electoral candidates.

Question 18

The primary function of a pressure group is to

  1. win and control governmental power
  2. elect their officials
  3. influence government policy
  4. organize worker to confront government
Answer and explanation

C: influence government policy

Pressure groups primarily seek to influence government policy on issues important to their members. Unlike political parties, their defining aim is not to win and exercise governmental power.

Question 19

The biased presentation of information in order to influence public opinion is referred to as

  1. mobilization
  2. propaganda
  3. indoctrination
  4. distortion
Answer and explanation

B: propaganda

Propaganda presents information selectively or with bias to shape beliefs and public opinion. It aims to persuade rather than provide a balanced account of the available evidence.

Question 25

Dual mandate was proposed by

  1. Hugh Clapperton
  2. Hugh Clifford
  3. Lord Lugard
  4. George Goldie
Answer and explanation

C: Lord Lugard

Lord Lugard set out the dual-mandate doctrine in his book The Dual Mandate in British Tropical Africa. The doctrine framed colonial rule in terms of claimed obligations to both Africans and the colonial power.

Question 31

By statute, the main responsibility of the armed forces of Nigeria is to

  1. form a military government
  2. maintain law and order
  3. defend the sovereignty and territorial integrity of the country
  4. take part in peacekeeping operations in troubled neighbouring countries
Answer and explanation

The armed forces' central defence responsibility concerns Nigeria's sovereignty and territorial integrity. Other authorized duties do not turn military takeover into a lawful constitutional function.

Question 35

The primary factor which led to the formation of political parties in colonial Nigeria was

  1. introduction of the elective principle and representative institution
  2. increased wealth of the indigenous elite
  3. granting of seif government by the colonial administration
  4. call by black people all over the world for a final assault on colonialism
Answer and explanation

A: introduction of the elective principle and representative institution

The elective principle created seats that organized parties could contest. This electoral opportunity directly encouraged party formation before the later grant of self-government.

Question 37

  1. the figures were unacceptable to the U.N.B
  2. the figures were controversial
  3. Nigerians were contented with the existing population figures
  4. there were preparations for a fresh census
Answer and explanation

The 1973 census figures became politically controversial and were eventually rejected. The dispute concerned the credibility and distribution of the population totals rather than satisfaction with the old figures.

Question 40

Local governments were created in Nigeria mainly to

  1. realise the objective of full employment
  2. bring government nearer to the people
  3. prevent the creation of more states
  4. encourage competition and rivalry amongst ethnic groups
Answer and explanation

B: bring government nearer to the people

Local government brings public administration and services closer to residents. This provides a means for addressing community needs and participation at a smaller geographical scale.
