Question 2

The essence of ECOWAS is to________

  1. bring political parties together
  2. foster interstate economic and political co-operation
  3. emerging political event
  4. expansion of scopes and powers
Answer and explanation

B: foster interstate economic and political co-operation

ECOWAS was established to advance cooperation and integration among West African states. Economic cooperation is central, with political cooperation supporting shared regional objectives.

Question 3

The head of government under the 1979 Constitution is the _____

  1. president
  2. prime minister
  3. speaker of the house of representatives
  4. senate president
Answer and explanation

A: president

The 1979 Constitution introduced the presidential system in Nigeria. The president combined the roles of head of state and head of government, rather than serving alongside a prime minister.

  1. impartiality
  2. neutrality

Question 8

One major merit of the Unitary system of government is that_______?

  1. is most democratic
  2. is cheaper to operate
  3. eradicates co-operation
  4. eliminate tyranny
Answer and explanation

B: is cheaper to operate

A unitary structure can reduce the cost of maintaining multiple autonomous tiers with separate constitutional institutions. Lower administrative cost is a possible advantage, not a guarantee of democracy or freedom from tyranny.

Question 9

The legal right to exercise power in a state is known as______

  1. Authority
  2. Sovereignty
  3. Coercion
  4. Influence
Answer and explanation

A: Authority

Authority is the legitimate or legally recognized right to exercise power. Coercion is the use of force or threats, while influence can operate without a formal legal entitlement.

Question 18

Which of the following emphasizes the rule of law and human right protection?

  1. constitutionalism
  2. delegated legislation
  3. decentralization
  4. centralization
Answer and explanation

A: constitutionalism

Constitutionalism requires government to operate within constitutional limits and respect protected rights. Merely concentrating or decentralizing administrative power does not establish these safeguards.

Question 22

The organ of the UN that served as custodial of some African countries prior to their independence is _________?

  1. The international court of justice
  2. the security council
  3. the trusteeship council
  4. the general assembly
Answer and explanation

The Trusteeship Council supervised the administration of UN trust territories and their progress toward self-government or independence. It did not itself serve as the government of every African colony.

Question 33

The elective principle was introduced by which constitution?

  1. Macpherson Constitution of 1951
  2. Clifford Constitution of 1922
  3. Richard Constitution of 1946
  4. Lyttleton Constitution of 1954
Answer and explanation

The Clifford Constitution of 1922 introduced the elective principle for a limited number of legislative council seats. The resulting franchise was restricted rather than universal.

Question 34

The primary reason for the establishment of political parties is to _______?

  1. Attain political power
  2. protect political interest
  3. make law
  4. lobby the government
Answer and explanation

A: Attain political power

Political parties organize to obtain and exercise political power, normally by contesting elections. Lobbying is more characteristic of groups seeking influence without forming government.

Question 37

Which of the following is a process of military disengagement from Nigeria politics?

  1. Increase in military budget
  2. conduct of free and fair elections
  3. Appointment of Army officers into political post
  4. strengthening the Army
Answer and explanation

B: conduct of free and fair elections

Free and fair elections provide a route for transferring political authority from military rulers to elected civilian representatives. Appointing soldiers to political posts would instead continue military involvement.

Question 38

Division of responsibilities into distinct branches to limit any branches from exercising the core functions of another is _______?

  1. fusion of power
  2. parliamentary system
  3. aristocracy
  4. separation of powers
Answer and explanation

D: separation of powers

Separation of powers allocates core governmental responsibilities to distinct branches. Its purpose includes preventing one branch from concentrating all legislative, executive and judicial authority.
