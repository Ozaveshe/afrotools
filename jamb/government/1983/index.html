Question 1

Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of the parliamentary system of government?

  1. Ministers are usually members of parliament
  2. The Prime Minister is politically responsible to parliament
  3. The Head of State is the most powerful organ of government
  4. The Head of Government may advise the Head of State to dissolve parliament
  5. The party in opposition provides the shadow government
Answer and explanation

C: The Head of State is the most powerful organ of government

In a parliamentary system, the head of government leads the executive and is accountable to parliament. The head of state normally has a distinct, largely ceremonial role.

Question 2

Capitalism is an economic system in which

  1. the economy of the State is centrally planned, and controlled
  2. private persons are permitted to undertake enterprises
  3. accumulation of private property is forbidden
  4. the means of production are owned and controlled by the state
  5. all big industries and the land are publicly owned for the common good
Answer and explanation

B: private persons are permitted to undertake enterprises

Capitalism permits private ownership and enterprise, with production decisions substantially coordinated through markets. Central state ownership of all major resources describes a different economic model.

Question 3

The process of depriving person of the right of voting is called

  1. Enfranchisement
  2. Disqualification
  3. Dismissal
  4. Prohibition
  5. Disenfranchisement
Answer and explanation

E: Disenfranchisement

Disenfranchisement means depriving someone of the right to vote. Enfranchisement is the opposite process: granting or extending voting rights.

Question 4

Bicameralism refers to

  1. a one chamber legislature
  2. the process of voting in the legislature
  3. the upper chamber in a legislature
  4. two-chamber legislature
  5. legislatures in all sovereign states
Answer and explanation

D: two-chamber legislature

Bicameralism means that the legislature has two chambers or houses. It describes the structure of the whole legislature, not just its upper chamber.

Question 5

The principle of, the separation of powers implies that the three main organs of government work

  1. separately
  2. independently and co-operatively
  3. against one another
  4. reluctantly and gradually for the executive
  5. together in the interest of other nations
Answer and explanation

B: independently and co-operatively

Separation of powers assigns distinct responsibilities to the branches while allowing checks and cooperation. It does not require hostile or completely isolated institutions.

Question 6

The main function of the judiciary is to

  1. serve as the watchdog of the executive
  2. enact laws
  3. execute the laws of the land
  4. interpret the laws
  5. protect the interest of accused persons
Answer and explanation

D: interpret the laws

The judiciary interprets and applies laws in deciding disputes. Enacting legislation and implementing government policy belong primarily to the legislature and executive respectively.

Question 8

An unwritten constitution operates in

  1. Guinea
  2. USA
  3. Great Britain
  4. China
  5. Nigeria
Answer and explanation

C: Great Britain

Great Britain forms part of the United Kingdom, whose constitution is uncodified. Its constitutional rules are drawn from several written and unwritten sources rather than one comprehensive text.

Question 11

The primary function of a legislature is

  1. appointing a president
  2. law making
  3. vetoing bills
  4. monitoring the judiciary
  5. re-assigning civil servants
Answer and explanation

B: law making

The primary function of the legislature is law-making. It may also scrutinize government and approve spending, but appointing a president or reassigning officials is not its defining task.

Question 12

According to Marxist theory, those who own and control the means of production in a capitalist society are

  1. Exploiters
  2. Colonialists
  3. Workers
  4. Shareholders
  5. Bourgeoisie
Answer and explanation

E: Bourgeoisie

In Marxist theory the bourgeoisie own and control the means of production. Workers who depend on selling labour power form the proletariat.

Question 13

While political parties aim at forming a government, pressure groups aim at

  1. imposing military rule
  2. causing social unrest
  3. influencing governmental decisions
  4. controlling a nation’s economy
  5. getting all workers to unite
Answer and explanation

C: influencing governmental decisions

Pressure groups seek to influence governmental decisions in support of interests or causes. Political parties instead seek office and the opportunity to form a government.

Question 14

When the electorate vote for representatives who in turn vote on their behalf we say it is

  1. an indirect election
  2. an unfair election
  3. a rigged election
  4. a disputed election
  5. a biased election
Answer and explanation

A: an indirect election

An indirect election uses an intermediate body of representatives who vote on behalf of the original voters. Direct election instead allows voters to choose the final office-holder themselves.
