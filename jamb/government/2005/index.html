Question 2

Nigeria supports the South-South cooperation as a foreign policy objective for mutual understanding among

  1. nations south of the equator
  2. sub-Saharan African countries
  3. developing countries
  4. Asian and sub-Saharan African countries
Answer and explanation

C: developing countries

South-South cooperation refers to collaboration among developing countries for shared development objectives. It is not limited to countries geographically south of the equator.

Question 4

The Nigerian-Cameroon crisis over the Bakassi Peninsula occurred owing to the interpretation of the treaty of 1913 and the

  1. resolution of the OAU
  2. Maroua Accord
  3. decision of the ECOWAS Tribunal
  4. decision of the International Court of Justice
Answer and explanation

B: Maroua Accord

The dispute involved the 1913 Anglo-German boundary agreement and the 1975 Maroua Declaration on the maritime boundary. The International Court later examined these instruments in its judgment.

Question 13

Nigeria's voting at the United Nations is guided primarily by

  1. her concern for Africa
  2. her national interests
  3. the cold war
  4. world peace
Answer and explanation

B: her national interests

A states national interest is the broad framework guiding its diplomatic choices, including UN votes. Regional concerns and peace objectives may form parts of that wider interest.

Question 15

  1. Lyttleton Constitution
  2. Independence Constitution
  3. Macpherson Constitution
  4. Clifford Constitution
Answer and explanation

A: Lyttleton Constitution

The 1954 Lyttleton Constitution established Nigerias fully federal constitutional structure. It preceded the Independence Constitution of 1960.

Question 20

The head of the Nigerian judiciary is the

  1. Solicitor-General of the Federation
  2. President of the Court of Appeal
  3. Chief Justice of Nigeria
  4. Attorney-General and Minister of Justice
Answer and explanation

C: Chief Justice of Nigeria

The Chief Justice of Nigeria heads the Supreme Court and chairs the National Judicial Council. The Attorney-General is an executive law officer, not the head of the judiciary.

Question 22

Under the 1999 Constitution, the appointment of ministers and ambassadors must be ratified by the

  1. National Council of States
  2. Presidency
  3. Senate
  4. House of Representatives
Answer and explanation

C: Senate

The 1999 Constitution makes ministerial and ambassadorial appointments subject to Senate confirmation. This gives the legislature a check on specified presidential appointments.

Question 24

The federal legislature in Nigeria under the 1999 Constitution is composed of

  1. 108 senators and 350 members of the House of Representatives
  2. 109 senators and 350 members of the House of Representatives
  3. 100 senators and 250 members of the House of Representatives
  4. 109 senators and 360 members of the House of Representatives
Answer and explanation

D: 109 senators and 360 members of the House of Representatives

The 1999 Constitution provides three senators for each of 36 states and one for the Federal Capital Territory: 109 in total. The House of Representatives has 360 members.

Question 27

The principle of checks and balances empowers the judiciary to

  1. invalidate the actions of the other arms
  2. administer the criminal justice system
  3. abrogate the law
  4. apply the law
Answer and explanation

A: invalidate the actions of the other arms

Judicial review allows courts to invalidate government action that exceeds lawful or constitutional authority. This is a check on other branches, not an unrestricted power to cancel any policy the judges dislike.
