Question 2

The civil service belongs to the organ of government called the

  1. legislature
  2. executive
  3. judiciary
  4. parastatals
Answer and explanation

B: executive

The civil service carries out administration and implements public policy. It therefore forms part of the executive machinery, rather than the legislature or judiciary.

Question 3

Ultimate power in a confederal state is

  1. concentrated in the central government
  2. constitutionally divided between the centre and the units
  3. exercised by the larger units
  4. vested in the constituent units
Answer and explanation

D: vested in the constituent units

The constituent states of a confederation retain ultimate sovereignty. The common central body exercises only the functions that those states agree to delegate.

Question 4

Federalism is a constitutional arrangement in which

  1. the national and regional governments derive their power from the constitution
  2. the national government acts only through the regional governments
  3. regional and local authorities exist at the pleasure of national government
  4. the national government does not reach individual citizens directly
Answer and explanation

A: the national and regional governments derive their power from the constitution

In a federation, the constitution gives the national and regional governments their respective powers. Regional authority is not merely a temporary concession from the national government.

Question 7

As a political ideology, communism seeks to

  1. concentrate national wealth in the hands of a few
  2. abolish collective ownership of property
  3. provide adequate opportunities for citizens to make profits
  4. eliminate all forms of socio-economic inequality
Answer and explanation

D: eliminate all forms of socio-economic inequality

Communism aims at a classless society with common ownership of productive resources. Its stated goal is the removal of socio-economic class inequalities, rather than concentrating wealth in private hands.

Question 8

Despite the doctrine of separation of powers, the legislature plays quasi-judicial role as a result of its

  1. investigative powers
  2. financial powers
  3. emergency power
  4. appropriation powers
Answer and explanation

A: investigative powers

Legislative investigations can involve receiving evidence, questioning witnesses and determining facts. These resemble judicial inquiry and explain the description quasi-judicial, without making the legislature an ordinary court.

Question 10

In the Westminster system of government formal legislation can take the following forms EXCEPT

  1. royal proclamations
  2. orders in council
  3. act of parliament
  4. ministerial pronouncement
Answer and explanation

D: ministerial pronouncement

A ministerial pronouncement is not, merely by being announced, formal legislation. Acts, orders in council and authorized royal proclamations can have legal force through recognized constitutional procedures.

Question 11

In the presidential system of government, the president is

  1. elected only through the electoral college
  2. empowered to dissolve the legislature
  3. head of state and head of government
  4. vested with absolute powers
Answer and explanation

C: head of state and head of government

A president in the presidential model combines the offices of head of state and head of government. The office is constitutionally limited rather than endowed with absolute power.

Question 12

Delegated legislation is disadvantageous because it

  1. erodes the principle of separation of powers
  2. make hasty review of legislation possible
  3. disallows quick action during emergencies
  4. increases the work of the legislature
Answer and explanation

A: erodes the principle of separation of powers

Delegated legislation allows executive bodies to make rules under legislative authority. A common criticism is that this reduces the practical separation between making and administering laws.

  2. local government area
  3. politically demarcated area
  4. constituency
Answer and explanation

A constituency is an electoral district whose voters choose one or more representatives. Its boundaries define the population represented by the elected office-holder.

Question 16

Franchise ensures

  1. participation in public affairs
  2. classless democratic system
  3. the emergence of ideal leadership
  4. equal representation
Answer and explanation

A: participation in public affairs

The franchise provides a right to participate in public affairs through voting. It does not by itself guarantee ideal leaders, a classless society or equal representation of every group.

Question 17

Free and fair elections do NOT accommodate

  1. obedience to the rules of the game
  2. equality of all votes
  3. freedom of choice by voters
  4. partial electoral administration
Answer and explanation

D: partial electoral administration

An election administrator must act impartially. Partial administration favours a contestant and undermines equal treatment, unlike respect for rules and freedom of choice.

Question 18

Proportional representation is often criticized because it

  1. tends to preserve the multi-party system
  2. is too simple to operate
  3. ensures that each party has minister
  4. provides that each state represented according to its population
Answer and explanation

A: tends to preserve the multi-party system

Critics argue that proportional representation can preserve many parties and make coalition bargaining more likely. Supporters value the same feature because it gives smaller parties representation.

Question 19

  1. represent the masses
  2. do not allow misrule
  3. represent groups who otherwise would be left out
  4. provide a forum for confronting the government
Answer and explanation

Interest groups can give organized representation to people or causes that might otherwise be overlooked. Their contribution does not guarantee the prevention of every abuse of government.

Question 22

Which of these sets represents the disadvantages of colonialism?

  1. exposure to western education, cultural imperialism and external trade relations
  2. cultural imperialism, external manipulation and foreign control of the domestic economy
  3. external manipulation, civil service evolution and liberal democracy
  4. liberal democracy, foreign domination and cultural imperialism
Answer and explanation

B: cultural imperialism, external manipulation and foreign control of the domestic economy

Cultural domination, external manipulation and foreign control of the economy describe losses of autonomy associated with colonialism. The other sets include institutions or contacts that are not inherently disadvantages.

Question 23

Imperialism was adopted by Europe to

  1. expand its economic and political base
  2. develop the economies of the colonies
  3. establish a democratic society similar to that of Europe
  4. end ethnic rivalries in the colonies
Answer and explanation

A: expand its economic and political base

Imperialism extends a state’s power and influence over other territories. Economic access and political control are central features of that expansion, unlike a guarantee of democracy for conquered peoples.

Question 31

In Nigeria, the function of the Council of State is

  1. judicial
  2. investigative
  3. legislative
  4. advisory
Answer and explanation

D: advisory

The Council of State advises on specified public matters. It is not a legislative chamber or a court and does not replace those institutions' constitutional powers.

Question 32

Under the military administration, supreme court decisions are subject to review by

  1. Sharia Court of Appeal
  2. no other court of law
  3. Federal Court of Appeal
  4. Judicial tribunals
Answer and explanation

B: no other court of law

The Supreme Court is the final court in the judicial hierarchy, so no other court hears an appeal from it. This is distinct from a court's limited ability to revisit its own decisions.

Question 36

In Nigeria, public corporations are also known as

  1. multinational corporations
  2. private enterprises
  3. parastatals
  4. co-operative societies
Answer and explanation

C: parastatals

Public corporations are commonly described as parastatals because they operate as government-established bodies outside ordinary ministries. Their public ownership distinguishes them from private enterprises and cooperatives.
