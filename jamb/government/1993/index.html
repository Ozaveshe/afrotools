Question 2

The civil service belongs to the organ of government called the

  1. legislature
  2. executive
  3. judiciary
  4. parastatals
Answer and explanation

B: executive

The civil service carries out administration and implements public policy. It therefore forms part of the executive machinery, rather than the legislature or judiciary.

Question 3

Ultimate power in a confederal state is

  1. concentrated in the central government
  2. constitutionally divided between the centre and the units
  3. exercised by the larger units
  4. vested in the constituent units
Answer and explanation

D: vested in the constituent units

The constituent states of a confederation retain ultimate sovereignty. The common central body exercises only the functions that those states agree to delegate.

Question 4

Federalism is a constitutional arrangement in which

  1. the national and regional governments derive their power from the constitution
  2. the national government acts only through the regional governments
  3. regional and local authorities exist at the pleasure of national government
  4. the national government does not reach individual citizens directly
Answer and explanation

A: the national and regional governments derive their power from the constitution

In a federation, the constitution gives the national and regional governments their respective powers. Regional authority is not merely a temporary concession from the national government.

Question 7

As a political ideology, communism seeks to

  1. concentrate national wealth in the hands of a few
  2. abolish collective ownership of property
  3. provide adequate opportunities for citizens to make profits
  4. eliminate all forms of socio-economic inequality
Answer and explanation

D: eliminate all forms of socio-economic inequality

Communism aims at a classless society with common ownership of productive resources. Its stated goal is the removal of socio-economic class inequalities, rather than concentrating wealth in private hands.

Question 8

Despite the doctrine of separation of powers, the legislature plays quasi-judicial role as a result of its

  1. investigative powers
  2. financial powers
  3. emergency power
  4. appropriation powers
Answer and explanation

A: investigative powers

Legislative investigations can involve receiving evidence, questioning witnesses and determining facts. These resemble judicial inquiry and explain the description quasi-judicial, without making the legislature an ordinary court.

Question 9

Nazism as a doctrine was proposed by

  1. Benito Mussolini
  2. Adolf Hitler
  3. Karl Marx
  4. Josef Stalin
Answer and explanation

B: Adolf Hitler

Adolf Hitler formulated and promoted Nazi ideology, including its racist and authoritarian doctrines. Mussolini was associated with Italian Fascism, while Marx and Stalin were associated with communist movements.

Question 10

In the Westminster system of government formal legislation can take the following forms EXCEPT

  1. royal proclamations
  2. orders in council
  3. act of parliament
  4. ministerial pronouncement
Answer and explanation

D: ministerial pronouncement

A ministerial pronouncement is not, merely by being announced, formal legislation. Acts, orders in council and authorized royal proclamations can have legal force through recognized constitutional procedures.

Question 11

In the presidential system of government, the president is

  1. elected only through the electoral college
  2. empowered to dissolve the legislature
  3. head of state and head of government
  4. vested with absolute powers
Answer and explanation

C: head of state and head of government

A president in the presidential model combines the offices of head of state and head of government. The office is constitutionally limited rather than endowed with absolute power.

Question 12

Delegated legislation is disadvantageous because it

  1. erodes the principle of separation of powers
  2. make hasty review of legislation possible
  3. disallows quick action during emergencies
  4. increases the work of the legislature
Answer and explanation

A: erodes the principle of separation of powers

Delegated legislation allows executive bodies to make rules under legislative authority. A common criticism is that this reduces the practical separation between making and administering laws.

Question 13

The principle of the rule of law stipulates that

  1. only judges interpret the law
  2. law makers are above the law
  3. everyone is equal before the law
  4. lawyers make laws
Answer and explanation

C: everyone is equal before the law

Equality before the law is a core rule-of-law principle. Lawmakers and other officials remain legally accountable rather than being inherently above the law.

Question 14

Gerrymandering means

  1. the second ballot system
  2. single transferable vote
  3. the manipulation of constituency boundaries
  4. the tyranny of the majority
Answer and explanation

C: the manipulation of constituency boundaries

Gerrymandering manipulates constituency boundaries to obtain political advantage. It is different from voting methods such as the single transferable vote.
