Question 2

An indispensable feature of any government is

  1. a written constitution
  2. the independence of the judiciary
  3. the separation of powers
  4. political power
Answer and explanation

D: political power

A government must possess political power to make and carry out public decisions. Written constitutions, judicial independence and separation of powers are not features of every governmental system.

Question 3

The unrestrained power of a state over its citizens defines the concept of

  1. nationalism
  2. self-determination
  3. nation – state
  4. sovereignty
Answer and explanation

D: sovereignty

Sovereignty describes the state's supreme governing authority over its territory and population. Nationalism and self-determination concern political identity and collective choice rather than that authority.

Question 4

Case-law are made by the

  1. legislatures
  2. council of ministers
  3. judiciary
  4. president
Answer and explanation

C: judiciary

Case law develops through judicial decisions and the legal principles applied in them. It is therefore produced by courts, rather than by legislation or presidential action.

Question 6

Bicameral legislatures are popular in

  1. Unitary systems
  2. Federal systems
  3. Confederal systems
  4. rigid systems
Answer and explanation

B: Federal systems

Two chambers are common in federal systems because they can represent both citizens and constituent units. Bicameralism provides an additional channel for regional or state interests.

Question 7

One of the weakness of confederation is the

  1. over-centralization of authority
  2. lack of central army
  3. tendencies towards secession
  4. absence of local independence
Answer and explanation

C: tendencies towards secession

A confederation leaves substantial sovereignty with its member states. Weak central authority and strong member autonomy can encourage withdrawal or secession and weaken the union.

Question 10

Unlike the British, the United States constitution is

  1. rigid, federal and written
  2. flexible, unitary and unwritten
  3. rigid written and confederal
  4. flexible, federal and unwritten
Answer and explanation

A: rigid, federal and written

The United States has a written federal constitution with a special amendment process. These features make it rigid and federal, unlike the traditional classification of the British constitution.

Question 12

Governments whose central concern is the equitable distribution of wealth are said to be

  1. feudalist
  2. capitalist
  3. socialist
  4. fascist
Answer and explanation

C: socialist

Socialism emphasizes social ownership or control and a more equitable distribution of resources. This redistributive objective distinguishes it from the other ideological descriptions listed.

Question 14

The notion of checks and balances guarantees that

  1. the executive is able to control the legislature
  2. the judiciary can stop all executive actions
  3. each branch of government acts as a watchdog over other branches
  4. the legislature is subordinate to the judiciary
Answer and explanation

C: each branch of government acts as a watchdog over other branches

Checks and balances give each branch ways to scrutinize or restrain the others. They do not place the legislature permanently under the judiciary or allow courts to stop every executive action.

Question 15

The principle of collective responsibility implies that

  1. the prime minister can dissolve the entire parliament
  2. the head of state can dismiss the prime minister
  3. each offending minister can be reassigned
  4. the cabinet stands or falls together
Answer and explanation

D: the cabinet stands or falls together

Collective cabinet responsibility means ministers stand together behind government policy. Loss of confidence affects the cabinet as a whole rather than only one minister.

Question 23

The process of removing an elected official by the electorate after an election is termed

  1. impeachment
  2. plebiscite
  3. recall
  4. referendum
Answer and explanation

C: recall

Recall allows eligible voters to remove an elected representative before the ordinary end of the term, under the applicable procedure. Impeachment is undertaken through institutions rather than directly by the electorate.

Question 24

Groups which seek to bring about changes in government policies without actually controlling the personnel of government are known as

  1. trade unions
  2. pressure groups
  3. secret societies
  4. elite groups
Answer and explanation

B: pressure groups

Pressure groups aim to influence government decisions without themselves taking control of the government. Trade unions can be one type, but pressure groups is the wider category.

Question 27

Which of the following led to the introduction of indirect rule in Nigeria by the British?

  1. Need for adequate financial resources
  2. Desire for rapid development
  3. Speedy transition to independence
  4. Manpower and personnel shortages
Answer and explanation

D: Manpower and personnel shortages

Using existing rulers reduced the number of British officials needed to administer a large territory. Personnel shortages therefore helped make indirect rule attractive to colonial administrators.

Question 30

The Willink Commission was set up to

  1. solve boundary problems
  2. review revenue allocation formula
  3. examine issues relating to the welfare of groups
  4. draw up a new constitution
Answer and explanation

C: examine issues relating to the welfare of groups

The Willink Commission examined fears expressed by minority groups and possible constitutional safeguards. It was not primarily a revenue-allocation or boundary-drawing commission.

Question 31

The state legislatures of the second republic in NIGERIA had the following EXCEPT

  1. speaker
  2. senate leader
  3. deputy speaker
  4. clerk of the house
Answer and explanation

B: senate leader

State legislatures were Houses of Assembly and had no Senate. A Senate leader belongs to the federal upper chamber, unlike the speaker and clerk found in state assemblies.
