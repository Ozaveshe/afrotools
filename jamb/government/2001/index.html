Question 3

Where the constitution is supreme, unconstitutional acts of the executive and the legislature can be checked by the courts through

  1. recall
  2. judicial review
  3. vote of no confidence
  4. impeachment.
Answer and explanation

B: judicial review

Judicial review allows courts to assess whether executive or legislative actions comply with the constitution. Unconstitutional action can be declared invalid under a system of constitutional supremacy.

Question 4

The central decision-making organ of a confederation is made up of

  1. technocrats appointed by the units
  2. politicians elected from the confederal constituencies
  3. politicians nominated by the government of member states
  4. representatives of pressure groups.
Answer and explanation

C: politicians nominated by the government of member states

A confederation is formed by sovereign member states that send representatives to its common institutions. The central body consequently derives its membership and authority from those governments.

Question 5

Which of the following is true of a parliamentary system of government?

  1. clear separation of government organs.
  2. strict operation of bicameral legislature.
  3. removal of government by impeachment.
  4. adherence to majority rule.
Answer and explanation

D: adherence to majority rule.

A parliamentary government depends on support or confidence in the legislature, where decisions follow majority rules. It does not require two chambers or strict separation of executive and legislature.

Question 7

The central point of capitalism, as expounded by Karl Marx, is that

  1. capitalist profit is the surplus value obtained from workers labour
  2. workers are inherently incapable of being owners of their labour
  3. capitalists shall always increase worker earning capacity through wages
  4. capitalists shall always readily consent to workers welfare demands.
Answer and explanation

A: capitalist profit is the surplus value obtained from workers labour

Marx explains capitalist profit through surplus value: workers produce value beyond the value paid for their labour power. Owners appropriate that surplus through their ownership of productive assets.

Question 9

An important function of a constitution is that it

  1. provide a framework for the study of government
  2. facilities cross-fertilization of ideas of governance
  3. serves as the fountain head of authority for the exercise of power
  4. promotes citizen participation in government and administration.
Answer and explanation

C: serves as the fountain head of authority for the exercise of power

A constitution establishes the legal basis and limits of governmental authority. Public institutions derive their powers from this constitutional framework rather than simply from the wishes of individual officials.

Question 10

when a bill passed by the legislature is vetoed by the executive, the action underscores the principle

  1. probity and accountability
  2. separation of powers
  3. collective responsibility
  4. checks and balances.
Answer and explanation

D: checks and balances.

A presidential veto checks the legislature by withholding assent to a bill. The legislature may also have an override procedure, illustrating mutual checks between the branches.

Question 11

In the legislative process, a bill is a

  1. motion accepted for debate
  2. motion rejected after debate
  3. proposal before the legislature
  4. law passed by the legislature
Answer and explanation

C: proposal before the legislature

A bill is a legislative proposal presented for consideration. It becomes an enacted law only after completing the required legislative and assent procedures, so a proposal must be distinguished from the final law.

Question 12

One of the advantages of a bicameral over a unicameral legislature is that it

  1. is cheap to maintain
  2. promotes social equality
  3. takes less time for the bills to be passed
  4. prevents the passage of ill-considered bills.
Answer and explanation

D: prevents the passage of ill-considered bills.

A second chamber provides additional scrutiny that can catch weaknesses in proposed laws. This can reduce ill-considered legislation, though it does not guarantee that every bill will be sound.

Question 14

  1. devolution
  2. rigging
  3. gerrymandering
  4. delimitation

Manipulating constituency boundaries to gain seats is specifically called gerrymandering. Delimitation is the general process of drawing boundaries, which need not be partisan.

  4. achieve goals affecting other associations.

Question 18

The political neutrality of civil servants implies that they

  1. are not allowed to join any organization or group
  2. have no dealings with politicians
  3. are not allowed to be involved in partisan politics
  4. are not allowed to vote.
Answer and explanation

C: are not allowed to be involved in partisan politics

Political neutrality requires civil servants to perform official duties impartially and avoid partisan activity incompatible with their office. It does not mean that they cannot vote or work with elected officials.

Question 19

The idea of making the civil service permanent, neutral and anonymous is to

  1. enhance efficiency in administration
  2. ensure loyalty and support
  3. prevent opposition to government
  4. make civil servants a functional elite.
Answer and explanation

A: enhance efficiency in administration

Permanence preserves administrative experience, neutrality supports impartial service, and anonymity keeps official work focused on institutional responsibility. Together they support effective administration across changes of government.

Question 50

  1. the economic interest of the West in Nigeria
  2. Nigeria's increased international influence
  3. the growing trade between Nigeria and the East
  4. Nigeria's concern for decolonization in Africa
