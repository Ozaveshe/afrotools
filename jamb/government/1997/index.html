Question 1

Authority refers to the

  1. might to secure compliance from others within a given social setting
  2. power to exercise might over others behaviour
  3. mandate to exercise power over others
  4. Ability to compel others to act in a particular way
Answer and explanation

C: mandate to exercise power over others

Authority involves an accepted mandate or right to exercise power. Mere strength or the ability to compel behaviour describes power without necessarily establishing authority.

Question 2

Government is different from other political organizations because

  1. it has legitimate power over citizens
  2. its officials have fixed terms of office
  3. it can punish those who violate its rules
  4. it is made up of elected officials
Answer and explanation

A: it has legitimate power over citizens

Government exercises legitimate public authority over citizens within its jurisdiction. Other organizations may make internal rules, but do not possess this general governmental mandate.

Question 4

The official report of proceeding in parliament is known as the

  1. Hansard
  2. diary of events
  3. gazette
  4. summary of proceedings
Answer and explanation

A: Hansard

Hansard is the official report of parliamentary proceedings. It records debates and related business, rather than serving as a general government gazette.

Question 5

An important feature of the federal system of government is the existence of a

  1. strong national legislature
  2. president with veto power
  3. court system with impartial judges
  4. multiple tiers of government
Answer and explanation

D: multiple tiers of government

Federal government involves constitutionally established levels of government, each with assigned responsibilities. Multiple tiers are fundamental, while a presidential veto is not required in every federation.

Question 6

An important aspect of an unwritten constitution is that it

  1. is easy to understand by everybody
  2. safeguards the monarchy
  3. contains customary laws and conventions
  4. is not easily amended
Answer and explanation

C: contains customary laws and conventions

An uncodified constitution includes conventions and customary practices alongside written legal sources. It need not be collected in a single constitutional document.

Question 7

The ideology that advocates the complete control of the sources of power is

  1. totalitarianism
  2. socialism
  3. liberalism
  4. democracy
Answer and explanation

A: totalitarianism

Totalitarianism seeks to control all significant sources of political and social power. Liberalism and democracy preserve independent institutions and limits on government.

Question 9

One basic characteristic of parliamentary system of government is that the

  1. upper and lower houses of the legislature have equal powers
  2. cabinet is part of the legislature
  3. prime minister is usually a member of the upper house
  4. opposition party members are also included in the cabinet
Answer and explanation

B: cabinet is part of the legislature

In the parliamentary model, cabinet ministers are members of the legislature and remain accountable to it. This connects the executive to parliament rather than requiring equal powers for two chambers.

Question 10

In a presidential system, the theory of separation of powers is not absolute because the president

  1. can declare a state of emergency
  2. can be impeached by the court
  3. assents to bills
  4. can declare laws null and void
Answer and explanation

C: assents to bills

Presidential assent is part of the law-making process, although the president belongs to the executive. This interaction illustrates checks and balances rather than complete isolation of the branches.

Question 11

The principle of checks and balances is found mainly in

  1. parliamentary system
  2. presidential system
  3. monarchies
  4. oligarchies
Answer and explanation

B: presidential system

The presidential model separates the branches and gives each powers that check the others. These checks are particularly prominent where the executive does not depend on continuing parliamentary confidence.

Question 14

The order used by the court against unnecessary detention is

  1. interlocutory injunction
  2. habeas corpus
  3. that of extradition
  4. mandamus
Answer and explanation

B: habeas corpus

Habeas corpus brings the legality of detention before a court. If detention lacks lawful authority, the court can order release.
