Question 1

Proportional representation favours a

  1. multi-party system
  2. three party system
  3. two party system
  4. one party system
Answer and explanation

A: multi-party system

Proportional representation allows smaller parties to gain seats in relation to their vote shares. This makes representation by several parties more likely than under winner-takes-all rules.

Question 4

When a state is subject to no other authority it is said to be

  1. powerful
  2. legitimate
  3. authoritative
  4. sovereign
Answer and explanation

D: sovereign

A sovereign state is not subordinate to another state's governing authority. This describes independence, rather than simply military power or the legitimacy of a particular government.

Question 6

The major advantage of the principle of separation of powers is that it provides for

  1. an executive premier
  2. a powerful legislature
  3. a very strong executive
  4. checks and balances
Answer and explanation

D: checks and balances

Distributing power among separate branches allows them to restrain one another. These checks and balances reduce the risk of unchecked control by any single branch.

Question 7

The cabinet can be described as a link between the

  1. executive and the legislature
  2. legislature and the electorate
  3. executive and the judiciary
  4. legislature and the local government councils.
Answer and explanation

A: executive and the legislature

In a parliamentary system, cabinet ministers are drawn from and accountable to the legislature while directing executive policy. Their dual role links the legislative and executive branches.

Question 8

Unicameral refers to

  1. a two-chamber legislature
  2. the process of secret voting in the legislature
  3. the lower chamber in a legislature
  4. a one chamber legislature
Answer and explanation

D: a one chamber legislature

A unicameral legislature has one chamber. A bicameral legislature has two chambers, such as a Senate and a House of Representatives.

Question 9

While pressure groups aim at influencing government decisions the primary aim of political parties is to

  1. promote the welfare of their members
  2. execute particular programs
  3. influence legislation in order to benefit their members
  4. control political powers.
Answer and explanation

D: control political powers.

Political parties seek to win and exercise political power through government. Pressure groups primarily seek to influence public decisions affecting their interests without forming the government themselves.

Question 11

  1. citizens choose their own government
  2. citizens enjoy services provided by government
  3. government does not discriminate in its provision of services to different parts of the Nations

Question 14

An electoral system in which the candidate who receives the largest number of votes wins is

  1. the preferential ballot
  2. the second ballot
  3. proportional system
  4. plurality system
Answer and explanation

D: plurality system

Under plurality voting the candidate with more votes than any other candidate wins. An absolute majority of all votes is not necessarily required.

Question 15

Which of the following is NOT usually associated with the activities of political parties?

  1. providing political education
  2. selecting and supporting candidates for public office
  3. forming the government
  4. controlling the judiciary
Answer and explanation

D: controlling the judiciary

Political parties educate voters, nominate candidates and seek to form governments. The judiciary should decide cases independently rather than be controlled by a party.

Question 17

An unwritten constitution is one which

  1. embodies only tradition and customs
  2. relies on the memories of elders and priests
  3. codifies the basic laws in one document
  4. embodies the basic laws in more than one document
Answer and explanation

D: embodies the basic laws in more than one document

An uncodified constitution draws on several sources instead of one comprehensive constitutional document. These can include statutes, judicial decisions and conventions.

Question 18

Fascism is a system of government which

  1. encourages political dissent and opposition
  2. discourages opposition
  3. promotes equality
  4. promotes the international brotherhood of man
Answer and explanation

B: discourages opposition

Fascist systems suppress political dissent and organize power around authoritarian leadership. They do not protect the open disagreement and pluralism associated with democratic politics.

Question 19

  2. promotes equality
  3. promotes the international brotherhood of man
  4. discourages opposition

In a modern democracy the ultimate source of sovereignty is the

  1. legislature
  2. people
  3. supreme court

Democratic sovereignty ultimately rests with the people. Elected institutions exercise authority on their behalf and must remain accountable to them.

Question 21

Which of these is a common feature of totalitarian regimes?

  1. Existence of opposition groups
  2. competing political parties
  3. a single recognized party
  4. freedom of association
Answer and explanation

C: a single recognized party

Totalitarian regimes commonly recognize a single ruling party and suppress organized political competition. Free opposition parties and freedom of association conflict with that concentration of control.

Question 22

Which of the following according to Marx, is the correct order in which societies progress?

  1. Capitalism, feudalism, communism, socialism
  2. Feudalism, socialism, capitalism, communism
  3. Communism, socialism, capitalism, feudalism
  4. Feudalism, capitalism, socialism, communism
Answer and explanation

D: Feudalism, capitalism, socialism, communism

The conventional Marxist sequence shown here moves from feudalism to capitalism, then socialism and communism. This is a theoretical account of historical development, not a claim that every society follows an identical path.

Which of the following is NOT necessarily a characteristic of a nation state?

  2. sovereignty

Question 24

Public opinion refers to views and ideas expressed by

  1. citizens regarding government policies or other issues
  2. government regarding the public interest
  3. elites about what the public wants
  4. students about the public
Answer and explanation

A: citizens regarding government policies or other issues

Public opinion comprises citizens’ views on public issues, including government policy. It is not simply what government or a small elite says that the public wants.

Question 25

An issue over which both the centre and the states can exercise authority in a federal system is

  1. shared
  2. split
  3. exclusive
  4. concurrent
Answer and explanation

D: concurrent

Concurrent powers may be exercised by both central and state governments under the constitution. Exclusive powers, by contrast, are reserved to one level.

Question 26

In traditional Hausa Fulani political system, political authority was vested in the

  1. Emir
  2. Talakawa
  3. Alkali
  4. Emirate Council
Answer and explanation

A: Emir

The Emir headed the emirate and exercised political authority through officials and institutions. The Alkali performed judicial duties, while talakawa referred to ordinary people.

Question 28

Igbo traditional system of government encouraged participation through

  1. Ozo title
  2. Segmentary kinship
  3. divine kingship
  4. town assembly
Answer and explanation

D: town assembly

Community assemblies provided a forum for discussing issues and reaching collective decisions in the village-assembly model of Igbo government. Participation was broader than possession of an Ozo title alone.
