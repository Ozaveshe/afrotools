Question 2

The organs of government which are normally fused in a military regime are the

  1. Civil Service and Parastatals
  2. Legislature and the Executive
  3. Executive and the Judiciary
  4. Judiciary and the Legislature
Answer and explanation

B: Legislature and the Executive

A military regime commonly combines executive administration and lawmaking in its ruling council. This fuses executive and legislative powers rather than maintaining their ordinary institutional separation.

Question 4

A Constitution is rigid if it

  1. cannot be amended
  2. is found only in one written document
  3. requires special procedures for amendment
  4. is changed only by judicial interpretation
Answer and explanation

C: requires special procedures for amendment

A rigid constitution requires a special amendment procedure beyond ordinary lawmaking. It can still be amended, but the process is deliberately more demanding.

Question 5

In a unitary state, power is concentrated in the

  1. local governments
  2. constituent units of the state
  3. major ethnic groups
  4. national government
Answer and explanation

D: national government

A unitary state places ultimate constitutional authority in the national government. Local or regional bodies exercise powers provided within that central constitutional structure.

Question 6

Fundamental rights are best guaranteed by

  1. independent judiciary
  2. good leadership
  3. effective legislature
  4. efficient civil service
Answer and explanation

A: independent judiciary

An independent judiciary can enforce fundamental rights without improper political pressure. Its ability to provide impartial legal remedies is a direct safeguard for those rights.

Question 7

The main objective of pressure groups is to

  1. win political power
  2. conduct free and fair elections
  3. mobilize support on behalf of the government
  4. protect the interests of its members
Answer and explanation

D: protect the interests of its members

Pressure groups seek to advance and protect their members interests by influencing public decisions. Unlike political parties, they do not primarily seek to form the government themselves.

Question 8

Franchise means

  1. resident aliens can vote
  2. French people can vote
  3. right to vote
  4. executive order
Answer and explanation

C: right to vote

Franchise is the right to vote in elections. The term concerns electoral entitlement, which is exercised by people who meet the applicable legal qualifications.

Question 10

Under proportional representation, elections are won on the basis of

  1. simple majority of votes
  2. absolute majority of votes cast
  3. Two-thirds majority of voters cast
  4. votes received relative to those of other parties
Answer and explanation

D: votes received relative to those of other parties

Proportional representation allocates seats broadly according to each partys share of the vote. It does not require every successful party to obtain an absolute or two-thirds majority.

Question 12

The electorate means

  1. elected members of the Assembly
  2. candidates for election
  3. electoral officers
  4. citizens qualified to vote
Answer and explanation

D: citizens qualified to vote

The electorate consists of people legally qualified to vote. It is distinct from the candidates standing for election and officials administering it.

Question 13

The term 'Rule of Law' refers to situations in which

  1. Lawyers are the rulers
  2. laws are supreme
  3. the judiciary is independent
  4. parliament makes laws
Answer and explanation

B: laws are supreme

The rule of law means that law is supreme over arbitrary authority. Officials and citizens are accountable under legal rules rather than rulers personal wishes.
