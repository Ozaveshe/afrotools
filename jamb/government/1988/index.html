Question 1

The right to direct and command people is

  1. authority
  2. power
  3. opinion
  4. obedience
Answer and explanation

A: authority

Authority is the recognized right to direct others and expect obedience. Power is the ability to secure compliance, which may exist without that recognized right.

Question 3

When sovereignty rest with component states in a political system the constitution is referred to as

  1. flexible
  2. unitary
  3. confederal
  4. federal
Answer and explanation

C: confederal

In a confederation, the participating states retain their sovereignty and delegate limited functions to a common body. That retained sovereignty distinguishes it from a federal arrangement.

Question 5

In a parliamentary system of government, the offices of head of state and head of government are

  1. fused
  2. separated
  3. not defined
  4. indistinguishable
Answer and explanation

B: separated

The parliamentary model separates the head of state from the head of government. A prime minister leads the government while a monarch or president performs the distinct head-of-state role.

Question 6

Citizenship may be changed by

  1. Renunciation
  2. Remarriage
  3. Divorce
  4. conviction
Answer and explanation

A: Renunciation

Renunciation is a formal act of giving up citizenship under the applicable legal procedure. It can change a person's citizenship status, unlike divorce by itself.

Question 8

Government by the few is

  1. dictatorship
  2. monarchy
  3. oligarchy
  4. autocracy
Answer and explanation

C: oligarchy

Oligarchy means government by a small group. Monarchy identifies rule by a monarch, while autocracy describes concentrated personal authority rather than specifically rule by a few.

Question 9

The doctrine of separation of power is associated with

  1. Montesquieu
  2. Locke
  3. Marx
  4. Hobbes
Answer and explanation

A: Montesquieu

Montesquieu gave a major formulation of the separation of legislative, executive and judicial powers. The division was intended to protect liberty by preventing concentrated power.

Question 13

The principle of collective responsibility means that ministers

  1. are collectively responsible to the head of state
  2. are collectively responsible for cabinet decisions
  3. are heads of their respective departments
  4. must always show a sense of responsibility to their fellow ministers
Answer and explanation

B: are collectively responsible for cabinet decisions

Collective responsibility requires ministers to support cabinet decisions publicly as a team. A minister who cannot support a decision may need to resign rather than publicly oppose it while remaining in office.

Question 14

One of the important advantages of federalism is that

  1. any component state can secede at any time
  2. each state can develop at its own pace
  3. each state can only spend money allocated to it by the centre
  4. citizens cannot be taxed by both state and federal governments
Answer and explanation

B: each state can develop at its own pace

Federal autonomy allows constituent states to pursue policies suited to local needs within the constitution. It does not imply an unrestricted right to secede or prevent shared taxation powers.

Question 16

In simple plurality electoral system, the winner receives

  1. more than half of the votes cast
  2. overwhelming majority of all votes cast
  3. more votes than those for all other candidates combined
  4. the highest vote cast in favour of any candidate
Answer and explanation

D: the highest vote cast in favour of any candidate

Plurality requires more votes than any other individual candidate. The winner can have less than half of the total votes if support is divided among several candidates.

Question 18

An essential feature of democracy is

  1. rigid constitution
  2. people's consent
  3. supremacy of parliament
  4. bicameral legislature
Answer and explanation

B: people's consent

Democratic authority rests on the consent of the governed, expressed through political participation and accountable institutions. A rigid constitution or two legislative chambers is not essential in every democracy.

Question 20

Which of the following is NOT a mode of constitutional change?

  1. party manifesto
  2. formal amendment
  3. judicial decision
  4. statutory revision
Answer and explanation

A: party manifesto

A party manifesto sets out political commitments but does not itself change constitutional law. Formal amendments and authoritative legal changes can alter constitutional arrangements.

Question 22

Attempts to influence legislation by persuading legislators are known a

  1. socialization
  2. gerrymandering
  3. lobbying
  4. electioneering
Answer and explanation

C: lobbying

Lobbying seeks to persuade legislators or other public officials to support a particular decision. It is distinct from campaigning to elect candidates or redrawing constituencies.

Question 26

The public complaints commission is empowered to investigate complaints and

  1. prosecute false complainants
  2. report findings to appropriate authorities for action
  3. reprimand authorities against whom complaints are made
  4. refer complaints to traditional rulers
Answer and explanation

B: report findings to appropriate authorities for action

The Public Complaints Commission investigates administrative complaints and reports findings or recommendations to the appropriate authority. It does not itself replace a prosecutor or disciplinary authority.

Question 28

  1. meritocracy
  2. oligarchy
  3. theocracy
  4. aristocracy
Answer and explanation

Theocracy describes government in which political authority is closely joined to religious authority. The emir's combined political and spiritual roles explain the classification in this question.
