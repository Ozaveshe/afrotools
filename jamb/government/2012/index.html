Question 1

The distinctive attribute of a state is the monopoly of

  1. control
  2. power
  3. violence
  4. justice
Answer and explanation

C: violence

In the standard definition associated with the modern state, the state claims a monopoly on the legitimate use of physical force within its territory. Violence here refers to that coercive force, not unlimited permission to use it.

Question 3

Political values are acquired in any given society through

  1. political re-orientation
  2. political campaign
  3. political socialization
  4. political indoctrination
Answer and explanation

C: political socialization

Political socialization is the process through which people learn political values, beliefs and patterns of behaviour. It occurs through institutions and everyday social experience.

Question 4

In a democratic government, political sovereignty is vested in the

  1. legislature
  2. elite
  3. executive
  4. electorate
Answer and explanation

D: electorate

Political sovereignty in a democracy rests ultimately with the electorate, which authorizes representatives through elections. Government institutions exercise powers on that popular basis.

Question 5

One judicial function performed by the executive is

  1. Granting of amnesty
  2. Implementing judicial orders
  3. Ensuring obedience to the law
  4. Appointing judges
Answer and explanation

A: Granting of amnesty

Granting amnesty is commonly classified as an executive exercise of mercy related to offences and punishment. That connection with criminal justice distinguishes it from ordinary law enforcement or appointments.

Question 9

The private ownership of the means of production is a feature of

  1. capitalism
  2. socialism
  3. communalism
  4. communism
Answer and explanation

A: capitalism

Capitalism is characterized by private ownership of productive assets and production for profit. Socialism and communism instead emphasize social or common ownership.

Question 10

In a feudal system, the two major classes are the serfs and the

  1. masses
  2. vassals
  3. lords
  4. elite
Answer and explanation

C: lords

The principal contrast is between landholding lords and dependent serfs who worked the land. Lords exercised social and economic power over the estates on which serfs owed labour or dues.

Question 11

An example of a country with a flexible constitution is

  1. South Africa
  2. Britain
  3. Benin Republic
  4. the United States of America
Answer and explanation

B: Britain

Britains constitution is conventionally described as flexible because many constitutional rules can be changed by ordinary legislation. It lacks the single special amendment process typical of a rigid codified constitution.

Question 12

The rule of law is negation of

  1. equality before the law
  2. supremacy of the law
  3. Limited power
  4. absolute power
Answer and explanation

D: absolute power

The rule of law rejects absolute, unchecked power. Government authority is constrained by legal rules rather than resting solely on an individuals will.

Question 13

To ensure the rights and freedom of citizens, the powers of the arms of government must be

  1. fused
  2. incorporated
  3. separated
  4. rotated
Answer and explanation

C: separated

Separating the powers of government helps prevent one institution from controlling every stage of public decision-making. This provides an important safeguard for rights and freedoms.

Question 14

Delegated legislation is made by bodies others than the

  1. president
  2. governor
  3. parliament
  4. judiciary
Answer and explanation

C: parliament

Delegated legislation is made by bodies other than the primary legislature using authority it has granted. Parliament supplies the enabling law and may retain scrutiny powers.

Question 17

Which of the following is used in gauging public opinion?

  1. constitution
  2. educational institution
  3. mass media
  4. electoral college
Answer and explanation

C: mass media

Mass media can help gauge public opinion by reporting debate, interviews and opinion polls. A representative survey is more reliable than treating every published view as the view of everyone.

Question 19

The commission charged with the conduct of federal elections in Nigeria is

  1. NEC
  2. FEDECO
  3. INEC
  4. NECON
Answer and explanation

C: INEC

INEC organizes federal elections in Nigeria under the constitutional framework introduced in 1999. NEC, FEDECO and NECON were electoral bodies associated with earlier periods.

Question 22

An electoral system in which parties are assigned seats in the parliament commensurate to the number of votes polled is

  1. Absolute majority
  2. Simple majority
  3. proportional representation
  4. indirect election
Answer and explanation

C: proportional representation

Proportional representation allocates seats in relation to the votes won by parties. It aims to reflect each party’s electoral support in the composition of parliament.

Question 23

The bringing of a session of a parliament to an end through royal proclamation is known as

  1. political impasse
  2. dissolution of parliament
  3. vote of no confidence
  4. prorogation of parliament
Answer and explanation

D: prorogation of parliament

Prorogation brings a parliamentary session to an end through the formal constitutional process. It is distinct from dissolving parliament before a general election.
