Question 1

In a federal state, power is shared

  1. between the central government and the local authorities
  2. among the states of the federation
  3. among the major regions of the country
  4. between the central government and other coordinate units
Answer and explanation

D: between the central government and other coordinate units

Federalism constitutionally divides authority between a central government and coordinate constituent units. Each level has powers in its own assigned sphere.

Question 2

In a federal system of government, the centre is

  1. superior to the other components
  2. inferior to the other components
  3. equal to the other components
  4. of unlimited jurisdiction
Answer and explanation

C: equal to the other components

In a federation, central and constituent governments derive their assigned powers from the constitution. They are coordinate within their respective spheres, rather than one having unlimited jurisdiction.

Question 3

In a presidential system of government, ministers are

  1. individually responsible to the president
  2. individually responsible to the senate
  3. collectively responsible to the president
  4. collectively responsible to the electorate
Answer and explanation

A: individually responsible to the president

In the presidential model, ministers serve under the president and are individually accountable to that office. They do not depend on collective parliamentary confidence in the cabinet.

Question 5

In the parliamentary system of government, formal legislation can take the following forms except

  1. royal proclamations
  2. order in council
  3. acts of parliament
  4. ministerial pronouncement
Answer and explanation

D: ministerial pronouncement

A ministerial pronouncement is a statement and does not by itself constitute formal legislation. Acts, authorized orders in council and relevant royal proclamations can have formal legal status.

Question 6

One of the distinctive features of democracy is that it

  1. connotes civil rule
  2. facilitates popular participation
  3. provides for a unicameral legislature
  4. is not associated with one - part system
Answer and explanation

B: facilitates popular participation

Democracy gives people opportunities to participate in public decisions and select representatives. Civilian administration alone does not establish that participation, and a particular number of chambers is not essential.

Question 9

Bicameral legislature are a common feature of

  1. monarchical systems
  2. confederal systems
  3. federal systems
  4. unitary systems
Answer and explanation

C: federal systems

Federal legislatures commonly have two chambers so that both the population and constituent units can be represented. Bicameralism is common in federations, though not exclusive to them.

Question 10

In a federal constitution, legislative powers that are shared by two levels of government are referred to as

  1. exclusive
  2. extra ordinary
  3. residual
  4. concurrent
Answer and explanation

D: concurrent

Concurrent powers are law-making powers shared by different levels of government. Exclusive powers belong to one level, while residual powers are those left outside the enumerated lists.

Question 12

The process of learning the norms and values associated with a political system is referred to as political

  1. socialization
  2. indoctrination
  3. culture
  4. participation
Answer and explanation

Political socialization is how people learn the norms, values and attitudes of a political system. Participation is political activity, while culture is the broader pattern of shared beliefs.

Question 17

The notion of equality before the law is the same as the principle of

  1. supremacy of the constitution
  2. rule of law
  3. independence of the judiciary
  4. social equality
Answer and explanation

Equality before the law is a central element of the rule of law. It requires legal rules to apply without arbitrary privilege based on a person’s status.

Question 21

Indirect legislation can be achieved by

  1. judicial interpretation and precedent
  2. passing of bills and making of decrees
  3. passing a private member's bill
  4. the two legislative chambers considering a bill
Answer and explanation

Question 22

Naturalization is a process of acquiring citizenship by

  1. persons of dual nationality
  2. foreign visitors to a country
  3. resident foreigners of good character
  4. persons born abroad
Answer and explanation

C: resident foreigners of good character

Naturalization provides a route to citizenship for eligible foreign residents who meet legal conditions, commonly including residence and good character. It does not occur automatically through a short visit.

Question 26

The independence Constitution can be described as

  1. monarchical and presidential
  2. Republican and parliamentary
  3. monarchical and parliamentary
  4. federal and republican
Answer and explanation

C: monarchical and parliamentary

Nigeria retained the British monarch as head of state at independence in 1960 and used a parliamentary government led by a prime minister. The republic began in 1963.

Question 31

  2. suggest an equitable revenue allocation formula

  4. recommend solutions to the problem of the minorities
Answer and explanation

Question 32

The duty of an Alkali under the Hausa-Fulani political system is to

  1. adjudicate under Islamic laws
  2. make Islamic laws
  3. execute Islamic laws
  4. make treaties under Islamic laws
Answer and explanation

A: adjudicate under Islamic laws

An Alkali is a judge who decides cases under Islamic law. Adjudication is distinct from political treaty-making and from the executive enforcement of judgments.
