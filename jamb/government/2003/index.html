Question 1

In a parliamentary system, the functions of the head of state and the head of government are vested in

  1. the inner cabinet
  2. an individual
  3. two different individuals
  4. the ministerial council
Answer and explanation

C: two different individuals

A parliamentary system normally separates the head of state from the head of government. The latter directs the cabinet, while the former performs the distinct head-of-state role.

Question 2

Government by the wealthy is known as

  1. plutocracy
  2. oligarchy
  3. aristocracy
  4. democracy
Answer and explanation

A: plutocracy

Plutocracy means government by the wealthy. Oligarchy is the broader category of rule by a few and does not necessarily specify wealth as the basis of rule.

Question 4

The unrestrained power of the state over its citizens is underlined by

  1. sovereignty
  2. nationalism
  3. self-determination
  4. patriotism
Answer and explanation

A: sovereignty

Sovereignty is the concept of supreme state authority within its territory. Nationalism and patriotism concern identity or attachment, while self-determination concerns a people's political choice.

Question 6

One of the duties of the legislature is to

  1. implement laws
  2. adjudicate disputes
  3. promulgate decrees
  4. exercise oversight
Answer and explanation

D: exercise oversight

Legislative oversight examines the executive's conduct and implementation of policy. It is distinct from administering laws or resolving cases as a court.

Question 7

The independence of the Judiciary can be undermined through the

  1. politicization of the appointment of judges
  2. appointment of the minister of justice as the Attorney-General
  3. confirmation of the appointment of judges by the legislature
  4. payment of the salaries of judicial officers by the government
Answer and explanation

A: politicization of the appointment of judges

Politicizing judicial appointments can make selection depend on partisan loyalty rather than competence and impartiality. Ordinary salary payment and constitutional confirmation procedures do not inherently destroy judicial independence.

Question 10

Capitalism is a system of economic organization based on

  1. a mixed economy that takes all interests into consideration
  2. individual ownership of the means of production
  3. very fair distribution of the means of production
  4. trading among people who own and control their items of trade
Answer and explanation

B: individual ownership of the means of production

Capitalism is based on private ownership of productive resources and enterprise. It does not by definition guarantee equal distribution of wealth or require a centrally planned economy.

Question 11

Election as a political process is significant because it

  1. is associated with campaigns for public office
  2. facilitates the recall process
  3. facilitates constitutional change of government
  4. enables citizens to vote
Answer and explanation

C: facilitates constitutional change of government

Elections provide a constitutional route for replacing leaders and governments. Campaigns and voting are parts of the process, while peaceful lawful transfer is a central political significance.

Question 12

Associations whose main interest is to influence public policies without having to capture power are

  1. communal groups
  2. trade unions
  3. political parties
  4. pressure groups
Answer and explanation

D: pressure groups

Pressure groups aim to influence public policy without themselves taking governmental office. Political parties instead seek to capture and exercise political power.

Question 13

Constitutional disputes in states with written constitutions are resolved by

  1. ombudsman
  2. judiciary
  3. electorate
  4. legislature
Answer and explanation

B: judiciary

Courts resolve legal disputes about the meaning and application of written constitutions. This is a judicial function rather than the ordinary role of an ombudsman or electorate.

Question 14

The citizenship of a country could be acquired through

  1. parliamentary legislation
  2. birth and naturalization
  3. registration and arbitration
  4. presidential proclamation
Answer and explanation

B: birth and naturalization

Birth and naturalization are recognized routes to citizenship, with conditions set by national law. Arbitration is a dispute-resolution method rather than a normal citizenship route.

Question 15

Public opinion becomes politically relevant when it

  1. criticises people in power
  2. influences the decision of government
  3. aggregates views and interests
  4. is in support of government
Answer and explanation

B: influences the decision of government

Public opinion becomes an effective political influence when it affects government decisions. It can support or oppose a government; criticism alone is not the defining condition.

Question 17

The civil service embraces all workers in

  1. private corporations
  2. public and private companies
  3. ministries
  4. public corporations
Answer and explanation

C: ministries

The civil service carries out administration in government ministries and departments. Employees of private companies and public corporations do not all belong to the civil service.

Question 19

What distinguishes a political party from other social institutions is the desire to

  1. influence the international community on local issues
  2. promote the interest of party members
  3. win elections and form a government
  4. influence government policy in certain directions
Answer and explanation

C: win elections and form a government

The defining electoral purpose of a political party is to win elections and form or participate in government. Other social organizations may influence policy without seeking to govern.

Question 21

The type of government operated in Nigeria between October 1st, 1979 to December 31st, 1983 is called a

  1. collegial system of government
  2. presidential system of government
  3. unitary system of government
  4. parliamentary system of government
Answer and explanation

B: presidential system of government

Nigeria’s Second Republic operated a presidential system from October 1979 to December 1983. The executive president combined the roles of head of state and head of government.
