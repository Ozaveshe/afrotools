Question 1

Choice in economic life is necessitated by

  1. The need to construct scale of preference
  2. The opportunity cost of consumption
  3. Unlimited wants
  4. Scarcity of economic resources
Answer and explanation

D: Scarcity of economic resources

Scarce economic resources cannot satisfy every want. People must therefore choose between competing uses of those resources and accept the opportunity cost of their choices.

Question 2

Which of these is the real cost of satisfying any want in the sense of the alternative that has to be foregone?

  1. Variable cost
  2. Opportunity cost
  3. Total cost
  4. Prime cost
Answer and explanation

B: Opportunity cost

Opportunity cost is the value of the next-best alternative forgone. It measures the real sacrifice involved in choosing one use of resources over another.

Question 8

If a person receives a higher wage than would be necessary to induce him to work, he is said to be receiving

  1. Rent
  2. Profit
  3. Interest
  4. Period of training
Answer and explanation

A: Rent

Economic rent is payment above transfer earnings: the minimum needed to keep a factor in its present use. The excess wage above the worker’s required inducement is therefore rent.

Question 14

In drawing an individual’s demand curve for a commodity, which of the following is NOT kept constant? The

  1. Individual’s money income
  2. Price for substitutes
  3. Price of complementary goods
  4. Price of the commodity under consideration
Answer and explanation

D: Price of the commodity under consideration

An individual demand curve shows how quantity demanded changes with that commodity’s own price. Income and the prices of related goods are held constant while the commodity’s price varies.

Question 28

  2. Technical economies enjoyed by varying the factors of production
  3. Economies of management by putting administrators where they are most efficient
  4. Marketing advantages attained through preferential treatment in the purchase and distribution of produce
Answer and explanation

Question 31

To control inflation, the monetary authorities of a country can

  1. Reduce taxes
  2. Advise government to increase its expenditure
  3. Engage in expansive monetary policy
  4. Engage in restrictive monetary policy
Answer and explanation

D: Engage in restrictive monetary policy

Restrictive monetary policy reduces monetary stimulus and can restrain aggregate demand. It is therefore a tool for reducing inflationary pressure, particularly when demand is rising faster than supply.

Question 33

Gresham’s law in Economics shows that

  1. Bad money drives good money out of circulation
  2. Good money drives bad money out of circulation
  3. Gold must be available to maintain the value of paper money
  4. The price level varies directly with the quantity of money
Answer and explanation

A: Bad money drives good money out of circulation

Gresham’s law concerns monies accepted at the same legal face value despite different intrinsic values. People tend to spend the overvalued money and retain the undervalued money, so bad money drives good money from circulation.

Question 35

The most important economic characteristic differenti ating commercial banks from other financial institutions is their.

  1. Function as a safe and profitable store place for savings
  2. Role in financing balance of payments deficits
  3. Status as an intermediary between savers and borrowers
  4. Ability to create and destroy money
Answer and explanation

D: Ability to create and destroy money

Commercial-bank lending creates deposit money, while repayment of loan principal removes corresponding deposit money. Taking savings and intermediating between savers and borrowers are also activities of other financial institutions.

Question 37

Which of the following causes of inflation is related to demand-pull inflation?

  1. Low productivity on farms and in firms
  2. Poor storage facilities
  3. Poor distribution system
  4. Increase in government expenditure on construction
Answer and explanation

D: Increase in government expenditure on construction

Higher government construction spending adds to aggregate demand. If demand expands faster than productive capacity, it can generate demand-pull inflation; the other options describe supply-side limitations.

Question 43

The term, balance of trade, can be defined as the

  1. Relation between value of exports and value of imports over a given period of time
  2. Balance owed to the rest of the world in payment for imports
  3. Price paid for imports in terms of exports
  4. Relation between payments of all kinds made by a country to the rest of the world, over a given period of time.
Answer and explanation

A: Relation between value of exports and value of imports over a given period of time

The balance of trade compares the value of merchandise exports with merchandise imports over a period. Exports exceeding imports give a surplus; imports exceeding exports give a deficit.

Question 48

The difference between Gross Domestic Product and Gross National Product is accounted for by

  1. Allowance for total depreciation
  2. Total interest payments
  3. Total tax and interest payments
  4. Net income from abroad
Answer and explanation

Gross national product equals gross domestic product plus net factor income from abroad. The adjustment accounts for income residents earn abroad less income non-residents earn domestically.
