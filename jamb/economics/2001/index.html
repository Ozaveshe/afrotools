Question 2

In a free market economy the price system allocates resources

  1. under government's directives
  2. to ensure general welfare
  3. to their best alternative uses
  4. to reduce poverty.
Answer and explanation

C: to their best alternative uses

The price system guides resources towards alternative uses according to demand and relative scarcity. The standard efficient-allocation result assumes appropriate competitive conditions; a price system does not automatically eliminate poverty or deliver every welfare objective.

Question 3

At a smooth interior consumer equilibrium, the slope of the indifference curve is

  1. half the slope of the budget constraint line
  2. equal to the slope of the budget constraint line
  3. greater than the slope of the budget constraint line
  4. less than the slope of the budget constraint line.
Answer and explanation

B: equal to the slope of the budget constraint line

At an interior optimum, the highest attainable indifference curve is tangent to the budget line. Their slopes are equal: the consumer's marginal rate of substitution matches the price ratio. Corner solutions require separate consideration.

Question 4

For a supply curve, an increase in the price of a commodity will result in

  1. a decrease in supply
  2. a decrease in the quantity supplied
  3. an increase in supply
  4. an increase in the quantity supplied.
Answer and explanation

D: an increase in the quantity supplied.

A rise in the good's own price causes movement along an ordinary upward-sloping supply curve, increasing quantity supplied. A change in supply means a shift of the entire curve due to another determinant, such as costs or technology.

Question 6

  2. Is an agent of socialisation
  3. Uses the scientific method in the study of human behaviour
  4. Deals with social problems
Answer and explanation

Economics studies human choices and interactions and uses systematic models, observation and evidence to investigate them. That combination of social subject matter and scientific inquiry makes it a social science.

Question 9

When a generalisation is made based on observed facts, it is known as

  1. deductive reasoning
  2. inductive reasoning
  3. theoretical reasoning
  4. normative reasoning.
Answer and explanation

B: inductive reasoning

Inductive reasoning moves from particular observations towards a general conclusion or pattern. Deductive reasoning starts from general premises and derives a more specific conclusion that follows from them.

Question 17

Internal economies of scale are expected to bring about

  1. An increase in short-run average cost
  2. An increase in long-run average cost
  3. A decrease in long-run average cost
  4. A decrease in short-run average cost
Answer and explanation

C: A decrease in long-run average cost

Internal economies of scale arise from expanding a firm's own operations, such as greater specialisation or spreading overhead costs. They lower long-run average cost as the firm increases its scale, rather than describing a movement along one fixed short-run plant curve.

Question 18

In the long- run a monopolist maximizes his profit when the marginal cost equals

  1. total revenue
  2. marginal revenue
  3. average cost
  4. price.
Answer and explanation

B: marginal revenue

At an interior profit maximum, the monopolist chooses output where marginal cost equals marginal revenue, with the appropriate crossing condition. Because a monopolist faces downward-sloping demand, marginal revenue is generally below price.

Question 19

The long run is a period during which a firm

  1. sells input to purchase fixed asset
  2. varies all its inputs
  3. sources all its inputs from within
  4. replaces all its inputs.
Answer and explanation

B: varies all its inputs

The long run is a planning period in which all factors of production can be varied, including plant and equipment. It does not mean the firm must replace every input or source them all internally.

Question 20

An important feature of perfect competition is that

  1. the movement of goods and services is restricted
  2. there is adequate knowledge of existing prices
  3. prices are centrally administered
  4. individual economic units can influence prices.
Answer and explanation

B: there is adequate knowledge of existing prices

Perfect competition assumes buyers and sellers have adequate information about relevant prices and market conditions. Individual firms are price takers, and entry, exit and resource movement are not restricted as part of the basic model.

Question 21

If government sets a binding price ceiling below the equilibrium price in an ordinary competitive market, what happens to quantity demanded?

  1. Quantity demanded and supplied will be equal.
  2. Quantity supplied will be greater than quantity demanded.
  3. Quantity demanded will increase.
  4. Quantity demanded will decrease.
Answer and explanation

C: Quantity demanded will increase.

The lower controlled price leads buyers to demand more along the existing demand curve. Sellers generally supply less, creating excess demand or a shortage. The increase in desired purchases is not necessarily an increase in the amount actually sold.

Question 24

The egg market has these schedules, with price in naira per dozen and quantities in dozens: price 100, demand 410, supply 570; price 90, demand 450, supply 530; price 80, demand 490, supply 490; price 70, demand 530, supply 450; price 60, demand 570, supply 410. What is the equilibrium price?

  1. N100
  2. N110
  3. N60
  4. N80
Answer and explanation

D: N80

Equilibrium occurs where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. At N80 per dozen, both are 490 dozen, so the market clears at that price. Higher listed prices give excess supply and lower listed prices give excess demand.

Question 26

Which of lhe following is an example of a fixed cost?

  1. Fuel cost
  2. Electricity bill
  3. Rent on a building
  4. Transport cost
Answer and explanation

C: Rent on a building

Rent on a building is typically fixed over the relevant short-run lease period: it must be paid even when output changes. Fuel and production-related transport generally vary with activity; electricity may include both fixed and variable elements.

Question 29

The losses suffered by a sole proprietor are

  1. usually equal to the amount invested
  2. usually less than the amount invested
  3. limited to the amount invested
  4. not limited to the amount invesled.
Answer and explanation

D: not limited to the amount invesled.

A sole proprietor bears unlimited liability for business obligations. If business assets are insufficient, personal assets may also be at risk, so the possible financial loss is not limited to the original amount invested in the business.

Question 31

An industry is a

  1. company producing unrelated commodities
  2. group of firms producing related commodities
  3. group of firms producing distinct commodities
  4. firm producing differentiated commodities.
Answer and explanation

B: group of firms producing related commodities

An industry is a group of firms producing the same or closely related goods or services. A single firm is one business within that group, while a collection of unrelated producers does not define one industry.

Question 36

In national income accounting. NNP is derived by subtracting from the GNP

  1. Transfer payments
  2. Indirect taxes
  3. Allowance for wastage
  4. Capital consumption
Answer and explanation

D: Capital consumption

Net national product equals gross national product minus consumption of fixed capital, commonly called depreciation. Subtracting the wearing out or using up of productive capital converts the gross measure into a net measure.

Question 38

An increase in the discount rate is an indication of a Central Banks intention to pursue

  1. an expansionary monetary policy
  2. a disciplined monetary policy
  3. a dynamic monetary policy
  4. a contractionary monetary policy.
Answer and explanation

D: a contractionary monetary policy.

A higher discount rate makes borrowing from the central bank more expensive. In the standard policy framework this tends to restrain bank borrowing and credit expansion, signalling contractionary monetary policy.

Question 42

National debt is an expected outcome of a

  1. deficit budget
  2. consolidated budget
  3. balanced budget
  4. surplus budget.
Answer and explanation

A: deficit budget

A budget deficit arises when government spending exceeds its revenue. If the gap is financed by borrowing, it adds to public debt. A surplus provides resources that can instead be used to repay debt or build assets.

Question 44

Money may not be accepted as a store of value if

  1. its value is unstable
  2. it is easily transferable
  3. its value is stable
  4. it is easily divisible
Answer and explanation

A: its value is unstable

A store of value should preserve purchasing power reasonably reliably over time. If money's value is unstable, people may prefer other assets for saving. Transferability and divisibility help its usefulness but do not by themselves undermine its role as a store of value.

Question 47

GNP is not a good measure of social welfare because there are unrecorded

  1. indirect taxes
  2. social costs
  3. government subsidies
  4. transfer payments
Answer and explanation

B: social costs

National output measures can omit or fail to deduct social costs such as pollution and congestion caused by production. Output may rise while these costs reduce wellbeing, which is one reason GNP alone is an incomplete measure of social welfare.

Question 49

The major reason why nations engage in international trade is that

  1. it allows free movement of factors of production
  2. it makes possible the use of capital- intensive method of production.
  3. nations are substantially different in their endowments of economic resources
  4. it affords the trading nations the opportunity to earn high profit.
Answer and explanation

C: nations are substantially different in their endowments of economic resources

Countries differ in resources, technology and production opportunities, leading to different opportunity costs. Trade allows them to benefit from those differences. Cross-border movement of production factors is not required for goods trade to create gains.
