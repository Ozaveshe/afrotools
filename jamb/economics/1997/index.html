Question 1

With a given level of money income, a consumer maximizes satisfaction from the consumption of goods and services when he

  1. total utility derived from each good or service is increasing at an increasing rate
  2. marginal utility derived from each good or service is increasing at the same rate
  3. marginal utility derived per naira spent is the same for all the goods and services
  4. total utility derived from all the goods and services is large.
Answer and explanation

C: marginal utility derived per naira spent is the same for all the goods and services

At an interior consumer optimum, the marginal utility obtained from the last naira spent is equal across the goods purchased. If one good offered more extra utility per naira, shifting spending towards it would increase satisfaction without increasing the budget.

Question 2

Ike’s scale of preference reveals that he prefers bananas to pawpaw, pawpaw to oranges and oranges to bananas. Ike’s preferences are therefore

  1. inconsistent
  2. consistent
  3. transitive
  4. rational
Answer and explanation

A: inconsistent

Transitive preferences would imply that someone who prefers bananas to pawpaw and pawpaw to oranges also prefers bananas to oranges. Preferring oranges to bananas instead creates a cycle, so the stated preferences are inconsistent with transitivity.

Question 3

One of the features of a free market economy is that

  1. resources are directed by the price mechanism
  2. workers do not earn equal wages
  3. the profit motive is severely constrained
  4. decision on the economy are taken by bureaucratic structures
Answer and explanation

A: resources are directed by the price mechanism

In a free market, prices guide buyers' and producers' decisions and help direct resources towards different uses. This is the defining coordination mechanism; central bureaucratic allocation describes a planned system instead.

Question 6

When a country’s population is experiencing increas ing returns, that country is said to be

  1. overpopulated
  2. economically poor
  3. over producing goods and services
  4. underpopulated
Answer and explanation

D: underpopulated

In optimum-population theory, an underpopulated country has too few people to make the best use of its resources and technology. Additional population can then raise output per person, giving increasing returns as the country moves towards its optimum population.

Question 7

In a country, 45% of the population is below age 15 and 30% is above age 60. Using these age groups to identify dependants, what does this imply?

  1. Dependency ratio is high
  2. Population is optimum
  3. Active population is large
  4. Population is growing according to Malthus theory
Answer and explanation

A: Dependency ratio is high

The two dependent age groups total 45% + 30% = 75%, leaving 25% in the intervening age group. The age-dependency ratio is 75/25 × 100 = 300 dependants per 100 working-age people, which is high. This age-based measure does not identify each person's actual employment.

Question 9

Labour productivity is the ratio of

  1. Labour to output
  2. Worker-hours to output
  3. Output to worker-hours
  4. Average product to worker-hours
Answer and explanation

C: Output to worker-hours

Labour productivity measures output produced per unit of labour input. Dividing output by the worker-hours used gives output per worker-hour. Reversing the ratio measures labour input required per unit of output instead.

Question 10

The type of cost which has to be covered for a firm to continue production in the short-run is the

  1. Overhead cost
  2. Fixed cost
  3. Marginal cost
  4. Average variable cost
Answer and explanation

D: Average variable cost

A firm can continue producing in the short run if its price covers average variable cost at the chosen output. Revenue then covers variable costs and may contribute towards fixed costs, which remain payable even if the firm temporarily shuts down.

Question 15

Given a market demand curve Q = 120 - 2p and a supply curve Q = 4 p, the equilibrium price and quantity respectively are

  1. 20 and 80
  2. 30 and 120
  3. 40 and 60
  4. 60 and 240
Answer and explanation

A: 20 and 80

At equilibrium quantity demanded equals quantity supplied: 120 − 2 p = 4 p. Hence 6 p = 120 and p = 20. Substituting back gives Q = 4 × 20 = 80, which also equals 120 − 2 × 20.

Question 16

If a demand curve that intersects a perfectly inelastic supply curve shifts rightward, then

  1. the equilibrium price and quantity will increase
  2. only the price will increase
  3. only the quantity will increase
  4. the price will remain constant
Answer and explanation

B: only the price will increase

Perfectly inelastic supply is a vertical supply curve, so the available quantity does not respond to price. A rightward demand shift raises the price at which buyers take that fixed quantity; equilibrium quantity remains unchanged.

Question 18

If a positive percentage increase in consumers' income produces a larger percentage increase in quantity demanded, the income elasticity of demand is

  1. Less than one
  2. Equal to one
  3. Greater than one
  4. Equal to zero
Answer and explanation

C: Greater than one

Income elasticity equals the percentage change in quantity demanded divided by the percentage change in income. When both increase and the quantity percentage is larger, this ratio exceeds one: demand is income-elastic.

Question 23

The shares that must be redeemed first at liquidation of a company are

  1. preference shares
  2. non-voting ordinary shares
  3. ordinary shares
  4. debentures
Answer and explanation

A: preference shares

Among the share classes listed, preference shares have priority over ordinary shares in distributions of capital on liquidation, subject to their terms. Debentures represent debt rather than shares; creditors' claims are considered before distributions to shareholders.

Question 24

If farmers operate on the backward-bending portion of a supply curve for their crops, how would their output respond to a rise in crop price?

  1. increase their output
  2. decrease their output
  3. keep their output constant
  4. hoard their output
Answer and explanation

B: decrease their output

On a backward-bending portion of supply, a higher price is associated with a smaller quantity supplied. This describes the assumed curve in the question; it is not a claim that every farmer or every agricultural market behaves that way.

Question 26

If demand for an agricultural product is price-inelastic, what is the effect of a fall in price on the farmer's sales revenue, other things equal?

  1. a price rise results in its fall
  2. a price fall results in its fall
  3. a price fall results in its rise
  4. a price rise leaves the income unchanged
Answer and explanation

B: a price fall results in its fall

With inelastic demand, quantity demanded increases by a smaller percentage than price falls. The extra sales do not offset the lower price, so total sales revenue falls. This is a revenue result; net profit additionally depends on costs.

Question 27

Plywood industries are often located near the source of

  1. finance
  2. labour
  3. power
  4. raw materials
Answer and explanation

D: raw materials

Plywood production needs timber, which is bulky to transport. Locating near timber supplies can reduce inbound transport and handling costs. This makes access to raw materials an important location factor for such industries.

Question 32

The most important attribute of money is

  1. homogeneity
  2. relative scarcity
  3. divisibility
  4. general acceptability
Answer and explanation

D: general acceptability

Money must be generally accepted in payment if it is to function as a medium of exchange. Divisibility and uniformity help money work well, but an object with those qualities still fails as money if people will not accept it.

Question 33

Which of the following is inflationary?

  1. an increase in taxation
  2. an increase in savings
  3. a decrease in money supply
  4. an increase in government expenditure
Answer and explanation

D: an increase in government expenditure

An increase in government expenditure raises aggregate demand, other things equal. If spending presses against available productive capacity, it can raise prices. Higher saving, monetary contraction and demand-reducing taxation generally work in the opposite direction in this demand-side comparison.

Question 37

The use of legally permissible means to reduce tax liabilities is known as tax

  1. evasion
  2. avoidance
  3. relief
  4. exemption
Answer and explanation

B: avoidance

In the standard distinction, tax avoidance uses legally permitted arrangements to reduce tax liability, whereas tax evasion involves unlawful concealment or non-payment. A relief or exemption is a specific provision rather than the general term for this behaviour.

Question 40

If the marginal propensity to consume is 0.75 and private investment increases by N10 billion while government expenditure decrease by N15 billion, GDP will decrease by

  1. N12 billion
  2. N15 billion
  3. N20 billion
  4. N25 billion
Answer and explanation

C: N20 billion

The simple expenditure multiplier is 1/(1 − 0.75) = 4. The net change in autonomous spending is N10 billion − N15 billion = −N5 billion. Multiplying by 4 gives a GDP change of −N20 billion, a decrease of N20 billion.

Question 41

Investment is N100 million and consumption is C = N200 million + 0.75 Y. Given Y = C + I, where Y is income and I is investment, what is income to the nearest million?

  1. N100 million
  2. N300 million
  3. N1,000 million
  4. N1,200 million
Answer and explanation

D: N1,200 million

Substitute consumption and investment into the identity: Y = 200 + 0.75 Y + 100, in millions of naira. Therefore 0.25 Y = 300 and Y = 1,200 million. Checking: consumption is 1,100 million, which plus investment of 100 million gives 1,200 million.

Question 42

The total money values of all goods and services produced in a country during a certain period is measured by using the

  1. Income method
  2. Expenditure method
  3. Output method
  4. Factor method
Answer and explanation

C: Output method

The output method measures production by adding the value added by producers, or equivalently the value of final goods and services. Intermediate inputs must not be counted again, because their value is already contained in final output.

Question 47

Counter trading is an international arrangement whereby

  1. goods and services are exchanged in a manner similar to barter
  2. goods and services are sold on the counter
  3. crude oil is sold to a foreign country
  4. goods are shipped to countries on which the United Nations has placed a trade embargo
Answer and explanation

A: goods and services are exchanged in a manner similar to barter

Countertrade links an international purchase to a reciprocal commitment to take goods or services in return. It resembles barter, although arrangements can include money and more complex linked transactions. It is not simply selling goods over a retail counter.

Question 49

The organization which provide credits to help countries achieve short-term balance in their balance of payments is the

  1. Economic Community of West African States
  2. International Monetary Fund
  3. International Bank for Reconstruction and Development
  4. African Development Bank
Answer and explanation

B: International Monetary Fund

The IMF provides financing to help member countries address balance-of-payments difficulties while they implement economic adjustment. Development banks primarily support development financing, so the IMF is the organisation identified by this balance-of-payments role.
