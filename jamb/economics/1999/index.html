Question 1

In a firm three employees earn N5,500 each, four earn N3,300 each, two earn N5,000 each and one earns N7,000. The mean income of the employees is

  1. N5,200
  2. N4,760
  3. N4,670
  4. N2,080
Answer and explanation

C: N4,670

Total pay is 3 × N5,500 + 4 × N3,300 + 2 × N5,000 + N7,000 = N46,700. There are 3 + 4 + 2 + 1 = 10 employees, so the mean is N46,700/10 = N4,670 per employee.

Question 2

Abubakar has the choice of buying either a house or a Mercedes Benz car for N1.5m plus NO.5m running cost. If he decides to buy the Mercedes Benz car, his opportunity cost is

  1. N1.5m
  2. N2.0m
  3. the house
  4. the car
Answer and explanation

C: the house

Opportunity cost is the next-best alternative forgone. Because Abubakar chooses the car instead of the house, the house is what he gives up. The purchase price and running cost are money costs, which are distinct from identifying the forgone alternative.

Question 3

The shape of a production possibility frontier is determined by the

  1. increasing relative costs
  2. returns to scale
  3. diminishing returns to a fixed factor
  4. increasing returns to a variable factor.
Answer and explanation

A: increasing relative costs

The usual bowed-out production possibility frontier reflects increasing opportunity cost: producing more of one good requires giving up progressively more of the other. This occurs when resources differ in suitability for the two activities; constant opportunity cost would give a straight frontier.

Question 4

Normative economics deals with

  1. what is and not what should be
  2. facts and not figures
  3. facts and figures
  4. value judgements
Answer and explanation

D: value judgements

Normative economics makes value judgements about what ought to happen, such as which policy is fair or desirable. Positive economics instead describes or explains what happens and develops claims that can be tested against evidence.

Question 8

In a planned economy, what shall be produced is determined primarily by

  1. What the consumer wants
  2. Government decisions
  3. Price mechanism
  4. The pattern of consumer spending
Answer and explanation

B: Government decisions

In a planned economy, the central planning authority makes the main decisions about which goods and services to produce. Consumer preferences may inform planning, but they do not allocate resources primarily through decentralised market prices.

Question 14

The downward sloping part of the long-run average cost curve of a firm may be attributable to

  1. diminishing returns
  2. the law of variable proportions
  3. diseconomies of scale
  4. increasing returns to scale.
Answer and explanation

D: increasing returns to scale.

When output can increase more than proportionately as all inputs expand, unit costs can fall. This is associated with increasing returns to scale and the downward part of long-run average cost. Diminishing returns to one variable input is a short-run concept.

Question 15

The main function of price in the economic system is to

  1. allocate scarce resources among competing uses
  2. ensure equitable distribution of income and wealth
  3. determine the demand for and the supply of goods and services
  4. remove all the constraints on production of goods and services.
Answer and explanation

A: allocate scarce resources among competing uses

Relative prices signal scarcity and help coordinate buyers' and sellers' decisions. They guide resources among competing uses. This allocation role does not guarantee equal incomes or remove every technological and resource constraint.

Question 17

One of the characteristics of an imperfect market is the

  1. large number of buyers and sellers in the market
  2. awareness of market conditions by buyers
  3. availability of substitutes
  4. heterogeneity of products.
Answer and explanation

D: heterogeneity of products.

Product differentiation means sellers offer goods that buyers regard as different, giving firms some influence over their own prices. This is one source of imperfect competition. Perfect competition instead assumes a homogeneous product among sellers.

Question 19

In perfect competition, every firm is a price

  1. Maker
  2. Taker
  3. Giver
  4. Bidder
Answer and explanation

B: Taker

An individual perfectly competitive firm is too small to influence the market price. It accepts that price and chooses its output accordingly, so it is a price taker rather than a price maker.

Question 20

Total fixed cost is N1,000, total variable cost is N2,500 and output is 100 units. What is average total cost per unit?

  1. N60
  2. N45
  3. N35
  4. N30
Answer and explanation

C: N35

Total cost equals fixed cost plus variable cost: N1,000 + N2,500 = N3,500. Dividing by 100 units gives average total cost of N35 per unit. Both types of cost must be included.

Question 23

A land-tenure arrangement that fragments farmland into small holdings can impede agricultural production because

  1. Land fragmentation makes possible easy access to land ownership
  2. It discourages specialisation of factors of production
  3. It limits opportunities for large-scale agricultural production
  4. Small landholders generally do not get enough fertilisers to use
Answer and explanation

C: It limits opportunities for large-scale agricultural production

Scattered small plots can make mechanisation and coordinated large-scale operations difficult. Fragmentation can therefore limit economies of scale in farming. This explains a possible effect of that tenure arrangement rather than attributing every agricultural difficulty to land ownership.

  2. encourage the production and processing of export commodities
  3. erect structures that will facilitate the processing of imported raw materials
  4. erect economic structures near the port
Answer and explanation

An export processing zone is intended to encourage production and processing for external markets, often through infrastructure and special operating arrangements. Being near a port or handling imported inputs can support that purpose but is not its primary objective.

Question 27

Concentrating industries in one place is advantageous because there are gains in terms of

  1. external economies
  2. internal economies
  3. economies of scale
  4. cost economies.
Answer and explanation

A: external economies

External economies arise from an industry's or locality's wider development, such as a shared skilled labour pool, specialised suppliers and transport links. These benefits can reach several nearby firms, unlike internal economies arising from a single firm's own expansion.

  2. selling up of industries for regional development
  3. a situation in which local industries are set up to produce goods formerly imported
  4. the bann ing of imports which are substitutes for domestically produced goods.
Answer and explanation

C: a situation in which local industries are set up to produce goods formerly imported

Import-substitution industrialisation develops domestic industries to make goods previously imported. Its defining feature is replacing foreign production with local production, not merely banning imports or distributing industry evenly among regions.

Question 30

Optimum population is defined as the level of population at which

  1. per capita output is maximized
  2. population is neither increasing nor decreasing
  3. total national income is maximized
  4. marginal efficiency of population is maximized”
Answer and explanation

A: per capita output is maximized

Optimum population is the population size that maximises output per person for given resources and technology. It is not necessarily the size at which total output is highest, and it does not require zero population growth.

Question 31

When a worker earns more than the wage just sufficient to keep him in his job the excess income is called

  1. an opportunity cost
  2. an economic rent
  3. transfer eamings
  4. surplus value
Answer and explanation

B: an economic rent

Economic rent is the part of a factor's payment above its transfer earnings, the minimum required to keep it in its present employment. The worker's excess above that minimum is therefore economic rent.

Question 36

Non-bank financial intermediaries do not

  1. provide credit facilities
  2. accept deposits
  3. accept demand deposits
  4. sell shares.
Answer and explanation

C: accept demand deposits

In the standard bank/non-bank distinction, non-bank intermediaries can provide finance or sell investment products but do not operate ordinary demand-deposit current accounts used for payments. Some accept other forms of savings, so excluding all deposits would be too broad.

Question 37

Inflation which is caused mainly by increases in the prices of factors of production is described as

  1. Cost-push inflation
  2. Demand-pull inflation
  3. Crawling inflation
  4. Spiral inflation
Answer and explanation

A: Cost-push inflation

Higher input prices raise firms' production costs and can reduce aggregate supply, putting upward pressure on output prices. Inflation originating from that cost increase is cost-push inflation, rather than inflation initially caused by excessive aggregate demand.

Question 38

The relationship between the value of money and the price level is

  1. direct
  2. unpredictable
  3. diverse
  4. inverse.
Answer and explanation

D: inverse.

Money's value in terms of goods and services is its purchasing power. As the general price level rises, a fixed amount of money buys less; when prices fall it buys more. The relationship is therefore inverse.

Question 45

In the standard competitive market model, a sales tax will leave the price paid by buyers unchanged if

  1. supply is inelastic
  2. demand is inelastic
  3. supply is perfectly elastic
  4. demand is perfectly elastic.
Answer and explanation

D: demand is perfectly elastic.

Perfectly elastic demand is horizontal: buyers will not pay more than the given price. A tax therefore lowers the net price received by sellers rather than raising the buyers' price, with quantity adjusting. Sellers bear the tax in this limiting case.

Question 48

Gains from trade depends on

  1. comparative advantage
  2. absolute advantage
  3. distributive cost advantage
  4. absolute cost advantage.
Answer and explanation

A: comparative advantage

Comparative advantage means a lower opportunity cost of producing a good. Countries can gain by specialising according to relative opportunity costs and exchanging, even when one country has an absolute productivity advantage in every good.

Question 49

Favourable terms of trade is importaflt to a country because it facilitates

  1. economic prosperity for importers and exporters
  2. export of hard currency by the government
  3. economic prosperity for the country
  4. import of hard currency by the government.
Answer and explanation

C: economic prosperity for the country

More favourable terms of trade mean a country's exports can buy more imports per unit exported. This increases its trading purchasing power and can support national welfare, although overall prosperity also depends on production, trade volumes and distribution of the gains.
