Question 1

The price of a good rises from N5 to N8 and the quantity demanded falls from 200 to 190 units. Over this price range, the demand curve is

  1. fairly inelastic
  2. fairly elastic
  3. perfectly inelastic
  4. perfectly elastic
Answer and explanation

A: fairly inelastic

Using initial values, quantity falls by 10/200 = 5%, while price rises by 3/5 = 60%. Elasticity magnitude is 0.05/0.60, about 0.083, below one. Demand changes but is relatively inelastic.

Question 2

In economic analysis, a statement is said to be normative if it

  1. can be tested scientifically
  2. relates to value judgment
  3. is contradictory
  4. is incorrect
Answer and explanation

B: relates to value judgment

A normative statement expresses a value judgement about what ought to happen. A positive statement instead describes a claim that can, in principle, be tested against evidence.

Question 3

Utility is the satisfaction derived from

  1. production
  2. distribution
  3. consumption
  4. demand
Answer and explanation

C: consumption

Utility means the satisfaction a consumer obtains from consuming a good or service. Production and distribution make goods available, but consumption is the activity from which this satisfaction is derived.

Question 4

If there is an increase in demand without a corresponding increase in supply, there will be

  1. a fall in price
  2. a rise in price
  3. a shift in the supply curve to the right
  4. a shift in the demand curve to the left
Answer and explanation

B: a rise in price

An outward shift of demand with an unchanged upward-sloping supply curve creates excess demand at the old price. Buyers' competition raises the equilibrium price.

Question 8

When two variables are positively related, the graph of the relationship

  1. is a straight line
  2. is a downward-sloping curve
  3. is an upward-sloping curve
  4. has a negative intercept
Answer and explanation

C: is an upward-sloping curve

A positive relationship means that higher values of one variable are associated with higher values of the other. Its graph slopes upward; it need not be a straight line.

Question 9

For an inferior good, a decrease in real income leads to

  1. an outward shift of the demand curve
  2. an inward shift of the demand curve
  3. a lower equilibrium price
  4. a change in quantity demanded
Answer and explanation

A: an outward shift of the demand curve

An inferior good has negative income elasticity: demand increases when real income falls, other things equal. This is an outward shift of the demand curve rather than a movement caused by its own price.

  3. private ownership and control

Question 12

The long-run average cost curve is called a planning curve because it shows what happens to costs when

  1. variable inputs are increased
  2. fixed factors are increased
  3. different sizes of plants are built
  4. a bigger size of plants is built
Answer and explanation

C: different sizes of plants are built

The long-run average cost curve compares the lowest average costs achievable at different output levels when plant size can change. It helps a firm choose among different scales of production.

Question 21

For a firm, value added can be defined as the difference between the

  1. value of its output and the cost of production
  2. total revenue and total cost
  3. input prices and product prices
  4. value of its output and inputs purchased from other firms
Answer and explanation

D: value of its output and inputs purchased from other firms

Value added is the value of a firm's output minus the value of intermediate goods and services bought from other firms. Subtracting all production costs instead would give a profit measure.

Question 22

  2. mobilized as short-term debt instruments
  3. Mobilized as long-term debt instrument
  4. Traded on a recognized stock market
Answer and explanation

A listed security has been admitted to a recognized stock exchange under that market's listing rules. Listing concerns eligibility for exchange trading, not whether the instrument is short-term debt.

Question 25

The term “Near Money” is best described as

  1. a financial asset that is convertible to cash
  2. a financial instrument that is readily convertible to cash
  3. a government financial instrument that is convertible to cash
  4. time deposits with low interest rates
Answer and explanation

B: a financial instrument that is readily convertible to cash

Near money consists of liquid financial assets that can be converted into money readily but are not themselves the ordinary means of payment. Ready convertibility distinguishes the best definition here.

Question 26

  1. funds mobilized are private or public
  2. securities are primary or secondary
  3. debt instruments provided are long-term or short-term
  4. securities are in debentures or ordinary shares
Answer and explanation

C: debt instruments provided are long-term or short-term

Money markets deal primarily in short-term financial instruments, while capital markets provide longer-term finance. The maturity of the instruments is the relevant distinction.

Question 29

The major objectives of a revenue allocation formula in a country is to

  1. share revenue between the public and private sectors
  2. ensure the financial viability of the country
  3. share revenue between the different tiers of government
  4. divert revenue from areas of surplus to areas of need
Answer and explanation

C: share revenue between the different tiers of government

A revenue allocation formula distributes public revenue among levels of government, such as federal, state and local authorities. It determines their shares rather than dividing revenue between private firms and government.

Question 30

The effectiveness of devaluation as a solution to a balance of payments problems depends on the

  1. relative elasticities of demand
  2. foreign investments of business
  3. value of visible and invisible goods
  4. value of invisible items of both domestic and foreign trades
Answer and explanation

A: relative elasticities of demand

The trade-balance response to devaluation depends on how strongly export and import quantities respond to price changes. Demand elasticities determine whether those volume changes outweigh the price effects.

Question 32

An advantage of the soleproprietorship over the partnership form of business organization is that

  1. it enjoys limited liability for debt in the event of failure
  2. its existence is limited by an individual's life span
  3. it relies on the decision of friends to succeed
  4. the possibility of conflict in management is virtually non-existent

A sole proprietor makes the ownership decisions alone, avoiding disagreements between partners over management. This does not remove business risk or give the proprietor limited liability.

Capital expenditure acquires or improves assets that provide benefits over time; recurrent expenditure funds ongoing operations. The nature of what is purchased is the key distinction.

Question 35

To compare the standard of living among nations, the most widely used economic indicator is

  1. per capita nominal income
  2. real domestic output
  3. real gross national income
  4. real per capita income
Answer and explanation

D: real per capita income

Real income per person adjusts national income for both population size and price changes. It is therefore more useful than total or nominal income for comparing average material living standards.

Question 39

The theory of comparative advantage states that a commodity should be produced in that nation where the

  1. absolute cost is least
  2. absolute money cost is least
  3. opportunity cost is least
  4. production possibility curve increases
Answer and explanation

C: opportunity cost is least

Comparative advantage means producing a good at a lower opportunity cost: fewer units of other output must be sacrificed. It differs from absolute advantage, which concerns input productivity.

Question 40

The effect of an increase in the personal income tax is to

  1. raise the absolute price level
  2. distort the economy
  3. reduce unemployment
  4. reduce the disposable income
Answer and explanation

D: reduce the disposable income

Disposable income is income remaining after direct personal taxes. Holding gross income constant, an increase in personal income tax leaves households with less income available to spend or save.

Question 42

The demand for labour is an example of

  1. competitive demand
  2. derived demand
  3. composite demand
  4. joint demand
Answer and explanation

B: derived demand

Firms hire workers because labour helps produce goods and services demanded by customers. Demand for labour is therefore derived from demand for the output it helps create.

Question 49

When a union is composed of workers with same skill, it is called

  1. an industrial union
  2. a workers' union
  3. a craft union
  4. a technical union
Answer and explanation

C: a craft union

A craft union organizes workers who share a particular trade or skill, potentially across different employers. An industrial union instead groups workers across occupations within an industry.
