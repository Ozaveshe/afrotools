Question 7

Double fertilisation producing an embryo and endosperm is characteristic of

  1. angiosperms
  2. bryophytes
  3. pteridophytes
  4. algae
Answer and explanation

A: angiosperms

In angiosperms, one sperm fertilises the egg while another fuses with the central cell to initiate endosperm development. This embryo-plus-endosperm outcome is the characteristic double fertilisation of flowering plants.

Question 9

Mineral salts can be absorbed into the roots by

  1. osmosis only
  2. osmosis and diffusion
  3. diffusion and active transport
  4. imbibition only
Answer and explanation

C: diffusion and active transport

Mineral ions can enter root cells passively down suitable electrochemical gradients or by transport requiring metabolic energy. Osmosis specifically describes water movement, and imbibition is water uptake by materials.

Question 10

Which of the following is a common characteristic of crustaceans?

  1. Possession of a pair of antennae
  2. Possession of two pairs of antennae
  3. Each segment has a pair of walking legs
  4. Four pairs of walking legs on the cephalothorax
Answer and explanation

B: Possession of two pairs of antennae

Typical crustaceans possess two pairs of antennae: antennules and antennae. Their appendages are specialised rather than all serving as walking legs, and walking-leg counts differ among crustacean groups.

Question 12

The body of a snail is divided into head

  1. thorax and abdomen
  2. visceral mass and abdomen
  3. thorax and foot
  4. visceral mass and foot
Answer and explanation

D: visceral mass and foot

A snail has a head, a muscular foot and a visceral mass containing many internal organs. These are molluscan body regions; thorax and abdomen are not the corresponding three-part division for a snail.

Question 15

In a dicot stem, a companion cell is closely associated with a

  1. endodermal cell
  2. xylem vessel
  3. sieve tube
  4. pericyclic fibre
Answer and explanation

C: sieve tube

Companion cells are closely associated with sieve-tube elements in angiosperm phloem. They help maintain the living transport cells and participate in loading or unloading sugars for translocation.

Question 18

If a healthy plant is kept at a low positive light intensity,

  1. the rate of respiration may equal that of photosynthesis
  2. more carbon dioxide and water are taken in
  3. respiration may be halted
  4. the volume of oxygen released increases
Answer and explanation

A: the rate of respiration may equal that of photosynthesis

At the light compensation point, photosynthetic carbon uptake balances carbon dioxide release by respiration. Respiration continues in dim light, so low illumination does not stop it or necessarily increase oxygen output.

Question 19

Pancreatic juice contains amylopsin together with which enzyme and enzyme precursor?

  1. pepsin and trypsinogen
  2. rennin and steapsin
  3. steapsin and trypsinogen
  4. steapsin and ptyalin
Answer and explanation

C: steapsin and trypsinogen

Pancreatic juice contains pancreatic amylase (amylopsin), pancreatic lipase (steapsin) and trypsinogen. Trypsinogen is an inactive enzyme precursor that is activated in the small intestine, limiting digestion of pancreatic tissue.

Question 20

Which listed organ participates in destroying aged erythrocytes?

  1. pancreas
  2. liver
  3. lymph nodes
  4. kidney
Answer and explanation

B: liver

Macrophages in the liver help remove old red blood cells and recycle their components. The spleen and bone marrow also participate, so the liver is one removal site rather than the only one.

Question 22

Considering the ABO system, agglutination occurs when

  1. Group A serum mixes with Group A erythrocytes
  2. Group A serum mixes with Group B erythrocytes
  3. Group AB serum mixes with Group O erythrocytes
  4. Group B serum mixes with Group B erythrocytes
Answer and explanation

B: Group A serum mixes with Group B erythrocytes

Group A serum normally contains anti-B antibodies. These bind B antigens on group B erythrocytes and cross-link the cells, causing agglutination. Matching group A or B combinations do not have that ABO incompatibility.

Question 32

The irreversible life process by which new protoplasm is added to increase the size and weight of an organism can be termed

  1. anabolism
  2. catabolism
  3. growth
  4. development
Answer and explanation

C: growth

Growth is an irreversible increase in an organism’s size or mass through addition of living material. Anabolism builds molecules that contribute to growth, while development includes changes in form and differentiation.

Question 33

Which group of plant hormones can promote growth and enlargement of developing fruits?

  1. cytokinin, abscisic acid and ethylene
  2. gibberellin, ethylene and abscisic acid
  3. auxin, abscisic acid and ethylene
  4. auxin, cytokinin and gibberellin
Answer and explanation

D: auxin, cytokinin and gibberellin

Auxins, cytokinins and gibberellins can promote cell expansion, cell division and fruit growth. The response depends on the plant and developmental stage; these hormones are not interchangeable with ripening or stress signals.

Question 36

The sister chromatids of a duplicated chromosome are joined at a region called the

  1. spindle
  2. chromatid
  3. centromere
  4. aster
Answer and explanation

C: centromere

The two sister chromatids of a duplicated chromosome are joined at the centromere region. During division, spindle microtubules attach at kinetochores assembled there and help separate the chromatids.

Question 37

When the two alleles present in an organism are of the same type, the genotype is described as

  1. heterozygous
  2. heterogamous
  3. homozygous
  4. homologous
Answer and explanation

C: homozygous

An organism is homozygous at a locus when its two alleles are the same, such as AA or aa. Different alleles, such as Aa, make it heterozygous; this describes genotype rather than mating behaviour.

Question 41

Lichen is an example of

  1. a saprophytic organism
  2. a symbiotic association
  3. an epiphytic plant
  4. a carnivorous plant
Answer and explanation

B: a symbiotic association

A lichen is an association of a fungus with a photosynthetic partner, typically a green alga or cyanobacterium. The partners live together in an organised structure rather than forming a single flowering or carnivorous plant.

Question 44

Which of the following diseases can be caused by a bacterium?

  2. poliomyelitis
  3. malaria
  4. syphilis
Answer and explanation

Syphilis is caused by the bacterium Treponema pallidum. Ringworm is a fungal infection, poliomyelitis is caused by poliovirus and malaria is caused by Plasmodium parasites.

Question 46

A 5.0 g oven-dried soil sample weighs 4.8 g after furnace heating for 24 hours and cooling. Assuming all mass lost represents the humus being estimated, how much is present per 100 g of dry soil?

  1. 40.0 g per 100 g dry soil
  2. 4.4 g per 100 g dry soil
  3. 4.0 g per 100 g dry soil
  4. 0.4 g per 100 g dry soil
Answer and explanation

C: 4.0 g per 100 g dry soil

The mass lost is 5.0 − 4.8 = 0.2 g. Relative to the initial 5.0 g of dry soil, this is (0.2 ÷ 5.0) × 100 = 4.0 g per 100 g. This estimate assumes that the mass loss represents the organic fraction being measured.
