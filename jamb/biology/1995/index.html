Question 2

Which of the following organelles are likely to be present in cells that are actively respiring and photosynthesizing?

  1. Nucleolus and centrioles
  2. Mitochondria and chloroplasts
  3. Lysosomes and ribosomes
  4. Golgi apparatus and endoplasmic reticulum
Answer and explanation

B: Mitochondria and chloroplasts

Chloroplasts carry out photosynthesis, while mitochondria support aerobic cellular respiration. A photosynthetic plant cell can contain both and use each organelle for its distinct energy-conversion pathway.

Question 3

Which reproductive feature is found in fungi, algae, mosses and ferns?

  1. show alternation of generations
  2. reproduce sexually by conjugation
  3. produce spores that are dispersed
  4. possess chlorophyll in their tissues
Answer and explanation

C: produce spores that are dispersed

Fungi and many algae, mosses and ferns form spores that can disperse and develop into new individuals. They do not all contain chlorophyll, and their life cycles do not all use the same reproductive mechanisms.

  1. Rat
  2. Lizard
  3. Fish
  4. Frog

A frog has moist, permeable skin through which oxygen and carbon dioxide can diffuse. This cutaneous respiration supplements lung breathing and is an important part of amphibian gas exchange.

Question 9

Birds maintain their body temperature with the help of their

  1. blood which maintains constant temperature
  2. feathers which cover the body
  3. skin which conserves moisture
  4. veins which transport fluid of constant temperature to all body tissues
Answer and explanation

B: feathers which cover the body

Feathers trap air near the body and reduce heat loss, providing insulation. Birds also produce metabolic heat and regulate circulation and behaviour, so feathers support temperature control rather than generating heat themselves.

Question 10

Secondary thickening in dicotyledonous stem is controlled by the

  1. xylem
  2. phloem
  3. cambium
  4. pericycle
Answer and explanation

C: cambium

The vascular cambium produces secondary xylem inward and secondary phloem outward, increasing stem thickness. Cork cambium also contributes to secondary growth by producing protective outer tissues.

Question 16

The end product of glycolysis in plants and animals is

  1. pyruvic acid
  2. citric acid
  3. aspartic acid
  4. malic acid
Answer and explanation

A: pyruvic acid

Glycolysis splits a six-carbon glucose molecule into two three-carbon pyruvate molecules. It also yields a small net amount of ATP and reduced NAD. Pyruvate then enters other pathways according to cellular conditions.

Question 17

Within plant tissues, respiratory gases move around the cells mainly through the

  1. lenticels
  2. stomata
  3. guard cells
  4. intercellular spaces
Answer and explanation

D: intercellular spaces

Intercellular spaces allow gases to diffuse through plant tissues. Stomata and lenticels provide openings to the outside, while the connected internal air spaces distribute gases among cells.

Question 19

In the simple antagonistic-muscle model of bending the elbow, the biceps muscle

  1. contracts and the triceps muscle relaxes
  2. relaxes and the triceps muscle contracts
  3. and the triceps muscle contract
  4. and the triceps muscle relax
Answer and explanation

A: contracts and the triceps muscle relaxes

During a simple elbow-flexion movement, the biceps contracts as a flexor while the triceps, its antagonist, relaxes. Their opposing actions allow controlled bending and straightening of the arm.

Question 20

When a healthy shoot of a flowering plant is illuminated from one side, auxins accumulate on the

  1. non-illuminated side of the shoot
  2. illuminated side of the shoot
  3. upper side of the shoot
  4. lower side of the shoot
Answer and explanation

A: non-illuminated side of the shoot

In a young shoot exposed to light from one side, auxin becomes more concentrated on the shaded side. Greater elongation there bends the shoot toward the light, producing positive phototropism.

Question 23

The term caryopsis is used to describe a fruit in which the

  1. testa and pericarp are separate
  2. seed and endocarp are fused
  3. testa and pericarp are fused
  4. seed coat and fruit wall are impermeable
Answer and explanation

C: testa and pericarp are fused

A caryopsis is a dry, one-seeded fruit in which the seed coat is closely fused with the fruit wall. Cereal grains are examples. This fusion distinguishes it from fruits whose seed coat and pericarp remain separate.

Question 32

In a food chain involving a primary producer, a primary consumer as well as a secondary consumer, the sharing of trophic energy is in the form that the

  1. primary consumer has more energy than the primary producer
  2. secondary consumer takes up all the energy contents of the primary consumer
  3. energy is shared equally between the three groups of organism
  4. secondary consumer gets only a small portion of the energy captured by the primary producer
Answer and explanation

D: secondary consumer gets only a small portion of the energy captured by the primary producer

Only part of the energy at one feeding level becomes available to the next. Organisms use energy in metabolism and release heat, and not all biomass is consumed or assimilated, so less reaches secondary consumers.

Colonization of a bare rock surface is termed

  1. evolution
  2. speciation
  3. primary succession
  4. secondary succession
Answer and explanation

Primary succession begins on a surface without an established soil community, such as newly exposed bare rock. Pioneer organisms contribute to changes that can eventually allow other species to establish.

  2. Targeted application of approved molluscicides by trained programme staff
  3. Screening windows and doors with mosquito nets

Answer and explanation

Question 42

The chromosome complement of a normal diploid somatic cell is represented by

  1. 2^N
  2. 23
  3. 2N
  4. N
Answer and explanation

C: 2N

A diploid cell contains two sets of chromosomes, conventionally written 2N or 2n. N denotes one set. The actual number differs among species, so a fixed count such as 23 is not a universal somatic-cell number.
