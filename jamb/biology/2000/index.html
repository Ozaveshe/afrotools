Question 3

Which features are typically associated with monocots?

  1. Fibrous root system, branched network of veins and one seed leaf
  2. Fibrous root system, two seed leaves and floral parts in threes
  3. One seed leaf, petals in threes or groups of threes and parallel venation of leaves
  4. One seed leaf, net-veined leaves and petals in three or multiples of three
Answer and explanation

C: One seed leaf, petals in threes or groups of threes and parallel venation of leaves

Typical monocots have one cotyledon, parallel leaf veins and floral parts arranged in threes or multiples of three. These characteristics are useful together when comparing monocots with typical eudicots.

Question 4

The set of fins that controls steering, balancing and change of direction and pitch in fish is

  1. dorsal and anal
  2. pectoral and pelvic
  3. caudal and dorsal
  4. anal and pelvic
Answer and explanation

B: pectoral and pelvic

The paired pectoral and pelvic fins help a fish manoeuvre and maintain its orientation. They can affect steering, balance and pitching, while the caudal fin is particularly important in propulsion.

Question 6

A true coelom is absent in which animal group?

  1. mollusca
  2. reptilia
  3. arthropoda
  4. Cnidaria (coelenterates)
Answer and explanation

D: Cnidaria (coelenterates)

Cnidarians have a digestive cavity but no true coelom lined by mesoderm. A digestive cavity is not a coelom. Molluscs and arthropods have a reduced coelom, while reptiles have a well-developed body cavity.

Question 18

Short-sightedness can be corrected by lenses which are

  1. convex
  2. biconvex
  3. plano-convex
  4. concave
Answer and explanation

D: concave

A concave lens diverges incoming light. This reduces the excessive convergence associated with myopia, helping distant objects focus on the retina rather than in front of it.

Question 20

For net growth in an organism’s body material, the rate of

  1. food storage must be low
  2. catabolism must exceed that of anabolism
  3. anabolism must exceed that of catabolism
  4. food storage must be high
Answer and explanation

C: anabolism must exceed that of catabolism

Anabolism builds complex substances, whereas catabolism breaks them down. Net accumulation of body material requires synthesis to exceed breakdown over the growth period.

Question 22

  1. meat
  2. butter
  3. sugar
  4. biscuits
Answer and explanation

B: butter

Butter is predominantly fat, which releases more energy per gram on oxidation than carbohydrate or protein. For equal masses of ordinary forms of these foods, butter therefore usually supplies the most energy.

The chamber of the mammalian heart with the thickest wall is the

  1. right ventricle
  2. left auricle
  3. right auricle
  4. left ventricle
Answer and explanation

D: left ventricle

The left ventricle pumps blood through the systemic circulation and must generate higher pressure than the right ventricle. Its muscular wall is consequently the thickest heart chamber wall.

Question 24

Serum differs from blood plasma because it

  1. contains blood cell and fibrinogen
  2. contains soluble food and mineral salts
  3. lacks the blood protein fibrinogen
  4. lacks blood cells and albumin
Answer and explanation

C: lacks the blood protein fibrinogen

Serum is the liquid remaining after blood has clotted. Fibrinogen is converted into fibrin during clot formation, so serum lacks the fibrinogen present in plasma.

Question 27

A dry, air-containing soil sample has bulk volume q cm³. Water initially occupies p cm³. After mixing, allowing trapped air to escape without loss of water or soil, the total mixture volume is r cm³. Which relation follows?

  1. r=p+q
  2. r>p+q
  3. q=r-p
  4. r<p+q
Answer and explanation

D: r<p+q

The initial soil volume includes air-filled pore spaces. When water enters those spaces and air escapes, the final mixture occupies less than the sum of the separately measured water and soil volumes.

Question 41

The pollutants that contribute to the depletion of the ozone layer in the atmosphere are

  1. radioactive materials
  2. oxides of sulphur
  3. oxides of carbon
  4. chlorofluorocarbons
Answer and explanation

D: chlorofluorocarbons

Chlorofluorocarbons can reach the stratosphere, where ultraviolet light releases chlorine that catalyses ozone destruction. This reduces the protective stratospheric ozone layer.

Question 47

The mutation theory of organic evolution was propounded by

  1. Gregor Mendel
  2. Hugo de Vries
  3. Jean Lamarck
  4. Charles Darwin
Answer and explanation

B: Hugo de Vries

Hugo de Vries developed the historical mutation theory, presenting it in Die Mutationstheorie. He emphasised sudden heritable changes as a source of new forms, distinct from Darwin’s emphasis on natural selection.
