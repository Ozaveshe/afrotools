Question 1

An Amoeba and an unfertilised chicken ovum are each

  1. animal tissues
  2. organelles
  3. a single cell
  4. organisms
Answer and explanation

C: a single cell

Amoeba is a unicellular organism, while an unfertilised chicken ovum is a single reproductive cell. The ovum is not the whole shelled egg with its surrounding membranes and albumen.

Question 3

The organisms that move by means of flagella include

  1. Chlamydomonas and Euglena
  2. Planaria and Amoeba
  3. Amoeba and Hydra
  4. Paramecium and Planaria
Answer and explanation

A: Chlamydomonas and Euglena

Chlamydomonas and Euglena can swim using flagella. Amoeba moves by pseudopodia, and Paramecium uses many cilia rather than the flagellar swimming pattern of the first pair.

Question 4

Which pair occurs in photosynthetic plant cells but is absent from animal cells?

  1. cell membrane and cytoplasm
  2. chromatin and nucleolus
  3. cell wall and chloroplast
  4. cell membrane and lysosome
Answer and explanation

C: cell wall and chloroplast

Photosynthetic plant cells can have a cellulose wall and chloroplasts, whereas animal cells lack both. Cell membranes, cytoplasm, chromatin and nucleoli are shared eukaryotic features.

Question 14

Plants tend to prevent overcrowding by means of efficient

  1. water uptake
  2. seed germination
  3. pollination
  4. seed dispersal
Answer and explanation

D: seed dispersal

Seed dispersal carries offspring away from the parent and from one another. This can reduce local competition for light, water, nutrients and space.

Question 18

Supporting xylem tissue is typically least developed in

  1. mangrove swamp plants
  2. grassland plants
  3. submerged water plants
  4. desert plants
Answer and explanation

C: submerged water plants

Submerged plants receive mechanical support from the surrounding water and can absorb water across much of their surface. Their supporting and conducting xylem is often reduced compared with terrestrial plants.

Question 22

In his theory of evolution, Darwin implied that

  1. the struggle for existence among living organisms is sporadic
  2. the most successful organisms are those best adapted to their environment
  3. organs of the body which are not regularly, used by an organism will disappear
  4. any traits acquired by an organism during its lifetime can be passed on to its offspring
Answer and explanation

B: the most successful organisms are those best adapted to their environment

Natural selection favours inherited variants that improve reproductive success in the current environment. Individuals carrying favourable variants tend to leave more descendants, changing their frequency over generations.

Question 28

Examples of organisms in which extracellular digestion occurs are

  1. Fungus, Loranthus and housefly
  2. Rhizopus, sponges and earthworm
  3. Roundworm, tapeworm and Hydra
  4. Rhizopus, housefly and Hydra
Answer and explanation

D: Rhizopus, housefly and Hydra

Rhizopus releases digestive enzymes outside its cells. A housefly digests food externally or in its gut, and Hydra begins digestion in its gastrovascular cavity. All therefore have extracellular digestion.

Question 29

Typical mature mammalian erythrocytes are

  1. discoid and nucleated
  2. discoid and enucleated
  3. amoeboid and nucleated
  4. amoeboid and enucleated
Answer and explanation

B: discoid and enucleated

Typical mature mammalian red blood cells are disc-shaped and lack a nucleus. Losing the nucleus helps provide space for haemoglobin, although shape varies in some mammalian groups.

Question 31

The capture and digestion of insects by a pitcher plant is a special form of nutrition termed

  1. autotrophic
  2. heterotrophic
  3. chemosynthetic
  4. saprophytic
Answer and explanation

B: heterotrophic

Capturing and digesting insects supplies nutrients from other organisms, a heterotrophic supplement to the pitcher plant’s photosynthesis. The plant can still obtain much of its carbon by photosynthesis.

  3. The enclosed leaf will lose its green colour
  4. The leaves outside the flask serve as control
Answer and explanation

The outside leaves provide a comparison with leaves exposed to ordinary air. Caustic soda removes carbon dioxide inside the flask, so the enclosed leaf has a different carbon dioxide supply.

  1. 1/5
  2. ¼
  3. 1/3
  4. ½

With independent births and a probability of one half for a girl in this model, the previous four outcomes do not change the fifth. The probability remains 1/2.

Question 50

At a single locus with complete dominance, what proportion of offspring from two heterozygous parents, Aa × Aa, is expected to express the recessive phenotype?

  1. ¼
  2. ½
  3. ¾
  4. 4/4
Answer and explanation

A: ¼

For Aa × Aa, the equally likely allele combinations are AA, Aa, aA and aa. With complete dominance, only aa expresses the recessive phenotype, giving a probability of 1/4.
