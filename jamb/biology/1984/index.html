Question 1

The mouthparts of an adult housefly are adapted for

  1. Lapping and sponging
  2. Sucking and chewing
  3. Piercing and sucking
  4. Chewing and lapping
  5. Biting and chewing
Answer and explanation

A: Lapping and sponging

An adult housefly takes in liquid food using sponging mouthparts. Saliva can help dissolve suitable solid food before it is taken up. Its mouthparts do not chew solid pieces or pierce skin like those of a biting fly.

Question 3

Mosses, liverworts and ferns can be grouped together because they

  1. Are all aquatic plants
  2. All grow in deserts
  3. Are seedless plants
  4. Have undifferentiated plant bodies
  5. All produce colourless flowers
Answer and explanation

C: Are seedless plants

All three reproduce without seeds. Ferns have vascular tissues and distinct organs, whereas mosses and liverworts are bryophytes. Their habitats vary, and none produces flowers.

Question 5

Which of the following invertebrates does NOT possess antennae?

  1. Centipede
  2. Crustacean
  3. Millipede
  4. Insect
  5. Spider
Answer and explanation

E: Spider

Spiders are arachnids and have no antennae. Their pedipalps and sensory hairs perform sensory functions. Insects, centipedes and millipedes have one pair of antennae, while crustaceans typically have two pairs.

Question 6

Which statement is INCORRECT about the typical green, heart-shaped prothallus of a homosporous fern? It

  1. Is a flattened structure
  2. Is green because its cells contain chloroplasts
  3. Is the dominant plant generation
  4. Bears the sexual organs
  5. Is attached to the ground by rhizoids
Answer and explanation

C: Is the dominant plant generation

The prothallus is the small gametophyte that bears the sex organs. The much larger leafy fern is the dominant sporophyte generation. A typical free-living prothallus is green and attached to its substrate by rhizoids.

Question 7

Which cell constituent is not normally present in animal cells but is present in photosynthetic plant cells?

  1. Mitochondria
  2. Chloroplasts
  3. Ribosomes
  4. Golgi apparatus
  5. Vacuoles
Answer and explanation

B: Chloroplasts

Chloroplasts carry out photosynthesis in green plant cells. Typical animal cells do not have chloroplasts. Mitochondria, ribosomes and Golgi apparatus occur in both groups, and animal cells can also contain small vacuolar compartments.

Question 10

In a placental mammal, which organ provides the main exchange surface for oxygen and nutrients between the mother and the developing offspring?

  1. Amnion
  2. Chorion
  3. Umbilical cord
  4. Oviduct
  5. Placenta
Answer and explanation

E: Placenta

The placenta is the main exchange organ between maternal and fetal circulations. Oxygen and nutrients cross its exchange surface and reach the fetus through the umbilical circulation. Maternal and fetal blood normally remain in separate circulations.

Question 12

At equal pressure, osmosis between dilute solutions separated by a membrane permeable to water but not the solute is the net movement of

  1. Solute molecules from higher to lower concentration
  2. Solute molecules from lower to higher concentration
  3. Water from higher to lower solute concentration without a membrane
  4. Water across the membrane from lower to higher solute concentration
  5. Water across the membrane from higher to lower solute concentration
Answer and explanation

D: Water across the membrane from lower to higher solute concentration

Water moves from the more dilute solution, which has higher water potential, towards the more concentrated solution through the selectively permeable membrane. The direction stated assumes equal pressure and a solute that cannot cross the membrane.

Question 14

In the usual stabilising role of a fish’s dorsal and anal fins, they chiefly help with

  1. Upward movement
  2. Controlling rolling movement
  3. Downward movement
  4. Steering
  5. Buoyancy
Answer and explanation

B: Controlling rolling movement

Dorsal and anal fins help stabilise the fish and resist unwanted rotation such as rolling. Their contribution varies with species and swimming behaviour. Other fins can assist steering, while buoyancy depends on body density and, in many bony fish, a swim bladder.

Question 15

A jointed arthropod exoskeleton is NOT found in the

  1. Maggot
  2. Mosquito larva
  3. Earthworm
  4. Caterpillar
  5. Termite
Answer and explanation

C: Earthworm

An earthworm is an annelid supported mainly by a hydrostatic skeleton. The other animals are insects or insect larvae and have an external cuticle that is shed during growth. A soft-bodied larva can still possess an exoskeleton.

Question 16

Which blood component forms the initial plug at a damaged vessel and helps the clotting process?

  1. Leucocytes
  2. Platelets
  3. Haemolymph
  4. Haemoglobin
  5. Erythrocytes
Answer and explanation

B: Platelets

Platelets adhere at a damaged vessel and aggregate to form a temporary plug. They also support reactions that lead to a fibrin clot. Coagulation involves plasma factors as well as platelets; it is not caused simply by blood touching air.

Question 17

Pepsin is a digestive enzyme that breaks down

  1. Cellulose into glucose
  2. Carbohydrates into simple sugars
  3. Proteins into smaller peptides
  4. Fats into glycerol and fatty acids
  5. Sucrose into glucose and fructose
Answer and explanation

C: Proteins into smaller peptides

Pepsin begins protein digestion in the acidic stomach, cleaving proteins into smaller peptide fragments. It does not digest cellulose, fats or sucrose. Further protein digestion takes place in the small intestine.

Question 18

Alcoholic fermentation of glucose by yeast produces

  1. Carbon dioxide and ethanol
  2. Carbon dioxide and water
  3. Carbon dioxide and oxygen
  4. Carbon dioxide and glucose
  5. Ethanol and water
Answer and explanation

A: Carbon dioxide and ethanol

Yeast converts pyruvate into acetaldehyde, releasing carbon dioxide, and then reduces acetaldehyde to ethanol. This regenerates NAD⁺ so glycolysis can continue without oxygen. Carbon dioxide also helps bread dough rise.

Question 21

In the Biuret test, protein is mixed with sodium hydroxide solution. Which chemical is then added for a positive reaction?

  1. Mercurous nitrate
  2. Copper sulphate
  3. Mercuric nitrate
  4. Sodium carbonate
  5. Ammonium hydroxide
Answer and explanation

B: Copper sulphate

Copper(II) ions in alkaline solution interact with peptide bonds to produce a violet colour. Copper sulphate supplies these ions, while sodium hydroxide provides the alkaline conditions. The result indicates peptide bonds rather than a particular protein identity.

Question 22

Loss of pancreatic digestive secretions would impair digestion of which combination of food substances?

  1. Starch
  2. Starch, proteins and fats
  3. Oils and fats
  4. Proteins
  5. Carbohydrates and fats
Answer and explanation

B: Starch, proteins and fats

Pancreatic juice supplies amylase for starch, proteases for proteins and lipase for fats. Losing these secretions can therefore impair digestion of all three groups. Other digestive secretions may still act, so impaired digestion does not mean every digestive process stops.

Question 26

Which region normally has the same number of vertebrae in a rabbit, rat and human?

  1. Caudal
  2. Thoracic
  3. Lumbar
  4. Cervical
  5. Sacral
Answer and explanation

D: Cervical

Rabbits, rats and humans normally have seven cervical vertebrae. This is the neck region, including the atlas and axis. The numbers in the thoracic, lumbar, sacral and caudal regions differ between these animals.

Question 31

Which part of a vertebrate brain is especially associated with coordination of muscular activity and balance?

  1. Olfactory lobes
  2. Cerebral hemispheres
  3. Optic lobes
  4. Cerebellum
  5. Spinal cord
Answer and explanation

D: Cerebellum

The cerebellum helps coordinate movement, timing and balance by comparing intended movement with sensory feedback. It works with other parts of the nervous system rather than acting alone. Well-coordinated movement is therefore especially associated with cerebellar function.

Question 32

Which adaptation is primarily associated with swimming rather than bird flight?

  1. Streamlined body shape
  2. Powerful flight muscles
  3. Reduced body mass
  4. A broad sternum for muscle attachment
  5. Webbed feet
Answer and explanation

E: Webbed feet

Webbed feet spread the force of a backward stroke over a larger area of water, aiding swimming. Streamlining, strong flight muscles, a suitable sternum and reduced mass can support flight. A swimming bird may also fly, but the webbing is chiefly a swimming adaptation.

Question 33

What spatial pattern is most directly recorded by a line transect in an ecological survey?

  1. The total number of plants and animals in the whole habitat
  2. The entire population of a plant species
  3. The distribution of organisms along a line
  4. The heights of all trees in a section of forest
  5. The total number of young plants across the forest
Answer and explanation

C: The distribution of organisms along a line

A line transect records organisms or vegetation encountered along a selected line. It can show how occurrence changes across a habitat or environmental gradient. Estimating the total population of a whole habitat requires an appropriate sampling design and further analysis.

Question 37

In the usual forward flow through a systemic vascular bed, blood does NOT flow from

  1. Arteries to arterioles
  2. Arterioles to capillaries
  3. Capillaries to venules
  4. Arterioles back into the supplying artery
  5. Venules to veins
Answer and explanation

D: Arterioles back into the supplying artery

Blood normally travels from arteries into smaller arterioles, through capillaries, then into venules and veins. Flow back from arterioles into their supplying artery reverses this usual sequence. Local pressure changes and pathological reverse flow are outside this normal-flow comparison.

Question 39

The axial skeleton of a mammal does not include the bones of the

  1. Skull
  2. Tail
  3. Limbs
  4. Back
  5. Neck
Answer and explanation

C: Limbs

The axial skeleton includes the skull and vertebral column, including cervical, trunk and caudal regions where present. Limb bones belong to the appendicular skeleton together with their supporting girdles. The limbs are therefore the exception.

Question 41

Green plants support food webs chiefly through their role as

  1. Primary consumers
  2. Producers
  3. Decomposers
  4. Secondary consumers
  5. Scavengers
Answer and explanation

B: Producers

Photosynthetic plants use light energy to make organic compounds from carbon dioxide. This primary production supplies food and stored chemical energy to many other organisms in a food web. Plants also respire, but that does not remove their producer role.

Question 43

Which is a normal major component of clean air rather than an air pollutant at its usual atmospheric proportion?

  1. Noise
  2. Smoke
  3. Sulphur dioxide
  4. Carbon monoxide
  5. Molecular nitrogen
Answer and explanation

E: Molecular nitrogen

Molecular nitrogen makes up about 78% of dry air and is a normal atmospheric constituent. This differs from reactive nitrogen compounds that can contribute to pollution. Smoke, sulphur dioxide and carbon monoxide are pollutants, while excessive noise is environmental pollution.

Question 44

Which group consists entirely of abiotic environmental factors?

  1. Salinity, tide, plankton and turbidity
  2. Temperature, pH and soil insects
  3. Wind, altitude, humidity and light
  4. Conifers, wind, pH and rainfall
  5. Soil, water, bacteria and salinity
Answer and explanation

C: Wind, altitude, humidity and light

Abiotic factors are nonliving physical or chemical features of the environment. Wind, altitude, humidity and light all fit that description. Plankton, insects, conifers and bacteria are living organisms and are biotic components.

Question 45

Which disease, cause, symptom and transmission combination is correct?

  1. Cholera — virus — severe diarrhoea — contaminated water
  2. Malaria — protozoan — high fever — ordinary contact with an infected person
  3. Syphilis — virus — sexually transmitted disease — sexual contact
  4. Smallpox — virus — blister-like skin lesions — close contact with an infected person
  5. Sleeping sickness — bacterium — tiredness and disturbed sleep — tsetse-fly bite
Answer and explanation

D: Smallpox — virus — blister-like skin lesions — close contact with an infected person

Smallpox was caused by variola virus and could spread through close contact, producing characteristic skin lesions. Cholera and syphilis are bacterial diseases. Malaria and sleeping sickness are caused by protozoan parasites, with mosquito and tsetse-fly transmission respectively.

Question 50

Which sequence orders water-erosion forms from removal across a broad surface towards progressively larger channels?

  1. Rill → sheet → gully
  2. Gully → rill → sheet
  3. Sheet → gully → rill
  4. Sheet → rill → gully
  5. Rill → gully → sheet
Answer and explanation

D: Sheet → rill → gully

Sheet erosion removes soil across a relatively broad surface. Concentrated runoff can cut small rills, and larger channels are called gullies. This is a sequence of increasing channel development, not a rule that every site must pass through every stage.
