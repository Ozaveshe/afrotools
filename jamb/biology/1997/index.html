Question 3

Which plant tissue contains actively dividing, relatively undifferentiated cells?

  1. Meristem
  2. Phloem
  3. Epidermis
  4. Xylem
Answer and explanation

A: Meristem

Meristematic tissue consists of relatively undifferentiated cells that divide to produce new plant tissues. Its cells remain capable of active growth, unlike the more specialised tissues listed.

Question 6

Annelids differ from nematodes in that they

  1. exhibit bilateral symmetry
  2. are triploblastic
  3. are metamerically segmented
  4. possess complete digestive system
Answer and explanation

C: are metamerically segmented

Annelids have repeated body segments, a condition called metameric segmentation. Nematodes are unsegmented roundworms, although they share other general features such as bilateral symmetry and three germ layers.

Question 7

Which of the following perform similar functions?

  1. Ascospores and ascocarp
  2. antherozoids and rhizoid
  3. Sorus and indusium
  4. Strobili and inflorescence
Answer and explanation

D: Strobili and inflorescence

Strobili group reproductive structures on an axis, while an inflorescence groups flowers. Both organise reproductive units together, even though their component structures and the plants that bear them differ.

Question 8

In ferns, the mature sporophyte

  1. develops from a haploid zygote
  2. reproduces sexually to produce spores
  3. is haploid and dependent on the gametophyte
  4. is diploid and independent of the gametophyte
Answer and explanation

D: is diploid and independent of the gametophyte

The mature fern sporophyte is diploid and can live independently. It produces haploid spores by meiosis. The young sporophyte initially develops on the gametophyte before becoming an independent fern.

Question 9

The group of insects that undergoes complete metamorphosis is

  1. houseflies, beetles and cockroaches
  2. cockroaches, grasshoppers and bees
  3. houseflies, beetles and butterflies
  4. aphids, grasshoppers and butterflies
Answer and explanation

C: houseflies, beetles and butterflies

Houseflies, beetles and butterflies pass through egg, larva, pupa and adult stages. The distinct pupal stage is a feature of complete metamorphosis, unlike the nymphal development of cockroaches and grasshoppers.

Question 10

The nitrogenous substance that is excreted by birds in order to conserve water is

  1. ammonia
  2. urea
  3. uric acid
  4. nitric acid
Answer and explanation

C: uric acid

Birds eliminate much of their nitrogenous waste as uric acid and urates. These can be excreted with relatively little water, helping conserve water compared with eliminating large amounts of dissolved ammonia.

Question 17

Oozing out of water from the leaves of plants in a humid environment is known as

  1. transpiration
  2. osmosis
  3. pinocytosis
  4. guttation
Answer and explanation

D: guttation

Guttation is the release of liquid water from leaf edges or tips, usually through hydathodes. It can occur when root pressure is relatively high and transpiration is low, as in humid conditions.

  3. phosphorus
  4. iron

Calcium ions are required at several stages of the blood-clotting cascade. They help the clotting reactions proceed toward fibrin formation, alongside clotting proteins and platelet-related processes.

Question 19

Compared with aerobic respiration of glucose, alcoholic fermentation in yeast produces

  1. less amount of energy and water
  2. greater amount of energy and alcohol
  3. a smaller amount of usable energy and alcohol
  4. a greater amount of usable energy and water
Answer and explanation

C: a smaller amount of usable energy and alcohol

Alcoholic fermentation in yeast yields ethanol and much less usable energy per glucose than aerobic respiration. Only the small ATP yield of glycolysis is obtained, while fermentation regenerates NAD+ for glycolysis to continue.

Question 21

The process of deamination is essential for the

  1. digestion of protein
  2. secretion of bile
  3. formation of urea
  4. formation of antibody
Answer and explanation

C: formation of urea

Deamination removes nitrogen-containing amino groups from amino acids. In mammals, the resulting nitrogen can be incorporated into urea for excretion, preventing toxic ammonia from accumulating.

The companion cells are part of the

  1. pericycle

  4. xylem
Answer and explanation

Companion cells are associated with sieve-tube elements in angiosperm phloem. They provide metabolic support and participate in loading and unloading substances transported through the phloem.

Question 30

The substance that is responsible for apical dominance in plants is known as

  1. gibberellin
  2. tannin
  3. auxin
  4. kinin
Answer and explanation

C: auxin

Auxin produced near the shoot tip contributes to apical dominance, helping suppress the growth of lateral buds. Removing the tip changes hormonal interactions and can allow side shoots to grow.

Question 32

The ability of the eye to focus on both near and distant objects is termed

  1. image formation
  2. refraction
  3. hypermetropia
  4. accommodation
Answer and explanation

D: accommodation

Accommodation is adjustment of the eye’s optical power to focus objects at different distances. Changes in lens curvature help bring light from nearby or distant objects into focus on the retina.

  3. precipitation
  4. sedimentation

Question 42

During binary fission in a typical unicellular eukaryote, the nucleus divides by

  1. mitosis
  2. meiosis
  3. fragmentation
  4. mutation
Answer and explanation

A: mitosis

In a typical unicellular eukaryote reproducing by binary fission, mitosis separates duplicated nuclear chromosomes before the cell divides. This usually preserves the chromosome complement in the daughter cells.

Question 44

Both parents have sickle cell trait, with genotype HbA/HbS. What is the probability that a child has genotype HbA/HbA?

  1. ½
  2. ¼
  3. 1/8
  4. 0
Answer and explanation

B: ¼

Each HbA/HbS parent can contribute HbA or HbS. The four equally likely combinations are HbA/HbA, HbA/HbS, HbS/HbA and HbS/HbS. Only one has no HbS allele, giving a probability of one quarter for each pregnancy.

Question 49

Fossil records found in sedimentary rocks offer some explanation for the theory of evolution because

  1. the deposits have remains of organisms characteristic of when they were formed
  2. different strata have remains of organisms of the same kind
  3. only organisms with strong parts are fossilized
  4. most animal and plant fossils bear little resemblance to present-day living specimens
Answer and explanation

A: the deposits have remains of organisms characteristic of when they were formed

Fossils in sedimentary deposits can record organisms that lived during the period when those deposits formed. Comparing fossil assemblages through successive layers provides evidence of biological change over time.
