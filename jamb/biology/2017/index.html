Question 1

Piercing and sucking mouthparts for taking blood are found in female

  1. grasshoppers
  2. mosquitoes
  3. termites
  4. cockroaches
Answer and explanation

B: mosquitoes

Female mosquitoes have mouthparts adapted to pierce tissue and take a blood meal. The other listed insects have primarily biting and chewing mouthparts.

Question 4

Which group includes familiar animals adapted to arid environments?

  1. locust, camel, lizard and snakes
  2. monkeys, chameleon, earthworm and grasshopper
  3. monkeys, grasshopper, snail and snakes
  4. lungfish, duck, butterfly and lizards
Answer and explanation

Question 5

Suture joint is found in the

  1. hip
  2. ankle
  3. skull
  4. elbow
Answer and explanation

C: skull

Sutures are fibrous joints between many skull bones. They provide strong connections with little movement, unlike the freely movable joints of the limbs.

Question 6

The organelle responsible for osmoregulation in Paramecium is

  1. flame cell
  2. nephridia
  3. contractile vacuole
  4. Malpighian tubule
Answer and explanation

C: contractile vacuole

A contractile vacuole expels excess water entering a freshwater Paramecium. This helps maintain water balance in a cell whose surroundings are usually hypotonic.

Question 8

The platelets in mammalian blood are responsible for

  1. transporting oxygen
  2. initiating clotting
  3. removing carbon (IV) oxide
  4. destroying micro-organisms
Answer and explanation

B: initiating clotting

Platelets adhere at damaged blood vessels and help form a platelet plug. They also support clotting reactions that stabilise the plug with fibrin.

Question 10

When testing for the presence of starch in a leaf, the reason for dipping the decolourised leaf in hot water is to

  1. detect the starch
  2. kill the leaf
  3. soften the leaf
  4. remove the chlorophyll
Answer and explanation

C: soften the leaf

Alcohol removes chlorophyll but leaves the leaf brittle. Rinsing the decolourised leaf in hot water softens it before iodine is added to test for starch.

Question 11

  1. parasitism
  2. amensalism
  3. mutualism
  4. commensalism
Answer and explanation

D: commensalism

Commensalism describes a relationship in which one organism benefits while the other is not appreciably affected. A remora receiving transport under this assumption fits that definition.

Question 13

The causative organism of cholera is

  1. Clostridium sp
  2. shigella sp
  3. Vibrio cholerae
  4. Salmonella Typhi
Answer and explanation

C: Vibrio cholerae

Cholera is caused by toxigenic strains of Vibrio cholerae. Infection can produce severe watery diarrhoea through the action of cholera toxin in the intestine.

Question 20

In the usual diploid animal life cycle, chromosome number is halved during gamete production by

  1. implantation
  2. fertilisation
  3. mitosis
  4. meiosis
Answer and explanation

D: meiosis

In animals, meiosis in the germ line reduces chromosome number during gamete production. Fertilisation then joins haploid gametes and restores the diploid number.

Question 21

A dish weighs 20 g and contains 7.5 g of dry soil. After burning, dish and soil weigh 25 g. Assuming the mass lost represents humus, its percentage in the initial dry soil is

  1. 9.1
  2. 37.5
  3. 12.5
  4. 33.3
Answer and explanation

D: 33.3

The remaining soil weighs 25 − 20 = 5 g. The mass lost is 7.5 − 5 = 2.5 g, so the estimated humus percentage is (2.5 ÷ 7.5) × 100 = 33.3%.

  2. pericycle
  3. epidermis
  4. endodermis
Answer and explanation

Question 26

The respective tissues that transport water and manufactured food in plants are

  1. xylem and phloem
  2. phloem and tracheid
  3. phloem and xylem
  4. xylem and tracheid
Answer and explanation

A: xylem and phloem

Xylem carries water and dissolved mineral nutrients, while phloem transports sugars and other organic solutes between sources and sinks. The order is xylem, then phloem.

Question 30

With other conditions comparable, which soil type generally has the least ability to retain nutrients?

  1. sandy loam
  2. clay loam
  3. loam
  4. sand
Answer and explanation

D: sand

Sandy soil generally has less surface area and lower nutrient retention than comparable soils containing more clay and organic matter. Dissolved nutrients can be readily leached.
