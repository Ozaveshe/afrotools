Question 2

Which pair contains seed-plant groups whose fertilisation does not require sperm to swim through an external film of water?

  1. Ferns and algae
  2. Ferns and mosses
  3. Bryophytes and flowering plants
  4. Flowering plants and conifers
Answer and explanation

D: Flowering plants and conifers

Flowering plants and conifers use pollen and pollen tubes to deliver the male gametes without an external film of water. Ferns and mosses rely on swimming sperm for fertilisation. This reproductive adaptation helps seed plants reproduce in terrestrial environments.

Question 3

Which of these animals has radial symmetry?

  1. Squid
  2. Hydra
  3. Snail
  4. Cockroach
Answer and explanation

B: Hydra

Hydra has a body organised around a central oral-to-basal axis, with tentacles around the mouth. This is the radial body plan of a cnidarian polyp. Squid and cockroaches have bilateral body plans, while adult snails show asymmetry associated with torsion.

Question 5

In a bird’s quill feather, the tiny hooks interlock with neighbouring ridged structures called

  1. Vanes
  2. Rachises
  3. Barbules
  4. Barbs
Answer and explanation

C: Barbules

Barbules are the small branches on the feather barbs. Hooklets on some barbules engage neighbouring barbules, linking the barbs into a continuous vane. The rachis is the central shaft, while the vane is the broader feather surface.

Question 6

Which statement is NOT true of Spirogyra?

  1. It can reproduce by conjugation
  2. It can reproduce by fragmentation
  3. Its normal filaments are branched
  4. Its normal filaments are unbranched
Answer and explanation

C: Its normal filaments are branched

Spirogyra normally forms unbranched chains of cells with spiral chloroplasts. A filament can break into fragments that continue growing. Sexual reproduction involves conjugation between compatible cells rather than branching of the filament.

Question 8

Incomplete metamorphosis occurs in the

  1. Butterfly
  2. Grasshopper
  3. Mosquito
  4. Housefly
Answer and explanation

B: Grasshopper

A grasshopper develops from egg to nymph to adult, without a pupal stage. Nymphs resemble small adults but are not yet sexually mature and usually have undeveloped wings. Butterflies, mosquitoes and houseflies pass through larval and pupal stages.

Question 9

Most fishes are described as ectothermic because their body temperature is largely

  1. Constantly low
  2. Constantly high
  3. Dependent on the temperature of their surroundings
  4. Regulated at will
Answer and explanation

C: Dependent on the temperature of their surroundings

In most fishes, heat exchange with the surrounding water strongly influences body temperature. Ectothermic does not mean always cold: a fish in warm water can have a higher temperature than one in cold water. Some fish have specialised heat-retention adaptations.

Question 11

  1. Mandibles
  2. A pair of anal styles
  3. Spiracles
  4. A pair of cerci
Answer and explanation

B: A pair of anal styles

The adult male American cockroach has short anal styles that are absent from the adult female. Both sexes have mandibles, respiratory spiracles and cerci. Styles should therefore not be confused with the longer paired cerci.

Question 12

The paired fin groups of a typical bony fish are

  1. Caudal and ventral
  2. Ventral and pelvic
  3. Pelvic and pectoral
  4. Pectoral and dorsal
Answer and explanation

C: Pelvic and pectoral

Pectoral and pelvic fins occur as left–right pairs and correspond to the two paired appendage sets. Dorsal and caudal fins are median fins. Ventral is commonly used for the pelvic fin, so it does not name a separate second pair.

Question 13

In a typical young dicot stem with collateral vascular bundles, xylem and phloem differ from the radial arrangement in the root by lying

  1. On the same radius within each bundle
  2. Scattered independently throughout the ground tissue
  3. On alternate radii
  4. Only at the central pith
Answer and explanation

A: On the same radius within each bundle

In a collateral stem bundle, xylem lies towards the inside and phloem towards the outside along the same radius. In a typical root, xylem and phloem occupy alternating positions. This comparison concerns their relative arrangement, not simply the presence of either tissue.

Question 14

The oxygen gas released during oxygenic photosynthesis comes from

  1. Carbon dioxide
  2. Air
  3. Water
  4. Chlorophyll
Answer and explanation

C: Water

Water is split during the light-dependent reactions, supplying electrons and releasing oxygen. Carbon dioxide supplies carbon for carbohydrate formation. Chlorophyll captures light energy but is not the substance consumed to supply the released oxygen atoms.

Question 17

Which element is part of the haem group in haemoglobin?

  1. Sodium
  2. Potassium
  3. Calcium
  4. Iron
Answer and explanation

D: Iron

Each haem group contains an iron ion that binds oxygen reversibly. Haemoglobin combines these haem groups with globin protein chains to transport oxygen in red blood cells. Sodium, potassium and calcium have other physiological roles.

Question 18

Severe vitamin C deficiency causes

  1. Kwashiorkor
  2. Beriberi
  3. Pellagra
  4. Scurvy
Answer and explanation

D: Scurvy

Vitamin C supports normal collagen formation. Severe deficiency causes scurvy, which can involve fragile connective tissues, bleeding gums and poor wound healing. Beriberi is associated with thiamine deficiency and pellagra with niacin deficiency.

Question 20

A food extract gives a violet or purple colour after sodium hydroxide and copper(II) sulphate are added in the biuret test. This indicates the presence of

  1. Fats
  2. Carbohydrate
  3. Protein
  4. Reducing sugar
Answer and explanation

C: Protein

In the biuret test, copper ions in alkaline solution form a coloured complex with peptide bonds. Proteins contain many peptide bonds and can give the violet result. Fat and carbohydrate molecules do not have this protein backbone.

Question 22

Evidence of living tissues inside a mammalian tooth is found in the

  1. Gum
  2. Pulp cavity
  3. Cementum
  4. Enamel
Answer and explanation

B: Pulp cavity

The pulp cavity contains living connective tissue, blood vessels and nerves. Enamel is a hard, non-living covering of the crown. The gum surrounds the tooth, while cementum covers the root rather than containing the tooth’s central neurovascular tissue.

Question 23

Mammalian blood circulation is called double circulation because blood

  1. Passes through the heart twice in a complete circuit
  2. Moves in both arteries and veins
  3. Circulates in both the heart and other organs
  4. Enters a heart containing atria and ventricles
Answer and explanation

A: Passes through the heart twice in a complete circuit

Blood travels from the heart to the lungs and back, then from the heart to the body tissues and back. These pulmonary and systemic circuits make two passages through the heart in one complete journey through both circuits.

Question 24

During stomatal transpiration, which is the correct outward sequence for water? 1. Mesophyll; 2. Leaf veins; 3. Substomatal cavity; 4. Stomata.

  1. 3 → 2 → 1 → 4
  2. 2 → 3 → 1 → 4
  3. 2 → 1 → 3 → 4
  4. 1 → 2 → 3 → 4
Answer and explanation

C: 2 → 1 → 3 → 4

Water arrives in the leaf veins, moves into mesophyll tissues and evaporates into internal air spaces. Water vapour then diffuses through substomatal spaces and out through stomata. Thus the sequence is 2 → 1 → 3 → 4.

Question 25

The opening between the left atrium and left ventricle is guarded by the

  1. Auricular valve
  2. Tricuspid valve
  3. Ventricular valve
  4. Bicuspid valve
Answer and explanation

D: Bicuspid valve

The bicuspid, or mitral, valve lies between the left atrium and left ventricle. It closes during ventricular contraction to prevent blood flowing back into the atrium. The tricuspid valve occupies the corresponding opening on the right side.

Question 27

Which statement is NOT correct during normal quiet inhalation in mammals?

  1. External intercostal muscles contract
  2. The diaphragm rises into a more domed position
  3. The ribs rise
  4. Pressure inside the lungs falls below atmospheric pressure
Answer and explanation

B: The diaphragm rises into a more domed position

During inhalation, the diaphragm contracts and flattens downwards, while the external intercostal muscles help raise the ribs. The expanding chest allows lung volume to increase and pressure to fall, drawing air in. A rising, more domed diaphragm is associated with relaxation during expiration.

Question 28

Which equation summarises alcoholic fermentation of glucose?

  1. C6H12O6 + 2C2H5OH + 2CO2
  2. 6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2
  3. C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O + energy
  4. C6H12O6 → 2C2H5OH + 2CO2 + energy
Answer and explanation

D: C6H12O6 → 2C2H5OH + 2CO2 + energy

Alcoholic fermentation converts glucose into ethanol and carbon dioxide without consuming oxygen in the net equation. Coupled glycolysis captures a small amount of usable energy. The balanced products contain the same total numbers of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen atoms as the glucose.

  2. Articular processes and cervical ribs
  3. A large body and cervical ribs
  4. Transverse foramina and a large body
Answer and explanation

A: A short spinous process and transverse foramina

Typical mid-cervical vertebrae have transverse foramina and relatively small bodies, with short spinous processes. The atlas, axis and seventh cervical vertebra have special features, so the typical pattern should not be applied unchanged to every neck vertebra.

Question 33

A nastic plant movement is characterised by a direction that is

  1. Determined only by the direction of light
  2. Independent of the direction from which the stimulus comes
  3. Directed towards or away from the stimulus
  4. Determined only by an internal stimulus
Answer and explanation

B: Independent of the direction from which the stimulus comes

Nastic movements have a direction determined by the plant’s structure rather than the direction of the triggering stimulus. A leaf may fold after touch regardless of which side was touched. A directional growth response towards or away from a stimulus is a tropism.

Question 41

Which instrument rotates a plant to help investigate responses to directional light or gravity?

  1. Auxanometer
  2. Potometer
  3. Clinostat
  4. Photometer
Answer and explanation

C: Clinostat

A clinostat turns the plant so that a stimulus does not act persistently from one direction relative to it. Comparing suitable rotating and stationary setups helps investigate tropic responses. Rotation does not remove gravity; it changes the plant’s orientation relative to it.

Question 42

At a trophic level where animals feed directly on plants, they act as

  1. Secondary consumers
  2. Primary consumers
  3. Producers
  4. Predators of animals
Answer and explanation

B: Primary consumers

Plants are producers because they make organic material using an external energy source. An animal eating those plants obtains food directly from the producer level and acts as a primary consumer. Eating a primary consumer would place an animal at a higher trophic level.

  1. Poliomyelitis
  2. Tuberculosis
  3. Cholera
  4. Onchocerciasis
Answer and explanation

Question 46

Bacteria and fungi that break down dead organic material perform the ecological role of

  1. Producers
  2. Decomposers
  3. Scavengers
  4. Grazing consumers
Answer and explanation

B: Decomposers

Decomposers digest dead organic matter and make its chemical constituents available for further cycling. Many bacteria and fungi carry out this role. Not every bacterium or fungus has the same mode of nutrition, so the role depends on the organism’s activity.

Question 47

Swollen shoot disease of cacao is caused by a

  1. Virus
  2. Fungus
  3. Bacterium
  4. Protozoan
Answer and explanation

A: Virus

Cacao swollen shoot disease is associated with a group of related viruses. Infection can produce swollen shoots and declining tree health. Mealybugs can transmit the viruses, but the insect vector is different from the disease-causing agent.
