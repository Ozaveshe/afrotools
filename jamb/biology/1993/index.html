Question 1

On what structures are the units of inheritance situated?

  1. Golgi bodies
  2. Ribosomes
  3. Chromosomes
  4. Endoplasmic reticulum
Answer and explanation

C: Chromosomes

Genes are units of hereditary information carried in DNA organised into chromosomes. Ribosomes translate genetic messages, while the Golgi apparatus and endoplasmic reticulum handle production or processing of cellular materials.

Question 2

Production of naked seeds is a distinctive feature of the group of plant called?

  1. grasses
  2. conifers
  3. legumes
  4. palms
Answer and explanation

B: conifers

Conifers are gymnosperms and produce seeds not enclosed within an ovary-derived fruit. Grasses, legumes and palms are flowering plants, whose seeds develop within fruits. Naked does not mean lacking a seed coat.

Question 3

In which of these features do bryophytes differ from pteridophytes?

  1. Absence of flower
  2. Alternation of generation
  3. Dependence on water for reproduction
  4. Presence of a vascular system
Answer and explanation

D: Presence of a vascular system

Pteridophytes possess vascular tissues for water and food transport, while bryophytes lack a true xylem-and-phloem system. Both groups lack flowers and have alternating generations, and their motile sperm need water for fertilisation.

Question 4

In a typical fern with a green photosynthetic gametophyte, which structure occurs in both the sporophyte and gametophyte?

  1. Rhizoids
  2. Roots
  3. Chloroplasts
  4. Leaves
Answer and explanation

C: Chloroplasts

In a typical green fern life cycle, both the leafy sporophyte and the small gametophyte photosynthesise and contain chloroplasts. The gametophyte has rhizoids, while the sporophyte has true roots and leaves.

Question 5

Which typical organism is a single motile cell that carries out nutrition, growth, excretion and photosynthesis?

  1. Paramecium
  2. Euglena
  3. Amoeba
  4. Spirogyra
Answer and explanation

B: Euglena

A typical photosynthetic Euglena is a single cell with chloroplasts and a flagellum. It can carry out photosynthesis, movement and the other life processes within that cell. Spirogyra forms filaments rather than this flagellated unicellular body.

Question 13

Manufactured food in the plants is transported through the

  1. xylem
  2. phloem
  3. cambium
  4. cortex
Answer and explanation

B: phloem

Phloem transports organic products of photosynthesis, especially sugars, from source regions to sinks where they are used or stored. Xylem chiefly carries water and mineral nutrients from roots.

Question 22

A contractile vacuole in a freshwater protist functions chiefly

  1. to remove excess food
  2. for movement
  3. for digestion
  4. to remove excess water
Answer and explanation

D: to remove excess water

A contractile vacuole collects excess water and expels it from the cell. This is especially important for freshwater protists, which continually gain water by osmosis because their surroundings are more dilute.

Question 23

The response shown by the tips of the root and shoot of a plant to the stimulus of gravity is

  1. haptropism
  2. phototropism
  3. hydrotropism
  4. geotropism
Answer and explanation

D: geotropism

Geotropism, also called gravitropism, is directional growth in response to gravity. Roots usually grow toward gravity, while shoots grow away from it. Light, water and touch are different directional stimuli.

  2. the hypocotyl grows faster than the epicotyl
  3. both grow at the same rate
  4. the epicotyl outgrows the radicle
Answer and explanation

Question 31

  2. constrict to reduce the amount of blood flowing to the skin
  3. dilate to reduce the amount of blood flowing to the skin
  4. constrict to increase the amount of blood flowing to the skin
Answer and explanation

B: constrict to reduce the amount of blood flowing to the skin

When the body is cold, constriction of small blood vessels supplying the skin reduces skin blood flow. Less warm blood reaches the surface, helping reduce heat loss to the surroundings.

Question 32

Which path does sound entering the human ear follow?

  1. Oval window → ossicles → eardrum
  2. Eardrum → oval window → ossicles
  3. Eardrum → ossicles → oval window
  4. Ossicles → eardrum → oval window
Answer and explanation

C: Eardrum → ossicles → oval window

Sound causes the eardrum to vibrate. These vibrations pass through the middle-ear ossicles to the oval window, where they generate pressure waves in the fluid of the inner ear.

Question 45

A major mechanism by which carbon monoxide harms tissues is

  1. constricting the blood vessels
  2. killing the cells
  3. combining with haemoglobin
  4. rupturing the blood vessels
Answer and explanation

C: combining with haemoglobin

Carbon monoxide binds to haemoglobin, forming carboxyhaemoglobin. This reduces effective oxygen transport and delivery to tissues. The resulting oxygen deprivation is a major mechanism of carbon monoxide toxicity.

Question 49

Animals whose body temperature varies substantially are described as

  1. poikilothermic
  2. homoiothermic
  3. polythermic
  4. homeostatic
Answer and explanation

A: poikilothermic

Poikilothermic describes an animal whose body temperature varies substantially. This differs from homeothermy, in which body temperature is relatively stable. Heat source and temperature variability are related but distinct classifications.
