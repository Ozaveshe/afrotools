Question 1

The nucleus is considered the control organelle of a cell because it

  1. contains the genetic material
  2. contains the nuclear sap
  3. is bounded by the nuclear membrane
  4. is located at the centre of the cell
Answer and explanation

A: contains the genetic material

The nucleus contains genetic information used to direct protein production and many cellular activities. Its regulatory role comes from that information and its expression, rather than simply its position or enclosing membrane.

Question 4

In snails, the hard calcareous shells are secreted by the

  1. radula
  2. ctenidium
  3. pneumostome
  4. mantle
Answer and explanation

D: mantle

The mantle secretes the shell material of a snail, including its calcium-containing mineral layers and organic components. The shell protects the soft body while remaining associated with the mantle tissue.

Question 7

The ability of the cockroach to live in cracks and crevices is enhanced by the possession of

  1. wings and segmented body
  2. compound eyes
  3. claws on the legs
  4. dorso-ventrally flattened body
Answer and explanation

D: dorso-ventrally flattened body

A cockroach’s flattened body allows it to fit into narrow spaces with little vertical clearance. This helps it hide and move through cracks and crevices that a deeper-bodied animal could not enter.

Question 8

Which termite caste is typically pale and soft-bodied?

  1. king
  2. worker
  3. soldier
  4. queen
Answer and explanation

B: worker

Termite workers are generally pale and soft-bodied compared with the more strongly pigmented reproductive adults and the hardened heads of soldiers. They perform much of the colony’s feeding, care and construction work.

Question 9

The structures that prevent food particles from escaping through the fish gills are called gill

  1. arches
  2. filaments
  3. rakers
  4. lamellae
Answer and explanation

C: rakers

Gill rakers are projections along the gill arches that help retain food particles and protect the delicate gill surfaces. They differ from the filaments and lamellae, which provide large surfaces for gas exchange.

Question 11

Which listed meristematic tissue can produce additional vascular tissues in a flowering-plant stem?

  1. Epidermis
  2. Pericycle
  3. Xylem
  4. Cambium
Answer and explanation

D: Cambium

Cambium is a meristematic tissue whose cells divide to produce new tissues. Vascular cambium can form additional xylem and phloem, contributing to increasing stem thickness.

Question 12

Photosynthetic manufacture of carbohydrates in plants takes place in

  1. the leaves
  2. the green stems
  3. chlorophyll-containing parts
  4. flowering plants
Answer and explanation

C: chlorophyll-containing parts

Photosynthetic carbohydrate production requires photosynthetic tissues containing chlorophyll and the associated machinery. Such tissues may occur in leaves and green stems, so it is not restricted to one organ or to flowering plants.

Question 16

Small unicellular organisms can rely heavily on diffusion for exchange because they have

  1. a large volume to surface area ratio
  2. a large surface-area-to-volume ratio
  3. their bodies immersed in the nutrients
  4. their outer membrane made of cellulose
Answer and explanation

B: a large surface-area-to-volume ratio

A small unicellular organism has much surface area relative to its volume and short internal diffusion distances. These features help substances cross its surface and reach the cell interior without a large transport system.

Question 17

The heart of an adult frog consists of

  1. two auricles and two ventricles
  2. one auricle and one ventricle
  3. two ventricles and one auricle
  4. one ventricle and two auricles
Answer and explanation

D: one ventricle and two auricles

The adult frog heart has two atria, also called auricles, and one ventricle. The atria receive blood from different circuits, while the single ventricle pumps blood into the outflow pathways.

Question 24

Which measurement best estimates accumulated biomass while minimising the effect of changing water content?

  1. length
  2. volume
  3. surface area
  4. dry weight
Answer and explanation

D: dry weight

Dry mass estimates the amount of material remaining after water is removed. It therefore avoids temporary changes in water content that can alter fresh size, volume or mass without an equivalent increase in biomass.

Question 26

The outermost embryonic membrane in the mammal is the

  1. amnion
  2. chorion
  3. allantois
  4. yolk sac
Answer and explanation

B: chorion

The chorion is the outermost extraembryonic membrane in the mammalian conceptus. The amnion encloses the embryo and amniotic fluid, while the yolk sac and allantois have other developmental roles.

Question 27

Clusters of neurone cell bodies in the peripheral nervous system are called

  1. dorsal roots
  2. ventral roots
  3. ganglia
  4. synapses
Answer and explanation

C: ganglia

Ganglia are clusters of neurone cell bodies in the peripheral nervous system. Each cell body contains a nucleus. Nerve roots are bundles of processes, while synapses are communication junctions.

Question 30

The condition that encourages denitrification is

  1. low soil oxygen
  2. high soil nitrogen
  3. absence of soil bacteria
  4. lightning and thunderstorm
Answer and explanation

A: low soil oxygen

Low oxygen favours denitrification by organisms that use nitrate in anaerobic respiration. This can convert nitrogen compounds into gaseous forms that leave the soil, especially in waterlogged conditions.

Question 36

In an incompatible red-cell transfusion, agglutination occurs when

  1. white blood cells from two individuals meet
  2. two different antibodies meet
  3. two different antigens meet
  4. antibodies bind to their corresponding antigens on red blood cells
Answer and explanation

D: antibodies bind to their corresponding antigens on red blood cells

Agglutination can occur when antibodies bind to their corresponding antigens on red blood cells and link cells together. Compatibility matters because an antibody reacts with its specific target rather than with any different antigen.

Question 39

After one week of life, five chicks of the same sex, hatched simultaneously from eggs of the same hen and fed the same diet, weighed 45 g, 40 g, 35 g, 33 g and 30 g. This is an example of

  1. growth rate
  2. natural selection
  3. variation
  4. mutation
Answer and explanation

C: variation

The chicks have different body masses despite sharing several stated conditions. Differences among individuals of the same species are variation. A single set of masses does not establish their growth rates or the genetic cause of the differences.

Question 40

The phenotype of an individual can be summed up as the

  1. totality of the expressed traits

  4. physiological traits of the individual

Question 48

In whistling pine, leaves are reduced to brown scales while young stems remain green. Leaf reduction mainly helps in

  1. obtaining food
  2. conserving nutrients
  3. storing water
  4. reducing transpiration
Answer and explanation

D: reducing transpiration

Reducing leaves to small scales limits exposed leaf area and can reduce water loss. Green branchlets continue photosynthesis, allowing the plant to obtain carbon while retaining the reduced-leaf form.

Question 50

Which listed structure contains the genetic material altered by mutation?

  1. gametes
  2. chromosomes
  3. phenotype
  4. zygote
Answer and explanation

B: chromosomes

Mutations change genetic material. Among the listed choices, chromosomes contain that material and can undergo sequence or structural changes. A phenotype may change as a consequence, rather than being the molecular target.
