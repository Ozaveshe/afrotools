Question 3

A growth on a bare rock consists of a fungus associated with a photosynthetic alga. It is a

  1. Plant epiphyte
  2. Lichen
  3. Bryophyte
  4. Fern
Answer and explanation

B: Lichen

A lichen is an association involving a fungus and a photosynthetic partner, commonly an alga or cyanobacterium. It is not a single plant combining plant and fungal organs. Bryophytes and ferns are distinct land-plant groups.

Question 4

In a plant life cycle with alternation of generations, the diploid multicellular generation is the

  1. Gametophyte
  2. Spermatophyte
  3. Holophyte
  4. Sporophyte
Answer and explanation

D: Sporophyte

The sporophyte is the diploid multicellular generation and produces haploid spores by meiosis. Spores develop into the haploid gametophyte generation, which produces gametes. Fertilisation restores the diploid stage.

Question 7

A reproductive function of the earthworm clitellum is to

  1. Aid digestion
  2. Prevent drying of the whole body
  3. Assist locomotion
  4. Secrete a cocoon
Answer and explanation

D: Secrete a cocoon

The clitellum is a glandular band that secretes material forming a cocoon. Eggs and sperm enter this structure, which supports reproduction and early development. Movement instead depends mainly on body-wall muscles and anchoring bristles.

Question 10

In many true ferns, sporangia are grouped into clusters called

  1. Indusia
  2. Fronds
  3. Prothalli
  4. Sori
Answer and explanation

D: Sori

A sorus is a cluster of sporangia, and sori is the plural. An indusium may cover a sorus in some ferns. A frond is a fern leaf, while a prothallus is the small gametophyte generation.

Question 11

Why is a film of water needed for fertilisation in mosses?

  1. Because their habitat is moist
  2. Because water is needed, without any further mechanism
  3. Because the male gametes swim to the egg
  4. Because spores are produced
Answer and explanation

C: Because the male gametes swim to the egg

Moss sperm have flagella and move through a film of water to reach the egg in an archegonium. This explains the water requirement for fertilisation. Producing or dispersing spores is a different stage of the life cycle.

Question 14

In a butterfly species whose larvae feed on crop leaves, which stage causes the feeding damage?

  1. Maggot
  2. Nymph
  3. Caterpillar
  4. Pupa
Answer and explanation

C: Caterpillar

A butterfly larva is a caterpillar and commonly feeds actively on plant tissue. A pupa is the transforming stage and generally does not feed. Maggot describes many fly larvae, while nymph is used for immature stages in insects with incomplete metamorphosis.

Question 16

Secondary thickening of a typical dicot stem is driven by the activity of the

  1. Xylem parenchyma alone
  2. Secondary phloem alone
  3. Endodermis
  4. Cambium
Answer and explanation

D: Cambium

The vascular cambium divides to produce secondary xylem towards the inside and secondary phloem towards the outside. This increases stem girth. Cork cambium also contributes to the protective outer tissues during secondary growth.

Question 17

Why is a plant nutrient-culture bottle covered with an opaque material during a mineral-nutrition experiment?

  1. To prevent algal growth in the nutrient solution
  2. To make root growth possible only in complete darkness
  3. To stop mineral elements breaking down in light
  4. To prevent ordinary roots from carrying out photosynthesis
Answer and explanation

A: To prevent algal growth in the nutrient solution

Blocking light from the nutrient solution suppresses photosynthetic algae. Algae could otherwise use dissolved nutrients and change the culture conditions, interfering with the plant experiment. The covering need not exclude light from the plant’s leaves.

Question 22

In a typical eukaryotic cell, oxidative phosphorylation in cellular respiration takes place in the

  1. mitochondria
  2. ribosomes
  3. endoplasmic reticulum
  4. Golgi bodies
Answer and explanation

A: mitochondria

The respiratory electron-transport chain and ATP synthase are in the inner mitochondrial membrane. Glycolysis is a separate stage in the cytosol; ribosomes make proteins.

Question 24

The function of the loop of Henle is to

  2. concentrate amino acids in the kidney tissue
  3. build up sodium chloride in the medullary interstitial fluid
  4. increase the volume of urine
Answer and explanation

C: build up sodium chloride in the medullary interstitial fluid

The loop of Henle helps establish a medullary osmotic gradient. Salt leaves the ascending limb while that segment resists water movement; the gradient supports water recovery.

Question 27

The principal mechanical effect of lignifying sclerenchyma cell walls is to

  1. strengthen and support the plant
  2. transport synthesized food
  3. conduct water and salt
  4. protect the plant from injury
Answer and explanation

  1. blue and wrinkled
  2. round and yellow
  3. wrinkled and yellow
  4. round and greenish-yellow

Question 44

For X-linked recessive red–green colour-vision deficiency, which cross produces affected daughters only?

  1. affected mother × affected father
  2. affected mother × unaffected father
  3. carrier mother × affected father
  4. carrier mother × unaffected father
Answer and explanation

A: affected mother × affected father

An affected mother gives an affected X allele to every child; an affected father gives his affected X to every daughter. Thus all daughters receive two affected alleles.

Question 49

Nitrifying bacteria are important because they

  1. release nitrogen to the atmosphere
  2. convert Atmosphere nitrogen to ammonia
  3. combine ammonia with nitrogen
  4. oxidise ammonium through nitrite to nitrate
Answer and explanation

D: oxidise ammonium through nitrite to nitrate

Nitrification oxidises ammonium first to nitrite and then nitrate. Different microbial groups can perform the steps. Fixation introduces atmospheric nitrogen; denitrification can return nitrogen gas to air.
