Question 3

Angiosperms and gymnosperms belong to the plant group known as

  1. schizophyta
  2. bryophyta
  3. pteridophyta
  4. spermatophyta
Answer and explanation

D: spermatophyta

Angiosperms and gymnosperms are seed plants, traditionally grouped as Spermatophyta. Flowering plants enclose seeds within fruits, while gymnosperms have seeds not enclosed in an ovary-derived fruit.

Question 7

To facilitate gaseous exchange, breathing roots have

  1. stomata
  2. mitochondria
  3. cuticle
  4. lenticels
Answer and explanation

D: lenticels

Breathing roots project into air above oxygen-poor soil or mud. Lenticels allow gases to pass between the atmosphere and internal tissues, supporting respiration in the submerged root system.

Question 11

The function of maxillipeds in crayfish is to aid

  1. walking
  2. swimming
  3. feeding
  4. respiration
Answer and explanation

C: feeding

Maxillipeds are appendages near a crayfish’s mouth that handle food and help move it toward the mouthparts. Their position and specialisation distinguish them from walking legs and swimming appendages.

Question 12

The respiratory surface of a typical air-breathing land snail is associated with its

  1. radula
  2. mantle
  3. tentacle
  4. foot
Answer and explanation

B: mantle

In a typical air-breathing land snail, the vascularised mantle cavity acts as a lung. Gas exchange occurs across its moist lining; the radula scrapes food, and the muscular foot is used for movement.

Question 15

The gill rakers of fishes take part in

  1. feeding
  2. respiration
  3. swimming
  4. diffusion
Answer and explanation

A: feeding

Gill rakers help retain food and prevent it from escaping through the gill openings. In filter-feeding fish they can trap small particles. The thin gill filaments and lamellae, rather than the rakers, provide the main gas-exchange surface.

Question 20

One of the accessory organs of the digestive system is the

  1. kidney
  2. spleen
  3. liver
  4. lung
Answer and explanation

C: liver

The liver is an accessory digestive organ because it produces bile that helps emulsify fats in the small intestine. Food does not pass through the liver itself; bile enters the digestive tract through ducts.

Question 21

The element common to protein, carbohydrate and lipid is

  1. hydrogen
  2. sulphur
  3. nitrogen
  4. phosphorus
Answer and explanation

A: hydrogen

Proteins, carbohydrates and lipids all contain hydrogen, along with carbon and oxygen. Nitrogen is characteristic of proteins but not ordinary carbohydrates, while sulphur and phosphorus are not present in every member of all three classes.

Question 22

The crown of the mammalian tooth is covered with

  1. cement
  2. dentine
  3. caries
  4. enamel
Answer and explanation

D: enamel

Enamel forms the hard outer covering of the crown of a typical mammalian tooth. Dentine lies beneath it, and cementum covers the root. Dental caries is tooth decay rather than a normal covering tissue.

Question 23

When oxygen supply is insufficient for aerobic demand, human muscle cells can metabolise glucose with formation of

  1. fatty acids
  2. lactic acid (lactate)
  3. glycogen
  4. carbondioxide
Answer and explanation

B: lactic acid (lactate)

When oxygen supply is insufficient for aerobic demand, human muscle cells can convert pyruvate to lactate. This regenerates NAD+ so glycolysis can continue producing a limited amount of ATP from glucose.

Question 24

With other conditions and stomatal opening unchanged, which change generally increases transpiration?

  1. Increased humidity
  2. Reduced temperature
  3. Reduced wind speed
  4. Reduced humidity
Answer and explanation

D: Reduced humidity

Lower atmospheric humidity generally steepens the water-vapour gradient between a moist leaf and the air, increasing transpiration when stomatal opening and other conditions remain unchanged. Humid air reduces that gradient.

Question 25

An open U-tube initially has equal liquid levels. Side X contains weak sugar solution and side Y strong sugar solution. A membrane between the sides allows water but not sugar to pass. After 30 minutes of osmosis, the level will

  1. rise in X only
  2. rise in Y only
  3. be the same in X and Y
  4. fall in Y
Answer and explanation

B: rise in Y only

Water moves through the membrane from the weaker sugar solution in X toward the stronger solution in Y. Sugar remains separated, so the liquid level in Y rises while that in X falls until pressure effects balance the net movement.

Question 26

In an open U-tube, water passes through a membrane from weak sugar solution X to strong sugar solution Y; the membrane does not pass sugar. What is the most specific name for this process?

  1. diffusion
  2. osmosis
  3. active diffusion
  4. osmoregulation
Answer and explanation

B: osmosis

Osmosis is net movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane down its water-potential gradient. With equal starting levels, the stronger sugar solution has lower water potential and receives water from the weaker solution.

Question 27

Fibrinogen and prothrombin play important roles in the

  1. deamination of proteins
  2. clotting of blood
  3. detoxication of substances
  4. storage of vitamins
Answer and explanation

B: clotting of blood

Prothrombin is converted into thrombin during coagulation. Thrombin converts soluble fibrinogen into fibrin, which forms a mesh helping stabilise the blood clot. These proteins therefore participate in clotting.

Question 28

Deoxygenated blood flows into the right and left lungs through the

  1. pulmonary vein
  2. vena cava
  3. pulmonary artery
  4. subclavian vein
Answer and explanation

C: pulmonary artery

The pulmonary artery leaves the right ventricle and divides into branches carrying relatively deoxygenated blood to the lungs. Pulmonary veins return oxygenated blood to the left atrium after gas exchange.

Question 30

Directional movement of a whole organism in response to an external stimulus is termed

  1. tropism
  2. a taxis
  3. a nastic movement
  4. a phototrophic movement
Answer and explanation

B: a taxis

Taxis is a directional movement of an entire motile organism or cell in response to a stimulus. It may be toward a favourable stimulus or away from an unfavourable one, unlike growth bending of a rooted plant part.

Question 31

Which of the following vertebrae lacks a centrum?

  1. Atlas
  2. Axis
  3. Thoracic
  4. Lumbar
Answer and explanation

A: Atlas

The atlas is the first cervical vertebra. It is ring-shaped and lacks the typical vertebral body or centrum. The axis has a body and dens, while thoracic and lumbar vertebrae also have distinct vertebral bodies.

Question 38

Exchange of corresponding DNA segments between homologous chromosomes during meiosis is called

  1. test cross
  2. back cross
  3. crossing over
  4. mutation
Answer and explanation

C: crossing over

Crossing over exchanges corresponding DNA segments between non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes during meiosis. It produces new allele combinations without being the same process as a test cross or a new mutation.

Question 42

Which pair gives the broad distinction between organisms that produce organic food and those that obtain it from other organisms?

  1. carnivores and epiphytes
  2. consumers and parasites
  3. producers and consumers
  4. producers and saprophytes
Answer and explanation

C: producers and consumers

Producers make organic material from inorganic resources, while consumers obtain organic material from other organisms or their products. These broad feeding roles organise energy flow through an ecosystem. Decomposers are heterotrophs too.

Question 50

Nitrogen –fixing micro-organisms in leguminous plants live symbiotically in the

  1. root nodules
  2. tap roots
  3. branch roots
  4. root hairs
Answer and explanation

A: root nodules

Legumes form root nodules containing symbiotic nitrogen-fixing bacteria. The plant supplies carbon compounds, while the bacteria convert atmospheric nitrogen into forms that can enter the plant’s nitrogen metabolism.
