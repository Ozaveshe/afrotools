Question 1

In the usual pig intermediate host, the cysticercus of Taenia solium is found in the

  1. alimentary canal of cattle
  2. muscles of pig
  3. alimentary canal of pig
  4. muscles of cattle
Answer and explanation

B: muscles of pig

In the usual life cycle of Taenia solium, pigs act as intermediate hosts and cysticerci develop in their tissues, especially muscle. The adult tapeworm, by contrast, lives in the human intestine.

Question 3

Scales, shelled eggs and a bony skull are features found in both

  1. birds and reptiles
  2. fishes and birds
  3. reptiles and fishes
  4. birds and molluscs
Answer and explanation

A: birds and reptiles

Birds and reptiles both include animals with scales, shelled eggs and a bony skull. Bird scales can be seen on the legs and feet. These features are not the same as saying every species in each group lays eggs.

Question 4

Which arthropod group has no antennae?

  1. Crustacea
  2. Chilopoda
  3. Arachnida
  4. Diplopoda
Answer and explanation

C: Arachnida

Arachnids lack antennae. Their anterior appendages include chelicerae and pedipalps rather than the antennae found in insects, centipedes, millipedes and crustaceans.

Question 6

Which of the following is an organ?

  1. guard cell
  2. liver
  3. xylem bundle
  4. blood
Answer and explanation

B: liver

The liver is an organ composed of several tissue types working together. A guard cell is a single cell, blood is connective tissue and a xylem bundle is part of a conducting tissue system.

Question 9

A body arrangement in which one longitudinal plane divides the body into mirror-image left and right halves is called

  1. bilateral symmetry
  2. transverse section
  3. radial symmetry
  4. longitudinal section
Answer and explanation

A: bilateral symmetry

Bilateral symmetry means that one longitudinal plane divides the body into matching left and right halves. A section is a cut through a body, whereas symmetry describes how its parts are arranged.

Question 13

In the mature bark of woody stems, gaseous exchange takes place through

  1. micropyles
  2. stomata
  3. lenticels
  4. vessels
Answer and explanation

C: lenticels

Lenticels are regions in the bark through which gases can pass between internal tissues and the surrounding air. They provide exchange routes through the otherwise relatively impermeable outer tissues of a woody stem.

Question 14

Substances manufactured by the leaves are transported to other parts of the plant through the

  1. xylem
  2. companion cells
  3. sieve tubes
  4. cambium
Answer and explanation

C: sieve tubes

Sieve tubes are the main conducting pathways in angiosperm phloem for sugars and other organic solutes. Companion cells support their activity but are not the longitudinal sieve-tube conduits.

Question 17

Mammals can acclimatize to reduced oxygen partial pressure at high altitude by

  1. the stimulation of marrow to reduce the amount of erythrocytes produced
  2. increasing the rate at which erythrocytes are destroyed
  3. the stimulation of marrow to produce more erythrocytes
  4. increasing the breakdown of the protein portion of the erythrocytes
Answer and explanation

C: the stimulation of marrow to produce more erythrocytes

At high altitude, reduced oxygen partial pressure can stimulate erythropoietin signalling and increase red blood cell production in the bone marrow. More erythrocytes increase the blood’s oxygen-carrying capacity during acclimatization.

Question 19

  1. red palm oil and groundnut oil
  2. red palm oil and carrots
  3. rice and groundnut oil
  4. oranges and carrots
Answer and explanation

B: red palm oil and carrots

Red palm oil and carrots supply substantial provitamin A carotenoids, which the body can convert to vitamin A. Ordinary unfortified groundnut oil and rice are much poorer sources; oranges are less concentrated sources than red palm oil.

Question 20

The force that holds water together in the form of a stream within the xylem tube is the

  1. cohesion of water molecules to one another by hydrogen bonds
  2. force of gravity attracting the water molecules through the hydrogen bonds
  3. attraction between the water and xylem by osmotic force
  4. normal flow of water from the ground with the help of gravitational force
Answer and explanation

A: cohesion of water molecules to one another by hydrogen bonds

Hydrogen bonds between water molecules produce cohesion. This helps maintain a continuous column of water in xylem as transpiration creates tension. Attraction to the vessel walls is adhesion, a different interaction.

Question 22

The lymphatic system of mammals rejoins the blood circulatory system at the

  1. hepatic vein
  2. subclavian vein
  3. renal vein
  4. common iliac vein
Answer and explanation

B: subclavian vein

Lymph returns to the venous circulation near the junctions of the subclavian and internal jugular veins. The main lymphatic ducts discharge there, returning fluid collected from the tissues to the bloodstream.

Question 24

  1. lower part of the uterus
  2. upper part of the uterus
  3. lower part of the oviduct
  4. upper part of the oviduct
Answer and explanation

D: upper part of the oviduct

Human fertilization usually occurs in the ampulla, the widened part of the uterine tube toward the ovary. The early embryo then travels toward the uterus, where implantation normally occurs later.

A: carried above the ground by the elongating hypocotyls

Question 27

Most bacteria multiply rapidly by means of

  1. budding
  2. fragmentation
  3. binary fission
  4. spore formation
Answer and explanation

C: binary fission

Most bacteria reproduce by binary fission: DNA is copied and one cell divides into two daughter cells. Repeated divisions can rapidly increase a population when conditions support growth.

Question 28

Which is the most complete listed sequence for smell in mammals?

  2. dissolved chemicals → nasal sensory cell → brain
  3. chemicals → mucus membrane → sensory cells → brain
  4. dissolved chemicals → sensory cells → olfactory nerve → brain
Answer and explanation

D: dissolved chemicals → sensory cells → olfactory nerve → brain

Odorant molecules dissolve in nasal mucus and interact with receptors on olfactory sensory cells. Signals then travel through their nerve fibres to brain pathways where the smell is processed.

Question 35

The construction of dams may lead to an increase in the prevalence of

  1. typhoid fever, measles and yellow fever
  2. tuberculosis, leprosy and trypanosomiasis
  3. guinea worm, malaria and tuberculosis
  4. malaria, bilharziasis and onchocerciasis
Answer and explanation

D: malaria, bilharziasis and onchocerciasis

Water projects can change mosquito, snail and blackfly habitats. Under suitable local conditions, these changes can increase malaria, schistosomiasis and onchocerciasis risks; the effect is not inevitable at every dam.

Question 39

The sign + is used to indicate an association where an organism gains, while 0 is used where an organism is unaffected. An association indicated as + 0 is known as

  1. predation
  2. commensalism
  3. parasitism
  4. competition
Answer and explanation

B: commensalism

Commensalism describes an interaction in which one participant benefits while the other has no significant effect under the conditions considered. It is represented by the signs + and 0.

For the usual upright terrestrial biomass pattern, biomass is likely to increase in which sequence?

  1. 1 → 2 → 4 → 5
  2. 1 → 3 → 4 → 5
  3. 3 → 5 → 6 → 7
  4. 5 → 4 → 3 → 1
Answer and explanation

D: 5 → 4 → 3 → 1

In the usual upright biomass pattern for a terrestrial food chain, biomass tends to be greater toward the producer level. The feeding chain plant 1 → grasshopper 3 → praying mantis 4 → lizard 5 is therefore read in reverse for increasing biomass.

Question 43

If the offspring of a cross between brown mouse (bb) and a black mouse (BB) are allowed to interbreed, how many different genotypes would result?

  1. 2
  2. 3
  3. 4
  4. 5
Answer and explanation

B: 3

The first cross bb × BB produces Bb offspring. Crossing two of those offspring gives BB, Bb, bB and bb. Bb and bB are the same genotype, so there are three distinct genotypes: BB, Bb and bb.

Question 45

From an evolutionary standpoint, the older a fossil- bearing rock is the more likely it is to contain

  1. aves as opposed to amphibians
  2. invertebrates as opposed to vertebrates
  3. angiosperms as opposed to algae
  4. vertebrates as opposed to invertebrates
Answer and explanation

B: invertebrates as opposed to vertebrates

Invertebrate animal lineages appear in the fossil record before vertebrates. Very old fossil-bearing deposits are therefore more likely to preserve invertebrates than vertebrates, although the actual fossils depend on the environment and preservation.

Question 47

Which listed feature has the least direct mechanical role in climbing and balancing during arboreal life?

  1. possession of four limbs
  2. possession of claws
  3. development of a long tail
  4. counter shading of coat colour
Answer and explanation

D: counter shading of coat colour

Countershading affects how an animal is seen by others. It does not directly provide grip or mechanical balance for climbing, unlike limbs, claws and, in many tree-living species, a balancing tail.

Question 48

Which of the following is one of Lamarck’s theories?

  1. some variations are more favorable to existence in a given environment than others
  2. all living organisms are constantly involved in a struggle for existence
  3. the size of a given population remains fairly constant
  4. new species originate through the inheritance of acquired traits
Answer and explanation

D: new species originate through the inheritance of acquired traits

Lamarck proposed that characteristics acquired during an organism’s lifetime could be inherited. This historical idea differs from natural selection acting on inherited variation across generations.
