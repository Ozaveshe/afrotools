Question 1

Which property of viruses is also a characteristic of living organisms?

  1. possess transmittable characters
  2. move from one place to another
  3. respond to stimulation
  4. ingest food materials
Answer and explanation

A: possess transmittable characters

Viruses contain genetic material whose characteristics can pass to new virus particles when they replicate inside suitable host cells. Heredity is a life-associated property, although viruses do not carry out independent cellular metabolism.

Question 2

Which structure is common to freshwater Amoeba and Paramecium?

  1. Oral groove
  2. Trichocyst
  3. Contractile vacuole
  4. Cilia
Answer and explanation

C: Contractile vacuole

Freshwater Amoeba and Paramecium possess contractile vacuoles that collect and expel excess water. This helps maintain water balance as water enters from their relatively dilute surroundings.

Question 3

Hydra removes undigested food by

  1. passing it through the anus
  2. passing it through the mouth
  3. means of a contractile vacuole
  4. egesting it through the body surface
Answer and explanation

B: passing it through the mouth

Hydra has a gastrovascular cavity with a single opening. Food enters through this mouth, and undigested remains leave through the same opening because there is no separate anus.

Which listed animal can have homodont dentition?

  1. Rat
  2. Man
  3. Lizard
  4. Pigeon
Answer and explanation

C: Lizard

Some lizards have numerous similarly shaped teeth, the homodont pattern. Humans and rats have differentiated tooth types, while a pigeon has a toothless beak rather than homodont dentition.

Answer and explanation

Question 14

The nephridia in the earthworm form part of the

  1. reproductive system
  2. respiratory system
  3. circulatory system
  4. excretory system
Answer and explanation

D: excretory system

Earthworm nephridia collect fluid, reclaim useful substances and eliminate metabolic wastes. They form the excretory system rather than the reproductive, respiratory or circulatory system.

Question 16

Which listed plant cell type is living at maturity and has relatively thin primary walls?

  1. Collenchyma
  2. Sieve tube
  3. Xylem vessel
  4. Sclerenchyma
Answer and explanation

B: Sieve tube

Sieve-tube elements are living conducting cells with primary walls. At maturity they lose their nuclei and depend on companion cells. Collenchyma remains living but has unevenly thickened walls, while mature vessels and sclerenchyma are generally dead.

Question 18

The first step in the process of photosynthesis is the

  1. activation of the chlorophyll
  2. photolysis of water
  3. reduction of carbondioxide
  4. formation of sugar
Answer and explanation

A: activation of the chlorophyll

Photosynthesis begins when pigments absorb light and their electrons become excited. This supplies energy for the light reactions; water splitting and the subsequent carbon-fixation reactions depend on this initial capture of light energy.

Question 22

Immediately after a ring of bark and phloem is removed from a stem while the xylem remains intact,

  1. plant dies immediately
  2. plant dies after two days
  3. movement of food is not affected
  4. movement of mineral salts is hardly affected
Answer and explanation

D: movement of mineral salts is hardly affected

Removing a ring of bark interrupts phloem transport of sugars. If the deeper xylem remains intact, its initial upward transport of water and dissolved mineral salts can continue, although later root starvation damages the plant.

Question 25

Deficiency of which environmental factor causes etiolation in seedlings?

  1. mineral salts
  2. water
  3. carbondioxide
  4. light
Answer and explanation

D: light

Etiolation occurs when seedlings develop with insufficient light. They typically become pale and elongated as normal light-dependent development is limited. Adequate light promotes greening and a more compact growth pattern.

Question 26

Muscles are indirectly attached to bones by means of

  1. ligaments
  2. membranes
  3. tendons
  4. sutures
Answer and explanation

C: tendons

Tendons connect skeletal muscles to bones and transmit the force of contraction. Ligaments chiefly connect bone to bone at joints, helping stabilise them rather than serving as the normal muscle attachment.

Question 35

The part of the ear that is concerned with balance is the

  1. semicircular canals
  2. incus
  3. malleus
  4. Eustachian tube
Answer and explanation

A: semicircular canals

The semicircular canals detect rotational movements of the head and contribute to balance. Other vestibular structures detect tilt and linear acceleration. The incus and malleus transmit sound vibrations rather than detecting balance.

Question 36

If an organism has a diploid chromosome number of 2n = 16, how many chromosomes are present in each normal haploid gamete?

  1. 32
  2. 16
  3. 8
  4. 4
Answer and explanation

C: 8

Normal meiosis halves the chromosome number when producing gametes. A diploid cell with 2n = 16 therefore produces haploid gametes with n = 8 chromosomes, one chromosome from each homologous pair.

Question 37

In a Mendelian monohybrid cross, pure-breeding normal-winged Drosophila (NN) are crossed with pure-breeding short-winged flies (nn). Normal wings are dominant. When the F1 flies interbreed, what are the expected F1 and F2 phenotypes?

  1. F1 all short-winged; F2 all normal-winged
  2. F1 all normal-winged; F2 all short-winged
  3. F1 all short-winged; three quarters of F2 short-winged
  4. F1 all normal-winged; three quarters of F2 normal-winged
Answer and explanation

D: F1 all normal-winged; three quarters of F2 normal-winged

Let N represent the dominant normal-wing allele and n the short-wing allele. NN × nn gives only Nn offspring with normal wings. Crossing Nn × Nn gives NN, Nn, Nn and nn, so the expected normal-winged proportion is three quarters.

Question 44

In a brackish environment where dry-season evaporation exceeds freshwater input, salinity generally

  1. increases immediately after rain
  2. increases at the end of the rainy season
  3. decreases with increase in micro-organisms
  4. increases during the dry season
Answer and explanation

During a dry season, reduced freshwater input and evaporation can concentrate the salts in brackish water. Rainfall generally dilutes salts, so the seasonal effect depends on the balance of water inputs and losses.

Question 50

Free nitrogen is released to the atmosphere by

  1. nitrogen fixing bacteria
  2. nitrifying bacteria
  3. denitrifying bacteria
  4. saprophytic bacteria
Answer and explanation

C: denitrifying bacteria

Denitrifying microorganisms reduce oxidised nitrogen compounds such as nitrate, releasing gases that can include nitrogen gas. Nitrogen fixation moves atmospheric nitrogen into biologically usable compounds, the opposite overall direction.
