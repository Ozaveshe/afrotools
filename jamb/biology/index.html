A dry soil sample receives 50 mL of water and 35 mL drains out. If evaporation and other losses are negligible, what percentage of the added water is retained?

  1. 46.7%
  2. 25.0%
  3. 20.0%
  4. 30.0%
  5. 58.3%
Answer and explanation

D: 30.0%

Retained water = 50 − 35 = 15 mL. As a percentage of the 50 mL added, this is (15 ÷ 50) × 100 = 30%. This calculation uses the water added as the denominator; soil mass or soil volume would be needed for a soil-water-content percentage.

Question 50

Free nitrogen is released to the atmosphere by

  1. nitrogen fixing bacteria
  2. nitrifying bacteria
  3. denitrifying bacteria
  4. saprophytic bacteria
Answer and explanation

C: denitrifying bacteria

Denitrifying microorganisms reduce oxidised nitrogen compounds such as nitrate, releasing gases that can include nitrogen gas. Nitrogen fixation moves atmospheric nitrogen into biologically usable compounds, the opposite overall direction.

Nitrogen –fixing micro-organisms in leguminous plants live symbiotically in the

  1. root nodules
  2. tap roots
  3. branch roots
  4. root hairs
Answer and explanation

A: root nodules

Legumes form root nodules containing symbiotic nitrogen-fixing bacteria. The plant supplies carbon compounds, while the bacteria convert atmospheric nitrogen into forms that can enter the plant’s nitrogen metabolism.

Question 50

Fossil records found in sedimentary rocks offer some explanation for the theory of evolution because

  1. the deposits have remains of organisms characteristic of when they were formed
  2. different strata have remains of organisms of the same kind
  3. only organisms with strong parts are fossilized
  4. most animals and plant fossils bear little resemblance to present-day living specimens
Answer and explanation

A: the deposits have remains of organisms characteristic of when they were formed

Sedimentary layers can preserve remains of organisms that lived when the deposits formed. Comparing fossils in successive layers reveals changes through time and helps reconstruct the history of life.

Which homologous anatomical pattern supports common ancestry among whales, humans, birds and dogs?

  1. thick skin
  2. the underlying pentadactyl limb pattern, modified in different groups
  3. tail
  4. epidermal structures
Answer and explanation

B: the underlying pentadactyl limb pattern, modified in different groups

The shared underlying limb pattern in these vertebrates is evidence of common ancestry. The structures have been modified for swimming, grasping, flying or running, but comparable bones remain recognisable.
