Question 1

Root hairs develop as extensions of cells in the

  1. Root apex
  2. Epidermis of roots
  3. Vascular bundles
  4. Endodermis
  5. Pericycle
Answer and explanation

B: Epidermis of roots

A root hair is a tubular extension of a root epidermal cell. It increases the surface area available for absorbing water and mineral ions. The pericycle lies deeper inside the root and gives rise to lateral roots rather than root hairs.

The diagram represents a cross-section of part of a dicot plant. The main function of 6 is to

  1. Separate 5 from 7
  2. Produce more of 5 and 7
  3. Produce cork
  4. Translocate water and mineral salts
  5. Conduct carbon dioxide to the other parts
Answer and explanation

B: Produce more of 5 and 7

The vascular cambium divides to produce secondary xylem towards the inside and secondary phloem towards the outside. It therefore adds more of the tissues labelled 7 and 5. Cork is produced by cork cambium, a different meristem.

  1. Surround the inner tissues
  2. Produce cork
  3. Produce root hairs
  4. Produce lateral roots
  5. Produce more of 3
Answer and explanation

D: Produce lateral roots

Lateral roots arise from the pericycle, the cell layer just inside the endodermis. New root primordia develop internally and grow out through the surrounding tissues. Root hairs instead arise from epidermal cells on the root surface.

Question 5

In a typical dicot leaf, guard cells differ from ordinary epidermal pavement cells because they

  1. Have no definite shape
  2. Lack nuclei
  3. Are defined by being smaller
  4. Contain chloroplasts
  5. Lack vacuoles
Answer and explanation

D: Contain chloroplasts

In a typical dicot leaf epidermis, guard cells contain chloroplasts whereas ordinary epidermal pavement cells usually do not. Guard cells also possess nuclei and vacuoles. Their changes in turgor regulate the opening of the stomatal pore.

Question 7

In botanical growth-form terminology, herbs differ from shrubs because herbs

  1. Do not produce fruits
  2. Are necessarily useful to herbalists
  3. Have nonwoody stems
  4. Are only annuals
  5. Are only perennials
Answer and explanation

C: Have nonwoody stems

In botanical usage, herbs are herbaceous plants with nonwoody above-ground stems. Shrubs retain woody stems. Herbs can be annual, biennial or perennial, and the word does not mean that the plant must be medicinal.

Question 9

A hydrogen-to-oxygen atomic ratio of 2:1 is commonly associated with which class of organic compounds?

  1. Proteins
  2. Carbohydrates
  3. Fats
  4. Fatty acids and glycerol
  5. Amino acids
Answer and explanation

B: Carbohydrates

Many common carbohydrates have hydrogen and oxygen atoms in a 2:1 ratio, as in glucose, C₆H₁₂O₆. This ratio is a useful introductory clue, but it does not alone prove that an unknown compound is a carbohydrate; there are exceptions and other compounds with the same ratio.

Question 10

  1. Magnesium and iron
  2. Calcium and potassium
  3. Calcium and sulphur
  4. Potassium and sulphur
  5. Phosphorus and potassium.
Answer and explanation

A: Magnesium and iron

Magnesium is the central metal atom in chlorophyll. Iron is required for processes involved in chlorophyll synthesis, although it is not the central atom of the chlorophyll molecule. Deficiency of either can therefore impair normal green pigmentation.

Question 15

Movement of a plant part whose direction is independent of the direction of an external stimulus is called

  1. Taxis
  2. Tropism
  3. Haptotropic movement
  4. Nastic movement
  5. Phototropism
Answer and explanation

D: Nastic movement

A nastic movement is a plant-part response whose direction is independent of the direction from which the stimulus arrives. In a tropism, the direction of growth depends on the stimulus direction. Phototropism concerns light, and thigmotropism concerns directional touch.

  2. Lateral buds

  4. Dry outer scale leaves
  5. Fleshy leaf bases
Answer and explanation

E: Fleshy leaf bases

The onion bulb stores much of its food in thick, fleshy leaf bases or scales. These surround a short basal stem. The dry outer protective scales are not the principal succulent storage tissue.

  1. multiple fruit
  2. Simple fruit
  3. Aggregate fruit
  4. Dehiscent fruit
  5. Indehiscent fruit.
Answer and explanation

C: Aggregate fruit

An aggregate fruit develops from several separate carpels of a single flower. Each carpel contributes a fruitlet. A multiple fruit instead develops from flowers grouped in an inflorescence, while a simple fruit comes from one ovary.

Question 22

Which of the following diseases is NOT caused by a virus?

  1. Rinderpest
  2. Maize rust
  3. Newcastle disease
  4. Swine fever
  5. Cassava mosaic disease.
Answer and explanation

B: Maize rust

Maize rust is caused by fungi, such as Puccinia sorghi in common rust. The other listed diseases are viral: rinderpest, Newcastle disease, swine fever and cassava mosaic disease. A fungal rust pathogen is biologically different from a virus.

Question 24

An organism with two identical alleles at a particular gene locus is called

  1. A heterozygote
  2. A hybrid
  3. An allelomorph
  4. A homozygote
  5. A diploid
Answer and explanation

D: A homozygote

An organism with two identical alleles at a particular gene locus is homozygous at that locus. For example, AA and aa are homozygous genotypes, whereas Aa is heterozygous. Diploid describes chromosome-set number rather than whether the two alleles match.

  3. They can grow in soils poor in nitrogenous nutrients
  4. They supplement their nitrogen supply by digesting insects
  5. Examples include butterworts, sundews and pitcher plants
Answer and explanation

Insectivorous plants attract some insects as prey, not solely as pollinators. Digestion of prey supplies nutrients such as nitrogen, helping these plants live in nutrient-poor habitats. They generally still use photosynthesis to make carbohydrates.

  2. Atlas, remaining cervical, axis, thoracic, lumbar
  3. Atlas, axis, thoracic, remaining cervical, lumbar
  4. Atlas, axis, remaining cervical, thoracic, lumbar
  5. Atlas, thoracic, remaining cervical, axis, lumbar
Answer and explanation

D: Atlas, axis, remaining cervical, thoracic, lumbar

The atlas is the first cervical vertebra, C 1, and the axis is the second, C 2. The remaining cervical vertebrae follow, then the thoracic vertebrae and then the lumbar vertebrae. Atlas and axis are therefore part of the cervical region, not separate regions.

Question 37

Positive phototropic bending of a coleoptile is brought about by

  1. Auxin moving away from the region that elongates more
  2. An even auxin distribution across both sides
  3. Auxin inhibiting growth on the side that elongates more
  4. Auxin accumulating on the shaded side and promoting greater elongation there
  5. The absence of auxin in the coleoptile
Answer and explanation

D: Auxin accumulating on the shaded side and promoting greater elongation there

Under one-sided illumination, auxin becomes more concentrated on the shaded side of a coleoptile. At growth-promoting concentrations, cells there elongate more than cells on the illuminated side. The unequal elongation bends the shoot towards the light.

Cassava’s swollen storage organ is a storage root. It lacks the axillary buds associated with nodes on a stem tuber. Storing food alone does not make an organ a stem: roots and modified stems can both act as food stores.

Question 39

The main function of the middle-ear ossicles—malleus, incus and stapes—is

  1. Transmission of vibrations
  2. Regulation of air pressure
  3. Support of the inner ear
  4. Maintenance of balance during movement
  5. Secretion of oil
Answer and explanation

A: Transmission of vibrations

The malleus, incus and stapes form a chain that transmits vibrations from the eardrum to the oval window of the inner ear. This transfer helps sound energy enter the fluid-filled inner ear. Balance is mainly sensed by the vestibular apparatus, not the ossicles.

Question 43

In Rhizopus, the storage carbohydrate is

  1. Glucose
  2. Paramylon
  3. Glycogen
  4. Starch
  5. Oil
Answer and explanation

C: Glycogen

Rhizopus is a fungus, and glycogen is a fungal storage polysaccharide. It consists of branched chains of glucose units. Starch is the usual plant storage polysaccharide, while oil is a lipid rather than a carbohydrate.
