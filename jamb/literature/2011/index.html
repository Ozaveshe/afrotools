Question 2

In Sons and Daughters, which dramatic category fits the resolution of the family’s conflict over the children’s careers?

  1. tragedy
  2. comedy
  3. farce
  4. melodrama
Answer and explanation

B: comedy

The conflict resolves when James allows his younger children to pursue their chosen artistic careers. That conciliatory outcome supports comedy rather than a tragic ending in destruction.

Question 7

In Romeo and Juliet, which event marks the denouement of the feud?

  1. at the family feast
  2. when Romeo kills Paris at the tomb
  3. at the reconciliation of the feuding families.
  4. when Romeo is informed of Juliet’s death
Answer and explanation

C: at the reconciliation of the feuding families.

The denouement resolves the central conflict after the deaths of the lovers. The families recognise the consequences of their feud and reconcile at the end of the play.

Question 8

In Romeo and Juliet, who brings Romeo the news of Juliet’s apparent death in Mantua?

  1. Balthasar
  2. Friar Lawrence
  3. Boy
  4. Friar John
Answer and explanation

A: Balthasar

Balthasar travels to Mantua and reports that Juliet is dead. Friar John instead carries the undelivered explanatory letter; confusing those messengers obscures the tragedy’s crucial misunderstanding.

Question 17

What kind of society does George Orwell portray in Nineteen Eighty-Four?

  1. a useless past
  2. a totalitarian future
  3. an unstable moment
  4. a peaceful atmosphere
Answer and explanation

B: a totalitarian future

Orwell imagines a society dominated by an intrusive Party that controls information and suppresses independent thought. That imagined political order is a totalitarian future relative to the novel’s publication.

Question 18

In Nineteen Eighty-Four, the Party’s oppression destroys Winston’s dreams of what?

  1. freedom and democracy
  2. internal security
  3. wealth and capitalism
  4. sovereignty
Answer and explanation

A: freedom and democracy

Winston desires freedom of thought and resistance to the Party’s total control. Oppression destroys those hopes; the novel does not present his central aspiration as acquiring wealth or improving state surveillance.

Question 27

In Andrew Marvell’s To His Coy Mistress, what does the imagery of death principally emphasise?

  1. appreciate God's power
  2. underscore life's transience
  3. condemn the lady
  4. scare the lady

Question 28

In D. H. Lawrence’s Bat, which device describes the delayed discovery of the flying creatures’ identity?

  1. elision
  2. hyperbole
  3. suspense
  4. oxymoron
Answer and explanation

C: suspense

The speaker’s delayed recognition of the flying creatures holds back their true identity. The movement from apparent swallows to bats creates suspense as readers follow that discovery.

Question 30

What tone is created by the comic train-carriage episode in Wendy Cope’s Sonnet VII?

  1. Persuasive
  2. Humorous
  3. Optimistic
  4. Mournful
Answer and explanation

B: Humorous

The sonnet jokes that reading poetry aloud can clear a train carriage and provide solitude. This playful reversal makes its tone humorous, even while it comments on attitudes towards poetry.

Question 31

The large space above the proscenium in a theatre from which the scenes are controlled is called

  1. aside
  2. setting
  3. anachronism
  4. flies
Answer and explanation

D: flies

The flies are the upper stage space used to suspend and move scenery and equipment. An aside is a kind of speech, while anachronism concerns something placed in the wrong historical time.

  3. Directs a play
  4. Acts in a film
Answer and explanation

  3. be ready for pleasure
  4. be presented on stage
Answer and explanation

Question 39

The plot of a story generally refers to the

  1. intrigue made by a character against the hero
  2. way the writer ends the story
  3. way in which the events of the story are organised
  4. way in which the writer begins the story
Answer and explanation

Plot is the organisation of a story’s events and their relationships. It includes more than the opening or ending, and does not mean only a character’s scheme against another character.

  1. Personification and simile
  2. Irony and suspense
  3. Alliteration and synecdoche
  4. Rhyme and refrain

A: Personification and simile

The moon is treated as a woman who delays the speaker’s desires, which is personification. Comparing her with a stepmother or dowager using like creates a simile.

Question 45

  2. metaphor
  3. personification
  4. simile

A heart of stone directly represents emotional hardness or lack of sympathy. No literal stone heart is intended, and the direct comparison makes the phrase a metaphor.

  1. Death
  2. Rainfall
