Question 11

There are many indicators with which to assess or measure corruption. One of them is the affluent living habit of the public official compared to his/her income. Corruption occurs when a public official expects to be induced to perform an act which that public official is ordinarily required to do by law. Corruption can slow down development. One of the most widely discussed consequences of corruption is the distortion of governmental expenditure. This often results in public money being wasted on white elephant projects, rather than people-oriented services such as health and education. As a result, more opportunities are presented for corrupt use or diversion of funds. Raising the ethical standards of governance can lead to many benefits especially for the economic, political and social development of a country. Fighting corruption and promoting governance is therefore crucial to developing an environment that facilitates the social, political and economic development of the people. However, while there are often general statements made about the effects of corruption on poverty and development, there is not an explicit recognition that corruption is more than just wealth misappropriation or abuse of power. Corruption impoverishes countries and deprives their citizens of good governance. It destabilizes economic system. When organized crime and other illegal activities flourish, basic public functions are eroded and the quality of life of the people is reduced. Bribery, for example is universally regarded as a crime, but it also reflects socio-economic problems that require broad-based preventive measures and the involvement of the society at large. Another implication of global measures against corruption is making government work better by improving the economy. Finally, redesigning political and regulatory structures will reduce corruption and other anti-system players that encourage corrupt practices.

According to the writer, corruption is triggered off by

  1. unnecessary affluence
  2. selfish interests
  3. private officials
  4. public officers
Answer and explanation

B: selfish interests

The passage describes an official expecting an inducement for a duty already required by law. That pursuit of personal benefit shows selfish interests; affluent living is described as an indicator rather than the trigger.

Question 26

Choose the most appropriate interpretation: Though Mr. Iro is our new chairman, he views other members with a jaundiced eye.

  1. He takes a rather forceful position on dealing with his members
  2. He takes an unfavourable position concerning his members
  3. He takes a sickly view of his members
  4. He takes a rather hazy view of his members
Answer and explanation

B: He takes an unfavourable position concerning his members

A jaundiced view is an unfavourable or prejudiced way of looking at someone. Mr Iro’s attitude towards the other members is therefore negative, rather than simply forceful or unclear.

  2. People are not concerned about who rules them
  3. The rulers are not concerned about the people
  4. People who rule are not interested in the ruled
Answer and explanation

B: People are not concerned about who rules them

Not being interested in who rules means not being concerned about the identity of the ruler. Option B preserves that meaning; the other choices change who is interested or introduce a different claim.

Question 28

  1. it is always good to get midway in anything one does
  2. it is always good to act with moderation
  3. it is always good to move away from the fore front
  4. it is always good to work very hard
Answer and explanation

B: it is always good to act with moderation

To steer a middle course is to avoid extremes and act moderately. The expression describes a balanced approach, not a literal halfway position or simply working hard.

Question 30

  2. They were unhappy because they had been despised by their opponents
  3. They were ashamed because they had been defeated
  4. They moved with their tails between their legs
Answer and explanation

C: They were ashamed because they had been defeated

To leave with your tail between your legs is to feel defeated and ashamed. In this football context, option C captures that idiomatic meaning; the phrase does not describe a happy departure.

  3. He managed to get himself out of a difficult situation
  4. He managed to talk on his way

Question 35

Choose the most appropriate interpretation: The woman was mournful as her husband was found dead drunk.

  1. She was sad because her husband was absolutely drunk
  2. She was apprehensive that her husband would drink as soon as he recovered from drunken stupor
  3. She was sad because her husband was drunk and always as helpless as a dead man
  4. She was mourning because her husband drank and died
Answer and explanation

A: She was sad because her husband was absolutely drunk

In dead drunk, dead intensifies drunk and means completely or absolutely. The sentence says the husband was extremely drunk and the woman was sad; it does not say that he died.

Question 38

Choose the option opposite in meaning to “redundant”: The teacher said that Ali's essay was full of many redundant details.

  1. unexplained
  2. strange
  3. necessary
  4. useful
Answer and explanation

C: necessary

Redundant details are unnecessary because they add nothing needed. Necessary is therefore the direct opposite; useful concerns benefit rather than whether the details are required.

Question 39

Choose the option opposite in meaning to “myriad”: His father surmounted the myriad of obstacles on his way.

  1. most
  2. few
  3. all
  4. many
Answer and explanation

B: few

A myriad is a very large number. Few expresses the opposite quantity, whereas many has a similar meaning and all or most describe the proportion rather than a small number.

Question 48

Choose the option opposite in meaning to “plethora”: The report of the committee contained a plethora of details.

  1. shortage
  2. simplicity
  3. multitude
  4. spectrum
Answer and explanation

A: shortage

A plethora is an excessive or very large amount. A shortage is too little, so it gives the opposite quantity. Multitude also indicates a large number, while simplicity concerns complexity.
