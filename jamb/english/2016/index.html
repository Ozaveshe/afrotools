Question 1

It is not difficult to make a film, but it is very difficult to make a good film: technically polished, suited to its audience, and telling its story clearly through the precise balance of visual and audio. Film-making is a job for professionals; the amateur is always a disaster. The only reasonable exception is the local newsreel made on a shoestring and intended to serve the same purpose as a parish magazine or works news-sheet. Here the content can overcome the limitations of the maker, and his temptation to try to use such a production more widely and for a different audience should be firmly resisted. Many people have a wrong approach to film-making. Too often a film begins because someone suggests brightly, “Why don’t we make a film about this?” The idea of film is generally attractive, provided that not too much money is required. A film is then made, frequently without adequate planning or with only the sketchiest concept of how it might be used. It is right that film should be considered as a possible way of putting a story over, but the objective must be clear and the nature of the audience understood first. Then the suitability of film must be assessed and the means of reaching the audience by film ensured. Only then is it advisable to go ahead and find the money before embarking on the complex process of getting the film made. Adapted from Baman P. and Ellis N. (1977), Manual of Public Relations.

The expression, visual and audio, as used in the passage, refers to

  1. vision and auras
  2. sight and colour
  3. picture and sound
  4. color and props
Answer and explanation

C: picture and sound

Visual concerns what is seen and audio concerns what is heard. In a film, that is the combination of picture and sound. The other pairs omit sound or introduce unrelated elements such as auras or props.

Question 2

The word professionals, as used in the passage suggests

  1. specialists
  2. entrepreneurs
  3. detractors
  4. commentators
Answer and explanation

A: specialists

The writer contrasts professionals with amateurs, emphasising the expertise needed to make a good film. Specialists is therefore the closest meaning. Entrepreneurs run businesses, detractors criticise and commentators give opinions; those roles do not necessarily imply film-making expertise.

Question 36

Choose the option that best explains the sentence: His property went down the drain

  1. He completely lost his property
  2. He kept his property down in the rain
  3. His property was completely drained
  4. He drained his property by the canal
Answer and explanation

A: He completely lost his property

To go down the drain means to be lost or wasted. Applied to his property, it means he lost it. The sentence is figurative and does not describe rain, a canal or liquid being drained from the property.

Question 38

Choose the option that best explains the sentence: At the end of the competition, the team brought up the rear

  1. The team was booed
  2. The team competed strongly
  3. The team came last
  4. The team was suspended indefinitely
Answer and explanation

C: The team came last

To bring up the rear is to be at the back or last. In a competition, this means the team finished last. The expression does not say that the team was booed, performed strongly or was suspended.

Question 66

  1. However tired you are
  2. However tired are you
  3. However you are tired
  4. However are you tired
Answer and explanation

A: However tired you are

However followed by an adjective means no matter how much that quality applies. The clause uses statement order: however tired you are. The other alternatives put the words in question order or separate however from tired.

Question 69

  1. agreeing
  2. to agreed
  3. to agree
  4. agreed
Answer and explanation

D: agreed

Question 72

Choose the option that best completes the sentence: Ado struck Isa with_____blow across his shoulder.

  1. mightily
  2. a mighty
  3. mightiest
  4. the mightier
Answer and explanation

B: a mighty

Blow is a singular countable noun here, requiring a determiner and an adjective. A mighty blow is a complete phrase. Mightily is an adverb; the comparative and superlative alternatives lack the comparison or form needed here.

Question 76

Choose the option that best completes the sentence: _____you do, slow down and take your time

  1. Whatever
  2. However
  3. Wherever
  4. Whenever
Answer and explanation

A: Whatever

Whatever you do means no matter what activity you undertake. Whatever supplies the object of do. However, wherever and whenever concern manner, place or time and do not supply that missing object.
