Question 1

The growth in the world's population is in observance of the divine mandate and effective management of the world health system through legislation, enactment and sensitization. In the past, the growing human population was reduced through scarcity of food, floods, earthquakes, wars, etc. But in this time and age, man has found solutions to some of the mentioned catalysts to death among the human population. There is now a clarion call to man to take responsibility in reducing the world's population, which may culminate in food scarcity, by consciously sensitizing people to the benefits of family planning.

According to the passage, what concern does the writer express about population growth?

  1. That population growth was for the good of all
  2. That a high population growth rate was dangerous to all
  3. That without population the world would be empty
  4. That population explosion is outdated
Answer and explanation

B: That a high population growth rate was dangerous to all

The writer warns that population growth may lead to food scarcity and calls for family planning. B best reflects that concern. The passage does not say population growth benefits everyone, that the world would otherwise be empty, or that population explosion is outdated.
