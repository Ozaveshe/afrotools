Question 37

Choose the option closest in meaning to “remarks” in this sentence: “Adisa had very good remarks on his report card”

  1. details
  2. design
  3. comment
  4. ideas
Answer and explanation

C: comment

Remarks on a report card are comments about the student, so C is the closest meaning. Details are particulars, a design is an arrangement, and ideas are thoughts.

Question 38

Choose the option closest in meaning to “surmount” in this sentence: “We must work hard in order to surmount our difficulties.”

  1. decrease
  2. overcome
  3. remove
  4. meet
Answer and explanation

B: overcome

To surmount difficulties is to overcome them, so B is the best answer. Decreasing difficulties only makes them smaller; meeting them means encountering them. “Overcome” most directly expresses dealing successfully with the difficulties.

Question 39

  1. riches
  2. fees
  3. income
  4. support
Answer and explanation

C: income

Revenue is income received, including the money a government collects through taxes and duties. C is therefore the broad matching word. Fees are only one possible type of income; riches refer to wealth, and support can mean many kinds of assistance.

Question 40

  1. investigate
  2. punish
  3. spy on
  4. watch
Answer and explanation

A: investigate

Question 45

Choose the word that most suitably fills the gap: “I passed my entrance examination ______ unfortunately, I was not accepted by the university.”

  1. and
  2. because
  3. for
  4. but
Answer and explanation

D: but

The second clause contrasts with the expectation created by passing the examination: the speaker nevertheless did not gain admission. “But” makes that contrast explicit and is the most suitable choice. “And” merely adds information; “because” and “for” would introduce a reason.

Question 46

Choose the word or phrase that most suitably fills the gap: “The only witness to the theft was so shocked that he couldn't describe exactly ______.”

  1. what the thief looked like
  2. how the thief looked like
  3. that the thief looked like
  4. which the thief looked like
Answer and explanation

A: what the thief looked like

A description of appearance answers “what someone looks like”. The embedded clause therefore reads “what the thief looked like”. “How ... looked like” combines two incompatible patterns; “that” does not request the missing description, and “which” would need a specified choice.

Question 47

Choose the word or phrase that most suitably fills the gap: “The lady accompanied by her two friends ______.”

  1. has arrived
  2. have arrived
  3. have been arriving
  4. is being arrived
Answer and explanation

A: has arrived

The subject is the singular noun “lady”, so the verb is “has arrived”. “Accompanied by her two friends” adds information but does not make the subject plural. “Have” does not agree with the subject, and “is being arrived” is not a standard passive construction.

Question 50

Choose the word or phrase that most suitably fills the gap: “This is the lady ______ stole my car.”

  1. who
  2. which
  3. whom
  4. would have
Answer and explanation

A: who

“Who” refers to the lady and acts as the subject of “stole”. “Whom” is an object form, “which” normally refers to things here, and “would have” cannot introduce this relative clause.

Question 51

Choose the word or phrase that most suitably fills the gap: “If you were around, I ______ happy.”

  1. would be
  2. would have been
  3. was
  4. have been
Answer and explanation

A: would be

This is a hypothetical present situation: “If you were around, I would be happy.” The usual pattern is if + past form, followed by would + base verb. “Would have been” normally describes a past result; “was” and “have been” do not express the intended hypothetical result.

Question 53

Choose the word or phrase that most suitably fills the gap: “While I was gone, who ______ here?”

  1. came
  2. comes
  3. have come
  4. has come
Answer and explanation

A: came

“While I was gone” identifies a completed past period, so the past simple “came” fits. “Comes” is present simple. “Has come” links an event to the present, while “have come” also fails singular agreement with “who” in this question.

Question 65

  1. mount
  2. sit
  3. by
  4. in
Answer and explanation

A: mount

To mount a horse is to get onto it to ride. “Should” takes the base verb “mount”. “Sit” would need “on” before the horse, and “by” and “in” are prepositions rather than the required verb.

Question 68

Choose the option closest in opposite meaning to “agreed” in this sentence: “They all agreed that John was a fast runner after the race.”

  1. cheered
  2. denied
  3. said
  4. shouted
Answer and explanation

B: denied

Agreeing that a claim is true expresses acceptance; denying it expresses rejection. “Denied” is therefore the closest opposite. “Said” and “shouted” describe ways of expressing something without necessarily accepting or rejecting it, while “cheered” expresses approval.

Question 69

Choose the option closest in opposite meaning to “empty” in this sentence: “Last year our well was empty.”

  1. few
  2. full
  3. shallow
  4. low
Answer and explanation

B: full

An empty well contains no water; a full well contains water up to its capacity. “Full” is the opposite in this context. “Shallow” describes depth, “low” describes level, and “few” refers to a small number of countable things.

Answer and explanation

Question 71

Choose the word or phrase that most suitably fills the gap: “Olu is a very good friend on ______ I can rely.”

  1. where
  2. which
  3. whom
  4. who
Answer and explanation

C: whom

In formal English, “whom” follows a preposition when it refers to a person: “on whom I can rely”. “Who” is the subject form, “which” refers to things, and “where” refers to a place.
