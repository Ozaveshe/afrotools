Question 36

Choose the option opposite in meaning to “idiot” in this sentence: “As an idiot, the boy is weak in class”

  1. a deviant
  2. a dunce
  3. an expert
  4. a genius
Answer and explanation

D: a genius

The sentence uses “idiot” to suggest very low intelligence. “A genius”, someone with exceptional intellectual ability, gives the clearest opposite. A dunce is similar in meaning, a deviant concerns conduct, and an expert describes skill in a particular area.

Question 38

Choose the option opposite in meaning to “flexible” in this sentence: “The principal was advised to be flexible on critical issues”

  1. livid
  2. cautious
  3. evasion
  4. rigid
Answer and explanation

D: rigid

Flexible means willing or able to adapt a position to circumstances. Rigid means fixed and unwilling to change, giving the opposite. Livid concerns anger, cautious means careful, and evasion concerns avoidance.

Question 40

Choose the option opposite in meaning to “efficiency” in this sentence: “Dupe was promoted for her efficiency”

  1. ability
  2. incompetence
  3. inconsistency
  4. rudeness
Answer and explanation

B: incompetence

Efficiency is the ability to do work effectively with little waste. Incompetence means lacking the ability to do a task properly and is the strongest opposite here. Ability supports efficiency, while inconsistency and rudeness describe other weaknesses.

Question 46

Choose the option opposite in meaning to “adduced” in this sentence: “The economist concluded that several factors have been adduced to explain the fall in the birth rate.”

  1. affirmed
  2. diffused
  3. mentioned
  4. refuted
Answer and explanation

D: refuted

To adduce factors is to put them forward as evidence or reasons. To refute them is to reject or show them to be wrong, the opposing action among these choices. Affirmed and mentioned both involve putting something forward; diffused means spread out.

Question 47

Choose the option opposite in meaning to “antidote” in this sentence: “The presidential system is an antidote to some political ailments.”

  1. an answer
  2. a reply
  3. an inquiry
  4. an obstacle
Answer and explanation

D: an obstacle

Figuratively, an antidote is a remedy that counters a problem. An obstacle hinders progress instead of helping to solve the problem, making D the best contrast here. An answer is closer to a solution, while a reply and an inquiry do not express the opposing effect.

Question 48

Choose the option opposite in meaning to “callous” in this sentence: “Ola thought that her father was very callous”

  1. parlous
  2. compassionate
  3. wicked
  4. cheerful
Answer and explanation

B: compassionate

Callous describes someone who is insensitive to other people’s suffering. Compassionate means caring about that suffering and is the opposite. Wicked is another negative quality; cheerful concerns mood, and parlous means dangerous or precarious.

Question 52

Choose the option nearest in meaning to “derogatory” in this sentence: “Okibe was rusticated for his derogatory remark about the principal”

  1. complementary
  2. unsavoury
  3. unwarranted
  4. lackluster
Answer and explanation

B: unsavoury

A derogatory remark expresses disrespect or a low opinion of someone. Unsavoury, meaning unpleasant or objectionable, is the nearest choice here. Unwarranted means unjustified, which is different; lackluster means dull, and complementary means forming a useful combination.

Question 53

Choose the option nearest in meaning to “sovereignty” in this sentence: “Justice is difficult to enforce because people are unwilling to accept any loss of sovereignty.”

  1. autonomy
  2. position
  3. leadership
  4. kingdom
Answer and explanation

A: autonomy

Sovereignty concerns independent authority or the power to govern oneself. Autonomy expresses that independence. Position and leadership do not necessarily include independence, while a kingdom is a territory or state rather than the quality of self-government.
