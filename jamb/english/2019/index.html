Question 36

Choose the option opposite in meaning to “idiot” in this sentence: “As an idiot, the boy is weak in class”

  1. a deviant
  2. a dunce
  3. an expert
  4. a genius
Answer and explanation

D: a genius

The sentence uses “idiot” to suggest very low intelligence. “A genius”, someone with exceptional intellectual ability, gives the clearest opposite. A dunce is similar in meaning, a deviant concerns conduct, and an expert describes skill in a particular area.

Question 38

Choose the option opposite in meaning to “flexible” in this sentence: “The principal was advised to be flexible on critical issues”

  1. livid
  2. cautious
  3. evasion
  4. rigid
Answer and explanation

D: rigid

Flexible means willing or able to adapt a position to circumstances. Rigid means fixed and unwilling to change, giving the opposite. Livid concerns anger, cautious means careful, and evasion concerns avoidance.

Question 40

Choose the option opposite in meaning to “efficiency” in this sentence: “Dupe was promoted for her efficiency”

  1. ability
  2. incompetence
  3. inconsistency
  4. rudeness
Answer and explanation

B: incompetence

Efficiency is the ability to do work effectively with little waste. Incompetence means lacking the ability to do a task properly and is the strongest opposite here. Ability supports efficiency, while inconsistency and rudeness describe other weaknesses.

Question 46

Choose the option opposite in meaning to “adduced” in this sentence: “The economist concluded that several factors have been adduced to explain the fall in the birth rate.”

  1. affirmed
  2. diffused
  3. mentioned
  4. refuted
Answer and explanation

D: refuted

To adduce factors is to put them forward as evidence or reasons. To refute them is to reject or show them to be wrong, the opposing action among these choices. Affirmed and mentioned both involve putting something forward; diffused means spread out.

Question 47

Choose the option opposite in meaning to “antidote” in this sentence: “The presidential system is an antidote to some political ailments.”

  1. an answer
  2. a reply
  3. an inquiry
  4. an obstacle
Answer and explanation

D: an obstacle

Figuratively, an antidote is a remedy that counters a problem. An obstacle hinders progress instead of helping to solve the problem, making D the best contrast here. An answer is closer to a solution, while a reply and an inquiry do not express the opposing effect.

  2. position

Answer and explanation

Sovereignty concerns independent authority or the power to govern oneself. Autonomy expresses that independence. Position and leadership do not necessarily include independence, while a kingdom is a territory or state rather than the quality of self-government.

Answer and explanation

A: liable

Answer and explanation

Question 68

Choose the option that best completes the sentence: There are ______ I need to buy.

  1. lot of thing that
  2. lots of things that
  3. lot of things that
  4. lots of thing that
Answer and explanation

B: lots of things that

“Lots of” can introduce a plural count noun, so “lots of things that” fits both the quantity expression and the plural verb “are”. The singular form requires “a lot of”; “thing” also needs to be plural here.

Question 69

Choose the option that best completes the sentence: It is generally ______ that we should eat less fat and more vegetables.

  1. agreeing
  2. to agreed
  3. to agree
  4. agreed
Answer and explanation

D: agreed

“It is generally agreed that” is the standard construction for an opinion accepted by people in general. “Agreed” follows “is” here. Agreeing and the two infinitive forms do not fit this construction.

  1. two-day
  2. two-days

Answer and explanation

A: two-day

When a number and unit form a compound adjective before a noun, the unit stays singular and the words are joined with a hyphen: a two-day seminar. “Two days” is appropriate as a separate duration, but not in this position.

Question 72

Choose the option that best completes the sentence: Ado struck Isa with ______ blow across his shoulder.

  1. mightily
  2. a mighty
  3. mightiest
  4. the mightier
Answer and explanation

B: a mighty

“Blow” is a singular count noun and needs an appropriate determiner. “A mighty” supplies the article and an adjective meaning powerful. Mightily is an adverb; mightiest lacks its article, and the mightier introduces an unsupported comparison.

Question 76

  1. Whatever
  2. However
  3. Wherever
  4. Whenever
Answer and explanation

A: Whatever

Whatever supplies the object of “do” and means no matter what: whatever activity you do, take your time. However concerns manner, wherever concerns place, and whenever concerns time; they do not supply the missing object in this sentence.

Question 80

Choose the option that best completes the sentence: My sister plays tennis with ______.

  1. he's
  2. his
  3. he
  4. him
Answer and explanation

D: him

After the preposition “with”, a personal pronoun takes its object form: him. He is a subject form, his is possessive, and he’s means he is or he has. Only “with him” correctly completes this sentence.
