Question 1

Zaria owes ₦13,600, buys goods on credit for ₦69,000 and pays suppliers ₦51,600. A ₦3,000 payment cheque is returned unpaid and suppliers allow ₦1,500 discount. Find the closing amount owed.

  1. ₦32,500
  2. ₦32,000
  3. ₦31,000
  4. ₦29,500
Answer and explanation

A: ₦32,500

Closing creditors equal ₦13,600 + ₦69,000 − ₦51,600 + ₦3,000 − ₦1,500 = ₦32,500. The returned cheque restores the debt previously treated as paid.

Question 3

When reconciling from the cash-book bank balance to the bank-statement balance, which item is added?

  1. Uncredited cheques
  2. Direct payments by the bank
  3. Bank charges
  4. Unpresented cheques
Answer and explanation

D: Unpresented cheques

Unpresented cheques have already reduced the cash-book bank balance but not the statement balance. Adding them back when moving from cash book to bank statement reconciles that timing difference.

Question 4

Sales ₦20,000; cost of sales ₦10,000; operating expenses recorded ₦2,500, including prepaid expenses ₦500. Find net profit.

  1. ₦12,500
  2. ₦10,000
  3. ₦8,000
  4. ₦7,500
Answer and explanation

C: ₦8,000

Gross profit is ₦20,000 − ₦10,000 = ₦10,000. Current-period expenses are ₦2,500 − ₦500 = ₦2,000, so net profit is ₦8,000.

Question 5

Sales ₦20,000; cost of sales ₦10,000; operating expenses recorded ₦2,500, including prepaid expenses ₦500. Find gross profit margin.

  1. 100%
  2. 50%
  3. 40%
  4. 30%
Answer and explanation

B: 50%

Gross profit is ₦10,000 on sales of ₦20,000. Gross margin uses sales as its denominator, giving ₦10,000/₦20,000 × 100 = 50%.

Question 6

The main object of book keeping is to record economic.

  1. ₦125,000
  2. ₦114,500
  3. ₦105,500
  4. ₦105,250
Answer and explanation

Premises carry ₦9,000 prior depreciation plus ₦9,000 this year, leaving ₦72,000. Debtors net of 5% allowance are ₦33,250. Total assets shown are ₦105,250.

Question 10

The most convenient cash book used by a petty trader operating in an area where there is no banking facility is

  1. Four column
  2. Three column
  3. Single column.
  4. two column
Answer and explanation

C: Single column.

A single-column cash book records cash receipts and payments without a bank column. It suits a small trader who has no banking transactions to record.

Question 12

Which of the following errors will affect the trial balance totals?

  1. Posting discount allowed to the debit side of the discount allowed account.
  2. Omission of one account from the list when extracting from the ledgers.
  3. Failure to post sales of N 2,000 and purchases of N 2,000 from subsidiary ledgers.
  4. Omission of sales of N 3,000 and purchase of N 2,000.
Answer and explanation

B: Omission of one account from the list when extracting from the ledgers.

Leaving an account balance out of the trial balance omits one side of the ledger totals and can prevent agreement. Complete omission of a transaction from both sides does not have that effect.

Question 13

An expense account is closed by a debit to

  1. An asset account and credit to the expense account.
  2. The expense account and a credit to an asset account.
  3. Profit and loss account and a credit to the expense account.
  4. The expense account and a credit to profit and loss account.
Answer and explanation

C: Profit and loss account and a credit to the expense account.

Closing transfers an expense balance to profit and loss. Debit profit and loss to recognise the expense and credit the expense account to reduce its balance to zero.

Question 20

The items entered in an opening statement of affairs of an enterprise that keeps incomplete record are

  1. Sales income
  2. Receipt from debtors
  3. Fixtures and fittings
  4. Rent paid
Answer and explanation

C: Fixtures and fittings

An opening statement of affairs records assets and liabilities at the opening date. Fixtures and fittings are assets; sales, receipts and rent payments are period transactions.

Question 22

Question 23

The simplest form of single entry procedure consist of keeping a

  1. Day book or general journal
  2. Cash book and ledger accounts showing debtors and creditors balances
  3. Cash journal, sales journal and purchases journal
  4. Day book in which transaction are described in chronological order.
Answer and explanation

B: Cash book and ledger accounts showing debtors and creditors balances

A basic single-entry system commonly keeps a cash book plus personal accounts for debtors and creditors. It lacks the complete double-entry classification needed for all ledger balances.

Question 26

When a customer’s cheque is returned unpaid, what treatment is required in a debtors-control system, including the customer subsidiary ledger?

  1. Debit bank; credit customer and control account
  2. Credit bank; debit customer and control account
  3. Debit customer; credit control account and bank
  4. Credit control account; debit bank and customer
Answer and explanation

B: Credit bank; debit customer and control account

Dishonour reverses the earlier receipt: credit bank and debit receivables control in the general ledger. Also debit the individual customer’s subsidiary account to restore the amount owed.
