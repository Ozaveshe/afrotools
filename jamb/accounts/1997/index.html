Question 1

The most important reason for studying accounting is that

  1. the information provided by accounting is useful in making decisions
  2. accounting plays an important role in a society
  3. the study of accounting leads to a challenging career
  4. accounting provides gross profit information.
Answer and explanation

A: the information provided by accounting is useful in making decisions

Accounting supplies information that supports decisions about resources and performance. This decision usefulness is broader than simply calculating gross profit or offering a career.

Question 3

Creditors use accounting information for the purpose of

  1. planning sales to a company
  2. controlling a company’s affairs
  3. investing in a company
  4. assessing a company liquidity.
Answer and explanation

D: assessing a company liquidity.

Creditors need to assess whether the business can meet its obligations when due. Liquidity information helps them evaluate that short-term payment capacity.

Question 4

The instruments that are generated when firms enter into business transactions with others are called

  1. purchases documents
  2. journals
  3. source documents
  4. invoices.
Answer and explanation

C: source documents

Source documents provide the initial evidence of a transaction, such as an invoice or credit note. Journals are accounting records prepared from that evidence.

Question 6

The effects of the payment of a liability is that it

  1. Increases both assets and liabilities
  2. Increases assets and decreases liabilities
  3. Decreases assets and increases liabilities
  4. Decreases both assets and liabilities
Answer and explanation

D: Decreases both assets and liabilities

Paying a liability uses cash or bank, reducing an asset, and settles an amount owed, reducing a liability. Both sides of the accounting equation decrease equally.

Question 8

The term posting in accounting refers to

  1. recording entries in the journal
  2. transferring the balances in the ledger to the trial balance
  3. tracing amounts from the journal to the ledger to find errors
  4. transferring entries to the ledger from the journal.
Answer and explanation

Posting transfers entries from a journal or other book of original entry into the appropriate ledger accounts. It is different from preparing the trial balance.

Question 9

Erroneous rearrangement of financial figures such as writing N624 as N264 is called

  1. transposition
  2. principle
  3. commission
  4. omission
Answer and explanation

A: transposition

Transposition means reversing the order of digits. Writing 624 as 264 changes the positions of 6 and 2, so this is a transposition error rather than omission.

Question 11

Which of the following demonstrates the imprest system?

  1. Float → expenses paid → cash in bank → float
  2. Float → cash from bank → expenses paid → float
  3. Float → expenses paid → cash from bank → float
  4. Float → cash in bank → expenses paid → float
Answer and explanation

C: Float → expenses paid → cash from bank → float

An imprest begins with an authorised float. Expenses reduce that cash, then reimbursement from the bank restores the original float for the next period.

Question 12

When a sum of money appears on the credit side of the cash book. but not on the debit side of the bank statement, the sum is regarded as

  1. Uncredited cheques
  2. Dishonoured cheques
  3. Direct remittance to the bank
  4. Unpresented cheques
Answer and explanation

D: Unpresented cheques

A cheque issued is credited in the cash book immediately. Until the payee presents it, the bank has not debited the account, so it is an unpresented cheque.

  2. ₦770

Start with the bank statement’s ₦722 credit balance. Add deposits not yet credited, ₦100, and subtract issued cheques not yet presented, ₦122: ₦722 + ₦100 − ₦122 = ₦700.

Question 21

The process of allocating the cost of an intangible asset over its useful life is known as

  1. depreciation
  2. extraction
  3. depletion
  4. amortization
Answer and explanation

D: amortization

Amortisation systematically allocates the cost of an intangible asset with a finite useful life over that life. Depletion concerns natural resources, while depreciation concerns tangible assets.

Question 22

The principal use of control accounts is to

  1. Localize errors within the ledgers
  2. prevent fraud
  3. increase sales
  4. record assets and liabilities
Answer and explanation

A: Localize errors within the ledgers

A control account summarises a ledger’s balances independently. Comparing the total with individual balances helps locate errors within that ledger, although it does not prevent all fraud.

Question 40

On dissolution, a partner has a debit capital balance and is insolvent. Under the rule in Garner v Murray, who bears the resulting deficiency?

  1. Borne by all the partners
  2. Borne by the insolvent partner,
  3. Written off,
  4. Borne by the solvent partners
Answer and explanation

D: Borne by the solvent partners

Under the Garner v Murray rule, the remaining deficiency of an insolvent partner is borne by the solvent partners in their capital proportions. The insolvent partner cannot meet that shortfall.

Question 43

Alabede Ltd issued 50,000 ordinary shares with nominal value ₦1 each at ₦2.50 per share. Find total share premium.

  1. ₦125,000
  2. ₦100,000
  3. ₦75,000
  4. ₦50,000
Answer and explanation

C: ₦75,000

Share premium is the issue price above nominal value: ₦2.50 − ₦1.00 = ₦1.50 per share. Multiplying by 50,000 shares gives total premium of ₦75,000.
