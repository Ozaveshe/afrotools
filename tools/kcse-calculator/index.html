How the current KCSE seven-subject selection works

The earlier “best seven of eight” shortcut is no longer sufficient. Under the KNEC regular-candidate rule used from the 2023 examination, Mathematics must count. The calculator then selects the stronger of English and Kiswahili, followed by the five highest grades left in the pool. If the other language is strong enough, it can be one of those five.

Each counted subject carries 12 points for A through 1 point for E. The seven points are added to an aggregate from 7 to 84, then matched to KNEC’s published aggregate bands. This calculator displays the arithmetic and the selected subjects so the result can be checked.

Frequently asked questions

Why is Mathematics always counted?

KNEC’s current regular-candidate regulation makes Mathematics mandatory in the seven-subject overall grade selection.

Can I calculate KUCCPS weighted cluster points here?

No. KUCCPS explicitly advises graduates not to attempt it because the performance indices needed for the official calculation are supplied by KNEC to KUCCPS and are not given to individual graduates.

Does C+ automatically qualify me for a degree course?

No. A programme can also require specific subject grades and a competitive weighted cluster score. Cut-offs depend on the last competitively placed applicant, and placement also depends on choices and vacancies.
