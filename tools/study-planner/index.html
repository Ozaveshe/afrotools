🍅 Pomodoro Technique
Try 25 minutes of focused work followed by a short break. Adjust the interval if a different rhythm helps you concentrate.
🧠 Active Recall
After reading, close your notes and retrieve the main ideas from memory. Check what you missed, then try again later.
🔁 Spaced Repetition
Revisit material over several days instead of relying on one long session. Let your results guide how often each topic returns.
🌅 Match the task to your energy
Place demanding subjects in the part of the day when you focus best. That may be morning, afternoon, or evening.
📝 Daily Review
Begin with a brief check of earlier work, then spend most of the session on the current target.
💤 Sleep Matters
Protect a consistent sleep routine during revision. Avoid building a plan that depends on repeated overnight cramming.

Private weekly study timetable for exam preparation

Use the planner for school-leaving, entrance, professional, or university exams. Presets are editable subject samples, not official syllabus or paper-date records. Add the exact date for each paper from the relevant examination authority.

The planner gives higher-priority subjects a larger share of the complete sessions that fit your availability. You can track sessions, download a text copy, or use the browser print dialog for PDF. Saving is optional and stays on this device.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does subject priority affect the timetable?

High-priority subjects receive a larger share of the available sessions. The total never exceeds the complete sessions that fit your weekly capacity.

Can I save or export my timetable?

You can explicitly save it on this device, download a text copy, or use Print / PDF. Nothing is uploaded by the planner.

Are preset exam dates official paper dates?

No. Presets are editable subject samples. Confirm every exact paper date on the linked official timetable before relying on a countdown.
