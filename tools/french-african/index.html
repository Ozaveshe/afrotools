French usage is country- and register-specific

French usage differs between countries, cities, professions, generations and formal or informal settings. This compact phrasebook cannot represent all Francophone African varieties.

What the “African French” label proves

It proves only that 20 historical rows in this page carry that category label. The current dataset does not establish a country, community, profession, generation, or formal or informal register for those rows.

How to use the general rows

The other 75 rows can support general French recognition practice. They still do not prove that wording, pronunciation, or formality is preferred in a particular Francophone African community.

Before public or professional use

Confirm important wording with a speaker or qualified reviewer from the intended country and setting. The optional cloud translator also returns a draft to verify, not a country-specific cultural guarantee.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which countries does this dataset cover?

The 95-row dataset does not contain reliable per-entry country coverage. General-French rows and rows labelled “African French” are separated, and missing country or register provenance is shown as unverified.

Does this page verify a single “African French” variety?

No. Francophone African usage cannot be represented as one country-neutral variety. This route shows missing country and register provenance rather than assigning a row to a community without evidence.

Are pronunciation previews verified?

No. Listen controls use a synthetic browser voice. They do not verify pronunciation for any country, community, or register.
