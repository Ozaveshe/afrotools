How period math works

Resources: monthly income × months + period funding.
Expenses: monthly expense total × months + tuition and one-off costs.
Balance: resources − expenses.

  • A four-month term multiplies only fields labelled “per month.”
  • Term tuition and setup costs are added once.
  • Monthly equivalents divide the full period totals by the same entered month count.

Planning arithmetic only. The result does not predict prices, recommend savings, assess a loan or prove affordability. Add every cost you expect and allow for uncertainty outside this calculator.

Monthly costs and whole-period costs are different

A common budget error is multiplying tuition by every month or forgetting that monthly rent repeats through the term. This calculator assigns each input one cadence. Monthly resources and expenses repeat for the entered number of months; period funding, tuition and setup amounts occur once in the chosen window.

The monthly equivalent is a comparison view, not a payment schedule. A term fee may still be due upfront even when its monthly equivalent looks manageable. Keep due dates, payment timing and emergency costs in a separate cash-flow calendar.

Frequently asked questions

Where are the country and university averages?

They were removed because costs vary by institution, housing choice, academic calendar and student. The calculator now uses only entered amounts.

Should I enter an annual fee as a monthly amount?

No. Set the planning period that contains the fee and enter it once under tuition and school fees. Only use a monthly field if that amount is actually paid every month.

Does a positive balance mean the plan is affordable?

Not necessarily. It only means entered resources exceed entered expenses. Missing costs, due dates, emergencies and changes in income can alter the real outcome.
