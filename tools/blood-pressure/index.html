Blood pressure measurement check

Review two adult readings without turning a home measurement into a diagnosis. The card accounts for measurement setup and applies separate pregnancy and recent-postpartum safety boundaries.

Symptoms matter more than this card

Seek local emergency help now for chest pain, trouble breathing, new weakness or numbness, trouble speaking, seizure, fainting, or another severe sudden symptom. If pregnant or within 6 weeks after birth, urgent warning signs also include a severe or persistent headache, major vision change, pain below the ribs, vomiting or sudden swelling. Do not wait for this tool or for the pressure to fall.

Measurement setup

Before measuring

Make the reading interpretable

  1. Avoid exercise, smoking and caffeine for 30 minutes where practical; empty your bladder.
  2. Rest quietly for at least 5 minutes. Sit supported with feet flat and legs uncrossed.
  3. Use a validated upper-arm device and the correct cuff size on bare skin.
  4. Support the arm at heart level. Stay still and silent.
  5. Take two readings at least 1 minute apart and keep both.

Technique cannot explain away a very high reading or warning symptoms. Follow the urgent boundary shown by the tool.

Threshold boundary

Different contexts use different prompts

  • General adult: WHO's 140/90 mmHg clinic threshold needs readings on two different days for diagnosis. A home pair is only a snapshot.
  • Very high adult reading: over 180 systolic or over 120 diastolic requires a repeat and immediate clinical contact if it remains that high; emergency symptoms require emergency help.
  • Pregnancy or first 6 weeks after birth: 140 systolic or 90 diastolic prompts maternity contact; 160 systolic or 110 diastolic is a severe boundary needing urgent assessment.
