Seek immediate local emergency maternity care

Do not wait to complete this guide if any of these signs apply. Call the local emergency service or go to the nearest emergency maternity unit now.

  • Vaginal bleeding
  • Convulsions or fits
  • Severe headache with blurred vision
  • Severe abdominal pain
  • Fever with severe weakness
  • Fast or difficult breathing
  • A sudden or concerning change in fetal movement
  • Any symptom your maternity team told you needs urgent care

Urgent warning signs

WHO’s pregnancy and childbirth guide identifies emergency signs including bleeding, convulsions, severe headache with blurred vision, severe abdominal pain, fever with severe weakness, and difficult breathing.

ANC contact reference

WHO recommends a minimum eight-contact antenatal model. National programmes and individual care plans can alter timing or add contacts.

Population statistics are not personal predictions

WHO maternal mortality ratios describe population-level outcomes per 100,000 live births. They cannot calculate one person’s probability or determine individual care.

Questions about this guide

Does this guide calculate my chance of maternal death?

No. There is no validated personal mortality model here. The guide does not total points, calculate probability, or label a pregnancy low or high risk.

What should I do if I have a warning sign?

Seek immediate local emergency maternity care. Do not wait for a result, PDF or scheduled appointment. Local emergency numbers and referral routes differ, so use the service available where you are.

Does every country use the same ANC schedule?

No. The eight-contact model is a WHO reference. Confirm the current national schedule and your individual contact plan with a qualified local maternity professional.

What does the country selection change?

It labels the conversation guide with country context only. It does not apply a country mortality ratio, make a risk comparison or claim that services are free or available.
