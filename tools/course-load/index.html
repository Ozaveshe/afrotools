What the arithmetic means

Three separate checks

  1. Credit range: compares the course sum only with your entered min/max.
  2. Remaining credits: subtracts earned and current credits, assuming every current course is completed and counts.
  3. Weekly time: subtracts entered commitments from 168 calendar hours without a workload verdict.

Before registration

  • Confirm every prerequisite and co-requisite.
  • Check compulsory and elective group rules.
  • Check repeats, exclusions and transferred-credit treatment.
  • Get written approval for any exception.
  • Confirm aid, visa, housing and scholarship load rules separately.

Method and limitations

No generic overload diagnosis

The tool reports “below entered minimum,” “inside entered range,” or “above entered maximum.” These are comparisons with your own inputs, not an AfroTools recommendation. An institutional exception or programme-specific rule may change what is permitted.

No graduation date estimate

Credits remaining is arithmetic only. It cannot determine when courses will be offered, whether prerequisites are satisfied, whether current courses will be passed, or whether residency, GPA, capstone, placement and clearance requirements are complete.

No weekly workload diagnosis

The time inventory simply accounts for entered hours within a 168-hour week. Course difficulty, health, disability, care obligations, assessment timing and learning needs cannot be reduced to a universal threshold.

Course-load planning questions

Where should I get the minimum and maximum credits?

Use the current registration guide, programme handbook, student portal or written advice from the registry or department. Record the source and checked date in the planner.

Does “inside entered range” mean I should register?

No. You must still verify prerequisites, timetable clashes, course availability, programme structure and any personal or funding constraints.

Why does the tool not estimate my graduation year?

A division of remaining credits by an invented “optimal” load ignores course sequencing, failed or repeated courses, offering cycles, placements, capstones and institutional approval.
